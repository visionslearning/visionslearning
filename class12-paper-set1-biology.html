SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1
(i–x) MCQ Answers with reasoning
(i) (B) Spermiation — Release of sperms from Sertoli cells into the lumen of seminiferous tubules is called spermiation. (ii) (B) Law of purity of gametes — Each gamete carries only one allele for a trait; the paired alleles separate at meiosis — hence "purity of gametes". (iii) (C) Sense strand — The coding strand has the same sequence as the mRNA (except T→U). Also called sense strand or non-template strand. (iv) (C) DNA ligase — DNA ligase joins Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand by forming phosphodiester bonds. (v) (D) 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA) — CO₂ + RuBP → 2 molecules of 3-PGA. This is the first stable product of the Calvin cycle. (vi) (B) Nitrosomonas — Nitrosomonas converts NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ (first step of nitrification). Nitrobacter converts NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻. (vii) (B) Bacillus thuringiensis — Bt toxin gene from this bacterium is inserted into cotton to make it pest-resistant. (viii) (C) E.O. Wilson — coined the term 'biodiversity' in 1986. (ix) (C) tRNA — Transfer RNA (tRNA) carries amino acids to the ribosome and reads the codon — acts as adapter molecule. (x) (C) Semi-conservative — Meselson and Stahl (1958) showed each new DNA has one old strand + one new strand.
SECTION B — Very Short Answers 1 mark each
(i) Spermatogenesis
Spermatogenesis is the process of formation of spermatozoa (sperm cells) from spermatogonia in the seminiferous tubules of the testis. Path: Spermatogonium → Primary spermatocyte → Secondary spermatocyte → Spermatid → Spermatozoon
(ii) Test cross
A test cross is the crossing of an organism with an unknown genotype (possibly dominant phenotype) with a homozygous recessive individual to determine the genotype of the unknown. If all offspring show dominant phenotype → parent was homozygous dominant (TT) If half show recessive → parent was heterozygous (Tt)
(iii) Codon
A codon is a sequence of three consecutive nucleotides (triplet) on mRNA that codes for a specific amino acid or signals start/stop of translation. Example: AUG = Methionine (start codon); UGA = Stop codon Total codons = 4³ = 64; 61 code for amino acids, 3 are stop codons
(iv) Replication fork
The replication fork is the Y-shaped region formed when the DNA double helix unwinds and separates at the point of active replication. DNA polymerase adds nucleotides at this site. In eukaryotes, multiple replication forks form simultaneously (bidirectional replication).
(v) Photophosphorylation
Photophosphorylation is the synthesis of ATP from ADP + Pi using light energy in the chloroplast thylakoid membrane during the light reactions of photosynthesis. Types: Cyclic (only PS I involved, only ATP produced) and Non-cyclic (PS I + PS II, produces ATP + NADPH + O₂)
(vi) Nitrogen fixation
Nitrogen fixation is the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) to ammonia (NH₃) or nitrates, making it available to plants. N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16ADP + 16Pi (enzyme: nitrogenase) Examples: Rhizobium (symbiotic), Azotobacter (free-living)
(vii) Vector in rDNA technology
A vector is a DNA molecule (usually a plasmid or bacteriophage) used to carry and introduce foreign DNA into a host cell for cloning and expression. Properties: ability to replicate independently, must have restriction sites, selectable marker, origin of replication.
(ix) Secondary metabolites
Secondary metabolites are organic compounds produced by plants that are not directly involved in primary metabolic processes (growth, reproduction) but help in defence, pollination and competition. Examples: Alkaloids (morphine, caffeine), Terpenes (rubber, camphor), Phenolics (tannins), Resins
SECTION C — Short Answer Solutions 2 marks each
(ii) Mendel's law of dominance
Law of Dominance: When parents differing in a pair of contrasting characters are crossed, only one character (dominant) appears in F₁. The other character (recessive) is suppressed but not lost. Example: Tall (TT) × Dwarf (tt) → F₁: All Tall (Tt) Tallness (T) is dominant over dwarfness (t) The recessive 't' allele is present but not expressed in F₁ It reappears in F₂ in 1/4 proportion (tt)
(iii) DNA replication vs Transcription (4 differences)

(v) C₄ pathway — 2 plants, advantage
C₄ pathway (Hatch-Slack pathway): A modified form of photosynthesis where CO₂ is first fixed in mesophyll cells to form 4-carbon compounds (OAA, malate) before entering the Calvin cycle in bundle sheath cells. C₄ plants: Maize (Zea mays), Sugarcane (Saccharum), sorghum, Amaranthus Advantage: No photorespiration — CO₂ is concentrated in bundle sheath (high CO₂/O₂ ratio), so RuBisCO acts only as carboxylase. Highly efficient in hot, dry, high-light conditions.
(vi) Ammonification and nitrification
Ammonification: Decomposition of organic nitrogen (proteins, urea) in dead organisms and waste → NH₄⁺ (ammonium). Organic N → NH₄⁺ (by bacteria like Bacillus, Clostridium) Nitrification: Oxidation of ammonium to nitrites and nitrates by chemolithotrophic bacteria (two steps). Step 1: NH₄⁺ + 1½O₂ → NO₂⁻ + H₂O + 2H⁺ (by Nitrosomonas) Step 2: NO₂⁻ + ½O₂ → NO₃⁻ (by Nitrobacter) Final product: Nitrates (NO₃⁻) — absorbed by plants
(vii) Bt toxin and mode of action
Bt toxin is a protein (Cry protein) produced by the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis. The cry gene encoding this toxin has been inserted into crop plants (Bt-cotton, Bt-brinjal) to confer pest resistance. Mode of action: 1. Bt toxin is produced as an inactive protoxin (pro-Cry protein) in the transgenic plant. 2. When an insect pest (cotton bollworm, Helicoverpa) eats the plant, the alkaline gut dissolves the toxin crystal. 3. The activated toxin binds to specific receptors on the midgut epithelial cells. 4. This creates pores → cells swell and lyse → insect dies. Harmless to mammals (no activation in acidic stomach)
(ix) Restriction enzymes and sticky ends
Restriction enzymes (restriction endonucleases) are enzymes that cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences (restriction sites). They are called "molecular scissors" of genetic engineering. How they create sticky ends: Example: EcoRI recognises 5'−GAATTC−3' and cuts between G and A 5'—G↓AATTC—3' → 5'—G + AATTC—3' 3'—CTTAA↑G—5' → 3'—CTTAA + G—5' This creates single-stranded overhangs called "sticky ends" (cohesive ends) These sticky ends allow complementary foreign DNA to join using DNA ligase

SECTION D — Short Answer II Solutions 3 marks each
(ii) Dihybrid cross YYRR × yyrr
Parents: Yellow Round (YYRR) × Green Wrinkled (yyrr) F₁: All Yellow Round (YyRr) — by law of dominance F₂ (YyRr × YyRr): Each parent produces 4 gametes: YR, Yr, yR, yr F₂ phenotypic ratio: 9 Yellow Round : 3 Yellow Wrinkled : 3 Green Round : 1 Green Wrinkled Genotypic classes: 9 Y_R_ : 3 Y_rr : 3 yyR_ : 1 yyrr This confirms Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment — Y and R genes assort independently.
(iii) Transcription in prokaryotes
Transcription: Synthesis of RNA from DNA template using RNA polymerase. In prokaryotes, it occurs in the cytoplasm. Initiation: RNA polymerase (holoenzyme with σ factor) binds to the promoter region upstream of the gene. The σ factor recognises the −10 (Pribnow box) and −35 sequences. DNA strands separate to form transcription bubble. Elongation: RNA polymerase moves 3'→5' along template strand, synthesising RNA 5'→3'. Complementary ribonucleotides are added (A pairs with U, T pairs with A, G pairs with C). Core enzyme (without σ) is sufficient. Termination: RNA polymerase reaches a terminator sequence. Two types: (1) Rho-independent — RNA forms hairpin loop → polymerase detaches. (2) Rho-dependent — Rho protein unwinds RNA-DNA hybrid. Product = primary transcript (mRNA in prokaryotes, directly used for translation).
(vi) Recombinant DNA technology — steps
Step 1 — Gene isolation: The gene of interest (e.g., human insulin gene) is isolated using restriction enzymes that cut at specific sequences, creating sticky ends. Step 2 — Cloning vector preparation: A plasmid vector is cut with the same restriction enzyme, creating compatible sticky ends. Step 3 — Recombination: Foreign gene is inserted into the vector; DNA ligase seals the nicks → recombinant DNA (rDNA). Step 4 — Transformation: Recombinant plasmid is introduced into host cell (E. coli) by heat shock or electroporation. Step 5 — Selection: Transformed colonies identified using antibiotic resistance or blue-white screening. Step 6 — Expression: Host cells multiply; the inserted gene is expressed → desired protein produced at large scale. Example: Human insulin (Humulin) — produced in E. coli, purified and used to treat diabetes.
(vii) Biomagnification with DDT
Biomagnification: Progressive increase in concentration of non-biodegradable toxic substance at each successive trophic level of a food chain. DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane) — persistent organochlorine pesticide that accumulates in fatty tissues. DDT concentration through food chain: Water: 0.003 ppm Phytoplankton (producers): 0.04 ppm (×13) Zooplankton (1° consumers): 0.5 ppm (×12) Small fish (2° consumers): 2 ppm (×4) Large fish (3° consumers): 25 ppm (×12) Fish-eating birds (top): 75 ppm (×3) Effect: Eggshell thinning in birds (osprey, eagle) → reproductive failure. Now banned in most countries.
SECTION E — Long Answer Solutions 4 marks each
(ii) Mendel's monohybrid cross + incomplete dominance
Monohybrid cross: TT (Tall) × tt (Dwarf) F₁: All Tt (Tall) — only dominant trait expressed (Law of Dominance) F₁ × F₁: Tt × Tt → F₂ ratio: 1TT : 2Tt : 1tt F₂ phenotypic ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf This proves: (1) Law of Segregation — alleles separate in F₁. (2) Recessive trait reappears in F₂. Incomplete Dominance (Codominance of neither allele): In Snapdragon (Antirrhinum): Red (RR) × White (rr) → F₁: All Pink (Rr) Neither red nor white is completely dominant; heterozygote shows intermediate phenotype = Pink F₂ (Rr × Rr): 1 RR (Red) : 2 Rr (Pink) : 1 rr (White) → ratio 1:2:1 Genotypic ratio = Phenotypic ratio = 1:2:1
(iii) DNA structure + semi-conservative replication + mutation
DNA Structure (Watson-Crick, 1953): • Two polynucleotide chains wound around each other in antiparallel double helix • Sugar-phosphate backbone on outside; bases face inward • Base pairing: A=T (2H bonds), G≡C (3H bonds) — Chargaff's rules • Diameter: 2 nm; pitch: 3.4 nm; 10 bp per turn Semi-conservative replication: 1. Helicase unwinds and separates the two strands at origin 2. Primase adds RNA primer; DNA polymerase III adds complementary nucleotides (5'→3') 3. Leading strand synthesised continuously; lagging strand in Okazaki fragments 4. Primers removed; gaps filled by DNA Pol I; ligase joins fragments Result: 2 identical DNA molecules, each with 1 old + 1 new strand Proved by: Meselson-Stahl experiment (1958) using ¹⁵N/¹⁴N density gradient Mutation: A sudden, heritable change in DNA sequence. 1. Point mutation: Change in single nucleotide. Example: Sickle cell anaemia (A→T substitution, codon GAG→GTG; Glu→Val in haemoglobin) 2. Chromosomal mutation: Change in chromosome structure or number. Example: Down syndrome (trisomy 21 — 3 copies of chromosome 21)
(v) Recombinant DNA technology for insulin + PCR
Production of human insulin by rDNA: 1. The human insulin gene (two chains A and B) was synthesised chemically. 2. Inserted separately into plasmid vectors using restriction enzymes and ligase. 3. Recombinant plasmids introduced into E. coli. 4. E. coli expressed both chains → chains extracted and combined using disulfide bonds → active insulin. 5. Purified and marketed as Humulin (Eli Lilly, 1982) — first commercial rDNA product. PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) — invented by Kary Mullis (Nobel Prize 1993): Used to amplify a specific DNA sequence in vitro exponentially. Three steps (one cycle, ~30 cycles total): 1. Denaturation (94°C): DNA double helix is heated → two strands separate 2. Annealing (50–65°C): Primers (short synthetic DNA) bind to complementary sequences on each strand 3. Extension (72°C): Taq polymerase (heat-stable) synthesises new DNA from each primer using dNTPs After n cycles: 2ⁿ copies of target DNA (30 cycles → ~10⁹ copies) Uses: DNA fingerprinting, diagnosis of genetic diseases, forensics, COVID testing.
(vi) Ecosystem energy flow + conservation
Ecosystem: A structural and functional unit of nature where biotic and abiotic components interact. Energy flow through food chain (10% law — Lindeman, 1942): Producers (10,000 J) → Primary consumers (1,000 J) → Secondary consumers (100 J) → Tertiary consumers (10 J) Only 10% of energy is transferred at each trophic level; 90% is lost as heat, respiration and excretion. Energy flow is unidirectional — not cyclic (unlike nutrients). In situ conservation (conservation in natural habitat): 1. Wildlife sanctuaries — Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary, Kaziranga (one-horned rhino) 2. National Parks — Jim Corbett (tigers), Gir National Park (Asiatic lions) Ex situ conservation (outside natural habitat): 1. Zoological parks — captive breeding, Delhi Zoo, Mysore Zoo 2. Seed banks — NBPGR (New Delhi), Svalbard Global Seed Vault — store seeds at −196°C
