Centre of Mass

⚛️ Systems of Particles 📚 MH Study

The centre of mass is the unique point where the entire mass of a body — or a system of particles — appears to be concentrated.

It represents the mass-weighted average position of all matter in the system, and plays a central role in analysing motion and forces.

Slide 02 / 05  ·  Key Formulae

🔢 Formulae

Mathematical expressions for CM position
👥
Two Particles
xcm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂) / (m₁ + m₂)
Weighted average of positions of two masses along a line.
🔣
General Form (N Particles)
xcm = Σ(mᵢ xᵢ) / Σmᵢ
Summation over all particles in the system.
📐
3D Position Vector
r⃗cm = Σ(mᵢ r⃗ᵢ) / M
M = total mass of the system. Extends to all three dimensions.
Slide 03 / 05  ·  Important Points

⚡ Key Points

Must-know concepts
1
CM always lies closer to the heavier mass in a two-body system.
2
For a uniform symmetric body (sphere, ring, rod), CM coincides with the geometric centre.
3
CM can lie outside the material of an object (e.g., a ring or hollow sphere).
4
Position of CM is independent of the coordinate system — only mass distribution matters.
5
Without external forces, CM moves with constant velocity (or stays at rest).
Slide 04 / 05  ·  CM & Motion

🚀 CM & Momentum

Connecting Newton's Laws to the CM

⚡ Linear Momentum

  • Total momentum = total mass × velocity of CM
  • If no external force acts, total momentum is conserved.
  • CM continues in uniform motion.
P⃗total = M · v⃗cm

🔬 Newton's 2nd Law (System)

  • Net external force = total mass × acceleration of CM.
  • Internal forces cancel in pairs (Newton's 3rd Law).
  • Only external forces change the state of CM.
F⃗ext = M · a⃗cm

📋 Summary

Everything in a glance
