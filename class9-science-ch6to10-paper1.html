Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
Ch6 i. What are angiosperms?
Answer Angiosperms are flowering plants whose seeds are enclosed within a fruit (angiosperm = enclosed seed); they are the most advanced and largest group of plants. Examples: mango, rose, wheat, sunflower.
Ch7 ii. What is a food web?
Answer A food web is an interconnected network of multiple overlapping food chains in an ecosystem where an organism may feed on more than one type of organism and may also be eaten by multiple predators.
Ch8 iii. What is pasteurisation?
Answer Pasteurisation is the process of heating milk or other food to a specific temperature (72°C for 15 seconds or 63°C for 30 minutes) to kill harmful microbes without completely sterilising the food; named after Louis Pasteur.
Ch9 iv. Define biodiversity.
Answer Biodiversity refers to the variety of all living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) and their ecosystems on Earth, including genetic diversity, species diversity, and ecosystem diversity.
Ch10 v. What is a search engine? Give one example.
Answer A search engine is a software program that searches the internet for information based on keywords entered by the user and displays a list of relevant websites. Examples: Google, Bing, Yahoo.

Ch6 A. Classification of plants — Cryptogams and Phanerogams. (4M)
Plant Kingdom Plants are classified into two main groups: 1. Cryptogams (hidden reproductive organs, no seeds): • Thallophyta: Simple body, no roots/stem/leaves. E.g., Algae (Spirogyra, Ulva) • Bryophyta: 'Amphibians of plant kingdom'; grow in moist places; no vascular tissue. E.g., Moss (Funaria), Liverworts (Marchantia) • Pteridophyta: First vascular plants; reproduce by spores; no seeds. E.g., Fern, Horsetail (Equisetum) 2. Phanerogams (visible reproductive organs, bear seeds): • Gymnosperms: Naked seeds (not in fruit). E.g., Pine, Cycas, Pinus • Angiosperms: Seeds enclosed in fruit (most evolved). E.g., Mango, Wheat
📐 Draw classification chart: Kingdom Plantae → Cryptogams (Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta) and Phanerogams (Gymnosperms, Angiosperms)
Ch7 B. Energy flow in an ecosystem — food chain and 10% law. (4M)
Energy Flow Food Chain: A linear sequence showing how energy is transferred from one organism to another through feeding. Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle Trophic levels: Each feeding level. Producers = 1st trophic level; Primary consumers = 2nd; Secondary consumers = 3rd. 10% Law (Lindemann's Law): Only 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level. 90% is lost as heat, respiration, excretion. If grass has 10,000 J → Grasshopper gets 1,000 J → Frog gets 100 J → Snake gets 10 J → Eagle gets 1 J. Significance: Explains why food chains are short (3-4 levels) and why large predators are few in number.
📐 Draw energy pyramid showing 10,000 J → 1,000 J → 100 J → 10 J at each trophic level.
Ch8 C. Useful microbes — explain with examples how microbes help humans. (4M)
Useful Microbes 1. In Food Production: • Lactobacillus → converts milk to curd (yoghurt) • Yeast (Saccharomyces) → fermentation → bread making (CO₂ makes bread rise), alcohol production • Acetobacter → vinegar from alcohol 2. In Medicine: • Penicillium → Penicillin antibiotic (kills bacteria) • Bacteria used to produce insulin (for diabetes treatment) 3. In Agriculture: • Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules → fix nitrogen from air → fertilise soil • Decomposers break dead matter → enrich soil with nutrients 4. In Environment: • Bacteria decompose sewage in biogas plants → produce methane fuel
Ch9 D. Explain global warming — causes, effects and control measures. (4M)
Global Warming Definition: Rise in the average global temperature due to the increased concentration of greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O vapour) in the atmosphere — they trap outgoing infrared radiation. Causes: • Burning fossil fuels → CO₂ increase • Deforestation → less CO₂ absorbed • Industrial emissions, cattle farming (CH₄) Effects: • Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers • Rise in sea level → flooding of coastal areas • Extreme weather events (droughts, floods, cyclones) • Loss of biodiversity; extinction of species Control: • Reduce fossil fuel use → switch to renewable energy • Afforestation (plant more trees) • International agreements (Kyoto Protocol, Paris Agreement)

Ch6 A. Explain the characteristics of Gymnosperms and Angiosperms with two examples of each. Also distinguish between monocots and dicots. (4M)
Gymnosperms (Naked seed plants): • Seeds are not enclosed in a fruit (naked/exposed seeds) • Do not produce flowers; produce cones • Perennial, mostly evergreen trees • Examples: Pine (Pinus), Cycas, Cedar (Cedrus), Fir Angiosperms (Enclosed seed plants): • Seeds enclosed within a fruit (most evolved plants) • Produce flowers and fruits; most diverse plant group • Examples: Mango, Rose, Wheat, Sunflower, Coconut Monocots vs Dicots: • Monocots: 1 cotyledon, parallel venation, fibrous roots. E.g., Wheat, Maize, Grass, Bamboo • Dicots: 2 cotyledons, reticulate (network) venation, tap roots. E.g., Mango, Rose, Pea, Sunflower

Ch9 C. What is environmental management? Explain the importance of conservation of biodiversity and methods used. (4M)
Environmental Management: The process of protecting and sustainably managing the environment — its air, water, soil, and biodiversity — so that natural resources are available for present and future generations. Importance of Biodiversity Conservation: • Maintains ecological balance and food chains • Provides food, medicine, oxygen, raw materials • Prevents soil erosion and regulates climate • Each species plays a role in the ecosystem; loss of one affects others Methods of Conservation: 1. In-situ (on-site): Protect species in their natural habitat • Wildlife sanctuaries (e.g., Sanjay Gandhi NP) • National parks, Biosphere reserves • Sacred groves (Devrai in Maharashtra) 2. Ex-situ (off-site): Protect species outside natural habitat • Zoos, botanical gardens, aquaria • Seed banks, gene banks

Ch7 A student collected data from a forest ecosystem and drew the following food chain:

Grass → Rabbit → Fox → Tiger

Answer the following questions: (4M)
i. Identify the producer and consumers: Producer: Grass (makes its own food by photosynthesis) Primary consumer: Rabbit (herbivore, eats grass) Secondary consumer: Fox (carnivore, eats rabbit) Tertiary consumer: Tiger (carnivore, top predator) ii. If grass has 10,000 J of energy, how much energy does the tiger receive? Using 10% Law: Grass (10,000 J) → Rabbit (1,000 J) → Fox (100 J) → Tiger (10 J) iii. What happens if all foxes are removed from this ecosystem? Rabbit population increases rapidly (no predator) → Rabbits overgraze grass → Grass decreases → Less food for tigers too → Ecosystem becomes unbalanced. iv. What is the role of decomposers in this food chain? Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead organisms at every level → return nutrients to soil → grass and other plants absorb these nutrients → energy cycle continues.
