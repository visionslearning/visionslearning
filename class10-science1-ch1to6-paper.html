Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
Ch1 i. State Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation.
Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: F = Gm₁m₂/d².
Ch2 ii. What is meant by 'periodicity of properties' in the Modern Periodic Table?
The repetition of similar physical and chemical properties of elements at regular intervals when arranged in order of increasing atomic number is called periodicity of properties.
Ch3 iii. What is a balanced chemical equation?
A balanced chemical equation is one in which the number of atoms of each element is equal on both the reactant side and the product side, obeying the law of conservation of mass.
Ch4 iv. State Joule's law of heating.
Joule's law states that the heat produced in a conductor is directly proportional to the square of the current (I²), the resistance (R), and the time (t) for which the current flows: H = I²Rt.
Ch6 v. Define the refractive index of a medium.
The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (c) to the speed of light in that medium (v): n = c/v.

Write short notes. (Any 3 out of 5)
6 Marks
Ch1 A. Free fall and acceleration due to gravity (2 marks)
  • Free fall: When an object falls only under the influence of gravity (no air resistance), it is said to be in free fall.
  • During free fall, the only force acting is gravitational force.
  • Acceleration due to gravity (g): The acceleration produced in a freely falling object due to Earth's gravity is g = 9.8 m/s² (near Earth's surface).
  • g is directed downward (towards Earth's centre).
  • Equations of motion apply: v = u + gt; s = ut + ½gt²; v² = u² + 2gs
  • For a freely falling body from rest: u = 0, so v = gt and s = ½gt².
Ch2 B. Dobereiner's Triads and Newlands' Octave Law (2 marks)
  • Dobereiner's Triads (1829): Dobereiner grouped elements into triads (groups of 3) where the atomic mass of the middle element was approximately the arithmetic mean of the other two. E.g., Li (7), Na (23), K (39) — Na ≈ (7+39)/2 = 23. ✓
  • Limitation: Could not be applied to all known elements.
  • Newlands' Law of Octaves (1866): When elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic mass, every 8th element has properties similar to the 1st (like musical octaves). E.g., Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F, Na (Na similar to Li).
  • Limitation: Only worked up to Calcium; failed for heavier elements.
Ch3 C. Types of Chemical Reactions (2 marks)
  • 1. Combination: Two or more substances combine to form a single product. A + B → AB. E.g., 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
  • 2. Decomposition: A single compound breaks into two or more simpler substances. AB → A + B. E.g., 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electrolysis)
  • 3. Displacement: A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its salt solution. E.g., Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
  • 4. Double Displacement: Exchange of ions between two compounds. E.g., NaCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
  • 5. Oxidation-Reduction (Redox): Simultaneous oxidation and reduction. E.g., CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
Ch4 D. Magnetic Effect of Electric Current (Electromagnet) (2 marks)
  • When electric current flows through a conductor, it produces a magnetic field around it. This is the magnetic effect of electric current (discovered by Oersted, 1820).
  • A solenoid (coil of wire) carrying current acts like a bar magnet — it has N and S poles.
  • An electromagnet is made by winding an insulated wire around a soft iron core and passing current through it.
  • Magnetic field strength increases with: more turns in coil, higher current, soft iron core.
  • Applications: Electric bell, electric motor, loudspeaker, MRI machines, cranes for lifting iron/steel.
📐 Diagram: Solenoid with soft iron core, battery, switch — label N-pole, S-pole, magnetic field lines.
Ch5 E. Specific Heat Capacity (2 marks)
  • Definition: Specific heat capacity of a substance is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of that substance by 1°C (or 1 K).
  • Symbol: c (or s)   SI Unit: J/(kg·K) or J/(kg·°C)
  • Formula: Q = mcΔT where m = mass, ΔT = change in temperature.
  • Specific heat of water = 4200 J/(kg·K) — very high, makes water a good coolant.
  • Specific heat of iron = 470 J/(kg·K); copper = 385 J/(kg·K).
  • Higher specific heat → substance heats up and cools down slowly.

Ch1 A. State and explain Newton's Law of Gravitation. Derive the expression for gravitational force. (4 marks)
Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation:
  • Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force which is:
    → Directly proportional to the product of their masses (m₁ × m₂)
    → Inversely proportional to the square of the distance (d²) between them.
  • Mathematical Expression:
    F ∝ m₁ × m₂ / d²
    F = G × m₁ × m₂ / d²
    where G = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (Universal Gravitational Constant)
  • Characteristics of Gravitational Force:
    • It is always attractive (never repulsive).
    • It acts along the line joining the centres of two objects.
    • It is a long-range force — acts even over very large distances.
    • It does not need any medium — acts through vacuum.
    • It is the weakest of all fundamental forces.
  • Relationship between g and G:
    g = GM/R²
    M = mass of Earth, R = radius of Earth, g = 9.8 m/s²

Ch3 B. What is a chemical equation? Explain the importance of balancing a chemical equation with an example. (4 marks)
Chemical Equation:
  • A chemical equation is a symbolic representation of a chemical reaction using chemical formulae of reactants and products, separated by an arrow (→).
  • Reactants are written on the left side; products on the right.
  • E.g., Hydrogen + Oxygen → Water: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O (unbalanced)
Why Balance a Chemical Equation?
  • Based on the Law of Conservation of Mass: Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
  • The total mass of reactants = total mass of products.
  • Number of atoms of each element must be equal on both sides.
Example — Balancing H₂ + O₂ → H₂O:
  • Unbalanced: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O (Left: 2H, 2O; Right: 2H, 1O — O not balanced)
  • Step 1: Place 2 before H₂O → H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (Left: 2H, 2O; Right: 4H, 2O — H not balanced)
  • Step 2: Place 2 before H₂ → 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (Left: 4H, 2O; Right: 4H, 2O ✓)
  • 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O ✓ (Balanced)
Information from a Balanced Equation:
  • Reactants and products, their physical states (s, l, g, aq), molar ratios, heat changes (exo/endothermic).
Ch6 C. Explain refraction of light with laws. What is Total Internal Reflection? State its conditions and one application. (4 marks)
Refraction of Light:
  • The bending of light when it travels obliquely from one transparent medium to another of different optical density is called refraction.
  • Occurs because the speed of light differs in different media.
Laws of Refraction:
  • 1st Law: Incident ray, refracted ray and normal at point of incidence are all in the same plane.
  • 2nd Law (Snell's): sin i / sin r = n = constant
  • Air → Glass (denser): angle of refraction < angle of incidence (bends towards normal).
  • Glass → Air (rarer): angle of refraction > angle of incidence (bends away from normal).
Total Internal Reflection (TIR):
  • When light travels from denser to rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, light is completely reflected back into the denser medium.
  • Conditions: (1) Light must go from denser to rarer medium. (2) Angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle C, where sin C = 1/n.
  • Application: Optical fibres use TIR to transmit light signals over long distances without loss — used in internet cables and medical endoscopes.

6 Marks
Ch1 A. Find the gravitational force between two objects of masses 50 kg and 80 kg placed 2 m apart. (G = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²) (2 marks)
Given: m₁ = 50 kg, m₂ = 80 kg, d = 2 m, G = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹
Formula: F = Gm₁m₂ / d²
F = (6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ × 50 × 80) / (2)²
F = (6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ × 4000) / 4
F = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1000
∴ F = 6.673 × 10⁻⁸ N
Ch1 B. A stone is dropped from a height of 80 m. Calculate the time taken to reach the ground. (g = 10 m/s²) (2 marks)
Given: s = 80 m, u = 0 m/s, g = 10 m/s²
Formula: s = ut + ½gt²
80 = 0 + ½ × 10 × t²
80 = 5t²
t² = 80/5 = 16
t = √16
∴ Time = 4 seconds
Ch4 C. A resistor of 20 Ω is connected to a 100 V supply. Calculate the heat produced in 5 minutes. (2 marks)
Given: R = 20 Ω, V = 100 V, t = 5 min = 300 s
Current I = V/R = 100/20 = 5 A
Formula: H = I²Rt
H = (5)² × 20 × 300
H = 25 × 20 × 300
∴ Heat produced H = 1,50,000 J = 1.5 × 10⁵ J
Ch5 D. Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water from 25°C to 75°C. (Specific heat of water = 4200 J/kg·K) (2 marks)
Given: m = 2 kg, T₁ = 25°C, T₂ = 75°C, c = 4200 J/kg·K
ΔT = T₂ – T₁ = 75 – 25 = 50°C = 50 K
Formula: Q = mcΔT
Q = 2 × 4200 × 50
∴ Q = 4,20,000 J = 4.2 × 10⁵ J
Ch6 E. The speed of light in vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s. The refractive index of glass is 1.5. Find the speed of light in glass. (2 marks)
Given: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, n = 1.5
Formula: n = c / v → v = c / n
v = (3 × 10⁸) / 1.5
∴ Speed of light in glass = 2 × 10⁸ m/s
