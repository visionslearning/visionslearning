Introduction to Business Planning Process (Idea to Execution)
Q1. What is Business Planning? Define it.4 Marks
❓ Question

Define Business Planning. Explain the concept in brief.

✅ Answer
Business Planning is the process of setting objectives and determining the best course of action to achieve them. It involves identifying resources, setting goals, anticipating problems and creating a roadmap for the future of the business.
Business Planning is a systematic process that converts a business idea into an executable plan. It includes defining the business vision, analysing the market, setting financial targets, and laying down strategies to achieve goals within a specific time frame.
4 marks — definition + explanation

Describe the steps of the business planning process from idea to execution.

✅ Answer
The Business Planning Process involves the following stages:

1Idea GenerationIdentifying a business opportunity or problem to solve

3Setting ObjectivesDefining SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)

Q3. What is Strategy? What is Concept of Strategy in a Globalized World?4 Marks
❓ Question

Define strategy and explain the concept of strategy in the context of globalization.

✅ Answer
Strategy is a long-term plan of action designed to achieve a specific goal. In business, it means deciding what to do, how to do it, and with what resources — to gain competitive advantage.
Concept of Strategy in a Globalized World:
In today's globalized world, businesses must think beyond local markets. A global strategy means:
  • Going beyond borders — selling products/services internationally
  • Adapting to local cultures — "Think Global, Act Local" (Glocalization)
  • Competing globally — facing competition from international firms
  • Using global resources — sourcing materials, talent and technology worldwide
  • Digital strategy — using internet, e-commerce and digital platforms to reach global customers
Example: A company like Reliance or Infosys uses global strategy — serving customers worldwide while adapting to local needs.
4 marks — definition (2) + points (2)

Impact of Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation (LPG)
Q1. What is LPG? Explain Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation.6 Marks
❓ Question

Explain the meaning of LPG — Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation.

✅ Answer
In 1991, India introduced the New Economic Policy (NEP) based on LPG to overcome an economic crisis.
TermMeaningKey Points
Liberalisation (L) Reducing government restrictions on business and trade • Removed industrial licensing (License Raj)
• Allowed foreign companies to enter India
• Reduced import duties and quotas
• Free pricing of goods
Privatisation (P) Transfer of ownership from government to private sector • Disinvestment in public sector companies
• Private sector allowed in areas previously reserved for government
• More efficient management
• Example: Air India sold to Tata Group
Globalisation (G) Integration of India's economy with the world economy • Free flow of goods, services, capital and technology
• Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) allowed
• India joined WTO in 1995
• MNCs entered Indian market
6 marks — 2 marks each for L, P, G

Q2. What is Growth Strategy? Explain its types.4 Marks
❓ Question

Define growth strategy and explain its types with examples.

✅ Answer
Growth Strategy is a business strategy aimed at increasing the company's market share, revenue, product range, or geographic presence.
  • Market Penetration: Selling more of existing products in existing markets (e.g., Amul increasing milk sales in India)
  • Market Development: Entering new markets with existing products (e.g., Indian companies entering African markets)
  • Product Development: Launching new products for existing customers (e.g., Apple launching iPhone 16)
  • Diversification: New products in new markets (e.g., Reliance — oil company entering retail and telecom)
4 marks — definition (1) + 3 types with examples (1 each)
Q3. What is Retrenchment Strategy? What are its types?4 Marks
❓ Question

Explain retrenchment strategy and its different forms.

✅ Answer
Retrenchment Strategy involves reducing or downsizing the business to improve efficiency and return to profitability during difficult times.
  • Turnaround Strategy: Taking steps to reverse declining performance — cutting costs, improving efficiency, changing management (see next topic)
  • Divestiture: Selling a part or division of the business to generate cash
  • Liquidation: Shutting down the entire business and selling all assets
  • Harvesting: Maximising short-term profit from a declining product before withdrawing it
4 marks — definition (1) + 3 types (1 each)

Business Re-structuring & Turn-around Strategy
Q1. What is Business Re-structuring? Explain with example.4 Marks
❓ Question

Define business re-structuring and explain why and how it is done.

✅ Answer
Business Re-structuring is the process of making significant changes to the organisation's structure, operations, finances or workforce to improve efficiency, profitability or competitiveness.
Why it is done:
• Financial crisis — debts, falling profits
• Change in market conditions — new competition, technology changes
• Post-merger integration
• Improving operational efficiency

How it is done:
• Cost cutting — reducing unnecessary expenses
• Workforce reduction (layoffs) or reorganisation
• Selling non-core business units
• Mergers and acquisitions
• Technology upgrades

Example: Tata Steel restructured by closing inefficient plants in Europe and focusing on India's growing steel demand.
4 marks
Q2. What is Turn-around Strategy? Explain with a simple example.4 Marks
❓ Question

Explain turn-around strategy with a practical example.

✅ Answer
Turn-around Strategy is a type of retrenchment strategy used when a business is in decline or loss. The aim is to reverse the negative trend and make the company profitable again.
Steps in Turn-around Strategy:
1. Diagnosis — Find the root cause of failure (bad management, high costs, wrong products)
2. Crisis Stabilisation — Stop losses immediately; cut unnecessary expenses
3. Change of Leadership — Often a new CEO or management team is brought in
4. Stakeholder Management — Communicate with investors, banks, employees
5. Restructuring — Close unprofitable divisions; focus on core strengths
6. Recovery — Grow profitability again

Simple Example — Starbucks India: When Starbucks was losing money, they changed their menu to include Indian-flavoured drinks, hired local managers and reduced prices. This turned their losses into profits.
💡 Another Example: Maruti Suzuki in the 1990s was struggling. After restructuring and introducing the Alto model at a low price, it turned around and became India's top car brand.

Q2. What is UPI? How has it changed India's payment system?4 Marks
❓ Question

Explain UPI (Unified Payments Interface) and its impact on Indian commerce.

✅ Answer
UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is a real-time digital payment system developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). It allows instant transfer of money between bank accounts using a smartphone.
Key features of UPI:
• Works 24×7 including holidays
• Instant transfer — money reaches in seconds
• No need to share bank details — only VPA (Virtual Payment Address) like yourname@upi
• Free for users — no transaction charges
• Apps: PhonePe, Google Pay, BHIM, Paytm

Impact on Indian Commerce:
• Transformed India into a less-cash economy
• Small vendors (vegetable sellers, auto-rickshaw drivers) now accept digital payments
• India leads the world in UPI transactions (₹20+ lakh crore monthly)
• Boosted e-commerce growth significantly
• Financially included millions of unbanked citizens

Q3. What is Remote Work? What is Free-lancing? Explain with advantages.4 Marks
❓ Question

Explain remote work and free-lancing as contemporary business practices.

✅ Answer
Remote Work:
Remote work means working from a location other than the company's office — typically from home or any location with internet. It became widespread during and after COVID-19.
Advantages: No commute · Work-life balance · Lower office costs for companies · Access to global talent
Disadvantages: Isolation · Difficulty in team coordination · Blurring of work-life boundaries

Free-lancing:
Free-lancing means working independently for multiple clients without being permanently employed by any single company. A freelancer is their own boss.
Examples: Graphic designers, content writers, web developers, photographers
Platforms: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com
Advantages: Flexibility · Multiple income sources · No fixed office · Work from anywhere
Disadvantages: No job security · No employee benefits (PF, health insurance) · Irregular income
4 marks — 2 marks each
Q4. What is Ethical Commerce? Why is it important?4 Marks
❓ Question

Define ethical commerce and explain its importance in modern business.

✅ Answer
Ethical Commerce means conducting business honestly, fairly and responsibly — respecting customers, employees, environment and society while making profits.
  • Fair pricing — not exploiting customers with unfair prices
  • Honest advertising — not making false claims about products
  • Fair treatment of employees — equal pay, safe working conditions
  • Environmental responsibility — not polluting, using eco-friendly practices
  • Data privacy — protecting customer data in digital business
Importance: Ethical businesses build trust and reputation, attract loyal customers, reduce legal risks and contribute positively to society. In the long run, ethical businesses are more sustainable than those that prioritise profit over principles.

Introduction to ESG (Environmental, Social and Governance)
Q1. What is ESG? Explain Environmental, Social and Governance components.6 Marks
❓ Question

Define ESG and explain each of its three components with examples.

✅ Answer
ESG (Environmental, Social and Governance) is a framework used to evaluate how a company manages its impact on the environment, its responsibility to society, and the quality of its internal governance and leadership.
ComponentMeaningExamples
E — Environmental How a company manages its impact on the natural environment • Reducing carbon emissions
• Using renewable energy (solar, wind)
• Water conservation
• Waste management and recycling
• Avoiding deforestation
S — Social How a company manages relationships with employees, suppliers, customers and communities • Fair wages and safe working conditions
• Diversity and inclusion (gender, disability)
• Community development programmes
• Customer privacy and data protection
• Education and healthcare for communities
G — Governance How a company is led, controlled and held accountable — internal policies and ethics • Transparent financial reporting
• Independent Board of Directors
• Anti-corruption and anti-bribery policies
• Equal rights for shareholders
• Whistleblower protection
Why ESG matters: Investors worldwide now invest in companies with good ESG scores. Companies with strong ESG practices attract better investors, employees and customers — and face fewer regulatory and legal problems.
6 marks — 2 marks each for E, S, G

Business Ethics, CSR and Sustainability
Q1. What is Business Ethics? State its importance.4 Marks
❓ Question

Define business ethics and explain why it is important in modern commerce.

✅ Answer
Business Ethics refers to the application of ethical principles and moral standards to business activities. It involves making decisions that are not just legal but also right, fair and honest.
  • Honesty and Integrity: Truthful dealing with customers, suppliers and employees
  • Fairness: Equal treatment, no discrimination
  • Accountability: Taking responsibility for actions and decisions
  • Transparency: Open and clear communication
  • Respect: For people, environment and laws
Importance of Business Ethics:
1. Builds trust with customers and investors
2. Protects company from legal problems
3. Improves employee morale and loyalty
4. Creates a positive brand image
5. Ensures long-term business sustainability
4 marks — definition (1) + importance (3 points)
Q2. What is CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility)? Explain with examples.6 Marks
❓ Question

Define CSR. Explain why companies practice CSR with examples.

✅ Answer
Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) is the responsibility of a business to conduct itself ethically and contribute to the economic development while improving the quality of life of the workforce, local communities and society at large.
Under the Companies Act 2013 (India): Companies with net worth of ₹500 crore or more, or turnover of ₹1000 crore or more, must spend at least 2% of average net profit on CSR activities.

Q3. What is Sustainability in Business? Why is it important?4 Marks
❓ Question

Define business sustainability and explain its three pillars.

✅ Answer
Business Sustainability means meeting the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their needs. Sustainable businesses create long-term value without harming the environment or society.
The Three Pillars of Sustainability ("Triple Bottom Line"):
PillarFocusExample
People (Social)Fair treatment of employees, communities, societyFair wages, safe workplaces, community development
Planet (Environmental)Protecting the natural environmentReducing emissions, using renewable energy, waste recycling
Profit (Economic)Ensuring financial viability and long-term growthProfitable business that doesn't harm people or planet
💡 Sustainable businesses are increasingly preferred by investors, customers and employees. A business that ignores sustainability faces future regulatory penalties and reputational damage.
4 marks — definition (1) + 3 pillars with examples (3)
