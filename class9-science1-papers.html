Q.6 — Answer the following briefly (any 2)[2×4 = 8 Marks]
6.1
Explain the modern periodic table. State the law of periodicity.
4
Modern Periodic Law
"Physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers." — Moseley (1913)
Structure
18 groups (vertical columns) × 7 periods (horizontal rows). 118 elements. Groups 1–2 = s-block, 3–12 = d-block (transition), 13–18 = p-block, Lanthanides/Actinides = f-block.
Trends across period
Atomic radius ↓, Electronegativity ↑, Ionization energy ↑, Metallic character ↓ (left to right in a period).
Trends down group
Atomic radius ↑, Ionization energy ↓, Metallic character ↑, Electronegativity ↓.
∴ Modern PT: 18 groups × 7 periods. Properties periodic function of atomic number.
6.2
Explain the effects of electric current — heating, magnetic, and chemical effects.
4
Heating effect
When current flows through a conductor, electrical energy converts to heat energy (Joule heating). H = I²Rt. Used in: electric heater, iron, bulb, fuse wire. Formula: P = I²R.
Magnetic effect
Electric current creates magnetic field around conductor (Oersted's discovery). Used in: electromagnet, electric motor, generator, doorbell. Right-hand thumb rule for field direction.
Chemical effect
Passage of current through electrolyte causes chemical decomposition (electrolysis). Used in: electroplating (Ag, Au, Ni plating), purification of metals, extraction of metals.
∴ 3 effects of current: Heating (heater), Magnetic (motor), Chemical (electroplating).

Q.6 — Answer the following briefly (any 2)[2×4 = 8 Marks]
6.1
Explain the refraction of light through a glass slab. State Snell's law.
4
Snell's Law
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r where n₁, n₂ = refractive indices, i = angle of incidence, r = angle of refraction.
Through glass slab
When light enters glass slab from air: bends TOWARDS normal (denser medium). When exits back to air: bends AWAY from normal. The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but laterally displaced.
Lateral displacement
The perpendicular distance between incident and emergent rays. Increases with: thickness of slab, angle of incidence, refractive index.
Why bending
Speed of light decreases in denser medium → direction changes. In glass: v = c/n = 3×10⁸/1.5 = 2×10⁸ m/s.
∴ Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r. Glass slab → lateral displacement, emergent ray ∥ incident ray.
6.2
Explain metallurgical processes: Concentration, Roasting, Smelting, Refining.
4
Concentration
Removal of gangue (impurities) from ore to increase metal content. Methods: Gravity separation, froth flotation, magnetic separation, chemical leaching.
Roasting
Heating sulphide ore in excess air → oxide ore + SO₂. 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂. Converts sulphide to oxide for easier reduction.
Smelting
Heating ore with coke (carbon) in a furnace → metal + slag. Reduction process. ZnO + C → Zn + CO.
Refining
Purification of crude metal. Electrolytic refining most common — crude metal as anode, pure metal as cathode, metal salt solution as electrolyte.
∴ Metallurgy steps: Concentration → Roasting → Smelting → Refining.

5B
Short note: Methods of heat transfer — Conduction, Convection, Radiation.
3
Conduction
Heat transfer through solids by vibration of particles (without actual movement of particles). Good conductors: metals. Poor conductors (insulators): wood, rubber, glass. e.g. Heating metal rod at one end → other end becomes hot.
Convection
Heat transfer in liquids and gases by actual movement of particles (convection currents). Hot fluid rises (less dense), cool fluid sinks. e.g. Sea breeze, boiling water, room heater.
Radiation
Transfer of heat energy as electromagnetic waves (infrared) without any medium. Fastest method. e.g. Heat from Sun reaches Earth through vacuum. Solar cooker, Thermos flask work on radiation principle.
∴ Conduction (solids, vibration), Convection (fluids, movement), Radiation (no medium, EM waves).
Q.6 — Answer the following briefly (any 2)[2×4 = 8 Marks]
6.1
Explain types of chemical reactions with examples: combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement.
4
Combination
Two or more substances combine to form one product. A + B → AB. e.g. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (magnesium burns in oxygen)
Decomposition
One compound breaks down into two or more simpler products. AB → A + B. e.g. 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electrolysis); CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (thermal)
Displacement
More reactive element displaces less reactive from its salt. A + BC → AC + B. e.g. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu (iron displaces copper)
Double displacement
Exchange of ions between two compounds. AB + CD → AD + CB. e.g. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃ (white precipitate)
∴ 4 types: Combination (+), Decomposition (break), Displacement (replace), Double displacement (exchange).
6.2
Explain gravitation — difference between gravity and gravitation. State factors affecting gravitational force.
4
Gravitation
The universal force of attraction between any two masses in the universe. Acts between ANY two objects (Earth-Moon, Sun-planets). Given by Newton's law: F = Gm₁m₂/d²
Gravity
The gravitational force exerted specifically by the Earth on objects on or near its surface. F = mg where g = 9.8 m/s². Gravity is a special case of gravitation.
Factors affecting F
  • Directly proportional to m₁ × m₂ (mass)
  • Inversely proportional to d² (distance squared)
  • Independent of medium between objects
  • Always attractive — never repulsive
g varies with
Altitude (decreases), Depth (decreases), Latitude (higher at poles), Shape of Earth (slightly oblate)
∴ Gravitation = universal force between any two masses. Gravity = Earth's pull. F = Gm₁m₂/d².
Q.7 — Solve the numericals (any 2)[2×3 = 6 Marks]
7.1
Find the image distance for a convex lens of focal length 15 cm when object is placed 30 cm from the lens.
3
Given
f = +15 cm (convex), u = −30 cm (object left of lens, sign convention)
Formula
1/v − 1/u = 1/f
Solution
1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/15 + 1/(−30) = 2/30 − 1/30 = 1/30 → v = +30 cm (real, on other side)
∴ v = +30 cm. Image is real, inverted, same size (since u = 2f).
7.2
Three resistors of 4Ω, 6Ω and 12Ω are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.
3
Given
R₁ = 4Ω, R₂ = 6Ω, R₃ = 12Ω, parallel combination
Formula
1/Rₚ = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
Solution
1/Rₚ = 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 2/12 + 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2 → Rₚ = 2 Ω
∴ Equivalent resistance in parallel = 2 Ω (always less than smallest individual resistance)
7.3
An object of height 4 cm is placed 20 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Find image distance and height.
3
Given
h = 4 cm, u = −20 cm, f = −10 cm (concave)
Image distance
1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−10) − 1/(−20) = −1/10 + 1/20 = −2/20 + 1/20 = −1/20 → v = −20 cm
Magnification
m = −v/u = −(−20)/(−20) = −1 → h' = m × h = −1 × 4 = −4 cm (real, inverted, same size)
∴ v = −20 cm (real, in front of mirror). Image height = −4 cm (inverted, same size).
