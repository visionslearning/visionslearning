3 · Tenses — The Complete Chart
🟦 PRESENT TENSES
TenseStructureUse / Signal WordsExample
Simple PresentS + V₁(s/es) + OHabits, facts, universal truths · always, usually, every dayShe eats an apple daily.
Present ContinuousS + is/am/are + V-ingAction happening now · now, at this moment, lookHe is reading a book.
Present PerfectS + has/have + V₃Completed with present effect · just, already, yet, ever, never, recentlyI have finished my work.
Present Perfect ContinuousS + has/have + been + V-ingAction started in past, still going · since, forShe has been studying for 2 hours.
🟥 PAST TENSES
TenseStructureUse / Signal WordsExample
Simple PastS + V₂ + OCompleted past action · yesterday, ago, last, in 2010He went to school.
Past ContinuousS + was/were + V-ingAction in progress at a past time · while, when, at that timeShe was sleeping when I called.
Past PerfectS + had + V₃Action completed before another past action · before, after, already, by the timeHe had left before she arrived.
Past Perfect ContinuousS + had + been + V-ingAction ongoing up to a point in past · for, since (+ past reference)They had been waiting for an hour.
🟩 FUTURE TENSES
TenseStructureUse / Signal WordsExample
Simple FutureS + will/shall + V₁Decision/prediction · tomorrow, next week, soonI will come tomorrow.
Future ContinuousS + will + be + V-ingAction in progress at future time · at this time tomorrowShe will be working at 9 AM.
Future PerfectS + will + have + V₃Completed before a future time · by, by the timeI will have finished by Monday.
Future Perfect ContinuousS + will + have + been + V-ingDuration up to a future time · for (+ future point)She will have been teaching for 10 years.

4 · Active & Passive Voice
🔄 How to Change Active → Passive
  1. Object of active sentence becomes the Subject of passive sentence.
  2. Use suitable form of to be (is/am/are/was/were/has been/have been/will be…).
  3. Main verb becomes V3 (past participle).
  4. Subject of active sentence becomes Object with "by" (or is omitted if unimportant).
📋 Passive Voice — All Tenses
TenseActivePassive
Simple PresentShe writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
Simple PastHe ate the food.The food was eaten by him.
Simple FutureThey will do the work.The work will be done by them.
Pres. ContinuousShe is writing a letter.A letter is being written by her.
Past ContinuousHe was eating food.Food was being eaten by him.
Present PerfectShe has written a letter.A letter has been written by her.
Past PerfectHe had finished work.Work had been finished by him.
ImperativeOpen the door.Let the door be opened.

⚠️ When subject is "someone/people/one" — omit "by ___" in passive.
⚠️ Voice — Points to Remember
  • Only transitive verbs (verbs with an object) can be made passive.
  • Intransitive verbs (run, sleep, die) cannot be made passive.
  • Modal passives: can/could/should/must/may + be + V3
    She must write it → It must be written.
  • Imperative passive: Let + object + be + V3
💡 In exams: always check tense first → choose correct 'be' form → put V3.
5 · Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)
🗣️ Rules for Changing Direct → Indirect
  1. Remove the inverted commas. Use a conjunction (that / to / if / whether / what / when…).
  2. Change the pronoun according to the subject of reporting verb.
  3. Change the tense of the reported speech (backshift) — if reporting verb is in past.
  4. Change adverbs of time and place (this→that, here→there, now→then, today→that day…).

❓ Types of Sentences in Narration
  • Statement: Use that
    He said, "I am happy." → He said that he was happy.
  • Question (Yes/No): Use if / whether
    She asked, "Are you ready?" → She asked if I was ready.
  • Question (Wh-): Use the same wh- word
    He asked, "Where do you live?" → He asked where I lived.
  • Command/Request: Use to + V1 (ordered/requested/advised/told)
    "Sit down." → He told me to sit down.
  • Exclamatory: Convert to statement + exclaimed with joy/sorrow
    "How beautiful!" → She exclaimed that it was very beautiful.

🏆 Board Exam Quick Tips — Class 9
  • In Tenses — look for signal words (since, for, ago, just, already, yet, still).
  • In Passive — remember the 4 steps: object → subject, choose 'be', add V3, subject → 'by object'.
  • In Narration — first identify type of sentence (statement/question/command), then change.
  • In Narration — pronoun change rule: 1st person changes acc. to subject of reporting verb; 2nd person changes acc. to object; 3rd person stays same.
  • Sentences: Simple has one subject + one predicate. Compound uses co-ordinating conjunctions. Complex uses subordinating conjunctions.
  • Adjective degrees: Positive → Comparative → Superlative. Ex: good → better → best.

2 · Active & Passive Voice — Complete
🔄 4-Step Method
  1. Object of active → New Subject
  2. Correct form of 'be' (based on tense + new subject)
  3. Main verb → V3
  4. Original subject → 'by + object pronoun'
💡 'by' phrase can be omitted when subject is unknown, obvious, or unimportant (e.g., "People say", "Someone has stolen").

👤 Pronoun Change Rules
📌 1st person (I, we, me, us, my, our) → changes according to subject of reporting verb.
📌 2nd person (you, your, yours) → changes according to object of reporting verb.
📌 3rd person (he, she, it, they) → no change.

He said to me, "I love your work." → He told me that he loved my work.
(I→he [subject], your→my [object of reporting verb = me])
🔁 No Tense Change — When?
✅ Tense does NOT change when:
  • Reporting verb is in Present or Future tense.
    She says, "I am happy." → She says that she is happy.
  • The reported speech is a universal truth or fact.
    He said, "The sun rises in the east." → He said that the sun rises in the east.
  • The reported speech is a historical fact.
    She said, "India got independence in 1947." → She said that India got independence in 1947.
  • Reported speech contains must for rules/necessity — stays as must.

5 · Transformation of Sentences
🔃 Affirmative ↔ Negative
AffirmativeNegative
He is always late.He is never early.
All students passed.No student failed. / Not a single student failed.
She is too weak to run.She is so weak that she cannot run.
As soon as he came, she left.No sooner had he come than she left.
Both Ravi and Raj came.Neither Ravi nor Raj was absent.
🔃 Assertive ↔ Interrogative
AssertiveInterrogative
Everyone loves their motherland.Who does not love their motherland?
Nobody can deny this truth.Can anybody deny this truth?
It is impossible to do this.Is it possible to do this?
He is the best player.Is he not the best player?
💡 Affirmative assertive → Negative interrogative & vice versa.
🔃 Simple ↔ Compound ↔ Complex
TypeExample
SimpleDespite being sick, she attended school.
CompoundShe was sick but she attended school.
ComplexAlthough she was sick, she attended school.

SimpleOn arriving home, he ate food.
ComplexWhen he arrived home, he ate food.
🔃 Exclamatory ↔ Assertive
ExclamatoryAssertive
What a beautiful garden!It is a very beautiful garden.
How bravely he fought!He fought very bravely.
What lovely weather!The weather is very lovely.
💡 What + a/an + adj + noun → Assertive: It is a very + adj + noun.
How + adj/adv → Assertive: S + V + very + adj/adv.

8 · Conditionals (If-Clauses)
🎯 4 Types of Conditional Sentences
TypeMeaningIf-clauseMain clauseExample
Type 0 (Zero)Universal / Scientific truthSimple PresentSimple PresentIf you heat water to 100°C, it boils.
Type 1 (Real)Possible future conditionSimple Presentwill + V1If it rains, we will stay home.

🏆 Board Exam Tips — Class 10
  • In Tenses — always find the signal word first; it tells you the tense directly.
  • In Voice — identify tense → apply correct passive formula → change pronouns.
  • In Narration — identify sentence type first → apply correct conjunction → backshift tense → change pronouns → change time/place words.
  • In Clauses — find the main verb and subordinating conjunction to identify clause type.
  • In Transformation — meaning must remain exactly the same — only structure changes.
  • In Degrees — "No other…as…as" (Positive), "more than any other" (Comparative), "the most" (Superlative).
  • In Conditionals — never use "will" in the if-clause of Type 1.
  • Revise irregular verbs (V1/V2/V3) — they appear in tenses, voice and narration questions.
