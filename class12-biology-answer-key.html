SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10
(i) Fertilisation in human females occurs in the
Answer: (B) Fallopian tube
Fertilisation — the fusion of sperm and ovum — normally occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube (oviduct). The fertilised egg (zygote) then travels to the uterus for implantation.
(ii) Phenotypic ratio in F₂ of dihybrid cross
Answer: (C) 9:3:3:1
In a dihybrid cross (AaBb × AaBb), the F₂ phenotypic ratio is 9:3:3:1 (9 dominant for both : 3 dominant for 1st only : 3 dominant for 2nd only : 1 recessive for both).
(iii) Enzyme used in PCR
Answer: (B) Taq polymerase
Taq polymerase (from Thermus aquaticus) is a heat-stable DNA polymerase used in PCR as it can withstand the high denaturation temperatures (94–96°C).
(iv) Correct sequence of Central Dogma
Answer: (A) DNA → RNA → Protein
Central Dogma of Molecular Biology (Francis Crick, 1958): Genetic information flows from DNA → RNA (transcription) → Protein (translation).
(v) Darwin's finches — example of
Answer: (D) Adaptive radiation
Darwin's finches on the Galapagos Islands show adaptive radiation — divergence from a common ancestor into many species with different beak shapes adapted to different food sources.
(vi) Term 'gene' coined by
Answer: (C) Johanssen
The term 'gene' was coined by Wilhelm Johanssen in 1909 to describe the unit of heredity.
(vii) NOT a greenhouse gas
Answer: (C) N₂
Nitrogen (N₂) is NOT a greenhouse gas. Greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O (nitrous oxide), water vapour, ozone, CFCs.
(viii) Ozone depletion mainly caused by
Answer: (A) CFCs
Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are the main cause of ozone depletion. CFCs release Cl atoms in stratosphere which catalytically destroy ozone: Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂.
(ix) First hormone produced by rDNA technology
Answer: (B) Insulin
Human insulin (Humulin) was the first hormone produced by recombinant DNA technology (Eli Lilly, 1982) — the human insulin gene was inserted into E. coli bacteria.
(x) Vaccination provides which immunity
Answer: (D) Artificial active immunity
Vaccination introduces antigens (killed/weakened pathogens) into the body, stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies — this is artificial active immunity.
SECTION B — Very Short Answers (1 mark each) Any 8 of 12
(i) Define fertilisation.
Fertilisation is the process of fusion of a male gamete (sperm) with a female gamete (ovum/egg) to form a diploid zygote (2n). In humans, it occurs in the fallopian tube.
(ii) Parthenogenesis
Parthenogenesis is the development of a new organism from an unfertilised egg without the involvement of a male gamete. Example: Honey bee (drones develop from unfertilised eggs), aphids, Komodo dragon.
(iii) Phenotype and genotype
Phenotype: The observable physical, biochemical and physiological characteristics of an organism resulting from the expression of genes. Example: Tall plant, red flower. Genotype: The genetic constitution (allelic combination) of an organism. Example: TT, Tt, tt.
(iv) Law of independent assortment
Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment: During gamete formation, the alleles of different genes assort independently of each other — genes on different chromosomes are inherited independently. Applies only to genes on different (non-homologous) chromosomes.
(v) Restriction enzyme
Restriction enzymes (restriction endonucleases) are enzymes that cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences called restriction sites. Example: EcoRI recognises 5'−GAATTC−3' and cuts between G and A. Use: They are the "molecular scissors" in recombinant DNA technology.
(vi) Define biodiversity
Biodiversity is the variety and variability of life on Earth — including the diversity of species (species diversity), genes within a species (genetic diversity) and ecosystems (ecosystem diversity). Term coined by: E.O. Wilson (1986)
(vii) Eutrophication
Eutrophication is the process by which a water body becomes enriched with mineral nutrients (especially nitrates and phosphates), leading to excessive growth of algae and aquatic plants, depletion of dissolved oxygen, and death of fish and other aquatic animals.
(viii) Biomagnification
Biomagnification (biological magnification) is the progressive increase in concentration of a pollutant/toxic substance at successive trophic levels of a food chain. Example: DDT — concentration increases from water → algae → small fish → large fish → birds.
(ix) Ecological pyramid
An ecological pyramid is a graphical representation showing the quantitative relationship (number, biomass or energy) between producers and consumers at successive trophic levels of a food chain. Types: Pyramid of numbers, pyramid of biomass, pyramid of energy
(x) Vaccine — definition and types
A vaccine is a biological preparation that provides active acquired immunity against a specific disease by introducing antigens (killed/weakened pathogens or their components) that stimulate antibody production without causing disease. Types: Live attenuated (BCG, polio oral), killed/inactivated (cholera), subunit (Hepatitis B), toxoid (tetanus), mRNA (COVID-19)
(xi) Transgenic animals
Transgenic animals are animals in which a foreign gene (transgene) has been artificially introduced into their genome using recombinant DNA technology. Example: Rosie — the first transgenic cow that produced human protein-enriched milk; transgenic mice for cancer research.
(xii) Mutation — definition and types
Mutation is a sudden, heritable change in the nucleotide sequence of DNA (gene mutation) or in the structure/number of chromosomes. Types: (1) Gene/point mutation — change in a single nucleotide; (2) Chromosomal mutation — change in chromosome structure or number (deletion, inversion, translocation, aneuploidy, polyploidy).
SECTION C — Short Answer I (2 marks) Any 8 of 12
(i) Double fertilisation in angiosperms
Double fertilisation is a unique feature of angiosperms in which two separate fertilisation events occur: 1st fertilisation: One sperm fuses with the egg cell → diploid zygote (2n) → develops into embryo 2nd fertilisation: Second sperm fuses with the two polar nuclei (2n) → triploid primary endosperm nucleus (3n) → develops into endosperm (food for embryo) Significance: Ensures nutritive tissue (endosperm) is formed only when fertilisation occurs — no wastage of energy. Discovered by: S.G. Nawaschin (1898)
(ii) Mendel's Law of Dominance
Law of Dominance: When two homozygous parents with contrasting traits are crossed, the trait that appears in F₁ hybrid is called dominant and the trait that is hidden is called recessive. Example: Tall (TT) × Dwarf (tt) → F₁: All Tall (Tt) Tallness is dominant; dwarfness is recessive The recessive allele is not lost — it reappears in F₂ generation (25%).

(v) Adaptive radiation — Darwin's finches
Adaptive radiation is the process in which organisms from a single ancestral species rapidly diversify into many new species, each adapted to a different ecological niche (food source, habitat). Darwin's Finches: When Darwin visited the Galapagos Islands, he found 14 species of finches, all descended from a single ancestral species from South America. Each species developed a different beak shape adapted to its food: • Large ground finch: thick beak for seeds • Warbler finch: thin beak for insects • Woodpecker finch: long beak for drilling wood This shows adaptive radiation from a common ancestor into diverse ecological niches.
(vi) Food chain, food web, 10% energy law
Food chain: A linear sequence showing the transfer of energy and nutrients from one organism to another through feeding relationships. Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle Food web: An interconnected network of multiple food chains in an ecosystem. 10% Energy Law (Lindeman, 1942): Only 10% of energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next; 90% is lost as heat, respiration and waste. If grass has 10,000 J → grasshopper gets 1,000 J → frog gets 100 J → snake gets 10 J This is why food chains rarely exceed 4–5 trophic levels.
(viii) Hardy-Weinberg principle
Hardy-Weinberg Principle: In a large random-mating population, allele frequencies and genotype frequencies remain constant from generation to generation (genetic equilibrium), provided certain conditions are met. Equation: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 where p = frequency of dominant allele, q = frequency of recessive allele Conditions for genetic equilibrium: 1. Large population size (no genetic drift) 2. Random mating (no sexual selection) 3. No mutation 4. No gene flow (no migration) 5. No natural selection
(xii) Transcription in eukaryotes + types of RNA
Transcription is the synthesis of RNA using DNA as template. In eukaryotes it occurs in the nucleus. Steps: 1. Initiation: RNA polymerase binds to promoter region of DNA 2. Elongation: RNA polymerase unwinds DNA and synthesises mRNA by adding complementary nucleotides (5'→3') 3. Termination: RNA polymerase reaches terminator sequence → pre-mRNA released 4. Processing: 5' capping, 3' polyadenylation, splicing (introns removed, exons joined) → mature mRNA Types of RNA and functions: • mRNA (messenger RNA) — carries genetic code from nucleus to ribosome • tRNA (transfer RNA) — brings specific amino acids to ribosome during translation • rRNA (ribosomal RNA) — structural and catalytic component of ribosomes
SECTION D — Short Answer II (3 marks) Any 6 of 10
(i) Dihybrid cross YYRR × yyrr — F₁ and F₂
Parents: Yellow Round (YYRR) × Green Wrinkled (yyrr) F₁: All YyRr (Yellow Round) — due to dominance of Y over y and R over r F₂: YyRr × YyRr → 4 types of gametes each: YR, Yr, yR, yr F₂ phenotypic ratio: 9 Yellow Round : 3 Yellow Wrinkled : 3 Green Round : 1 Green Wrinkled Genotypic ratio: 1YYRR : 2YYRr : 2YyRR : 4YyRr : 1YYrr : 2Yyrr : 1yyRR : 2yyRr : 1yyrr This confirms Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment — Y and R segregate independently
(ii) Structure of DNA double helix
DNA double helix — proposed by James Watson and Francis Crick in 1953 (based on X-ray data by Rosalind Franklin). Structure: 1. Two polynucleotide strands wound around a common axis in antiparallel fashion (one 5'→3', other 3'→5') 2. Sugar-phosphate backbone is on the outside 3. Nitrogenous bases face inward, held by hydrogen bonds 4. Base pairing: Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) — 2H bonds; Guanine (G) pairs with Cytosine (C) — 3H bonds 5. Diameter of helix = 2 nm; pitch = 3.4 nm; 10 base pairs per turn 6. Strands are complementary and antiparallel
(iv) Recombinant DNA technology — steps
Recombinant DNA technology (rDNA): Joining DNA from two different sources and introducing it into a host organism to obtain a desired product. Tools needed: Restriction enzymes (cut DNA), ligase (join DNA), vectors (plasmid/bacteriophage) Steps: 1. Gene cloning: Isolate desired gene; cut with restriction enzyme creating sticky ends; insert into vector (plasmid) using ligase → recombinant DNA 2. Transformation: Introduce recombinant plasmid into host cell (usually E. coli) by heat shock or electroporation 3. Selection of recombinants: Use antibiotic resistance markers or blue-white selection to identify transformed cells containing the gene 4. Host cells multiply → gene expressed → desired protein produced Example: Production of human insulin (Humulin)
(vi) Structure and functions of placenta + hormones
Placenta is a disc-shaped temporary organ formed by the interlocking of embryonic (chorionic villi) and uterine (decidua basalis) tissues during pregnancy. Functions: 1. Nutrition — supplies oxygen and nutrients from mother's blood to foetus 2. Excretion — removes CO₂ and waste from foetal blood 3. Protection — acts as immunological barrier; maternal antibodies (IgG) pass to foetus 4. Endocrine — secretes hormones Hormones secreted by placenta: 1. hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) — maintains corpus luteum; basis of pregnancy test 2. Progesterone — maintains pregnancy, prevents uterine contractions 3. Estrogen — stimulates uterine growth, prepares breast for lactation
(ix) Global warming — causes and effects
Global warming is the gradual increase in Earth's average surface temperature due to the enhanced greenhouse effect caused by rising concentrations of greenhouse gases. Causes: 1. Burning of fossil fuels → CO₂ ↑ 2. Deforestation → less CO₂ absorption 3. Agriculture and livestock → CH₄ and N₂O ↑ 4. Industrial processes → various greenhouse gases Effects on ecosystem: 1. Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers → rise in sea level → flooding of coastal areas 2. Extreme weather events — droughts, floods, cyclones 3. Disruption of ecosystems — coral bleaching, species extinction 4. Shift in crop zones and agricultural productivity 5. Spread of vector-borne diseases (malaria, dengue) to new regions
SECTION E — Long Answers (4 marks) Any 4 of 6
(i) Female reproductive system diagram + oogenesis flowchart
Female reproductive system includes: ovaries (pair), fallopian tubes (oviducts), uterus (womb), cervix, vagina. Ovaries: Primary female gonads — produce eggs (ova) and hormones (estrogen, progesterone) Fallopian tubes: Carry egg from ovary to uterus; site of fertilisation Uterus: Pear-shaped organ where foetus develops; inner lining = endometrium Oogenesis — production of female gametes: Oogonium (2n) → [mitosis] → Primary oocyte (2n) → [Meiosis I — arrested at prophase I] → Secondary oocyte (n) + 1st polar body (n) → [Meiosis II — completes only at fertilisation] → Ovum (n) + 2nd polar body (n) → Mature ovum (egg cell) Key: Primary oocyte is arrested in prophase I from fetal stage until puberty. Meiosis II only completes if fertilised.
(ii) Central Dogma + genetic code properties
Central Dogma of Molecular Biology (Francis Crick, 1958): DNA → (transcription) → RNA → (translation) → Protein In some viruses: RNA → (reverse transcription) → DNA (retroviruses like HIV) Genetic Code: The set of rules by which the sequence of nucleotides in an mRNA is translated into the amino acid sequence of a protein. A triplet of three nucleotides (codon) codes for one amino acid. Example: AUG = Methionine (start codon) Properties of genetic code: 1. Triplet — 3 nucleotides (codon) code for 1 amino acid (64 codons for 20 amino acids) 2. Degenerate — more than one codon may code for the same amino acid (e.g., UUU and UUC both code for Phe) 3. Universal — same codon codes for the same amino acid in almost all organisms 4. Non-overlapping — codons do not share nucleotides; each nucleotide belongs to only one codon 5. Commaless — no punctuation between codons; read continuously
(iii) Hardy-Weinberg principle + disturbing factors
Hardy-Weinberg principle states that in an ideal large, random-mating population, allele and genotype frequencies remain constant generation after generation (genetic equilibrium). Mathematical expression: p + q = 1 (allele frequencies) p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (genotype frequencies) p = frequency of A; q = frequency of a; p² = AA; 2pq = Aa; q² = aa Conditions required: 1. Very large population, 2. Random mating, 3. No mutation, 4. No migration, 5. No natural selection Factors that disturb H-W equilibrium (= forces of evolution): 1. Mutation — changes allele frequencies by producing new alleles 2. Natural selection — favours certain genotypes, changing allele frequencies 3. Genetic drift — random changes in allele frequencies in small populations (Founder effect, Bottleneck) 4. Gene flow (migration) — movement of individuals brings in or removes alleles 5. Non-random mating — sexual selection, assortative mating changes genotype frequencies
(iv) Biotechnology + rDNA tools + applications
Biotechnology: Technology based on the use of biological systems (living organisms or their components) to develop products and processes for human welfare. Recombinant DNA technology: The technology of joining DNA segments from different sources to create new combinations of genetic material. Four major tools: 1. Restriction endonucleases — "molecular scissors" — cut DNA at specific sequences 2. DNA ligase — "molecular glue" — joins DNA fragments 3. Vectors (plasmids, bacteriophage) — carry and replicate foreign DNA in host 4. Host cells — bacteria, yeast, plant/animal cells that receive and express recombinant DNA Applications: Agriculture: Bt crops (Bacillus thuringiensis toxin gene) — pest-resistant cotton (Bt-cotton), herbicide-tolerant crops, Golden Rice (Vitamin A enriched) Medicine: Human insulin (Humulin), HGH (Human Growth Hormone), vaccines (Hepatitis B), gene therapy (replacing defective genes), monoclonal antibodies
(v) Ecosystem — biotic components + eutrophication
Ecosystem: A structural and functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with their physical environment. Biotic components: 1. Producers (autotrophs) — synthesise food by photosynthesis. Example: green plants, algae, cyanobacteria 2. Consumers (heterotrophs):    • Primary consumers (herbivores) — eat plants: grasshopper, rabbit    • Secondary consumers (carnivores) — eat herbivores: frog, small birds    • Tertiary consumers — eat secondary consumers: eagle, lion 3. Decomposers (saprotrophs) — break down dead organic matter: bacteria, fungi → recycle nutrients Eutrophication: Process: Excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates from fertilisers, sewage) → algal blooms → algae die → decomposers consume O₂ → dissolved oxygen depletion → fish and aquatic animals die Causes: Agricultural runoff, untreated sewage, industrial effluents Effects: Loss of aquatic biodiversity, drinking water contamination, foul smell, "dead zones"
(vi) Biodiversity hotspots + ex-situ conservation
Biodiversity hotspot: A biogeographic region that is both a significant reservoir of biodiversity AND is threatened with destruction. Criteria: ≥1500 endemic plant species and has lost ≥70% of its original habitat. There are 34 global hotspots. Two hotspots in India: 1. Western Ghats & Sri Lanka — rich in endemic plants, amphibians, reptiles; threatened by deforestation and agriculture 2. Indo-Burma (Eastern Himalayas) — diverse freshwater biodiversity and endemic plants; threatened by habitat loss Ex-situ conservation: Conservation of endangered species OUTSIDE their natural habitat. 1. Zoological parks (zoos) — captive breeding of endangered animals; e.g., Project Tiger reserves, Asiatic lion in Gir 2. Botanical gardens — collection and preservation of plant species; e.g., Kew Gardens, Ooty Botanical Garden 3. Seed banks / Gene banks — preservation of seeds, gametes, embryos at −196°C; e.g., NBPGR (New Delhi), Svalbard Global Seed Vault
