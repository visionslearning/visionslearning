What is a dot map?

∴ A dot map is a distributional map in which each dot represents a specific, fixed number/value of a phenomenon to show its geographical distribution.

1
∴ Endogenetic movements are forces that originate from within the Earth and build up its surface by causing earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, folding and faulting.

1
∴ A glacier is a large, slow-moving mass of ice and snow formed in high mountain areas where snowfall accumulates and compresses over many years.

1
∴ Precipitation is the process by which condensed water vapour in clouds falls to Earth's surface in various forms — rain, snow, sleet, hail, frost, or dew.

1
∴ Ocean currents are large-scale, continuous, directional movements of ocean water caused by wind, temperature differences, Earth's rotation, and salinity variations.
Q.4 — Short note (any ONE) [4 Marks]
4A
Short note: Fold Mountains
4
Formation
Fold mountains are formed at convergent plate boundaries when two tectonic plates collide. The compressional forces cause rock layers to fold upward, forming mountain ranges.
Examples
Himalayas (India–Asia collision), Alps (Europe), Rockies (North America), Andes (South America), Atlas (Africa).
Features
Highest mountains on Earth | Sedimentary rock layers | Contains marine fossils at great heights | Young fold mountains = still rising.
Significance
Climate barriers | Source of rivers | Rich mineral deposits | Tourist destinations.
4B
Short note: Delta (Alternative)
4
Definition
A delta is a fan-shaped depositional landform formed at the mouth of a river where it meets the sea or a lake.
Formation
As river reaches sea, velocity decreases → river deposits its load of silt/sediment → builds up triangular land area called delta.
Examples
Ganga-Brahmaputra delta (Sundarbans) — world's largest | Nile delta | Mississippi delta.
Significance
Fertile agricultural land | Dense population | Good for fishing | Often flood-prone.
Q.5 — Give the Geographical Reason (any 2) [6 Marks — 3 each]
5.1
The western slopes of the Western Ghats receive heavy rainfall.
3
Reason
The Western Ghats act as a barrier to the moist South-West Monsoon winds blowing from the Arabian Sea.
Process
The moist winds are forced to rise over the Ghats → they cool as they rise → moisture condenses → heavy orographic (relief) rainfall on the windward (western) side.
Result
The leeward (eastern) side — Deccan Plateau — falls in the rain shadow area and receives very little rainfall. e.g. Mumbai gets 2200mm; Pune gets only 700mm.
∴ Western Ghats = orographic barrier → heavy windward rain → rain shadow on eastern side.
5.2
The Dead Sea has extremely high salinity.
3
Reason 1
The Dead Sea is a landlocked water body — it has no outlet. Water enters (from River Jordan) but cannot flow out.
Reason 2
The region has an extremely hot, arid climate with very high evaporation rates. As water evaporates, the salts dissolved in it are left behind.
Result
Over thousands of years, salts have accumulated, making its salinity approximately 340‰ (about 10 times more than normal ocean water = 35‰). Nothing survives — hence "Dead" Sea.
∴ No outlet + high evaporation → salt accumulates → extremely high salinity (340‰).
5.3
Earthquakes occur frequently along the Ring of Fire. (Alternative)
3
Reason
The Ring of Fire is a horseshoe-shaped zone around the Pacific Ocean where several tectonic plates meet (Pacific, North American, Philippine, Eurasian plates).
Process
At these convergent and transform plate boundaries, plates collide, slide, and subduct. This causes enormous stress which is released as earthquake energy.
Result
~90% of world's earthquakes and ~75% of volcanoes occur here. Countries: Japan, Philippines, Indonesia, Chile, USA (California).
∴ Ring of Fire = multiple plate boundaries → frequent stress release → earthquakes and volcanoes.
Q.6 — Answer the following briefly (any 2) [8 Marks — 4 each]
6.1
Explain the types of Distributional Maps.
4
1. Dot Map
Uses dots where each dot represents a fixed quantity. Dense dots = high concentration. Used for: population distribution, crop production. e.g. One dot = 10,000 people.
2. Choropleth
Uses shading (dark = high, light = low) to show distribution per administrative unit. Used for: population density, literacy rate. e.g. Dark shading = more density.
3. Isopleth
Uses lines joining places of equal value. Types: Isohyet (rainfall), Isotherm (temperature), Isobar (pressure), Contour (elevation). Used in: climate and weather maps.
4. Flow Map
Shows movement of goods, people, transport. Arrow width proportional to quantity. Used for: migration, trade routes.
∴ 4 types: Dot map, Choropleth, Isopleth, Flow map — each used for different types of data.
6.2
Explain the properties of sea water.
4
1. Salinity
Average salinity = 35‰. Causes: dissolved salts from rivers, volcanic activity, evaporation. Increases near tropics; decreases near poles and river mouths.
2. Temperature
Surface water warmer (near equator = 28°C); polar water cold (0°C). Decreases with depth. Thermocline = zone of rapid temperature change.
3. Density
Sea water denser than fresh water due to dissolved salts. Density increases with salinity and decreasing temperature. Denser water sinks → drives ocean circulation.
4. Colour
Ocean appears blue due to scattering and absorption of sunlight. Red Sea = reddish (algae). Yellow Sea = yellow (loess sediment). Black Sea = dark (low oxygen).
∴ Sea water properties: Salinity (35‰), Temperature, Density, Colour — all interrelated.
