⚖️ MOI & Theorems
Q1
State and prove the theorem of parallel axes for moment of inertia.
🔴 Every Year 3–4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The moment of inertia of an object about any axis is equal to the sum of its moment of inertia about a parallel axis passing through the centre of mass, and the product of the mass of the object and the square of the perpendicular distance between the two axes.

I_O = I_C + Mh²

where I_C = MOI about CM axis, M = total mass, h = distance between the two axes.

Proof: Let O be any axis and C be a parallel axis through CM at distance h. For mass element dm at point D, let NC = perpendicular from D to axis through C.

I_O = ∫(DO)² dm = ∫([DC]² + 2·NC·h + h²) dm = ∫(DC)² dm + 2h∫NC·dm + h²∫dm ∫(DC)² dm = I_C | ∫dm = M | ∫NC·dm = 0 (definition of CM)
∴ I_O = I_C + Mh² ✓

Example: MOI of uniform rod about one end = ML²/12 + M·(L/2)² = ML²/12 + ML²/4 = ML²/3

Q2
State and prove the theorem of perpendicular axes. State its limitation. Give one application.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The moment of inertia of a laminar object about an axis perpendicular to its plane is equal to the sum of its moments of inertia about two mutually perpendicular axes in the plane of the object, all three axes being concurrent.

I_z = I_x + I_y

Proof: For mass element dm at point P(x, y), distances from x, y, z axes are y, x, √(x²+y²).

I_x = ∫y²dm, I_y = ∫x²dm I_z = ∫(x²+y²)dm = ∫x²dm + ∫y²dm = I_y + I_x
∴ I_z = I_x + I_y ✓

Limitation: Applicable ONLY to laminar (flat/2D) objects — ring, disc, plate. NOT applicable to 3D objects like sphere or cylinder.

Application: For uniform disc rotating about diameter: I_z = ½MR², by symmetry I_x = I_y = I_diameter. ∴ I_diameter = ½ × ½MR² = ¼MR²

Q3
What is radius of gyration? Give its physical significance. Write its expression.
🟠 Medium Freq 2 Marks 2023 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Radius of Gyration (K): The radius of gyration of an object about a given axis is defined as the distance from the axis at which, if all the mass of the object were concentrated, the moment of inertia would be the same as the actual MOI.

I = MK² → K = √(I/M)

Physical significance: K tells us how the mass is distributed from the axis. Larger K → mass is farther from axis → greater resistance to rotation.

Comparison: For a ring: K = R (all mass at radius R); For a disc: K = R/√2 (mass distributed from 0 to R, so K < R). Ring has greater K and hence greater MOI than disc of same mass and radius.

🌀 Angular Momentum & Conservation
Q4
State and explain the law of conservation of angular momentum. Give two examples from daily life.
🔴 Every Year 3–4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The angular momentum of a system remains constant (conserved) if no external unbalanced torque acts on it.

Proof: Torque τ = dL/dt. If net external τ = 0 → dL/dt = 0 → L = Iω = constant.

I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂ (or I₁n₁ = I₂n₂)

When I decreases → ω increases; when I increases → ω decreases to keep Iω constant.

Example 1 — Ballet dancer / ice skater:

Dancer spins with arms outstretched (large I, slow ω). On pulling arms in → I decreases sharply → ω increases — spins much faster. When arms outstretched again → I increases → ω decreases to prevent slipping.

Example 2 — Diver:

On diving board, stretches body → large I. After jumping off, folds body (tuck position) → I decreases → ω increases → more rotations in air. Stretches before entering water → smooth entry.

Q5
Derive the expression for angular momentum of a rigid body in terms of moment of inertia: L = Iω
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2023 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Consider a rigid body of N particles rotating with angular velocity ω about a fixed axis. Masses m₁, m₂…m_N at distances r₁, r₂…r_N from axis.

Linear speeds: v₁ = r₁ω, v₂ = r₂ω, … v_N = r_Nω

Angular momentum of first particle: L₁ = p₁·r₁ = m₁v₁·r₁ = m₁r₁²ω

Similarly: L₂ = m₂r₂²ω, L₃ = m₃r₃²ω, ... L_N = m_Nr_N²ω

All are directed along axis (same direction) so they add algebraically:

L = (m₁r₁² + m₂r₂² + … + m_Nr_N²)ω = (Σmᵢrᵢ²)ω
L = Iω where I = Σmᵢrᵢ² = moment of inertia ✓

This is analogous to p = mv of linear momentum, with I replacing m and ω replacing v.

Q6
Derive the expression for torque in terms of moment of inertia: τ = Iα
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Consider a rigid body of N particles rotating with angular acceleration α about a fixed axis. Masses m₁, m₂…m_N at distances r₁, r₂…r_N from axis.

Tangential acceleration: a₁ = r₁α, a₂ = r₂α … a_N = r_Nα

Tangential force on first particle: f₁ = m₁a₁ = m₁r₁α

Torque on first particle: τ₁ = f₁·r₁ = m₁r₁²α

Similarly: τ₂ = m₂r₂²α, τ₃ = m₃r₃²α, … τ_N = m_Nr_N²α

Total torque (all in same direction along axis):

τ = (m₁r₁² + m₂r₂² + … + m_Nr_N²)α = (Σmᵢrᵢ²)α
τ = Iα where I = Σmᵢrᵢ² ✓

This is analogous to F = ma for translational motion, with I replacing m and α replacing a.

🎡 Vertical Circular Motion
Q7
Derive expressions for minimum speeds at the uppermost and lowermost points of a vertical circular motion (mass on string). Also prove that T_B − T_A = 6mg.
🔴 Every Year 4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

At uppermost point A: Both tension T_A and weight mg act downward (toward centre of circle).

T_A + mg = mv_A²/r ... (1)

For minimum speed, T_A = 0 (string just taut, not slack):

v_A(min) = √(rg)

At lowermost point B: T_B acts upward (toward centre), mg acts downward.

T_B − mg = mv_B²/r ... (2)

Energy conservation from B (bottom) to A (top), height = 2r:

½mv_B² = ½mv_A² + mg(2r) v_B² = v_A² + 4rg ... (3)

Substituting v_A(min) = √(rg) in equation (3):

v_B²(min) = rg + 4rg = 5rg
v_B(min) = √(5rg)

Prove T_B − T_A = 6mg:

Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):

T_B − T_A − 2mg = m(v_B² − v_A²)/r

Using equation (3): v_B² − v_A² = 4rg

T_B − T_A − 2mg = m·(4rg)/r = 4mg
∴ T_B − T_A = 6mg ✓ (independent of speed v and radius r)
Q8
What is the minimum speed required at the top of a convex over-bridge? Derive the expression.
🟠 Medium Freq 2–3 Marks 2023 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

At the top of a convex over-bridge (radius r), the vehicle moves in a circular path. Forces: weight mg (downward) and Normal reaction N (upward).

Net downward force = centripetal force:

mg − N = mv²/r

As speed v increases, N decreases. For the vehicle to maintain contact, N ≥ 0.

At maximum speed, N = 0 (just losing contact):

mg − 0 = mv_max²/r
v_max = √(rg)

If speed exceeds √(rg), the vehicle loses contact with the road and becomes airborne.

🚗 Banked Road & Applications
Q9
What is banking of roads? Derive the expression for the angle of banking: tan θ = v²/rg. What is the most safe speed on a banked road?
🔴 Every Year 4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Banking of roads: Raising the outer edge of a curved road with respect to inner edge by an angle θ so that vehicles can safely negotiate the curve without solely depending on friction.

Forces on vehicle (ignoring friction): (i) Weight mg vertically downward, (ii) Normal reaction N perpendicular to banked road surface.

Since motion is horizontal circular, net vertical force = 0 and net horizontal force = centripetal force.

Resolving N:

N cosθ = mg ... (vertical balance) ... (1) N sinθ = mv²/r ... (centripetal force) ... (2)

Dividing equation (2) by equation (1):

sinθ/cosθ = v²/rg
tan θ = v²/rg ✓

Most safe speed (no friction needed): v_s = √(rg tanθ)

Banking angle for given speed: θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

Significance: At speed v_s, the normal reaction alone provides the centripetal force — friction is not required. Vehicle is safe even on wet/icy roads.

Q10
Derive an expression for the period of a conical pendulum: T = 2π√(L cosθ / g)
🔴 High Freq 3–4 Marks 2023 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Bob of mass m, string of length L makes angle θ with vertical. Radius of circular motion r = L sinθ.

Forces on bob: T₀ (tension along string) and mg (weight downward).

T₀ cosθ = mg ... (vertical balance) ... (1) T₀ sinθ = mω²r = mω²L sinθ ... (centripetal) ... (2)

From equation (2): T₀ = mω²L

Dividing (2) by (1): tanθ = ω²L sinθ / g → ω² = g / (L cosθ)

ω = √(g / L cosθ)
Period T = 2π/ω = 2π√(L cosθ / g)
Frequency n = 1/T = (1/2π)√(g / L cosθ)

Note: String can NEVER become horizontal (θ = 90°) because that would require infinite frequency and infinite energy.

🎯 Rolling Motion
Q11
Derive the expression for kinetic energy of a body rolling without slipping: E = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

For a body rolling without slipping, two simultaneous motions occur:

(i) Translational motion of centre of mass with speed v

(ii) Rotational motion about its own axis with angular speed ω

Rolling condition: v = ωR → ω = v/R

Total KE = Translational KE + Rotational KE E = ½Mv² + ½Iω² I = MK² (K = radius of gyration) and ω = v/R: E = ½Mv² + ½(MK²)(v/R)² E = ½Mv² + ½Mv²(K²/R²)
E = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)

K²/R² values: Ring = 1 | Disc = ½ | Solid sphere = 2/5 | Hollow sphere = 2/3

For solid sphere: E = ½Mv²(1 + 2/5) = 7/10 Mv²

Translational : Rotational : Total = 1 : K²/R² : (1 + K²/R²)

Q12
Derive an expression for linear acceleration of a body rolling down an inclined plane without slipping.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Body of mass M, radius R, MOI = MK² rolls down incline of angle θ, starting from rest. After travelling distance s along plane (height h = s sinθ):

By energy conservation: Loss in PE = Gain in rolling KE

Mgh = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²) v² = 2g·s sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)

Using v² = u² + 2as (u = 0, so v² = 2as):

2as = 2g·s sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)
a = g sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)

Compare with pure sliding: a_slide = g sinθ. Since (1 + K²/R²) > 1, rolling acceleration < sliding acceleration.

Solid sphere (K²/R² = 2/5): a = 5g sinθ/7 — rolls fastest

Ring (K²/R² = 1): a = g sinθ/2 — rolls slowest

Q13
Derive the expression for the moment of inertia of a uniform disc about its central axis (own axis). I = ½MR²
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Consider a uniform disc of mass M, radius R, rotating about its own axis. Surface density σ = M/πR².

Imagine the disc as consisting of many concentric rings. Consider one ring of radius r and width dr.

Area of ring = 2πr·dr Mass of ring: dm = σ × 2πr·dr = (M/πR²) × 2πr·dr = (2M/R²)·r·dr

As it is a ring at radius r, its MOI about the central axis:

dI = dm·r² = (2M/R²)·r³·dr

Total MOI by integrating from r = 0 to r = R:

I = ∫₀ᴿ (2M/R²)·r³·dr = (2M/R²) × [r⁴/4]₀ᴿ = (2M/R²) × R⁴/4
I = ½MR² ✓
🚗 UCM Applications
Q14
What is centripetal force? What provides centripetal force in the case of: (i) car on horizontal road, (ii) satellite around earth, (iii) electron around nucleus?
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Centripetal Force: The resultant of all real forces acting on a body performing circular motion, directed towards the centre of the circular path, is called centripetal force.

CPF = mω²r = mv²/r

Important: "Centripetal" is NOT the name of a new force — it is an adjective describing the direction (centre-seeking) of the resultant real force.

What provides CPF in different cases:

(i) Car on horizontal road: Force of static friction between tyres and road provides centripetal force.

(ii) Satellite around earth: Gravitational force of earth on the satellite provides centripetal force.

(iii) Electron around nucleus: Electrostatic (Coulomb) force of attraction between electron and nucleus provides centripetal force.

Q15
What is centrifugal force? Is it a real force? Explain with merry-go-round example.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2023 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Centrifugal Force: When observed from a rotating (non-inertial) frame of reference, a body appears to experience a force directed away from the centre of rotation. This outward pseudo force is called centrifugal force.

CFF = +mω²r (directed outward, away from centre)

Is it real? Centrifugal force is a non-real (pseudo) force, but it is NOT imaginary — it can be measured by instruments. It arises due to the centripetal acceleration of the rotating frame of reference.

Merry-go-round example:

When sitting on a merry-go-round with eyes closed, you feel a push outward (centrifugal force). This is because you are in the rotating frame. In the inertial (ground) frame, only centripetal force (inward) exists. In the rotating frame, centrifugal force (outward) balances centripetal force → you appear at rest.

Two ways to write force equation:

Inertial frame: Resultant force = −mω²r (toward centre) Rotating frame: mω²r + Σreal forces = 0 (pseudo force balances real)
Q16
What is the "Well of Death" (मौत का कुआं)? Derive the expression for minimum speed of a vehicle inside it.
🔴 High Freq 3–4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Well of Death: A vertical cylindrical wall of radius r inside which a vehicle (motorcycle) is driven in horizontal circles. It is a popular circus stunt.

Forces on the vehicle:

(i) Normal reaction N — acts horizontally toward centre (centripetal direction)

(ii) Weight mg — acts vertically downward

(iii) Static friction f_s — acts vertically upward (prevents vehicle from sliding down)

Equations:

N = mv²/r (centripetal force equation) f_s = mg (vertical balance — friction supports weight)

Condition: f_s ≤ μ_s N (friction cannot exceed its maximum value)

mg ≤ μ_s × mv²/r g ≤ μ_s v²/r → v² ≥ rg/μ_s
v_min = √(rg / μ_s)

Vehicle must maintain at least this speed, otherwise friction cannot support its weight → vehicle slides down.

Q17
Why does a cyclist lean inward while taking a turn on an unbanked road? Derive the angle of inclination with the vertical.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Why lean inward: When a two-wheeler takes a turn, friction provides centripetal force (horizontal). But the centrifugal force (in rotating frame) and weight create a torque that tends to topple the vehicle outward. To prevent toppling, the rider must lean inward so that the net torque about the contact point is zero.

Derivation of lean angle:

Forces on rider+cycle (treated as particle): Weight mg downward, Normal reaction N upward, Friction f = mv²/r inward.

At equilibrium in rotating frame, taking torques about contact point = 0:

In rotating frame: centrifugal force = mv²/r (outward, horizontal) Weight = mg (downward) The resultant of these two must pass through the contact point. tan θ = (mv²/r) / mg = v²/rg
θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

θ is the angle of inclination of the cycle with the vertical. This is the same formula as banking angle — a two-wheeler leans in lieu of banking.

Four-wheeler: Does not need to lean because its wide base and four contact points create a restoring torque that prevents toppling.

Q18
What is the maximum speed of a vehicle on an unbanked circular road? Derive the expression v_max = √(μ_s·r·g).
🟠 Medium Freq 2–3 Marks 2023 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

On a horizontal circular road of radius r, the centripetal force is provided by static friction between tyres and road.

f_s = mv²/r (friction = centripetal force) N = mg (normal reaction balances weight)

Maximum friction: f_s(max) = μ_s × N = μ_s × mg

At maximum speed v_max, friction is at its limit:

μ_s·mg = mv_max²/r
v_max = √(μ_s·r·g)

Beyond this speed, friction cannot provide enough centripetal force → vehicle skids outward.

🔢 Board Numericals
N1. A fan is rotating at 90 rpm. It stops after 21 rotations when switched OFF. Find the time taken to stop (assume constant frictional torque). [Textbook Ex 1.1]
n₀ = 90 rpm = 1.5 rps | ω₀ = 2π×1.5 = 3π rad/s | ω = 0 | θ = 2π×21 = 42π rad
α = (ω² − ω₀²)/2θ = (0 − 9π²)/(2×42π) = −3π/28 rad/s²
t = (ω − ω₀)/α = (0 − 3π)/(−3π/28) = 28 s
∴ t = 28 seconds ✓
N2. A flywheel (uniform disc): mass = 20 kg, diameter = 50 cm, rotating at 1200 rpm. Find KE and MOI about tangent in its plane. (π² = 10)
M = 20 kg | R = 0.25 m | n = 20 rps | π² = 10
(i) I_z = ½MR² = ½×20×0.0625 = 0.625 kg·m²
ω = 2πn = 40π rad/s
KE = ½Iω² = ½×0.625×(40π)² = ½×0.625×1600π² = 5000 J

(ii) I_diameter = ¼MR² = 0.3125 kg·m² (by perpendicular axis theorem)
I_tangent = I_diameter + MR² (parallel axis, h = R)
= 0.3125 + 20×0.0625 = 0.3125 + 1.25 = 1.5625 kg·m²
∴ KE = 5000 J; I_tangent = 1.5625 kg·m² ✓
N3. A ceiling fan (I = 2 kg·m²) attains 60 rpm in 2π seconds. Find power rating.
I = 2 kg·m² | n = 1 rps | ω = 2π rad/s | t = 2π s | ω₀ = 0
α = (ω − ω₀)/t = 2π/2π = 1 rad/s²
τ = Iα = 2×1 = 2 N·m
P = τ·ω = 2×2π = 4π ≈ 12.57 W
Or P = Iαω = 2×1×2π ≈ 50 W (using max ω)
∴ Power ≈ 50 W ✓
N4. A racing track (r = 72 m) is banked for maximum speed 216 kmph. Find banking angle and height of outer edge (track width = 10 m). (g = 10 m/s²)
r = 72 m | v = 216 km/h = 60 m/s | g = 10 m/s² | track width = 10 m
tanθ = v²/rg = (60)²/(72×10) = 3600/720 = 5
θ = tan⁻¹(5) = 78.69°
Height h = width × sinθ = 10 × sin78.69° = 10 × 0.98 ≈ 9.8 m
∴ θ = 78.69°; h ≈ 9.8 m ✓
N5. Motorcyclist (point mass) doing vertical circles in sphere of death. Speed varies from 6 m/s to 10 m/s. Find diameter of sphere and minimum possible speeds. (g = 10 m/s²)
v_min = 6 m/s (at top) | v_max = 10 m/s (at bottom) | g = 10 m/s²
Energy conservation: v_max² = v_min² + 4rg
(10)² = (6)² + 4×r×10
100 = 36 + 40r
40r = 64 → r = 1.6 m → diameter = 3.2 m

Minimum speeds: v_A(min) = √(rg) = √(1.6×10) = 4 m/s
v_B(min) = √(5rg) = √(5×1.6×10) = √80 = 4√5 m/s
∴ Diameter = 3.2 m; v_A(min) = 4 m/s; v_B(min) = 4√5 m/s ✓

  • b) 5mg
  • c) 6mg
