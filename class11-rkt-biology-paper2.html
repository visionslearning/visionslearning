Q 1Multiple Choice Questions — 1 mark each ⭐ All Most IMP7 Marks
Ch1⭐ (i) Which of the following is NOT a characteristic unique to living organisms?
Answer (c) Growth — because non-living things like crystals, mountains also show growth (addition of material from outside). However, living growth is from inside (intrinsic) and involves cell division.
Ch2⭐ (ii) The scientific name of mango is Mangifera indica. In this name, 'Mangifera' represents the ________.
Answer (a) Genus — In binomial nomenclature: first word = Genus (capitalised), second word = species (lowercase). Both are italicised.
Ch5⭐ (iii) Which of the following is a polysaccharide?
Answer (d) Starch — polysaccharide (many glucose units). Glucose = monosaccharide; Sucrose = disaccharide; Amino acid = monomer of protein.
Ch7⭐ (iv) Meiosis occurs in ________.
Answer (b) Reproductive organs (gonads) — meiosis produces gametes (sperm, egg) with half the chromosome number (haploid). Mitosis occurs in somatic (body) cells.
Cell Structure⭐ (v) Which organelle is responsible for intracellular digestion?
Answer (c) Lysosome — contains hydrolytic enzymes (lysozymes) that digest worn-out organelles, bacteria, and food particles. Called "suicidal bags" of the cell.
Ch4⭐ (vi) Water vascular system is a characteristic feature of Phylum ________.
Answer (d) Echinodermata — water vascular system (hydraulic) used for locomotion, feeding, and respiration. Examples: Starfish (Asterias), Sea urchin (Echinus), Sea cucumber.
Ch7⭐ (vii) During Anaphase of mitosis, which structures move to the poles?
Answer (b) Chromatids (sister chromatids) — centromeres split and individual chromatids (now called chromosomes) are pulled to opposite poles by spindle fibres.
Q 2Very Short Answer — 1 mark each ⭐ All Most IMP7 Marks
Ch1⭐ (i) What is meant by 'Biodiversity'?
Biodiversity is the variety of life on Earth — including all species of plants, animals and microorganisms, their genetic diversity, and the ecosystems they form. India is one of the 17 mega-diverse countries.
Ch2⭐ (ii) Who proposed the Two Kingdom Classification and what were the two kingdoms?
Linnaeus proposed Two Kingdom classification: (1) Plantae — all plants; (2) Animalia — all animals. Limitation: did not include bacteria, fungi, or protists properly.
Ch5⭐ (iii) What is a peptide bond?
A peptide bond is the covalent bond formed between the carboxyl group (–COOH) of one amino acid and the amino group (–NH₂) of another, with the release of water (condensation reaction). It links amino acids in a polypeptide chain.
Ch7⭐ (iv) What is the significance of meiosis?
Meiosis maintains the chromosome number across generations (2n→n in gametes; fertilisation restores 2n). It also introduces genetic variation through crossing over (recombination) during Prophase I, which is the basis of evolution.
Cell Structure⭐ (v) What is the difference between smooth ER and rough ER?
Rough ER (RER) has ribosomes on its surface → synthesises and transports proteins. Smooth ER (SER) has no ribosomes → synthesises lipids, steroids, and detoxifies drugs/poisons (especially in liver cells).
Ch4⭐ (vi) Name the phylum and give one example of an organism with a radula.
Phylum Mollusca — radula is a file-like rasping organ used for feeding (scraping algae/food). Example: Snail (Pila), Octopus, Unio (freshwater mussel). Bivalves (oysters) lack radula.
Ch1⭐ (vii) What is ICBN? What does it stand for?
ICBN = International Code of Botanical Nomenclature — the set of rules and regulations governing the scientific naming of plants. Similarly ICZN (International Code of Zoological Nomenclature) covers animals.

Ch1⭐ 1. What are the three domains of life proposed by Carl Woese? How do they differ?
Answer Carl Woese (1990) proposed Three Domain Classification based on rRNA sequences: 1. Bacteria — true bacteria (eubacteria); prokaryotes with murein cell wall 2. Archaea — ancient bacteria (archaebacteria); live in extreme conditions (hot springs, salt lakes, methane areas); no murein in cell wall 3. Eukarya — all eukaryotes (Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) Key difference: Archaea more closely related to Eukarya than to Bacteria (based on rRNA analysis).

Ch5⭐ 3. What are lipids? Classify them with one example each.
Answer Lipids are hydrophobic biomolecules made of C, H, O — not soluble in water but soluble in organic solvents. They are not true polymers. Classification: • Fats/Oils (Triglycerides): Glycerol + 3 fatty acids; energy storage. E.g., butter (fat), olive oil • Phospholipids: Glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate; form cell membranes. E.g., lecithin • Steroids: 4 carbon rings; E.g., cholesterol, testosterone, estrogen • Waxes: Long-chain fatty acid + alcohol; protective coating. E.g., beeswax, cuticle wax on leaves
Ch7⭐ 4. Explain Prophase I of meiosis. Why is it the longest phase?
Answer Prophase I is the longest and most complex phase of meiosis, divided into 5 sub-stages (LEPZD): 1. Leptotene: Chromosomes condense and become visible 2. Zygotene: Homologous chromosomes pair up (synapsis) forming bivalents 3. Pachytene: Crossing over occurs — exchange of segments between non-sister chromatids at chiasmata → genetic recombination 4. Diplotene: Homologous chromosomes begin to separate; chiasmata visible 5. Diakinesis: Chromosomes fully condensed; nuclear envelope breaks down Why longest: Crossing over and synapsis are time-consuming; in human oocytes Prophase I can last years!
Cell Structure⭐ 5. What are plastids? Name their types and functions.
Answer Plastids are membrane-bound organelles found ONLY in plant cells. They contain their own DNA and ribosomes (semi-autonomous). Types: • Chloroplasts (green): Contain chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis; inner membrane has grana (thylakoid stacks) and stroma • Chromoplasts (coloured): Contain carotenoids/xanthophylls; give colour to flowers and fruits — attracts pollinators/dispersers • Leucoplasts (colourless): Store food — starch (amyloplast), fat (elaioplast), protein (aleuroplast) Plastids can interconvert: chloroplasts → chromoplasts (when fruits ripen)
Ch4⭐ 6. What are the main features of Phylum Echinodermata? Give three examples.
Answer Phylum Echinodermata (echino = spiny, derma = skin): • Spiny skin — endoskeleton of calcareous (CaCO₃) ossicles • Water vascular system — unique hydraulic system for locomotion, feeding, gas exchange • Radial symmetry in adults (bilateral in larvae) • Tube feet — for locomotion and prey capture • All marine; no excretory organs (excrete by diffusion) • Examples: Asterias (starfish), Echinus (sea urchin), Holothuria (sea cucumber), Ophiura (brittle star), Antedon (feather star/sea lily)

Ch1⭐ 8. What is a taxon? Give examples at different taxonomic levels.
Answer A taxon (plural: taxa) is any unit of classification at any level of the taxonomic hierarchy. Each level is a taxon. Examples: • Kingdom Animalia — taxon at kingdom level • Phylum Chordata — taxon at phylum level • Family Felidae (cats) — taxon at family level • Genus Panthera — taxon at genus level (lions, tigers, leopards) • Species tigris (Bengal tiger) — taxon at species level The higher the taxon, the more organisms it includes but they share fewer common features.
Ch7⭐ 9. What is the significance of mitosis? Where does it occur?
Answer Significance of Mitosis: 1. Growth: Increases cell number → organism grows 2. Repair and regeneration: Replaces damaged/dead cells (skin, blood, gut lining) 3. Maintains chromosome number: Daughter cells are genetically identical to parent (2n→2n) 4. Asexual reproduction: In unicellular organisms (Amoeba, Paramecium) Occurs in: Somatic (body) cells — actively dividing tissues like bone marrow, epithelium, meristematic tissue in plants, embryonic cells.
Ch4⭐ 10. What are the characteristic features of Phylum Mollusca?
Answer Phylum Mollusca (2nd largest animal phylum): • Soft body (mollis = soft) — usually protected by hard calcareous shell • Body divided into: head, foot, visceral mass, mantle • Mantle secretes the shell (CaCO₃) • Feeding by radula (file-like rasping organ) — absent in bivalves • Open circulatory system (except Cephalopods like octopus → closed) • Excretion by nephridia • Examples: Pila (apple snail), Unio (freshwater mussel), Octopus, Sepia (cuttlefish), Loligo (squid)

Q 4Answer in Brief. (Attempt any 4 of 6) — 3 marks each12 Marks
Ch2⭐ 1. Give an account of the classification and characteristic features of Kingdom Fungi.
Answer Kingdom Fungi — General Characters: • Heterotrophic — absorptive nutrition; secrete enzymes outside and absorb digested material (saprotrophic or parasitic) • Cell wall made of chitin (not cellulose) • Body made of thread-like hyphae → network called mycelium • Reproduce by spores (sexual and asexual) • Store food as glycogen (like animals, not starch like plants) Classes: • Phycomycetes: Aquatic/terrestrial; aseptate hyphae. E.g., Mucor, Rhizopus (bread mould), Albugo • Ascomycetes (sac fungi): Ascospores in asci. E.g., Yeast (Saccharomyces), Aspergillus, Penicillium, Neurospora • Basidiomycetes (club fungi): Basidiospores. E.g., Agaricus (mushroom), Puccinia (rust), Ustilago (smut) • Deuteromycetes (imperfect fungi): Only asexual reproduction known. E.g., Alternaria, Colletotrichum
Ch5⭐ 2. Explain the lock and key model of enzyme action. What factors affect enzyme activity?
Answer Lock and Key Model (Emil Fischer, 1894): • Enzyme has a specific active site — exact shape complementary to substrate • Substrate fits into active site like a key into a lock → enzyme-substrate complex forms • Reaction occurs → product released → enzyme unchanged and free for next reaction • Explains enzyme specificity — each enzyme has unique active site shape Factors affecting enzyme activity: 1. Temperature: Optimum ~37°C for human enzymes; above → denaturation (irreversible shape change) 2. pH: Each enzyme has optimum pH (pepsin = 2; trypsin = 8; salivary amylase = 7) 3. Substrate concentration: Increases rate until all active sites saturated (Vmax) 4. Inhibitors: Competitive (block active site) or non-competitive (change shape)

Cell Structure⭐ 4. Describe the structure of a chloroplast with a diagram.
Answer Chloroplast — Structure: • Double membrane (outer and inner envelope) • Stroma — fluid-filled matrix; contains: — Chloroplast DNA (circular) and 70S ribosomes (semi-autonomous) — Enzymes of Calvin cycle (dark/light-independent reactions) — Starch grains • Thylakoids — flattened membrane sacs inside stroma: — Stacked into grana (singular: granum) — Contain chlorophyll + other pigments (carotenoids) — Site of light-dependent reactions (photosystems I and II) • Lamellae — membranes connecting grana Function: Site of photosynthesis — light reactions in thylakoid; Calvin cycle in stroma.

Ch4⭐ 5. Write the salient features of Phylum Coelenterata (Cnidaria) with examples.
Answer Phylum Cnidaria / Coelenterata: • Diploblastic — two germ layers: ectoderm and endoderm (separated by mesoglea) • Cnidocytes (stinging cells) — contain nematocysts; used for prey capture, defence and attachment; unique to this phylum • Gastrovascular cavity (coelenteron) — digestion and distribution of food • Two body forms: — Polyp: sessile, cylindrical (e.g., Hydra, coral polyp) — Medusa: free-swimming, bell-shaped (e.g., Aurelia jellyfish) • Radial symmetry; mostly marine; some show alternation of generations (metagenesis) — polyp and medusa stages alternate Examples: Hydra, Aurelia (moon jellyfish), Physalia (Portuguese man-o-war), Adamsia (sea anemone), Corals, Obelia
Ch1⭐ 6. Explain the concept of a 'species'. What is the Biological Species Concept?
Answer Species: The fundamental unit of classification — the smallest group in the taxonomic hierarchy. Biological Species Concept (Ernst Mayr, 1942): "A species is a group of actually or potentially interbreeding natural populations that are reproductively isolated from other such groups." Key features: • Members share common morphology and genetics • Can interbreed naturally and produce fertile offspring • Reproductively isolated from members of other species Examples: Homo sapiens (all modern humans); Panthera tigris (all tigers); Oryza sativa (all rice plants) Limitation: Cannot apply to asexually reproducing organisms (bacteria) or fossils.

Cell Structure⭐ 1. Describe the ultra-structure of a nucleus. Explain its functions in controlling cell activities.
Answer Nucleus — Ultra-structure (Robert Brown, 1831): 1. Nuclear envelope (nuclear membrane): Double membrane (outer + inner) with nuclear pores — allow exchange of molecules (RNA out, proteins in). Outer membrane continuous with ER. 2. Nucleoplasm (nuclear matrix): Fluid filling the nucleus; contains nucleotides, enzymes, ions for DNA replication and transcription. 3. Chromatin: DNA + histone proteins. During cell division → condenses into chromosomes. Euchromatin (active, loosely packed) and heterochromatin (inactive, dense). 4. Nucleolus: Dark, dense body; synthesises rRNA and assembles ribosomes. Disappears during division, reforms after. Functions of Nucleus: • Stores genetic information (DNA) — hereditary material • Controls all metabolic activities by directing protein synthesis (DNA → mRNA → protein) • Site of DNA replication — before cell division • Site of transcription — mRNA, tRNA, rRNA synthesis • Nucleolus manufactures ribosomes • Called the "control centre" or "director" of the cell
📐 Draw nucleus: outer nuclear envelope with pores, inner membrane, nucleoplasm, chromatin (scattered threads), nucleolus (dark body inside). Label all parts clearly.
Ch2⭐ 2. Describe Kingdom Plantae — its major divisions with key features and examples.
Answer Kingdom Plantae — General Features: Autotrophic (photosynthesis), eukaryotic, multicellular, cell wall of cellulose, store food as starch, have chlorophyll. Major Divisions: 1. Algae (Thallophyta): • Simple body (thallus); no vascular tissue; aquatic or moist habitats • E.g., Spirogyra (green algae), Ulva (sea lettuce), Fucus (brown algae), Gelidium (red algae) 2. Bryophyta (mosses, liverworts): • "Amphibians of plant kingdom" — need water for fertilisation; no vascular tissue • E.g., Funaria (moss), Marchantia (liverwort), Riccia 3. Pteridophyta (ferns, horsetails): • First vascular plants (xylem + phloem); reproduce by spores; no seeds • E.g., Fern (Dryopteris), Horsetail (Equisetum), Selaginella, Pteris 4. Gymnosperms (naked seed plants): • Seeds not enclosed in fruit; produce cones; mostly evergreen • E.g., Pine (Pinus), Cycas, Cedrus (deodar), Pinus 5. Angiosperms (flowering plants — most evolved): • Seeds enclosed in fruit (flowers → fruits); largest plant group • Monocots (1 cotyledon, parallel venation): wheat, maize, grass • Dicots (2 cotyledons, reticulate venation): mango, rose, pea
Ch5⭐ 3. Explain the types of RNA and their roles in protein synthesis.
Answer Three Main Types of RNA involved in Protein Synthesis: 1. mRNA (Messenger RNA) — ~5% of total RNA: • Carries genetic information from DNA (nucleus) to ribosomes (cytoplasm) • Sequence of codons (triplets) on mRNA determines amino acid sequence of protein • Synthesised by transcription (DNA → mRNA in nucleus) • Longest-lived RNA; acts as template for translation 2. tRNA (Transfer RNA) — ~15% of total RNA: • Clover-leaf shape; adaptor molecule — brings specific amino acid to ribosome • Has anticodon (3 bases) that pairs with codon on mRNA • Amino acid is attached at 3' end (–CCA sequence) • Smallest RNA; called "adapter molecule" of translation 3. rRNA (Ribosomal RNA) — ~80% of total RNA: • Structural and catalytic component of ribosomes • Forms peptide bonds (peptidyl transferase activity) • In eukaryotes: 28S, 18S, 5.8S, 5S rRNA; in prokaryotes: 23S, 16S, 5S • Most abundant RNA; synthesised in nucleolus Summary of protein synthesis: DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation at ribosome, with tRNA bringing amino acids) → Protein
