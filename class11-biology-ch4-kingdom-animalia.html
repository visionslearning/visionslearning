📚 Chapter 4 – Kingdom Animalia

Class 11 Biology  |  Notes + Key Points + Important Q&A

⭐ Key Points at a Glance

  • Animals are classified based on grades of organization, body symmetry, body cavity, germ layers, and segmentation.
  • Three body plans: Cell aggregate (Porifera), Blind sac (Cnidaria), Tube within tube (Annelida onwards).
  • Body symmetry types: Asymmetry, Radial Symmetry, Bilateral Symmetry.
  • Germ layers: Diploblastic (2 layers) vs Triploblastic (3 layers including mesoderm).
  • Body cavity: Acoelomate, Pseudocoelomate, Coelomate.
  • Porifera = pore-bearing; simplest multicellular animals (sponges).
  • Phylum Cnidaria has blind sac plan — same opening for ingestion and egestion.
  • From Annelida onwards, all phyla show tube-within-tube body plan.

🔬 4.1 Criteria Used for Animal Classification

1. Grades of Organization

  • Cellular level – cells perform all functions (e.g., Porifera)
  • Cell-Tissue level – cells organized into tissues
  • Tissue-Organ level – tissues form organs

2. Body Symmetry

  • Asymmetry – no symmetry (e.g., sponges)
  • Radial Symmetry – any plane through central axis divides body into equal halves (e.g., Cnidaria)
  • Bilateral Symmetry – only one plane divides body into equal halves (e.g., most animals)

3. Body Cavity (Coelom)

  • Acoelomate – no body cavity
  • Pseudocoelomate – false body cavity (not lined by mesoderm)
  • Coelomate – true body cavity lined by mesoderm

4. Germ Layers

  • Diploblastic – two germ layers: Ectoderm + Endoderm (e.g., Cnidaria)
  • Triploblastic – three germ layers: Ectoderm + Mesoderm + Endoderm (e.g., all higher animals)

5. Segmentation

  • Unsegmented – body not divided into segments
  • Segmented – body divided into repeated segments (e.g., Annelida)

🧬 4.2 Animal Body Plans

a. Cell Aggregate Plan Porifera

  • Cells do NOT form tissues or organs.
  • Minimal differentiation and division of labour among cells.
  • Found in Porifera (sponges).

b. Blind Sac Body Plan Cnidaria

  • Body is like a sac with a single opening.
  • Same opening used for ingestion and egestion.
  • Digestion occurs in this sac-like structure.
  • Example: Phylum Cnidaria (e.g., Hydra).

c. Tube Within Tube Body Plan Annelida onwards

  • Digestive system present in tube-like body cavity.
  • Mouth and anus at two separate ends.
  • All phyla from Annelida onwards show this plan.

🐾 4.3 Animal Classification – Major Phyla

Phylum Meaning Examples Key Features
Porifera pori = pores; feron = bearing Scypha, Euspongia (Bath sponge), Euplectella (Venus' flower basket) Aquatic (mostly marine), called sponges, cell aggregate body plan, asymmetry
Cnidaria cnidos = stinging cell Hydra, Jellyfish, Coral Blind sac body plan, diploblastic, radial symmetry, stinging cells (nematocysts)
Annelida annulus = little ring Earthworm, Leech, Nereis Tube within tube, true coelom, segmented, bilateral symmetry, triploblastic

🖼️ Visual Summary

Types of Symmetry

  • Asymmetry → e.g., Sponge (Porifera)
  • Radial Symmetry → e.g., Jellyfish (Cnidaria)
  • Bilateral Symmetry → e.g., Insect, Fish, Human

Diploblast vs Triploblast

  • Diploblast: Ectoderm + Endoderm (no mesoderm) → Cnidaria
  • Triploblast: Ectoderm + Mesoderm + Endoderm → Platyhelminthes and above

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. What are the criteria used for animal classification? IMP
The criteria used are:
  • Grades of organization (Cellular → Cell-Tissue → Tissue-Organ)
  • Body symmetry (Asymmetry, Radial, Bilateral)
  • Body cavity (Acoelomate, Pseudocoelomate, Coelomate)
  • Germ layers (Diploblastic, Triploblastic)
  • Segmentation (Unsegmented, Segmented)
Q2. What is cell aggregate body plan? Give an example. IMP
In the cell aggregate body plan, cells do not form tissues or organs. There is minimal differentiation and division of labour among cells. It is found in Porifera (sponges).
Q3. Describe the blind sac body plan with an example.
In the blind sac body plan, the body is like a sac with a single opening. Digestion is carried out in this sac-like structure where both ingestion and egestion take place through the same opening. Example: Members of Phylum Cnidaria (e.g., Hydra).
Q4. What is tube within tube body plan? IMP
In this body plan, the digestive system is present in a tube-like body cavity. The mouth and anus are present at two separate ends of the digestive system. Annelida onwards, all phyla show this type of body plan.
Q5. Differentiate between Diploblastic and Triploblastic animals. IMP
Diploblastic: Body wall made of two germ layers — Ectoderm and Endoderm. No mesoderm. Example: Cnidaria.
Triploblastic: Body wall made of three germ layers — Ectoderm, Mesoderm, and Endoderm. Example: Annelida, Arthropoda, Vertebrates.
Q6. What is Porifera? Give its characteristics. IMP
Porifera (pori = pores; feron = bearing) are pore-bearing animals.
  • They are aquatic — mostly marine, few freshwater.
  • Also called sponges.
  • Show cell aggregate body plan.
  • Examples: Scypha, Euspongia (Bath sponge), Euplectella (Venus' flower basket).
Q7. Define Radial Symmetry and Bilateral Symmetry.
Radial Symmetry: Any plane passing through the central axis divides the body into two equal halves. E.g., Jellyfish.
Bilateral Symmetry: Only one specific plane (sagittal plane) divides the body into two equal and mirror-image halves. E.g., insects, fish, humans.
Q8. What is Acoelomate, Pseudocoelomate, and Coelomate?
Acoelomate: No body cavity between body wall and gut (e.g., Platyhelminthes).
Pseudocoelomate: False body cavity — not lined by mesodermal epithelium (e.g., Nematoda).
Coelomate: True body cavity completely lined by mesoderm (e.g., Annelida, Arthropoda, Vertebrates).
Q9. What is the difference between segmented and unsegmented animals?
Segmented: Body is divided into repeated units (segments). E.g., Earthworm (Annelida).
Unsegmented: Body is not divided into segments. E.g., Porifera, Cnidaria.
Q10. Why is Hydra an example of blind sac body plan?
Hydra (Cnidaria) has a single body opening that acts as both mouth (ingestion) and anus (egestion). Digestion takes place inside the sac-like body cavity, which is why it is called the blind sac body plan.

🔁 Quick Revision – One Liners

  • Porifera = pore-bearing → sponges → cell aggregate plan → asymmetry.
  • Cnidaria → blind sac plan → diploblastic → radial symmetry.
  • Annelida onwards → tube within tube plan → triploblastic → bilateral symmetry.
  • Diploblastic = 2 layers; Triploblastic = 3 layers (with mesoderm).
  • Coelom = true body cavity lined by mesoderm.
  • Segmentation first appears in Annelida.
  • Euspongia = Bath sponge; Euplectella = Venus' flower basket.
