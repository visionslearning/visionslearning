Chapter 1 — Heredity and Evolution
Most repeated questions from last 5 years of SSC exam
7 Repeated Questions
Q1 🔴 5× MUST DO
Explain Mendel's monohybrid cross with a Punnett square. State the Law of Segregation. What is the phenotypic ratio in F₂ generation?
20192020202120222024
Monohybrid Cross (Tall × Dwarf — TT × tt):
  • P generation: TT (Tall) × tt (Dwarf)
  • Gametes: T and t
  • F₁ generation: All Tt (Tall) — dominant trait expressed
  • F₁ × F₁: Tt × Tt
F₂ Punnett Square:
T (from Tt)t (from Tt)
TTT (Tall)Tt (Tall)
tTt (Tall)tt (Dwarf)
  • Phenotypic ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
  • Genotypic ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
  • Law of Segregation: The two alleles for each character separate (segregate) during gamete formation so that each gamete receives only one allele of each gene pair.
Q2 🔴 5× MUST DO
Explain Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection. How does it explain evolution? Give an example. What are the limitations of this theory?
20192020202120232024
Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection (1859):
  • 1. Overproduction: All organisms produce more offspring than can survive.
  • 2. Variation: Individuals show natural heritable variations in traits.
  • 3. Struggle for existence: Organisms compete for limited food, space, and mates.
  • 4. Survival of the Fittest: Individuals with favourable variations survive and reproduce; others die out.
  • 5. Natural Selection: Nature selects individuals with advantageous traits.
  • 6. Inheritance: Favourable traits are passed to offspring → population gradually changes → evolution.
Example — Long neck of Giraffe:
  • Ancestral giraffes had varying neck lengths.
  • Long-necked ones reached more food → survived and reproduced more.
  • Short-necked ones couldn't compete → died out.
  • Over generations → all giraffes became long-necked.
Limitations:
  • Does not explain the origin of variations (Mendel's genetics explains this).
  • Does not explain sudden large changes (mutations).
  • Does not consider genetics and molecular biology.

Q4 🟠 4× VERY IMP
Explain the dihybrid cross performed by Mendel. State the Law of Independent Assortment. What is the F₂ phenotypic ratio?
2020202120232024
Dihybrid Cross (Round Yellow × Wrinkled Green seeds):
  • P generation: RRYY × rryy
  • F₁: All RrYy (Round Yellow — both dominant)
  • F₁ × F₁: RrYy × RrYy → 16 combinations in F₂
F₂ Phenotypic ratio: 9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green (9:3:3:1)

Law of Independent Assortment:
  • When two pairs of contrasting traits are present together, the factors (alleles) of one pair assort (separate) independently of the factors of the other pair during gamete formation.
  • This means the inheritance of one trait does not affect the inheritance of another trait (when genes are on different chromosomes).
📐 Draw 4×4 Punnett square for RrYy × RrYy showing all 16 combinations. Count: 9 R_Y_ : 3 R_yy : 3 rrY_ : 1 rryy = 9:3:3:1
Q5 🟢 3× IMP
What is speciation? Explain the role of geographical isolation in speciation with an example.
202020222023
Speciation: The process by which new species are formed from an existing species over time due to accumulation of genetic differences is called speciation.
  • Geographical Isolation: When a population is separated by a physical barrier (mountain, river, ocean), the two groups no longer interbreed → mutations and natural selection act independently on each group → over thousands of generations they become so different that they can no longer interbreed → new species formed.
  • Example: Darwin's finches in the Galápagos Islands — a single ancestral finch species from mainland South America spread to different islands. Each island had different food sources → beaks evolved differently → 14 new species of finches with different beak shapes formed.

Q7 🔴 5× MUST DO
Explain the process of photosynthesis. Write the overall chemical equation. What are the raw materials, products and conditions required? Explain the two stages.
20192020202120222023
Photosynthesis: Process by which green plants convert CO₂ and H₂O into glucose using sunlight and chlorophyll.
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → (light + chlorophyll) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
AspectDetail
Raw materialsCO₂ (from air), H₂O (from soil), sunlight (energy), chlorophyll (pigment)
ProductsGlucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) + Oxygen (O₂)
SiteChloroplasts (contain chlorophyll)
Two Stages:
  • 1. Light Reaction (Thylakoid membrane): Sunlight is absorbed by chlorophyll → water is split (photolysis): 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂; produces ATP and NADPH; O₂ is released.
  • 2. Dark Reaction / Calvin Cycle (Stroma): CO₂ is fixed using ATP and NADPH → glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is synthesised. Does not directly need light.
📐 Draw: Chloroplast with thylakoid (light reaction) and stroma (dark reaction). Show inputs and outputs of each stage.
Q8 🔴 5× MUST DO
🧪 ACTIVITY: A student takes two test tubes. In tube A she puts a water plant (Hydrilla) in water in sunlight. In tube B she puts the same plant covered with black paper. After 2 hours she observes gas bubbles in tube A but not in tube B. What gas is produced? Write the reaction. What is the conclusion?
20192020202220232024
🧪 Activity Based Answer
  • (i) Gas produced in Tube A: Oxygen (O₂) — produced during photosynthesis when water is split (photolysis).
  • (ii) Reaction: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → (sunlight + chlorophyll) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂↑
  • (iii) Why no bubbles in Tube B: Black paper blocked sunlight → photosynthesis could not occur → no O₂ produced → no bubbles.
  • (iv) How to confirm O₂: Insert a glowing splint near the bubbles in Tube A — it relights (O₂ supports combustion).
  • (v) Conclusion: Sunlight is essential for photosynthesis. Without light, no glucose or oxygen is produced. This experiment proves that light energy drives the photosynthesis reaction in green plants.
Q9 🟠 4× VERY IMP
Describe the human digestive system with a diagram. Explain the digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats step by step.
2019202120222024
Digestion of Carbohydrates:
  • Mouth: Salivary amylase converts starch → maltose.
  • Small intestine: Pancreatic amylase converts remaining starch → maltose; maltase converts maltose → glucose (absorbed into blood).
Digestion of Proteins:
  • Stomach: Pepsin (activated by HCl) converts proteins → peptides.
  • Small intestine: Trypsin and chymotrypsin → peptides → amino acids (absorbed into blood).
Digestion of Fats:
  • Small intestine: Bile salts emulsify fats (break into droplets); pancreatic lipase → fatty acids + glycerol (absorbed into lymph vessels/lacteals).
Absorption: Villi and microvilli in small intestine increase surface area for absorption of glucose, amino acids, fatty acids into blood/lymph.
📐 Draw: Digestive system — label mouth, oesophagus, stomach, liver, gall bladder, pancreas, small intestine, large intestine. Show arrows for peristalsis.

Q11 🟢 3× IMP
What are the different modes of nutrition? Explain autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition with examples.
201920222024
Modes of Nutrition:
  • Autotrophic Nutrition: Organisms that prepare their own food from simple inorganic substances (CO₂, H₂O) using energy.
    • Photoautotrophic: Use sunlight — green plants, algae, cyanobacteria. (Photosynthesis)
    • Chemoautotrophic: Use chemical energy — nitrifying bacteria (Nitrosomonas).
  • Heterotrophic Nutrition: Organisms that depend on other organisms for food.
    • Holozoic: Ingest solid food (animals, amoeba)
    • Saprophytic: Absorb nutrients from dead organic matter (fungi, bacteria)
    • Parasitic: Live on/in host and absorb food (tapeworm, Cuscuta)
    • Symbiotic: Mutual benefit (Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules)
Q12 🟢 3× IMP
🧪 ACTIVITY: Iodine test for starch — A leaf was boiled in water, then in alcohol, and then dipped in iodine. It turned blue-black. What does this indicate? Why was it boiled in alcohol?
202020222023
🧪 Activity Based Answer
  • (i) Blue-black colour indicates: Presence of starch in the leaf. Iodine reacts with starch to form a blue-black complex.
  • (ii) Why boiled in water first? To soften the leaf and kill the cells (stop enzymatic activity).
  • (iii) Why boiled in alcohol? To dissolve and remove the green chlorophyll pigment from the leaf — if chlorophyll is present, it masks the colour change with iodine. Alcohol is a solvent for chlorophyll.
  • (iv) After bleaching (decolourised leaf): The leaf is white/pale. When dipped in iodine — blue-black colour confirms starch is present (photosynthesis occurred).
  • (v) Conclusion: The leaf has undergone photosynthesis and stored starch as a product of glucose polymerisation.

Chapter 3 — Life Processes in Living Organisms Part 2
Circulation, Excretion, Nervous System, Reproduction
6 Repeated Questions
Q13 🔴 5× MUST DO
Draw a neat labelled diagram of the human heart. Explain the double circulation. What is the role of valves in the heart?
20192020202120222023
Double Circulation: In humans, blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit — this is called double circulation.
  • Pulmonary circulation: Right side of heart → Pulmonary artery → Lungs (oxygenated) → Pulmonary vein → Left side of heart.
  • Systemic circulation: Left side of heart → Aorta → All body organs (deoxygenated blood collected) → Vena cava → Right side of heart.
Structure of Human Heart:
  • 4 chambers: Right Auricle (RA), Right Ventricle (RV), Left Auricle (LA), Left Ventricle (LV).
  • LA and LV receive and pump oxygenated blood; RA and RV receive and pump deoxygenated blood.
Role of Valves:
  • Tricuspid valve (between RA and RV): Prevents backflow from RV to RA.
  • Bicuspid (Mitral) valve (between LA and LV): Prevents backflow from LV to LA.
  • Semilunar valves (in aorta and pulmonary artery): Prevent backflow into ventricles.
📐 Draw heart showing 4 chambers, valves, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, aorta, vena cava. Use red for oxygenated blood and blue for deoxygenated blood.
Q14 🔴 5× MUST DO
Explain the structure and function of a nephron with a neat labelled diagram. What is dialysis? When is it needed?
20192020202220232024
Nephron — Structure and Function:
  • Bowman's capsule + Glomerulus: Afferent arteriole brings blood under pressure → blood is ultrafiltered → filtrate (water, urea, glucose, salts) enters Bowman's capsule → blood cells and proteins remain in blood.
  • Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT): Reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, salts, 75% of water back into blood.
  • Loop of Henle: Concentrates urine by further water reabsorption; maintains salt balance.
  • Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT): Fine regulation of salt, water, pH; secretion of excess H⁺ and K⁺.
  • Collecting Duct: Final water reabsorption; concentrated urine drains into renal pelvis → ureter → bladder.
Dialysis (Artificial kidney):
  • When kidneys fail to filter waste from blood, dialysis is used as an artificial filtration process.
  • Patient's blood is passed through a dialysis machine with a semi-permeable membrane that removes urea and waste products.
  • Needed in: kidney failure, chronic kidney disease (CKD), poisoning.
📐 Draw nephron: Bowman's capsule, glomerulus, PCT, Loop of Henle, DCT, collecting duct. Label arrows: filtration, reabsorption, secretion.
Q15 🟠 4× VERY IMP
Explain the nervous system in humans. Draw a labelled diagram of a neuron. Explain how a nerve impulse is transmitted.
2019202120222024
Human Nervous System:
  • Central Nervous System (CNS): Brain + Spinal cord — processes information and controls body.
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS): Nerves from CNS to all body parts.
    • Sensory (afferent) nerves — carry impulses from sense organs to CNS.
    • Motor (efferent) nerves — carry impulses from CNS to muscles/glands.
  • Autonomic Nervous System: Controls involuntary functions (heart rate, digestion).
Neuron Structure:
  • Dendrites: Branch-like extensions receive signals from other neurons.
  • Cell body (soma): Contains nucleus; maintains cell.
  • Axon: Long fibre transmits impulse away from cell body.
  • Myelin sheath: Fatty covering that insulates axon and speeds transmission.
  • Synapse: Junction between two neurons; impulse transmitted by neurotransmitters.
Nerve Impulse Transmission:
  • Stimulus → Dendrites receive signal → Electrochemical impulse travels along axon → Reaches synapse → Neurotransmitters released → Bind to next neuron's receptor → New impulse generated → Reaches effector (muscle/gland) → Response.
📐 Draw neuron: dendrites, cell body (nucleus), axon, myelin sheath (Schwann cells), nodes of Ranvier, axon terminals. Label direction of impulse with arrow.

What are hormones? Name and explain the hormones secreted by the pancreas and their functions. What happens when insulin is not produced?

Hormones: Chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands directly into the blood to regulate body functions.
Pancreatic Hormones:
HormoneSecreted byFunction
Insulinβ (beta) cells of Islets of LangerhansDecreases blood glucose level — promotes uptake of glucose by cells; promotes glycogen storage
Glucagonα (alpha) cells of Islets of LangerhansIncreases blood glucose level — converts glycogen back to glucose when blood sugar is low

  • Control: Reduce fossil fuel use, reforestation, renewable energy, international agreements (Paris Agreement).

Q20 🟠 4× VERY IMP
Explain water pollution — causes, effects and prevention. What is eutrophication?
2020202120222024
Water Pollution: Addition of harmful substances to water bodies making it unfit for use.
  • Causes: Industrial effluents (heavy metals, chemicals); sewage; agricultural runoff (pesticides, fertilisers); oil spills; plastic waste; religious offerings.
  • Effects: Death of aquatic organisms; spread of waterborne diseases (cholera, typhoid); bioaccumulation of toxins in food chain; loss of drinking water; coral reef damage.
  • Prevention: Sewage treatment plants (STPs); industrial effluent treatment before discharge; banning plastic near water bodies; organic farming; river conservation projects (Namami Gange).
Eutrophication:
  • Excessive growth of algae (algal bloom) in water due to excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates) from agricultural runoff and sewage.
  • Algae cover the water surface → block sunlight → submerged plants die → algae decompose → bacteria consume all O₂ → aquatic animals suffocate and die (dead zone).
  • The water becomes foul-smelling and unfit for drinking.

Solar, Wind, Tidal, Biogas, Hydroelectric Energy
4 Repeated Questions
Q22 🔴 5× MUST DO
What are renewable and non-renewable sources of energy? Explain solar energy and wind energy as green energy sources with their advantages and disadvantages.
20192020202120222023
FeatureRenewable EnergyNon-Renewable Energy
DefinitionInexhaustible; naturally replenishedLimited; formed over millions of years
ExamplesSolar, wind, water, biogas, tidalCoal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear
PollutionLow/no pollutionHigh pollution (CO₂, SO₂)
FutureSustainableWill be exhausted
Solar Energy:
  • Source: Sun's electromagnetic radiation.
  • Uses: Solar cells (photovoltaic) — convert light → electricity; solar water heater; solar cooker; solar street lights.
  • Advantages: Free, inexhaustible, pollution-free, low maintenance, available everywhere.
  • Disadvantages: Only available in daytime; reduced on cloudy days; high initial cost; large area needed.
Wind Energy:
  • Source: Kinetic energy of wind moves turbine blades → generator → electricity.
  • Locations: Coastal areas, open plains, mountain passes (e.g., Jaisalmer, Tamil Nadu).
  • Advantages: Renewable, no fuel cost, no pollution, can be installed offshore.
  • Disadvantages: Depends on wind speed; noisy; can harm birds; requires large land area.
Q23 🟠 4× VERY IMP
Explain biogas as a source of energy. How is biogas produced? What are its components and uses? Why is biogas considered a green energy source?
2019202020222023
Biogas: A mixture of gases produced by the anaerobic decomposition (fermentation) of organic waste (animal dung, plant waste, kitchen waste) by microorganisms.

Components of Biogas:
  • Methane (CH₄): ~55–70% (main combustible component)
  • CO₂: ~25–40%; traces of H₂S, N₂, H₂
Production (Biogas Plant):
  • Organic waste (dung + water in 1:1 ratio) fed into the digester (airtight tank).
  • Anaerobic bacteria decompose the organic matter → biogas produced in dome.
  • Slurry (spent material) comes out → used as organic fertiliser.
  • Biogas stored in dome → piped to kitchen for cooking.
Uses of Biogas:
  • Cooking fuel (replaces LPG/firewood); electricity generation; street lighting.
Why Green Energy?
  • Uses waste materials → waste management solution.
  • Produces no net CO₂ (the CO₂ released was recently fixed by plants → carbon neutral).
  • Reduces use of firewood → prevents deforestation.
  • Slurry is better fertiliser than raw dung → improves soil health.
📐 Draw biogas plant: inlet → mixing tank → digester (airtight) → gas holder (dome) → outlet pipe to stove → slurry outlet.
Q24 🟢 3× IMP
🧪 ACTIVITY: A class observes a solar cooker being used during a school science fair. Explain the working principle of a solar cooker. What are its advantages and limitations? Under what conditions does it work best?
202120222024
🧪 Activity Based Answer
Solar Cooker — Working Principle:
  • Uses solar thermal energy (heat from sunlight) to cook food.
  • A flat plate collector with a reflective mirror concentrates sunlight into the cooking box.
  • The inner box is black (black absorbs maximum radiation).
  • Glass cover traps heat inside (greenhouse effect).
  • Temperature inside can reach 100–140°C — enough to cook food, boil water.
Advantages:
  • Uses free solar energy — no fuel cost.
  • No pollution, no CO₂ emissions.
  • Nutrients in food are preserved (slow cooking).
  • Simple construction, low maintenance.
Limitations:
  • Can only be used during daytime and in clear, sunny weather.
  • Cannot be used for frying (does not reach high enough temperatures).
  • Cooking is slow compared to conventional stove.
  • Requires direct sunlight — does not work in rain or heavy clouds.
Best conditions: Clear sunny day, maximum solar radiation, cooker oriented towards the Sun.
Q25 🟢 3× IMP
Explain hydroelectric power generation. What are its advantages and effects on environment? Name two major hydroelectric projects in India.
202020212023
Hydroelectric Power Generation:
  • Uses potential energy of water stored at a height (dam) — when water falls, KE rotates turbines → generator produces electricity.
  • Working: Dam → reservoir (stores water) → water released through penstocks (pipes) → turbine rotated → generator → electricity transmitted through power lines.
Advantages:
  • Renewable — water cycle continuously replenishes the source.
  • No air pollution, no fuel cost.
  • Dams also help in flood control and irrigation.
  • Long lifespan — dams last 50–100 years.
Environmental Effects:
  • Large area of forest and agricultural land submerged → loss of biodiversity.
  • Displacement of tribal communities (rehabilitation issues).
  • Disruption of aquatic ecosystem — fish migration blocked.
  • Risk of dam failure → massive flooding.
Major Projects in India:
  • Bhakra Nangal Dam (Himachal Pradesh / Punjab) — on Sutlej River
  • Tehri Dam (Uttarakhand) — on Bhagirathi River (largest in India)
  • Koyna Dam (Maharashtra) — on Koyna River
