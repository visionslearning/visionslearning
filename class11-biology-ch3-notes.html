🌳 3.1 Classification of Kingdom Plantae
Two Sub-kingdoms Phanerogams — seed producing plants; reproductive structures are visible (Phaneros = visible)
Cryptogams — spore producing plants; do not produce seeds and flowers; sex organs concealed (kryptos = hidden, gamos = marriage)
Classification Chart Kingdom Plantae
├── Cryptogams → Non-Vascular: Thallophyta | Bryophyta    Vascular: Pteridophyta
└── Phanerogams → Gymnosperms (naked seeds) | Angiosperms (enclosed seeds)
                                  └── Dicotyledonae | Monocotyledonae
🌊 Thallophyta (Algae)
Salient Features • Members mostly aquatic; some symbiotic, epizoic, epiphytic
• Plant body is thalloid — undifferentiated into root, stem and leaves
• Reserve food: starch; cell wall: cellulose/glucose or proteins
• Life cycle shows alternation of generation
• Classified based on pigments: chlorophyll, xanthophylls, phycobilin

HabitatMostly fresh waterMostly marineMarine, fresh water, deep sea

Cell wallCelluloseCellulose, fucans, alginCellulose and pectin

ExamplesChlorella, Spirogyra, Chara, Chlamydomonas, VolvoxLaminaria, Sargassum, FucusPorphyra, Gelidium, Gracillaria
⭐ Agar-agar from red algae (Gelidium) — used as solidifying agent in tissue culture medium ★
Brown algae kelps may grow up to 100 metres in height.
🌿 Bryophyta (Amphibians of Plant Kingdom)
Definition (Bryon = moss; phyton = plant) — ~960 genera, ~25,000 species. Mostly terrestrial; found in moist shady places. Need water for fertilization → called 'amphibious plants'.
  • Have root-like structures called rhizoids (unicellular in liverworts; multicellular in mosses)
  • Rhizoids absorb water and minerals; help in fixation of thallus on substratum
  • Life cycle shows sporophytic and gametophytic stages
  • Vegetative plant body (thalloid or leafy) = gametophytic generation
  • Spore-producing capsule = sporophytic generation
  • 2 groups: Liverworts (e.g. Riccia, Marchantia) | Mosses (e.g. Funaria, Sphagnum)
Economic Importance • Sphagnum provides peat used as fuel
• Used as packing material (significant water holding capacity)
• First living beings to grow on rocks; decompose rocks → form soil
• Dense layers of mosses prevent soil erosion — act as soil binders
⭐ Bryophytes = amphibious plants because they are terrestrial but need water for fertilization (like amphibians).
🌾 Pteridophyta (Vascular Cryptogams)
Definition (Pteron = feather; phyton = plant). Evolutionarily, first vascular and true land plants. First successful terrestrial plants with true roots, stem and leaves.
  • Only Cryptogams with vascular tissues → called vascular Cryptogams ★
  • Late Paleozoic era = age of Pteridophytes
  • ~400 genera and 11,000 species
  • Grow in moist and shady places; show sporophytic and gametophytic stages
  • Xylem: only tracheids | Phloem: only sieve cells
  • Secondary growth absent due to absence of cambium
  • Aquatic: Azolla, Marsilea | Xerophytic: Equisetum | Epiphytic: Lycopodium
Classification Psilopsida (Psilotum) | Lycopsida (Selaginella, Lycopodium) | Sphenopsida (Equisetum) | Pteropsida (Dryopteris, Pteris, Adiantum — ferns)
✅ Pteridophytes = vascular Cryptogams (have vascular tissue but no seeds). Used for medicinal purpose; ornamental plants; soil binders.
🌲 Gymnosperms (Naked Seeds)
Definition (Gymnos = naked; Sperma = seed). Primitive group of flowering plants producing naked seeds. ~70 genera and 1000 living species; India: 16 genera, 53 species.
  • Most are evergreen, shrubs or woody trees
  • Vascular plants: Xylem with tracheids | Phloem with sieve cells
  • Secondary growth seen due to presence of cambium
  • Coralloid roots of Cycas — association with blue green algae (symbiosis)
  • Roots of Pinus — association with endophytic fungi = mycorrhizae
  • Spores produced by microsporophyll (Male) and megasporophyll (Female)
Important Facts ★ • Ginkgo biloba = living fossil (found in living + fossil form; fossil forms > living forms)
• Sequoia sempervirens = tallest living plant (~366 feet; coast redwood of California)
• Taxodium mucronatum = girth of ~125 feet
• Zamia pygmaea = smallest Gymnosperm (~25 cm)
⭐ Cycas = ornamental plant. Pinus = source of pine wood, turpentine oil, pine resin.
🌸 Angiosperms (Enclosed Seeds)
Definition (Angios = enclosed vessel; Sperma = seed). Most advanced group of flowering plants. Seeds are enclosed within the fruit (ovary) ★.
  • Highly evolved; primarily adapted to terrestrial habitat
  • Angiosperms are heterosporous
  • Microspores (pollens) in microsporangia (anthers) | Megaspores in megasporangia (ovules)
  • Accessory whorls: calyx (sepals) + corolla (petals) arranged together to form flowers
  • Wolffia = smallest Angiosperm (1mm) | Eucalyptus grows to over 100 metres

🔄 3.4 Plant Life Cycle & Alternation of Generations
Definition Life cycle of a plant includes two phases: Sporophyte (diploid: 2n) and Gametophyte (haploid: n) that alternate with each other. This alternation = alternation of generations.
Flow of Alternation Sporophyte (2n) → Meiosis → Spores (n) → Mitosis → Gametophyte (n)
Gametophyte (n) → Gametes (n) → Fertilization → Zygote (2n) → Sporophyte (2n)
TypeDescriptionDominant phaseExamples
HaplonticMitosis occurs in haploid cells. Zygote is only diploid cell. Zygote → meiosis.Haploid (gametophyte)Some algae, fungi
DiplonticMitosis occurs only in diploid cells. Gametes by meiosis.Diploid (sporophyte)Animals
Haplo-diplonticMitosis in both diploid and haploid cells. Both multicellular haploid and diploid phases.Both phases presentLand plants, many algae
Dominant Phase Comparison ★ Bryophytes: Haploid gametophyte dominant; photosynthetic, independent. Sporophyte short-lived, depends partially on gametophyte.
Pteridophytes: Sporophyte dominant, independent, vascular. Haploid gametophyte generally autotrophic and short-lived.
Gymnosperms & Angiosperms: Gametophyte much reduced; exists within sporophyte.
⭐ Key exam fact: Bryophytes → gametophyte dominant | Pteridophytes → sporophyte dominant

🎯 Board Exam Questions — Chapter 3
★ 1 Mark Questions
  • What are Phanerogams? 1 mark → Seed producing plants with visible reproductive structures
  • What are Cryptogams? 1 mark → Spore producing plants; do not produce seeds/flowers; sex organs concealed
  • Why are Bryophytes called amphibious plants? 1 mark → They are terrestrial but need water for fertilization
  • What is agar-agar? 1 mark → Solidifying agent from red algae (Gelidium) used in tissue culture medium
  • What is a living fossil? Give example. 1 mark → Plant found in living as well as fossil form; e.g. Ginkgo biloba
  • What is mycorrhizae? 1 mark → Endophytic fungi associated with roots of Pinus (symbiosis)
  • Which is the dominant phase in Pteridophytes? 1 mark → Sporophyte
  • Tallest living Gymnosperm? 1 mark → Sequoia sempervirens (~366 feet; California)
★★ 2 Mark Questions
  • Differentiate between Cryptogams and Phanerogams. 2 marks
    Cryptogams: spore producing; no seeds/flowers; sex organs concealed; e.g. algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes.
    Phanerogams: seed producing; reproductive structures visible; e.g. gymnosperms, angiosperms.
  • Why are Bryophytes called amphibious plants? Give example. 2 marks
    Bryophytes are mostly terrestrial but need water for fertilization and completion of life cycle, just like amphibians live on land but need water for reproduction. Hence called amphibious plants. Example: Funaria (moss) — motile sperms need water to swim to egg.
  • Why are Pteridophytes called vascular Cryptogams? Justify. 2 marks
    Pteridophytes are Cryptogams — spore producers with no seeds/flowers. They are the ONLY Cryptogams with vascular tissues (xylem with tracheids and phloem with sieve cells). Hence they are called vascular Cryptogams. They are also the first vascular and true land plants.
  • Distinguish between Bryophyta and Pteridophyta. 2 marks
    Bryophyta: No vascular tissue | gametophyte dominant | rhizoids (root-like) | e.g. Funaria, Riccia.
    Pteridophyta: Vascular tissue present | sporophyte dominant | true roots | secondary growth absent | e.g. Nephrolepis, Selaginella.
  • Distinguish between Dicotyledonae and Monocotyledonae. (Any 4 points) 2 marks
    Dicots: 2 cotyledons; tap root; reticulate venation; tetra/pentamerous; open VB; secondary growth present.
    Monocots: 1 cotyledon; adventitious root; parallel venation; trimerous; closed VB; secondary growth absent.
★★★ 3 Mark Questions
  • Describe salient features of Bryophytes with examples. 3 marks
    • Mostly terrestrial; moist shady places; called amphibious plants (need water for fertilization)
    • ~960 genera, ~25,000 species
    • Root-like rhizoids (unicellular in liverworts; multicellular in mosses) — absorb water, minerals, fixation
    • Life cycle: sporophytic + gametophytic stages. Vegetative body = gametophyte; Capsule = sporophyte
    • Two groups: Liverworts (Riccia, Marchantia) and Mosses (Funaria, Sphagnum, Polytrichum)
    • Economic importance: Sphagnum = peat fuel; packing material; soil binder; prevent soil erosion
  • Describe salient features of Pteridophytes with examples. 3 marks
    • First vascular true land plants; only Cryptogams with vascular tissue (vascular Cryptogams) ★
    • ~400 genera, 11,000 species; late Paleozoic era = age of Pteridophytes
    • Pinnate leaves: microphylls (Selaginella) or macrophylls (Nephrolepis/fern)
    • Xylem: only tracheids | Phloem: only sieve cells | No secondary growth (no cambium)
    • Classified: Psilopsida (Psilotum), Lycopsida (Selaginella), Sphenopsida (Equisetum), Pteropsida (ferns)
    • Uses: medicinal purpose, soil binders, ornamental plants
  • Describe salient features of Gymnosperms with examples. 3 marks
    • Naked seeds (Gymnos = naked); ~70 genera, 1000 species
    • Mostly evergreen, shrubs or woody trees; vascular plants
    • Secondary growth due to cambium
    • Coralloid roots of Cycas → blue green algae | Pinus roots → mycorrhizae
    • Spores: microsporophyll (male) + megasporophyll (female)
    • Ginkgo biloba = living fossil | Sequoia sempervirens = tallest (~366 ft)
    • Cycas = ornamental; Pinus = pine wood, turpentine, pine resin
★★★★ 4 Mark Questions
  • Describe the classification of Algae with features and examples of each class. 4 marks
    1. Chlorophyceae (Green Algae): Fresh water; unicellular/colonial/filamentous; Chl a & b; stored food = starch; cellulose cell wall. e.g. Chlorella, Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas, Chara, Volvox.
    2. Phaeophyceae (Brown Algae): Mostly marine; body = holdfast + stipe + frond; Chl-a,-c + fucoxanthin; stored food = mannitol, laminarin; cellulose+algin cell wall. e.g. Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus.
    3. Rhodophyceae (Red Algae): Marine/fresh water/deep sea; Chl a,d + phycoerythrin; stored food = Floridean starch; cellulose+pectin cell wall. Agar-agar from Gelidium (tissue culture). e.g. Porphyra, Gelidium, Gracillaria.
  • Distinguish Dicotyledonae from Monocotyledonae with table. Give examples. 4 marks
    Dicots: 2 cotyledons; tap root; branched stem; reticulate venation; tetra/pentamerous flowers; open VB (cambium present); secondary growth common. e.g. Sunflower, Hibiscus, Pea.
    Monocots: 1 cotyledon; adventitious root; rarely branched; parallel venation; trimerous flowers; closed VB (no cambium); secondary growth absent except few. e.g. Maize (Zea mays), Jowar (Sorghum vulgare), Grass.
    Key: Monocots lack secondary growth because vascular bundles are closed (no cambium).
  • What is alternation of generations? Explain with reference to Bryophytes and Pteridophytes. 4 marks
    Alternation of Generations = alternation of sporophyte (2n) and gametophyte (n) phases in a plant's life cycle.
    Sporophyte → meiosis → spores → mitosis → gametophyte → gametes → fertilization → zygote → sporophyte.
    In Bryophytes: Haploid gametophyte is dominant, photosynthetic, independent. Sporophyte short-lived, depends on gametophyte.
    In Pteridophytes: Sporophyte dominant, independent, vascular. Gametophyte generally autotrophic and short-lived.
    In Gymnosperms & Angiosperms: Gametophyte much reduced and exists within sporophyte.
