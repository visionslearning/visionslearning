3.1 Classification of Kingdom Plantae ★ EXAM HOT
Kingdom Plantae
├── Cryptogams (spore-producing; sex organs concealed)
│   ├── Non-Vascular: Thallophyta | Bryophyta
│   └── Vascular: Pteridophyta
└── Phanerogams (seed-producing; reproductive structures visible)
    ├── Gymnosperms (naked seeds)
    └── Angiosperms (enclosed seeds)
        ├── Dicotyledonae
        └── Monocotyledonae

📌 Key Points

  • Phanerogams – seed producing plants (Phaneros = visible)
  • Cryptogams – spore producing plants; do not produce seeds and flowers (kryptos = hidden, gamos = marriage)
3.2A Thallophyta (Algae) ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Members are mostly aquatic; some symbiotic, epizoic, epiphytic
  • Plant body is thalloid – undifferentiated into root, stem and leaves
  • Reserve food in the form of starch; cell wall contains cellulose/glucose or proteins
  • Reproduction: vegetative, asexual and sexual
  • Life cycle shows alternation of generation
  • Classified based on pigments: chlorophyll, xanthophylls, phycobilin

3.2B Bryophyta ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • (Bryon = moss; phyton = plant) | ~960 genera, ~25,000 species
  • Mostly terrestrial; found in moist shady places
  • Need water for fertilization → called 'amphibious plants'
  • Have root-like structures called rhizoids (unicellular in liverworts; multicellular in mosses)
  • Rhizoids absorb water and minerals and help in fixation of thallus on substratum
  • Life cycle shows sporophytic and gametophytic stages
  • Vegetative plant body (thalloid or leafy) = gametophytic generation
  • Spore-producing capsule = sporophytic generation
  • 2 groups: Liverworts (e.g. Riccia, Marchantia) and Mosses (e.g. Funaria, Sphagnum)

📌 Economic Importance of Bryophyta

  • Some mosses provide food for herbivorous mammals, birds
  • Sphagnum provides peat used as fuel
  • Used as packing material (significant water holding capacity)
  • First living beings to grow on rocks; decompose rocks to form soil
  • Dense layers of mosses help in prevention of soil erosion (act as soil binders)
3.2C Pteridophyta ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • (Pteron = feather; phyton = plant)
  • Evolutionarily, first vascular and true land plants — first successful terrestrial plants with true roots, stem and leaves
  • Only Cryptogams with vascular tissues → called vascular Cryptogams ★
  • Late Paleozoic era is regarded as the age of Pteridophytes
  • ~400 genera and 11,000 species
  • Grow in moist and shady places; show sporophytic and gametophytic stages
  • In Xylem: only tracheids | In Phloem: only sieve cells
  • Secondary growth is not seen due to absence of cambium

📌 Classification of Pteridophytes

  • Psilopsida – Psilotum
  • Lycopsida – Selaginella, Lycopodium
  • Sphenopsida – Equisetum
  • Pteropsida – Dryopteris, Pteris, Adiantum (ferns)
  • Aquatic: Azolla, Marsilea | Xerophytic: Equisetum | Epiphytic: Lycopodium
3.3A Gymnosperms ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • (Gymnos = naked; sperma = seed) — primitive group of flowering plants producing naked seeds
  • ~70 genera and 1000 living species; in India: 16 genera and 53 species
  • Most are evergreen, shrubs or woody trees
  • Vascular plants with Xylem: tracheids | Phloem: sieve cells
  • Secondary growth seen due to presence of cambium
  • Coralloid roots of Cycas – association with blue green algae
  • Roots of Pinus – association with endophytic fungi called mycorrhizae
  • Ginkgo biloba – called 'living fossil' (found in living as well as fossil form)
  • Sequoia sempervirens – tallest living plant (~366 feet; coast redwood of California)
  • Zamia pygmaea – smallest Gymnosperms (~25 cm)
3.3B Angiosperms ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • (Angios = enclosed vessel; Sperma = seed) — most advanced group of flowering plants
  • Seeds enclosed within the fruit (ovary) ★
  • Highly evolved; primarily adapted to terrestrial habitat
  • Angiosperms are heterosporous
  • Wolffia – smallest Angiosperm (1mm) | Eucalyptus – grows to over 100 meters

3.4 Plant Life Cycle and Alternation of Generations ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Life cycle of a plant includes two phases: Sporophyte (diploid: 2n) and Gametophyte (haploid: n)
  • Special diploid cells of sporophyte divide by meiosis → haploid cells → gametophyte
  • Gametophyte produces male and female gametes → fertilization → diploid zygote → sporophyte
  • This alternation of sporophytic and gametophytic generations = alternation of generations
Types of Life Cycles – Diplontic, Haplontic, Haplo-diplontic ▶
TypeDescriptionExamples
DiplonticMitosis occurs only in diploid cells. Gametes formed through meiosis are haploid. Diploid zygote divides mitotically.Animals
HaplonticMitosis occurs in haploid cells. Zygote is the only diploid cell. Zygote later undergoes meiosis.Some algae and fungi
Haplo-diplonticMitosis occurs in both diploid and haploid cells. Both multicellular haploid (gametophyte) and multicellular diploid (sporophyte) phases exist.Land plants and many algae

In Bryophytes: Haploid gametophyte is dominant; sporophyte is short lived, partially dependent on gametophyte

In Pteridophytes: Sporophyte is dominant, independent; gametophyte is generally autotrophic and short lived

In Gymnosperms & Angiosperms: Gametophyte is much reduced and exists within sporophyte

Q2. Why are Bryophytes called amphibious plants? 2 marks ▶

Bryophytes are called amphibious plants because:

  • They are mostly terrestrial plants found in moist shady places
  • However, they need water for fertilization and completion of their life cycle
  • Just like amphibians live on land but need water for reproduction, Bryophytes also depend on water for sexual reproduction
  • Example: Funaria (moss) needs water for the motile sperms to swim to the egg
Q3. Why are Pteridophytes called vascular Cryptogams? 2 marks ▶

Pteridophytes are called vascular Cryptogams because:

  • They are Cryptogams – they do not produce seeds or flowers (spore producing plants)
  • They are the only Cryptogams with vascular tissues (xylem and phloem)
  • They are the first vascular and true land plants
  • Xylem has only tracheids; Phloem has only sieve cells

Q6. Describe salient features of Bryophytes with examples. 3 marks ▶

Bryophytes (Bryon = moss; phyton = plant):

  • Mostly terrestrial plants found in moist shady places
  • Called amphibious plants – need water for fertilization
  • Approximately 960 genera and about 25,000 species
  • Have root-like structures called rhizoids – unicellular in liverworts; multicellular in mosses
  • Rhizoids absorb water and minerals; help in fixation of thallus on substratum
  • Life cycle shows sporophytic and gametophytic stages
  • Vegetative plant body (thalloid or leafy) = gametophytic generation
  • Spore producing capsule = sporophytic generation
  • Divided into: Liverworts (e.g. Riccia, Marchantia) and Mosses (e.g. Funaria, Sphagnum)
  • Economic importance: Sphagnum provides peat; soil binders; packing material
Q7. Describe salient features of Pteridophytes with examples. 3 marks ▶

Pteridophytes (Pteron = feather; phyton = plant):

  • Evolutionarily, the first vascular and true land plants
  • Only Cryptogams with vascular tissues (xylem and phloem) → called vascular Cryptogams
  • ~400 genera and 11,000 species
  • Plants consist of pinnate (feather-like) leaves – microphylls (Selaginella) or macrophylls (Nephrolepis)
  • Xylem: only tracheids | Phloem: only sieve cells
  • Secondary growth absent due to absence of cambium
  • Show sporophytic and gametophytic stages
  • Classified into 4 groups: Psilopsida, Lycopsida, Sphenopsida, Pteropsida
  • Used for medicinal purpose; as soil binders; many as ornamental plants
  • Examples: Ferns, Horsetail, Azolla (aquatic), Equisetum (xerophytic)
Q8. Describe salient features of Gymnosperms with examples. 3 marks ▶

Gymnosperms (Gymnos = naked; Sperma = seed):

  • Primitive group of flowering plants producing naked seeds
  • ~70 genera and 1000 living species; in India: 16 genera and 53 species
  • Most are evergreen, shrubs or woody trees
  • Vascular plants: Xylem with tracheids; Phloem with sieve cells
  • Secondary growth seen due to presence of cambium
  • Root system is tap root type; some have symbiotic associations (Cycas + blue green algae; Pinus + mycorrhizae)
  • Stem mostly erect, aerial, solid and cylindrical
  • Spores produced by microsporophyll (Male) and megasporophyll (Female)
  • Ginkgo biloba = living fossil | Sequoia sempervirens = tallest living plant
  • Economic importance: Cycas = ornamental; Pinus = pine wood, turpentine oil, pine resin

Q10. Write about alternation of generations in plants. Compare it in Bryophytes and Pteridophytes. 4 marks ▶

Alternation of Generations:

Life cycle of a plant includes two phases – Sporophyte (diploid: 2n) and Gametophyte (haploid: n) that alternate with each other.

  • Diploid cells of sporophyte → meiosis → haploid spores → divide mitotically → gametophyte
  • Gametophyte produces male and female gametes → fertilization → diploid zygote → sporophyte
  • This alternation of two generations is called alternation of generations

In Bryophytes:

  • Haploid gametophyte is dominant
  • It is photosynthetic, independent, thalloid or erect
  • Sporophyte is short lived, multicellular and depends totally or partially on gametophyte for nutrition

In Pteridophytes:

  • Sporophyte is dominant, independent and vascular plant body
  • Haploid multicellular gametophyte is generally autotrophic and short lived
  • Gametophyte alternates with sporophyte
✍️ Key comparison: Bryophytes → gametophyte dominant | Pteridophytes → sporophyte dominant
Q11. Describe classification of Algae with features and examples of each class. 4 marks ▶

Algae are classified based on their pigments. There are 3 main classes:

1. Chlorophyceae (Green Algae):

  • Mostly fresh water; plant body unicellular, colonial, filamentous
  • Cell wall: cellulose | Pigments: chlorophyll a and b | Stored food: starch
  • Chloroplasts of various shapes (discoid, cup-shaped, spiral, etc.)
  • Examples: Chlorella, Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas, Chara, Volvox

2. Phaeophyceae (Brown Algae):

  • Mostly marine; body differentiated into holdfast, stipe and frond
  • Cell wall: cellulose, fucans, algin | Pigments: chlorophyll-a, -c, fucoxanthin | Stored food: mannitol, laminarin
  • Examples: Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus

3. Rhodophyceae (Red Algae):

  • Found in marine, fresh water and deep sea
  • Cell wall: cellulose and pectin | Pigments: chlorophyll a, d and phycoerythrin | Stored food: Floridean starch
  • Agar-agar from red algae (Gelidium) – used as solidifying agent in tissue culture
  • Examples: Porphyra, Gelidium, Gracillaria, Polysiphonia
