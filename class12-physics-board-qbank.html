Q51
2023Section C · 3M
Derive laws of reflection of light using Huygens' principle.
▼ Answer
✅ Answer
According to Huygens' principle, each point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets.
Let AB = incident wavefront, CE = reflected wavefront, v = speed of light.
Time for ray from B to E = Time for ray from A to C: t = AE/v = BC/v → AE = BC
In triangles AEC and ACB: AC is common, AE = BC → triangles are congruent
Therefore: ∠i = ∠r → angle of incidence = angle of reflection
Also: reflected ray lies in plane of incidence.

Q55

What is Brewster's law? Derive the formula for Brewster's angle.
▼ Answer
✅ Answer
Brewster's Law: When unpolarised light is incident at Brewster's angle (iB), the reflected light is completely plane polarised. At this angle, reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular.
i_B + r = 90° → r = 90° − i_B
By Snell's law: n = sin(iB)/sin(r) = sin(iB)/sin(90°−iB) = sin(iB)/cos(iB)
n = tan(i_B) → i_B = tan⁻¹(n)
Q56
2025Section C · 3M
Derive an expression for law of radioactive decay. Define one becquerel (Bq).
▼ Answer
✅ Answer
Rate of disintegration ∝ N (number of undecayed nuclei):
dN/dt = −λN
Integrating: ln(N/N₀) = −λt
N = N₀e^(−λt)
where λ = decay constant, N₀ = initial number of atoms.

One Becquerel (Bq): Activity of a radioactive sample in which one disintegration occurs per second. 1 Bq = 1 disintegration/second.

What is capillarity? Give any two applications of capillarity. Calculate the work done in blowing a soap bubble of radius 0.1 m. (Surface tension of soap solution = 30 dyne/cm)

Capillarity: The phenomenon of rise or fall of liquid in a capillary tube due to surface tension. Rise when contact angle < 90°, fall when > 90°.

Q58
2020Section D · 4M
What is a rectifier? With the help of a neat circuit diagram explain the working of a full wave rectifier.
▼ Answer
✅ Answer
Rectifier: A device that converts alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC).

Full Wave Rectifier:
• Uses two diodes D₁ and D₂ with a centre-tap transformer
• During positive half cycle: D₁ conducts, D₂ is reverse biased → current flows through load in one direction
• During negative half cycle: D₂ conducts, D₁ is reverse biased → current flows through load in same direction
• Output: Pulsating DC (both half cycles utilised)
• After filtering (capacitor), smooth DC is obtained
• Efficiency ≈ 81.2%
Q59
2022Section D · 4M
Derive an expression for a pressure exerted by a gas on the basis of kinetic theory of gases.
▼ Answer
✅ Answer
Consider N molecules in a cube of side L. For one molecule of mass m with velocity vₓ in x-direction:
Momentum change per collision with wall = 2mvₓ
Time between collisions = 2L/vₓ
Force by one molecule = 2mvₓ / (2L/vₓ) = mvₓ²/L
Total force by N molecules: F = m(v₁ₓ² + v₂ₓ² + ...)/L = mN⟨vₓ²⟩/L
Since ⟨vₓ²⟩ = ⟨v²⟩/3 (isotropic):
P = F/A = mN⟨v²⟩/(3L³) = mN⟨v²⟩/(3V)
P = (1/3)ρ⟨v²⟩ or PV = (1/3)Nm⟨v²⟩

✅ Answer
Transformer: A device that changes AC voltage from one value to another without change in frequency.

Construction: Two coils (primary Np turns, secondary Ns turns) wound on a soft iron laminated core.

Working: AC in primary → changing magnetic flux → EMF induced in secondary by mutual induction.

Equation: EMF per turn = dΦ/dt (same for both coils)
ε_s/ε_p = Ns/Np = k (turns ratio)
For ideal transformer (no losses): ε_p × I_p = ε_s × I_s
I_s/I_p = Np/Ns

Q63

What is Ferromagnetism? Explain it on the basis of domain theory.
▼ Answer
✅ Answer
Ferromagnetism: The property of certain materials (Fe, Co, Ni) to get strongly magnetised in the direction of applied magnetic field and retain magnetism even after removing the field.

Domain Theory:
• A ferromagnetic material consists of small regions called domains
• Each domain contains 10¹⁷ to 10²¹ atoms, all aligned in one direction
• In unmagnetised state: domains are randomly oriented → net magnetisation = 0
• When external field is applied: (1) domains aligned with field grow (domain wall movement), (2) other domains rotate towards field direction
• After field removal: domains retain alignment → permanent magnet
