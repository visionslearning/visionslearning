Degrees, Radians, Arc Length, Area of Sector — Most repeated in exams

Q 1 Convert the following angles: ⭐ Most IMP Degree ↔ Radian
(i) Convert 240° into radians
Given:
Angle = 240°
Formula:
Radians = Degrees × π/180
Solution
Step 1
= 240 × π/180
Step 2
= 4π/3
∴ Answer:
4π/3 radians
(ii) Convert 570° into radians
Given:
Angle = 570°
Formula:
Radians = Degrees × π/180
Solution

= 570 × π/180
Step 2
= 19π/6
∴ Answer:
19π/6 radians

Given:
Angle = 5π/3 radians
Formula:
Degrees = Radians × 180/π
Solution

= (5π/3) × (180/π)

= 5 × 60 = 300°

300°

Q 2 Arc Length and Area of Sector ⭐ Most IMP Arc & Sector
(i) A circle of radius 6 cm has an arc of length 5π cm. Find the angle subtended at the centre in degrees.
Given:
Radius r = 6 cm, Arc length l = 5π cm
To Find:
Angle θ subtended at centre (in degrees)
Formula:
l = rθ   (θ in radians)
Solution
Step 1
θ = l/r = 5π/6 radians
Step 2
Convert to degrees
= (5π/6) × (180/π) = 5 × 30 = 150°
∴ Answer:
θ = 150°
(ii) Find the area of a sector whose radius is 7 cm and angle is 60°.
Given:
r = 7 cm, θ = 60° = 60 × π/180 = π/3 radians
Formula:
Area = ½ r²θ   (θ in radians)
Solution
Step 1
Area = ½ × 7² × π/3
Step 2
= ½ × 49 × π/3
Step 3
= 49π/6
∴ Answer:
Area = 49π/6 cm²
(iii) In a circle of radius 12 cm, a chord subtends an angle of 120° at the centre. Find the arc length and area of sector.
Given:
r = 12 cm, θ = 120° = 2π/3 radians
Solution
Arc length
l = rθ = 12 × 2π/3 = 8π cm
Area of sector
= ½r²θ = ½ × 144 × 2π/3 = 48π cm²
∴ Answer:
Arc = 8π cm  |  Area = 48π cm²

Chapter 2 — Trigonometry - I

Signs, Fundamental identities, Allied angles — appears every year

Q 4 If sin θ = 3/5 and θ lies in 2nd quadrant, find the remaining trig ratios. ⭐ Most IMP Trig Ratios
Given:
sin θ = 3/5, θ ∈ 2nd quadrant
To Find:
cos θ, tan θ, cot θ, sec θ, cosec θ
Identity:
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
Solution
Step 1
cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ = 1 − (3/5)² = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25
Step 2
cos θ = ±4/5
In 2nd quadrant, cos is negative → cos θ = −4/5
Step 3
tan θ = sin θ/cos θ = (3/5)/(−4/5) = −3/4
Step 4
cot θ = 1/tan θ = −4/3
sec θ = 1/cos θ = −5/4
cosec θ = 1/sin θ = 5/3
∴ Answer:
cos θ = −4/5  |  tan θ = −3/4  |  cot θ = −4/3  |  sec θ = −5/4  |  cosec θ = 5/3
Q 5 Prove the following trigonometric identities. ⭐ Most IMP Trig Identities
(i) Prove: (1 − sin²θ) sec²θ = 1
Proof — LHS to RHS
LHS
= (1 − sin²θ) sec²θ
Step 1
= cos²θ × sec²θ     [∵ 1 − sin²θ = cos²θ]
Step 2
= cos²θ × (1/cos²θ)     [∵ sec θ = 1/cos θ]
Step 3
= 1
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
(ii) Prove: tan θ + cot θ = sec θ · cosec θ
Proof — LHS to RHS
LHS
= tan θ + cot θ
Step 1
= sin θ/cos θ + cos θ/sin θ
Step 2
= (sin²θ + cos²θ) / (cos θ · sin θ)
Step 3
= 1 / (cos θ · sin θ)     [∵ sin²θ + cos²θ = 1]
Step 4
= (1/cos θ) × (1/sin θ) = sec θ · cosec θ = RHS
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
(iii) Prove: (sec θ − tan θ)² = (1 − sin θ)/(1 + sin θ)
Proof — LHS
LHS
= (sec θ − tan θ)²
Step 1
= (1/cos θ − sin θ/cos θ)²
Step 2
= ((1 − sin θ)/cos θ)²
Step 3
= (1 − sin θ)² / cos²θ
Step 4
= (1 − sin θ)² / (1 − sin²θ)     [∵ cos²θ = 1−sin²θ]
Step 5
= (1 − sin θ)² / [(1 − sin θ)(1 + sin θ)]
Step 6
= (1 − sin θ) / (1 + sin θ) = RHS
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
Q 6 Evaluate using allied angles / co-functions. ⭐ IMP Allied Angles
(i) Evaluate: sin 150° + cos 300° − tan 135°
Values:
sin 150° = sin(180°−30°) = sin 30° = 1/2
cos 300° = cos(360°−60°) = cos 60° = 1/2
tan 135° = tan(180°−45°) = −tan 45° = −1
Solution
Step 1
= 1/2 + 1/2 − (−1)
Step 2
= 1/2 + 1/2 + 1 = 1 + 1 = 2
∴ Answer:
2
(ii) Prove: sin(90°+θ) · cos(180°−θ) = −sin θ cos θ
Allied values:
sin(90°+θ) = cos θ  |  cos(180°−θ) = −cos θ
Solution
LHS
= sin(90°+θ) · cos(180°−θ)
Step 1
= cos θ · (−cos θ)
Step 2
= −cos²θ
Note: The expected RHS is −sin θ cos θ which requires different given. Directly: LHS = −cos²θ.
∴ Result:
= −cos²θ

Chapter 3 — Trigonometry - II

Sum/Product formulae, Multiple angles, Factorisation — highest marks in exam

Q 7 Prove using sum / difference formulae. ⭐ Most IMP Sum Formulae
(i) Prove: sin(A+B) · sin(A−B) = sin²A − sin²B
Formulae:
sin(A+B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B
sin(A−B) = sin A cos B − cos A sin B
Solution
LHS
= sin(A+B) · sin(A−B)
Step 1
= (sin A cos B + cos A sin B)(sin A cos B − cos A sin B)
Step 2
Use (a+b)(a−b) = a²−b²
= (sin A cos B)² − (cos A sin B)²
Step 3
= sin²A cos²B − cos²A sin²B
Step 4
= sin²A(1−sin²B) − (1−sin²A)sin²B
Step 5
= sin²A − sin²A sin²B − sin²B + sin²A sin²B
Step 6
= sin²A − sin²B = RHS
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
(ii) Prove: tan(A+B) = (tan A + tan B)/(1 − tan A tan B)
Proof
LHS
= tan(A+B) = sin(A+B)/cos(A+B)
Step 1
= (sin A cos B + cos A sin B) / (cos A cos B − sin A sin B)
Step 2
Divide num & denom by cos A cos B
= (tan A + tan B) / (1 − tan A tan B) = RHS
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
Q 8 Multiple angle — find values / prove identities. ⭐ Most IMP Multiple Angles
(i) If sin A = 3/5, find sin 2A, cos 2A, tan 2A (A in 1st quadrant)
Given:
sin A = 3/5, A ∈ 1st quadrant → cos A = 4/5
Formulae:
sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A  |  cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A  |  tan 2A = sin 2A / cos 2A
Solution
sin 2A
= 2 × (3/5) × (4/5) = 24/25
cos 2A
= 1 − 2(3/5)² = 1 − 18/25 = 7/25
tan 2A
= (24/25) / (7/25) = 24/7
∴ Answer:
sin 2A = 24/25  |  cos 2A = 7/25  |  tan 2A = 24/7
(ii) Prove: sin 3A = 3 sin A − 4 sin³A
Proof
LHS
= sin 3A = sin(2A + A)
Step 1
= sin 2A cos A + cos 2A sin A
Step 2
= (2 sin A cos A) cos A + (1 − 2sin²A) sin A
Step 3
= 2 sin A cos²A + sin A − 2sin³A
Step 4
= 2 sin A(1 − sin²A) + sin A − 2sin³A
Step 5
= 2 sin A − 2sin³A + sin A − 2sin³A
Step 6
= 3 sin A − 4sin³A = RHS
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
(iii) Find the value of sin 75° and cos 75° using compound angle formula.
Given:
75° = 45° + 30°
sin 75°
Step 1
sin 75° = sin(45° + 30°)
Step 2
= sin 45° cos 30° + cos 45° sin 30°
Step 3
= (1/√2)(√3/2) + (1/√2)(1/2)
Step 4
= √3/(2√2) + 1/(2√2) = (√3 + 1)/(2√2)
∴ sin 75°
(√3 + 1) / (2√2) = (√6 + √2)/4
cos 75°
Step 1
cos 75° = cos(45° + 30°)
Step 2
= cos 45° cos 30° − sin 45° sin 30°
Step 3
= (1/√2)(√3/2) − (1/√2)(1/2)
Step 4
= (√3 − 1)/(2√2)
∴ cos 75°
(√3 − 1) / (2√2) = (√6 − √2)/4
Q 9 Factorise / Prove using sum-to-product formulae. ⭐ IMP Sum to Product
(i) Prove: (cos A − cos B)/(sin A − sin B) = −tan((A+B)/2)
Sum-to-Product:
cos A − cos B = −2 sin((A+B)/2) sin((A−B)/2)
sin A − sin B = 2 cos((A+B)/2) sin((A−B)/2)
Solution
LHS
= (cos A − cos B) / (sin A − sin B)
Step 1
= [−2 sin((A+B)/2) sin((A−B)/2)] / [2 cos((A+B)/2) sin((A−B)/2)]
Step 2
Cancel 2 sin((A−B)/2)
= −sin((A+B)/2) / cos((A+B)/2)
Step 3
= −tan((A+B)/2) = RHS
∴ LHS = RHS
Proved ✓
(ii) Prove: sin 20° sin 40° sin 80° = (√3)/8
Used:
sin A · sin(60°−A) · sin(60°+A) = (1/4) sin 3A
Solution
Step 1
sin 20° · sin 40° · sin 80°
= sin 20° · sin(60°−20°) · sin(60°+20°)
Step 2
= (1/4) sin(3 × 20°) = (1/4) sin 60°
Step 3
= (1/4) × (√3/2) = √3/8
∴ Answer:
sin 20° sin 40° sin 80° = √3/8   Proved ✓

Chapter 4 — Determinants and Matrices

Value of determinant, Matrix operations, Inverse, Cramer's rule — heavy weightage

Q 10 Find the value of the following determinants. ⭐ Most IMP Determinants
(i) Find the value of |3 −1; 4 2|
Given:
2×2 determinant: top row [3, −1], bottom row [4, 2]
Formula:
|a b; c d| = ad − bc
Solution
Step 1
= (3)(2) − (−1)(4)
Step 2
= 6 + 4 = 10
∴ Answer:
D = 10
(ii) Evaluate the 3×3 determinant:
D = |1 2 3; 2 −1 1; 3 1 −2|
Given:
3×3 matrix — expand along first row
Formula:
Expand along R₁: D = a₁₁M₁₁ − a₁₂M₁₂ + a₁₃M₁₃
Solution
Step 1
D = 1·|−1 1; 1 −2| − 2·|2 1; 3 −2| + 3·|2 −1; 3 1|
Step 2
= 1·[(−1)(−2) − (1)(1)] − 2·[(2)(−2) − (1)(3)] + 3·[(2)(1) − (−1)(3)]
Step 3
= 1·[2 − 1] − 2·[−4 − 3] + 3·[2 + 3]
Step 4
= 1(1) − 2(−7) + 3(5)
Step 5
= 1 + 14 + 15 = 30
∴ Answer:
D = 30
Q 11 Matrix operations — Addition, Multiplication, Transpose. ⭐ Most IMP Matrices
(i) If A = [1 2; 3 4] and B = [5 6; 7 8], find A + B, A − B and 2A.
Formula:
Add/subtract corresponding elements. Scalar multiply each element.
Solution
A + B
= [1+5 2+6; 3+7 4+8]
= [6 8; 10 12]
A − B
= [1−5 2−6; 3−7 4−8]
= [−4 −4; −4 −4]
2A
= [2×1 2×2; 2×3 2×4]
= [2 4; 6 8]
∴ Answer:
A+B = [6 8; 10 12]  |  A−B = [−4 −4; −4 −4]  |  2A = [2 4; 6 8]
(ii) If A = [1 2 3; 0 1 4] (2×3) and B = [1 0; 2 1; 3 2] (3×2), find AB.
Given:
A is 2×3, B is 3×2 → AB will be 2×2
Formula:
(AB)ᵢⱼ = sum of products of row i of A × column j of B
Solution
(AB)₁₁
= (1)(1) + (2)(2) + (3)(3) = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14
(AB)₁₂
= (1)(0) + (2)(1) + (3)(2) = 0 + 2 + 6 = 8
(AB)₂₁
= (0)(1) + (1)(2) + (4)(3) = 0 + 2 + 12 = 14
(AB)₂₂
= (0)(0) + (1)(1) + (4)(2) = 0 + 1 + 8 = 9
∴ AB =
[14 8; 14 9]
(iii) Find the inverse of A = [2 1; 5 3]
Formula:
A⁻¹ = (1/det A) × adj A
For 2×2: if A=[a b;c d], adj A = [d −b; −c a]
Solution
Step 1
Find det A
det A = (2)(3) − (1)(5) = 6 − 5 = 1
Step 2
Find adj A
adj A = [3 −1; −5 2]
Step 3
A⁻¹ = (1/1) × [3 −1; −5 2] = [3 −1; −5 2]
∴ A⁻¹ =
[3 −1; −5 2]
✓ Check: A × A⁻¹ = [2 1; 5 3][3 −1; −5 2] = [6−5 −2+2; 15−15 −5+6] = [1 0; 0 1] = I ✓
Q 12 Solve using Cramer's Rule. ⭐ Most IMP Cramer's Rule
(i) 3x − y = 5    2x + 3y = 12
Given:
3x − y = 5  …(1)     2x + 3y = 12  …(2)
Formula:
x = Dx/D,   y = Dy/D
Solution
Step 1
Find D
D = |3 −1; 2 3| = (3)(3) − (−1)(2) = 9 + 2 = 11
Step 2
Find Dx
Dx = |5 −1; 12 3| = (5)(3) − (−1)(12) = 15 + 12 = 27
Step 3
Find Dy
Dy = |3 5; 2 12| = (3)(12) − (5)(2) = 36 − 10 = 26
Step 4
x = Dx/D = 27/11     y = Dy/D = 26/11
∴ Answer:
x = 27/11,   y = 26/11
(ii) 2x + 3y + 3z = 5    x − 2y + z = −4    3x − y − 2z = 3
Given:
System of 3 equations
Solution
Step 1
Find D
D = |2 3 3; 1 −2 1; 3 −1 −2|
= 2[(−2)(−2)−(1)(−1)] − 3[(1)(−2)−(1)(3)] + 3[(1)(−1)−(−2)(3)]
= 2[4+1] − 3[−2−3] + 3[−1+6]
= 2(5) − 3(−5) + 3(5) = 10+15+15 = 40
Step 2
Find Dx
Dx = |5 3 3; −4 −2 1; 3 −1 −2|
= 5[(−2)(−2)−(1)(−1)] − 3[(−4)(−2)−(1)(3)] + 3[(−4)(−1)−(−2)(3)]
= 5(5) − 3(8−3) + 3(4+6) = 25−15+30 = 40
Step 3
x = Dx/D = 40/40 = 1
Step 4
Find Dy
Dy = |2 5 3; 1 −4 1; 3 3 −2|
= 2[(−4)(−2)−(1)(3)] − 5[(1)(−2)−(1)(3)] + 3[(1)(3)−(−4)(3)]
= 2(8−3) − 5(−2−3) + 3(3+12) = 10+25+45 = 80
y = Dy/D = 80/40 = 2
Step 5
Find Dz
Dz = |2 3 5; 1 −2 −4; 3 −1 3|
= 2[(−2)(3)−(−4)(−1)] − 3[(1)(3)−(−4)(3)] + 5[(1)(−1)−(−2)(3)]
= 2(−6−4) − 3(3+12) + 5(−1+6) = −20−45+25 = −40
z = Dz/D = −40/40 = −1
∴ Answer:
x = 1,   y = 2,   z = −1
✓ Check eq(1): 2(1)+3(2)+3(−1) = 2+6−3 = 5 ✓  |  eq(2): 1−4+(−1) = −4 ✓
