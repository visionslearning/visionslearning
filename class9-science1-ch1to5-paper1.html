Ch1 A. State Newton's Third Law of Motion with an example.
Statement: To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction; these forces act on different objects simultaneously.
Example: When a gun fires a bullet (action = bullet pushed forward), the gun recoils backward (reaction). Or: When we push a wall, the wall pushes us back with equal force.
Ch2 B. Differentiate between kinetic energy and potential energy.
  • Kinetic Energy (KE): Energy possessed by a body due to its motion. KE = ½mv². E.g., a moving ball.
  • Potential Energy (PE): Energy possessed by a body due to its position or state. PE = mgh. E.g., water stored in a dam.
  • KE depends on velocity; PE depends on height/position.
  • When a body falls, PE converts to KE; total mechanical energy remains constant.
Ch3 C. State Ohm's Law. Write its formula and give SI units.
Ohm's Law: At constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across it.
V = IR
where V = voltage (Volt), I = current (Ampere), R = resistance (Ohm Ω).
Ch4 D. What is the mole concept? Define molar mass.
  • Mole: A mole is the amount of a substance that contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles (atoms, molecules, or ions). It is the SI unit of amount of substance.
  • Molar mass: The mass of one mole of a substance expressed in grams is called molar mass. E.g., molar mass of water (H₂O) = 18 g/mol.
  • n = given mass / molar mass = m/M
Ch5 E. What are indicators? Name two natural indicators and their colours in acid and base.
  • Indicators: Substances that change colour in acidic or basic solutions, used to test whether a solution is acid, base, or neutral.
  • Litmus: Red in acid, Blue in base.
  • Turmeric: Yellow in acid/neutral, Red-brown in base.
  • Red cabbage juice: Red in acid, Green in base.

Ch1 A. A passenger in a moving bus falls forward when the bus stops suddenly.
Due to inertia of motion (Newton's First Law), the passenger's body tends to continue moving forward even after the bus stops. Since the lower body (feet) stops with the bus but the upper body continues to move forward due to inertia, the passenger falls forward.
Ch3 B. Household electrical appliances are connected in parallel, not in series.
In a parallel connection: (1) Each appliance gets the same (full) voltage of the supply. (2) Each appliance can be switched on/off independently. (3) If one appliance fails, others continue to work. In series, voltage is shared and all go off if one fails — unsuitable for domestic use.
Ch5 C. An aqueous solution of sodium chloride (NaCl) is neutral.
NaCl is a salt formed from a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH). When dissolved in water, it neither produces excess H⁺ ions nor OH⁻ ions — the ions from the acid and base completely cancel each other. Therefore, the solution is neutral with pH = 7.

Ch2 A. State and explain the Law of Conservation of Energy with an example of a freely falling body.
Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be converted from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

Freely Falling Body:
Consider a ball of mass m dropped from height h:
  • At top (height h): PE = mgh, KE = 0 → Total = mgh
  • At midpoint (h/2): PE = mgh/2, KE = mgh/2 → Total = mgh
  • Just before hitting ground: PE = 0, KE = mgh = ½mv² → Total = mgh
At every point, PE + KE = mgh = constant. PE converts entirely to KE during fall. Total mechanical energy is conserved (ignoring air resistance).

Ch5 B. Explain the properties of acids and bases. Give 3 properties each with examples.
Properties of Acids:
  • 1. Sour taste (e.g., lemon juice — citric acid).
  • 2. Turn blue litmus red.
  • 3. React with metals to produce hydrogen gas: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑
  • 4. React with bases (neutralisation): HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • 5. pH < 7; conduct electricity in solution (electrolytes).
Properties of Bases:
  • 1. Bitter taste, soapy/slippery feel (e.g., soap — NaOH base).
  • 2. Turn red litmus blue.
  • 3. React with acids to form salt and water (neutralisation).
  • 4. pH > 7; conduct electricity in solution.
  • 5. React with certain metals: 2NaOH + Zn → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂↑

Ch1 A. State Newton's Laws of Motion. Explain Newton's Second Law with derivation of F = ma.
Newton's Three Laws:
  • 1st Law: A body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force (Law of Inertia).
  • 2nd Law: The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and occurs in the direction of force.
  • 3rd Law: Every action has an equal and opposite reaction acting on different bodies.
Derivation of F = ma (Newton's 2nd Law):
  • Let mass = m, initial velocity = u, final velocity = v, time = t.
  • Initial momentum: p₁ = mu; Final momentum: p₂ = mv
  • Change in momentum = mv – mu = m(v–u)
  • Rate of change of momentum = m(v–u)/t = m × a (since a = (v–u)/t)
  • By 2nd Law: F ∝ m(v–u)/t → F = k × ma
  • Taking k = 1 (SI units): F = ma
SI unit of force = kg·m/s² = Newton (N).
Ch4 B. Explain the concept of atomic mass and molecular mass. Calculate the molecular mass of H₂SO₄.
Atomic Mass:
  • The relative mass of an atom compared to 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom is called atomic mass.
  • It is expressed in atomic mass units (u). E.g., H = 1u, O = 16u, N = 14u, C = 12u.
Molecular Mass:
  • The sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule is called molecular mass.
  • It is expressed in atomic mass units (u) or g/mol (molar mass).
Calculation — Molecular mass of H₂SO₄:
  • H₂SO₄ contains: 2 H atoms, 1 S atom, 4 O atoms
  • Atomic masses: H = 1, S = 32, O = 16
  • Molecular mass = (2×1) + (1×32) + (4×16)
  • = 2 + 32 + 64
  • = 98 u (or 98 g/mol)

Ch3 A. Explain the characteristics of series and parallel combinations of resistors. Solve: Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected (i) in series, (ii) in parallel. Find equivalent resistance in each case. If connected to 12 V battery, find current in each case.
Series Combination:
  • Same current flows through all resistors.
  • Total voltage = sum of individual voltages.
  • R_series = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
  • R_series = 2 + 3 + 6 = 11 Ω
  • Current I = V/R = 12/11 ≈ 1.09 A
Parallel Combination:
  • Same voltage across all resistors.
  • Total current = sum of branch currents.
  • 1/R_parallel = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
  • 1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 6/6 = 1
  • R_parallel = 1 Ω
  • Current I = V/R = 12/1 = 12 A
Ch2 B. (i) Define potential energy and give its SI unit and CGS unit. (ii) Give the relationship between Joule and erg.
(i) Potential Energy:
  • The energy possessed by an object due to its position or state is called potential energy.
  • Formula: PE = mgh (gravitational PE)
  • SI unit: Joule (J) = kg·m²/s²
  • CGS unit: erg = g·cm²/s²
(ii) Relationship between Joule and Erg:
  • 1 Joule = 1 kg·m²/s²
  • = 1000 g × (100 cm)²/s²
  • = 1000 × 10000 g·cm²/s²
  • 1 Joule = 10⁷ erg
