Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
i. What is refraction of light?
Refraction of light is the phenomenon of bending of light when it travels obliquely from one transparent medium to another medium of different optical density.
ii. Define the refractive index of a medium.
Refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (or air) to the speed of light in that medium: n = c/v.
iii. State Snell's law of refraction.
Snell's law states that the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media, i.e., sin i / sin r = n (refractive index).
iv. What is meant by 'optically denser medium'?
A medium in which the speed of light is less and whose refractive index is higher compared to another medium is called an optically denser medium.
v. Why does a coin placed at the bottom of a water-filled vessel appear raised?
Due to refraction of light at the water-air interface, the light rays from the coin bend away from the normal as they emerge from water to air, making the coin appear raised (at a shallower position).

Write short notes. (Any 3)
6 Marks
A. Laws of Refraction (2 marks)
  • First Law: The incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal to the refracting surface at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
  • Second Law (Snell's Law): The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media.
    sin i / sin r = n₂₁ = constant
  • When light goes from a rarer to a denser medium: angle of refraction < angle of incidence.
  • When light goes from a denser to a rarer medium: angle of refraction > angle of incidence.
B. Absolute Refractive Index (2 marks)
  • The absolute refractive index (n) of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (c) to the speed of light in that medium (v).
  • n = c / v
  • Speed of light in vacuum: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • Since v ≤ c always, refractive index n ≥ 1 for all media.
  • Examples: n of air ≈ 1.0003 (≈1), n of water ≈ 1.33, n of glass ≈ 1.5, n of diamond ≈ 2.42
  • Higher the refractive index → slower the light → optically denser medium.
C. Refraction through a Glass Slab (2 marks)
  • When a ray of light passes through a rectangular glass slab, it undergoes refraction at both surfaces (air→glass and glass→air).
  • At the first surface: ray bends towards the normal (denser medium).
  • At the second surface: ray bends away from the normal (rarer medium).
  • The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but displaced laterally — this displacement is called lateral displacement.
  • Lateral displacement increases as the angle of incidence increases and as the thickness of the slab increases.
📐 Draw diagram: incident ray → refraction at surface 1 → refraction at surface 2 → emergent ray (parallel, displaced)
D. Effects of Refraction in Daily Life (2 marks)
  • Apparent depth: Objects under water appear shallower than they are (e.g., bottom of a swimming pool appears raised).
  • Bent stick: A stick partially immersed in water appears bent or broken at the water surface.
  • Twinkling of stars: Light from stars refracts through layers of atmosphere of varying density, causing twinkling.
  • Mirages: In hot deserts, layers of air at different temperatures act as different media, causing refraction of light from distant objects — creating illusion of water.
  • Rainbow: Formed by refraction and total internal reflection of sunlight inside water droplets.

Answer the following questions in brief. (Any 2)

A. Explain refraction of light with the help of a neat labelled diagram. State the laws of refraction. (4 marks)
Refraction of Light:
  • When a ray of light travels from one transparent medium to another, it changes its direction at the interface. This bending of light is called refraction.
  • It occurs because the speed of light is different in different media.
  • The ray that falls on the surface is the incident ray; the ray that enters the second medium is the refracted ray; the perpendicular to the surface at the point of incidence is the normal.
  • The angle between the incident ray and the normal is the angle of incidence (i).
  • The angle between the refracted ray and the normal is the angle of refraction (r).
Laws of Refraction:
  • First Law: The incident ray, refracted ray and normal at the point of incidence are all in the same plane.
  • Second Law (Snell's Law):
    sin i / sin r = n₂₁ = n₂/n₁ = v₁/v₂
    The ratio is constant for a given pair of media and a given colour of light.
📐 Diagram: Two media (air above, glass below) separated by a horizontal line. Incident ray from air hits the surface; normal shown perpendicular; refracted ray in glass bending toward normal. Label: i (angle of incidence), r (angle of refraction), N (normal), Medium 1 (air), Medium 2 (glass).
B. What is the refractive index? Derive the relationship between refractive index, speed of light, and wavelength. (4 marks)
Refractive Index:
  • The refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1 is defined as:
    n₂₁ = speed of light in medium 1 / speed of light in medium 2 = v₁/v₂
  • Absolute refractive index (medium 1 = vacuum):
    n = c / v
    where c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (speed of light in vacuum), v = speed in medium.
  • Since speed (v) = frequency (f) × wavelength (λ), and frequency does not change during refraction:
    n = c/v = λ₀/λₘ
    where λ₀ = wavelength in vacuum, λₘ = wavelength in medium.
  • This means: higher refractive index → lower speed → shorter wavelength in that medium.
Key values of absolute refractive index:
  • Air ≈ 1.0003 | Water ≈ 1.33 | Glass ≈ 1.5 | Diamond ≈ 2.42
C. What is total internal reflection? State its conditions and give two applications. (4 marks)
Total Internal Reflection (TIR):
  • When a ray of light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium, and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, the light is completely reflected back into the denser medium. This is called Total Internal Reflection.
Conditions for TIR:
  • 1. Light must travel from a denser medium to a rarer medium.
  • 2. The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle (C).
  • At critical angle: the refracted ray grazes the surface (angle of refraction = 90°).
    sin C = 1/n (n = refractive index of denser medium w.r.t. rarer medium)
Applications:
  • Optical Fibres: Light travels through glass/plastic fibres by repeated total internal reflection — used in internet cables and medical endoscopes.
  • Sparkling of diamond: Diamond has a very high refractive index (2.42) and very small critical angle (~24°), causing TIR repeatedly inside, making it sparkle brilliantly.
  • Mirages: Hot air near ground acts as rarer medium → TIR of light from sky creates mirage effect.

Solve the following numericals. (Any 3)
6 Marks
A. A ray of light travels from air into glass. The angle of incidence is 30° and the angle of refraction is 19.47°. Calculate the refractive index of glass. (2 marks)
Given: i = 30°, r = 19.47°
Formula: n = sin i / sin r
sin 30° = 0.5000
sin 19.47° = 0.3333
n = 0.5000 / 0.3333
∴ Refractive index of glass n ≈ 1.50
B. The speed of light in vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Find the speed of light in water if the refractive index of water is 1.33. (2 marks)
Given: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, n = 1.33
Formula: n = c / v → v = c / n
v = (3 × 10⁸) / 1.33
v = 2.255 × 10⁸ m/s
∴ Speed of light in water ≈ 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s
C. The refractive index of glass is 1.5. Calculate the critical angle for glass-air interface. (sin⁻¹(0.667) = 41.8°) (2 marks)
Given: n (glass) = 1.5
Formula: sin C = 1/n
sin C = 1 / 1.5 = 0.6667
C = sin⁻¹(0.6667) = 41.8°
∴ Critical angle for glass-air = 41.8°
D. A ray of light passes from glass (n = 1.5) to water (n = 1.33). The angle of incidence in glass is 45°. Find the angle of refraction in water. (sin 45° = 0.707) (2 marks)
Given: n₁ = 1.5 (glass), n₂ = 1.33 (water), i = 45°
Formula (Snell's Law): n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
1.5 × sin 45° = 1.33 × sin r
1.5 × 0.707 = 1.33 × sin r
1.0605 = 1.33 × sin r
sin r = 1.0605 / 1.33 = 0.7974
r = sin⁻¹(0.7974) ≈ 52.9°
∴ Angle of refraction in water ≈ 52.9°
E. The wavelength of light in vacuum is 600 nm. Find its wavelength in glass of refractive index 1.5. (2 marks)
Given: λ₀ = 600 nm, n = 1.5
Formula: n = λ₀ / λₘ → λₘ = λ₀ / n
λₘ = 600 / 1.5
λₘ = 400 nm
∴ Wavelength of light in glass = 400 nm
