Centre of Mass

The centre of mass is the unique point where the entire mass of a body or system of particles appears to be concentrated. It is the weighted average position of all the mass in the system.

⚛️ FY BSc · Physics · Systems of Particles
🔢

Formula

For two particles
xcm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂) / (m₁ + m₂)
where m₁, m₂ are masses and x₁, x₂ are their positions
For many particles (general form)
xcm = Σ(mᵢ xᵢ) / Σmᵢ
Summation over all particles in the system
3D position vector
rcm = Σ(mᵢ rᵢ) / M
M = total mass of the system
⚡

Important Points

  • 1 The centre of mass always lies closer to the heavier mass in a two-body system.
  • 2 For a uniform symmetric body (sphere, ring, rod), the centre of mass coincides with the geometric centre.
  • 3 The centre of mass can lie outside the material of an object (e.g., a ring or hollow sphere).
  • 4 It does not depend on the choice of coordinate system — only on mass distribution.
  • 5 In the absence of external forces, the centre of mass moves with constant velocity (or stays at rest).
🚀

Centre of Mass & Momentum

Linear Momentum

  • The total momentum of a system equals total mass × velocity of CM.
  • If no external force acts, total momentum is conserved.
  • CM continues in uniform motion.
Ptotal = M · vcm

Newton's 2nd Law for a System

  • External force = total mass × acceleration of CM.
  • Internal forces cancel in pairs (Newton's 3rd law).
  • Only external forces change the state of CM.
Fext = M · acm
📋

Summary

⚖️

Centre of mass is the mass-weighted average position of a system.

📐

Its position depends on mass distribution, not the shape alone.

🔵

It can lie outside the material of an object (e.g., a ring or horseshoe).

🚀

Total momentum is related to the velocity of the centre of mass: P = Mvcm
