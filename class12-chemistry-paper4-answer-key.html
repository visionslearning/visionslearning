Q.1 (ii)
Micelles are formed by:
✔ Answer: (C) surface active agents (soaps)
Explanation Soaps/detergents = amphipathic molecules (hydrophilic head + hydrophobic tail) Above critical micelle concentration (CMC): hydrophobic tails cluster inward, heads face water → micelle Used for solubilising oils/greases (cleaning action)
Q.1 (iii)
At the anode in electrolysis:
✔ Answer: (A) oxidation occurs
Explanation Anode = positive electrode → attracts anions → anions lose electrons → oxidation at anode Cathode = negative electrode → attracts cations → cations gain electrons → reduction at cathode Mnemonic: AnOx (Anode Oxidation), RedCat (Reduction Cathode)
Q.1 (iv)
sp³d hybridisation gives which geometry?
✔ Answer: (B) trigonal bipyramidal
Explanation sp³d = 5 hybrid orbitals → trigonal bipyramidal (3 equatorial + 2 axial positions) Example: PCl₅ (sp³d), SF₄ (sp³d with 1 lone pair) sp³d² = 6 orbitals → octahedral (SF₆, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻)
Q.1 (v)

Section B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
Q.2 (i)
Define activation energy using Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution.
DefinitionActivation energy (Ea) is the minimum amount of extra energy (above the average kinetic energy of molecules) that reactant molecules must possess to undergo an effective collision and form products.
Maxwell-BoltzmannThe area under the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve to the right of Ea represents the fraction of molecules that can react. At higher T, this fraction increases → faster reaction.
Q.2 (ii)
What is the principle of dialysis?
PrincipleDialysis works on the principle that colloidal particles cannot pass through a semipermeable membrane (parchment paper, cellophane), but dissolved electrolytes and small molecules can diffuse through it into the surrounding water.
ApplicationPurification of colloidal sols from electrolyte impurities. Also: kidney dialysis in renal failure.
Q.2 (iii)
Define Henry's law constant (K_H).
DefinitionHenry's law constant (K_H) is the ratio of the partial pressure of a gas to its mole fraction in the liquid at a given temperature. It measures the solubility of the gas — higher K_H means lower solubility.
FormulaK_H = p/x   Unit: bar or atm. K_H increases with temperature (gases less soluble at higher T).
Q.2 (iv)
What is a nucleofuge? Give one example.
DefinitionA nucleofuge (leaving group) is a group that departs with its electron pair from the carbon centre during a substitution or elimination reaction.
Good nucleofugeStable as an anion. Examples: Br⁻ (best), Cl⁻, I⁻, TsO⁻, OTs⁻, H₂O. Good leaving groups leave as stable anions. F⁻ and OH⁻ are poor leaving groups.
Q.2 (v)
What are globular proteins? Give two examples.
DefinitionGlobular proteins are proteins that are compactly folded into a roughly spherical (globular) shape. They are generally soluble in water and have specific biological functions.
Examples(1) Haemoglobin — oxygen transport in blood
(2) Myoglobin — oxygen storage in muscles
(3) Enzymes (amylase, pepsin) — biological catalysts
Q.2 (vi)
What is a cross-linked polymer? Give one example.
DefinitionA cross-linked polymer has covalent bonds (cross-links) connecting different polymer chains, forming a three-dimensional network. This makes the polymer hard, rigid and infusible (thermosetting).
ExampleBakelite (C–C cross-links via CH₂ bridges), Vulcanised rubber (S–S cross-links), Melamine resin
Q.2 (vii)
Define a chelate complex. Give one example.
DefinitionA chelate complex is formed when a polydentate ligand uses two or more donor atoms to bond to the same central metal ion, forming a ring structure (chelate ring). Chelate complexes are more stable than non-chelate (chelate effect).
Example[Cu(en)₂]²⁺ — en (ethylenediamine, bidentate) forms two 5-membered chelate rings with Cu²⁺
Q.2 (viii)
What is the Tyndall cone?
DefinitionThe Tyndall cone is the cone-shaped illuminated beam of light that becomes visible when light is passed through a colloidal solution. It is caused by scattering of light by colloidal particles (Tyndall effect).
SignificanceUsed to distinguish a colloid from a true solution. Also seen in: smoke from car exhausts, beam through misty air, light through a window in a dusty room.
Q.2 (ix)
What are reducing sugars? Give two examples.
DefinitionReducing sugars are carbohydrates that can reduce Tollens' reagent, Fehling's solution, or Benedict's solution. They have a free aldehyde or hemiacetal group (free anomeric carbon) that acts as a reducing agent.
Examples(1) Glucose — reduces Tollens' reagent to give silver mirror
(2) Maltose, Lactose, Fructose — all reducing sugars
Non-reducing: Sucrose (no free anomeric carbon)
Q.2 (x)
Define lattice energy. State its sign.
DefinitionLattice energy is the energy released when one mole of a crystalline ionic compound is formed from its constituent gaseous ions at standard conditions.
SignNegative (exothermic — energy is released when ions attract and form lattice)
Example: Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g) → NaCl(s) + 787 kJ/mol (lattice energy = −787 kJ/mol)
Q.2 (xi)
What is the common ion effect? State one application.
DefinitionThe common ion effect is the suppression of ionisation (or solubility) of a weak electrolyte or sparingly soluble salt by the addition of a strong electrolyte containing a common ion (Le Chatelier's principle).
ApplicationBuffer solutions: CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa → pH maintained (common ion CH₃COO⁻). Also: controlled precipitation in qualitative analysis.
Q.2 (xii)
What is metallic bonding?
DefinitionMetallic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions (cations) arranged in a lattice and the sea of delocalised free electrons that surround them. The free electrons are shared by all metal cations ("electron sea" model).
Properties ExplainedHigh electrical conductivity (free electrons), thermal conductivity, malleability, ductility, metallic lustre — all due to metallic bonding.

Q.3 (i)
Derive the first-order integrated rate law. Show the plot of ln[A] vs t is linear.
Derivation −d[A]/dt = k[A] → d[A]/[A] = −k dt Integrating: ln[A] − ln[A]₀ = −kt ln[A] = −kt + ln[A]₀ (y = mx + c)
Graph Plot of ln[A] vs t: straight line with slope = −k (negative) and intercept = ln[A]₀ This confirms first-order kinetics.
Q.3 (ii)
Explain the Tyndall effect and Brownian motion in colloids.
Tyndall Effect When a beam of light passes through a colloid, the colloidal particles (1–100 nm) scatter the light in all directions → the path of light becomes visible (Tyndall cone). Reason: Colloidal particle size is comparable to wavelength of light → scattering occurs (not in true solutions — particles too small)
Brownian Motion Continuous zig-zag random motion of colloidal particles due to unequal bombardment from all sides by solvent molecules. Reason: Solvent molecules hit the colloidal particles randomly from all directions → net force is never zero → continuous random motion Significance: Prevents sedimentation (keeps colloid stable)
Q.3 (iii)
Explain abnormal molar mass and van't Hoff factor.
Abnormal Molar MassSometimes the experimentally determined molar mass differs from the theoretical molar mass. This is because the solute either dissociates (NaCl → more particles, lower M_apparent) or associates (CH₃COOH dimerises in benzene → fewer particles, higher M_apparent).
Van't Hoff Factor i = (observed colligative property)/(expected for non-electrolyte) = total particles/original moles Dissociation: NaCl → i = 2; CaCl₂ → i = 3; K₂SO₄ → i = 3 Association: CH₃COOH dimer → i = 0.5
Q.3 (iv)
Explain the mechanism of SN1 reaction for tert-butyl bromide.
Step 1 (slow) (CH₃)₃C–Br → (CH₃)₃C⁺ + Br⁻ (ionisation; forms tertiary carbocation) Rate = k[(CH₃)₃CBr] (unimolecular, first order)
Step 2 (fast) (CH₃)₃C⁺ + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃C–OH (nucleophilic attack on flat carbocation)
StereochemistryPlanar carbocation → Nu attacks from both sides → racemic mixture (50% inversion + 50% retention)

Write the ozonolysis reaction of 2-butene. Give all products.

Mechanism O₃ adds across C=C → molozonide (cyclic) → rearranges to ozonide → cleave with Zn/H₂O (reductive) or H₂O₂ (oxidative) Products show where the double bond was — useful in structure determination

Q.3 (vii)
List five methods to increase the rate of a chemical reaction.
Methods (1) Increase temperature: More molecules have Ea; reaction rate approximately doubles per 10°C rise.
(2) Increase concentration of reactants: More collisions per unit time.
(3) Add a catalyst: Lowers activation energy (alternative pathway).
(4) Increase surface area: For solid reactants — more exposed particles → more collisions.
(5) Increase pressure (for gaseous reactions): increases effective concentration → more collisions.
Q.3 (viii)
Explain crystal field splitting in an octahedral complex with diagram.
TheoryIn an octahedral complex, 6 ligands approach the central metal along the ±x, ±y, ±z axes. The d orbitals pointing toward ligands (d_z², d_x²-y²) face greater repulsion and have higher energy.
Splitting e_g set (d_z², d_x²-y²): raised by 3/5 Δ₀ above average (face ligands) t₂g set (d_xy, d_xz, d_yz): lowered by 2/5 Δ₀ below average (avoid ligands) Crystal field splitting energy: Δ₀ (large for strong field, small for weak field)
ApplicationsExplains colour (ΔE = hν), magnetism (high spin vs low spin), and stability of complexes

Q.3 (x)
Explain the aldol condensation of ethanal with mechanism.
Reaction 2CH₃CHO → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO (3-hydroxybutanal, aldol) → CH₃CH=CHCHO + H₂O (crotonaldehyde)
Mechanism Step 1: OH⁻ removes α-H from CH₃CHO → CH₂CHO⁻ (enolate) Step 2: Enolate C attacks the C=O of another CH₃CHO → alkoxide ion Step 3: Alkoxide ion picks up proton → aldol (3-hydroxybutanal) Step 4 (heating): Dehydration → conjugated aldehyde (crotonaldehyde)
Q.3 (xi)
Explain Anti-Markovnikov's addition (peroxide effect) with an example.
StatementIn the presence of peroxides (ROOR) as initiator, HBr adds to unsymmetrical alkenes in an anti-Markovnikov fashion — Br goes to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms.
Mechanism (free radical) Initiation: ROOR → 2RO• → RO• + HBr → ROH + Br• Propagation: Br• + CH₃–CH=CH₂ → CH₃–CH•–CH₂Br (more stable radical at secondary C) Br• adds to CH₂ end (less substituted) → secondary radical at C2 → stable Product: CH₃–CHBr–CH₃... no — actually: CH₃–CH•–CH₂Br → + HBr → CH₃–CH₂–CH₂Br (1-bromopropane) Anti-Markovnikov: Br at terminal carbon (opposite to Markovnikov)

Explain positive and negative deviations from Raoult's law — with examples and reasons.

Negative Deviation p_total < p_Raoult. A–B interactions stronger than A–A and B–B. ΔH_mix < 0 (exothermic), ΔV_mix < 0. Forms maximum boiling azeotrope. Example: Chloroform (CHCl₃) + Acetone (CH₃COCH₃): H-bond forms between C–H of CHCl₃ and O of acetone Example: HNO₃ + Water (68% HNO₃ azeotrope at 120.5°C)
Section E — Long Answer Questions 4 × 4 = 16 Marks
Q.5 (ii)
(a) Electrochemical series applications. (b) Calculate ΔG° for Zn/Cu cell.
Electrochemical Series Applications (1) Predicting displacement reactions: Metal higher (more negative E°) displaces metal lower (more positive E°). Zn displaces Cu from CuSO₄ solution. (2) Calculating E°_cell: E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode. Positive E°_cell → spontaneous reaction. (3) Hydrogen displacement from acids: Metals with E° < 0 (above H in series) displace H₂ from dilute acids (Zn, Fe, Mg etc.) (4) Strength of oxidising/reducing agents: Lower E° = stronger reducing agent; Higher E° = stronger oxidising agent.
ΔG° Calculation E°_cell = 1.10 V, n = 2 (two electrons transferred), F = 96500 C/mol ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96500 × 1.10 = −212,300 J/mol
Answer
ΔG° = −212.3 kJ/mol (negative → spontaneous reaction)
Q.5 (iii)
(a) Mechanism of nucleophilic addition to carbonyl. (b) Three tests to distinguish aldehyde from ketone.
Nucleophilic Addition Carbonyl carbon (C=O) is electrophilic (partially positive due to electronegativity of O). Step 1: Nucleophile (Nu⁻) attacks the electrophilic C of C=O → tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate Step 2: Proton transfer (from solvent H₂O or acid) → addition product Example: HCN addition: CH₃CHO + HCN → CH₃CH(OH)CN (cyanohydrin)
Tests (aldehyde vs ketone) (1) Tollens' test: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ → Aldehyde: silver mirror ✓  |  Ketone: no reaction (2) Fehling's test: Cu²⁺/alkaline → Aldehyde: red Cu₂O precipitate ✓  |  Ketone: no reaction (3) Schiff's test: Rosaniline decolorised by SO₂ → Aldehyde: pink/magenta colour restored ✓  |  Ketone: no reaction
Q.5 (v)
(a) Depression of freezing point. (b) Find molar mass: 2.5 g in 100 g benzene, ΔTf = 0.5°C, Kf = 5.12.
Depression of Freezing Point When non-volatile solute is added, vapour pressure decreases → solution must be cooled more to achieve solid-liquid equilibrium. ΔTf = Kf × m = Kf × (w_B × 1000)/(M_B × w_A) Kf = cryoscopic constant (for water = 1.86, benzene = 5.12, camphor = 37.7 K·kg/mol)
Numerical w_B = 2.5 g, w_A = 100 g, ΔTf = 0.5°C, Kf = 5.12 K·kg/mol M_B = (Kf × w_B × 1000)/(ΔTf × w_A) = (5.12 × 2.5 × 1000)/(0.5 × 100) = 12800/50
Answer
M_B = 256 g/mol
