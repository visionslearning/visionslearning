Mirrors1M
Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles and why?
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
  • (a) Concave mirror — converges light
  • (b) Convex mirror — gives wider field of view
  • (c) Plane mirror — gives exact image
  • (d) Concave mirror — forms enlarged image
Reason: Convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image and provides a wider field of view, so the driver can see more area behind the vehicle.
(ii)
Refraction1M
The critical angle for glass-air interface is 42°. What is the refractive index of glass?
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
  • (a) 1.33
  • (b) 1.49
  • (c) 1.60
  • (d) 2.00
sin θ_c = 1/n → n = 1/sin 42° = 1/0.669 ≈ 1.49
(iii)
Lenses1M
The power of a convex lens is +2 D and a concave lens is −1 D. What is the power of the combination?
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
  • (a) +1 D
  • (b) −1 D
  • (c) +3 D
  • (d) 2 D
P = P₁ + P₂ = +2 + (−1) = +1 D
Combined power = algebraic sum of individual powers. Positive result → converging combination.
(iv)
TIR1M
Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from ______.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
  • (a) rarer to denser medium at any angle
  • (b) denser to rarer medium at any angle
  • (c) rarer to denser at angle greater than critical angle
  • (d) denser to rarer medium at angle greater than critical angle
Two conditions for TIR:
  • Light must go from denser → rarer medium
  • Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle (i > θ_c)

Write the mirror formula.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
1/f = 1/v + 1/u
where f = focal length, v = image distance, u = object distance (all measured from pole P of mirror).
(ii)
TIR1M
State any two applications of total internal reflection.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
  • Optical fibre: Light undergoes repeated TIR inside a thin glass fibre. Used in internet cables, endoscopy, and communication.
  • Diamond brilliance: High refractive index (n ≈ 2.42) gives very small critical angle (≈ 24°). Light undergoes multiple TIR inside the diamond before emerging — produces brilliant sparkle.
Other applications: mirage, prism as reflector, periscope.
(iii)
Lenses1M
Define power of a lens. Write its SI unit.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
Power of a lens: The ability of a lens to converge or diverge a beam of light. It is defined as the reciprocal of the focal length in metres.
P = 1/f (metres)
SI unit: Dioptre (D). 1 D = 1 m⁻¹
Convex lens: P is positive. Concave lens: P is negative.
(iv)
Prism1M
What is angle of deviation in a prism?
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
Angle of deviation (d): The angle between the original direction of the incident ray and the direction of the emergent ray after passing through the prism.
d = i + e − A
where i = angle of incidence, e = angle of emergence, A = angle of prism (apex angle).

Refraction2M
State Snell's law of refraction. A ray of light passes from air into glass (n = 1.5) at an angle of incidence of 30°. Find the angle of refraction.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
Snell's Law: The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media and is equal to the refractive index.
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r  →  sin i / sin r = n₂/n₁ = n
n₁ = 1 (air), n₂ = 1.5 (glass), i = 30°
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
1 × sin 30° = 1.5 × sin r
0.5 = 1.5 × sin r
sin r = 0.5/1.5 = 0.333
r = sin⁻¹(0.333) ≈ 19.47°
Angle of refraction ≈ 19.5°

(C)
TIR2M
What is total internal reflection? State the conditions for it to occur. Give one application.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
Total Internal Reflection (TIR): When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, the light is completely reflected back into the denser medium. This is TIR.

Conditions:
  • Light must travel from denser → rarer medium (e.g., glass to air)
  • Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle (i > θ_c)
Critical angle:
sin θ_c = n₂/n₁ = 1/n  (for glass-air)
Application — Optical Fibre: Light enters one end of a thin glass fibre and undergoes repeated TIR along the length of the fibre without escaping. Used in internet cables, medical endoscopy, and communication systems.

📋 Q.4 — Short Answer II (3M each)

6 Marks
📌 Attempt any TWO of the following three questions.
(A)
Lenses3M
State the Lens Maker's formula. A convex lens (n = 1.5) has radii of curvature R₁ = 20 cm and R₂ = −20 cm. Find its focal length and power.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Lens Maker's Formula: Relates focal length of a lens to its refractive index and radii of curvature.
1/f = (n − 1) [1/R₁ − 1/R₂]
where n = refractive index of lens material, R₁ = radius of curvature of first surface, R₂ = radius of second surface.

n = 1.5, R₁ = +20 cm = +0.20 m, R₂ = −20 cm = −0.20 m
1/f = (1.5 − 1) × [1/0.20 − 1/(−0.20)]
1/f = 0.5 × [5 + 5]
1/f = 0.5 × 10 = 5 m⁻¹
f = 1/5 = 0.20 m = 20 cm
P = 1/f = 1/0.20 = +5 D
f = 20 cm | P = +5 D (converging)
(B)
Prism3M
Derive the formula for the refractive index of a prism in terms of angle of prism A and minimum deviation D_m.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Prism: Triangular transparent medium of apex angle A.
At minimum deviation D_m: The ray passes symmetrically through the prism, i.e., angle of incidence = angle of emergence (i = e) and angle of refraction at both surfaces is equal (r₁ = r₂ = r).

From geometry of prism: A = r₁ + r₂ = 2r r = A/2 ...(1) Total deviation: d = i + e − A = 2i − A (at minimum deviation, i = e) D_m = 2i − A → i = (A + D_m)/2 ...(2) Applying Snell's law at first surface (n₁ = 1 for air): sin i = n sin r Substituting (1) and (2):
n = sin i / sin r = sin[(A + D_m)/2] / sin(A/2)
This is the standard formula for refractive index of a prism.
(C)
Mirrors3M
Derive the mirror formula 1/f = 1/v + 1/u for a concave mirror.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Setup: Object AB is placed in front of a concave mirror at distance u from pole P. Image A'B' is formed at distance v. F is principal focus, C is centre of curvature, f is focal length.

Using similar triangles:
Ray 1: From B parallel to principal axis → reflects through F.
Ray 2: From B through C → reflects back on same path.
By similar triangles ABP and A'B'P: A'B'/AB = PA'/PA = v/u ...(1) By similar triangles FED and FA'B' (where D is on mirror near P): A'B'/ED = FA'/FD; Since ED = AB and FD ≈ FP = f: A'B'/AB = (v − f)/f ...(2) From (1) and (2): v/u = (v − f)/f vf = u(v − f) = uv − uf vf + uf = uv Dividing throughout by uvf:
1/u + 1/v = 1/f ✓ i.e. 1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Q.5
MirrorsLenses2M
An object is placed 30 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Find the position and nature of the image. Also find the magnification.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
u = −30 cm (object in front of mirror → negative)
f = −10 cm (concave mirror → negative focal length)
Find: v = ? and m = ?
Using mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
1/(−10) = 1/v + 1/(−30)
1/v = 1/(−10) + 1/30
1/v = −3/30 + 1/30 = −2/30
v = −15 cm

Magnification: m = −v/u = −(−15)/(−30) = −15/30 = −0.5
v = −15 cm (real, in front of mirror) | m = −0.5 (inverted, diminished)
Nature of image: Real, inverted, diminished (|m| < 1), formed 15 cm in front of the concave mirror.
