🌊 Huygens' Principle
Q1
State Huygens' principle. Using it, derive Snell's law of refraction (law of refraction).
🔴 Every Year 4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Huygens' Principle (2 points — both must be written):

1. Every point on a given wavefront acts as a secondary source of light and emits spherical secondary wavelets in all directions with the same speed as the original wave.

2. The new wavefront at any later instant is the common tangent (envelope) to all these secondary wavelets.

Derivation of Snell's Law using Huygens' Principle:

  Medium 1 (n₁, speed v₁)
  ────────A──────────────────── interface
           \  r₁  r₂
  Medium 2  \ B→→→→E (refracted wavefront CE)
  (n₂, v₂)  \
              C

  At t=0: wavefront touches interface at A
  At t=τ: other end of wavefront reaches B
  BC = v₁τ (in medium 1)
  AE = v₂τ (secondary wavelet from A in medium 2)

From triangle ABC:

sin i = BC / AC = v₁τ / AC

From triangle AEC:

sin r = AE / AC = v₂τ / AC

Dividing:

sin i / sin r = v₁ / v₂

Since v = c/n → v₁ = c/n₁ and v₂ = c/n₂:

sin i / sin r = v₁/v₂ = n₂/n₁ → n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r ✓ (Snell's Law)

Conclusion: Since v₂ < v₁ for glass, light slows down in denser medium — consistent with experiment. Newton's corpuscular theory wrongly predicted v₂ > v₁.

Q2
State Huygens' principle. Using it, verify the law of reflection of light.
🔴 High Freq 3–4 Marks 2023 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Huygens' Principle: (State same as Q1 above)

Verification of Law of Reflection:

Let AB be the incident plane wavefront hitting mirror MM'. At t=0, point A touches the mirror. By time t=τ, point B reaches the mirror at C (BC = vτ). Secondary wavelet from A has also travelled distance AE = vτ in the same medium (same speed).

BC = vτ and AE = vτ (same medium, same speed v)

Since AE = BC, triangles ABC and AEC are congruent (AE=BC, AC common, right angles at B and E):

∠BAC = ∠ECA → ∠i = ∠r ✓

Both incident and reflected rays lie in the same plane as the normal — law of reflection verified.

✨ Interference — YDSE
Q3
In Young's Double Slit Experiment, derive an expression for fringe width. What is the effect on fringe width if: (i) wavelength is increased, (ii) distance between slits is doubled, (iii) experiment is performed in water?
🔴 Every Year 4 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Setup: Two slits S₁ and S₂, separation = d. Screen at distance D. P at height y from centre O.

      S₁ ─────────────────── P (y above centre)
       |         D           |
  S ───|                     | screen
       |                     |
      S₂ ─────────────────── O (centre)
      |←── d ──→|

  Path difference: Δ = S₂P − S₁P = yd/D

Derivation:

S₂P² − S₁P² = [D²+(y+d/2)²] − [D²+(y−d/2)²] = 2yd (S₂P − S₁P)(S₂P + S₁P) = 2yd Since S₁P ≈ S₂P ≈ D: Δ = yd/D

Bright fringe: Δ = nλ → y_n = nλD/d

Fringe width: β = y_(n+1) − y_n

β = λD/d

Effects:

(i) λ increases → β = λD/d increases — fringes become wider ✓

(ii) d doubled → β = λD/2d = β/2 — fringe width becomes half ✓

(iii) In water (n=1.33): λ_water = λ_air/n → β_water = λD/nd = β/n — fringe width decreases ✓

Q4
State the conditions for constructive and destructive interference. What is the ratio of maximum to minimum intensity if the two sources have intensity ratio 4:1?
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Constructive Interference (Bright Fringe):

Path difference Δ = nλ (n = 0, 1, 2 …); phase difference δ = 2nπ

Resultant amplitude = A₁ + A₂; Intensity = maximum = I_max

Destructive Interference (Dark Fringe):

Path difference Δ = (2n−1)λ/2; phase difference δ = (2n−1)π

Resultant amplitude = |A₁ − A₂|; Intensity = minimum = I_min

Intensity ratio calculation:

I₁/I₂ = 4/1 → A₁/A₂ = 2/1 (since I ∝ A²) I_max = (A₁ + A₂)² = (2+1)² = 9 I_min = (A₁ − A₂)² = (2−1)² = 1
I_max : I_min = 9 : 1
Q5
What are coherent sources? Why are coherent sources necessary for sustained interference?
🟠 Medium Freq 2 Marks 2023 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Coherent Sources: Two sources of light are said to be coherent if they emit light waves of the same frequency, same wavelength, and maintain a constant phase difference between them at all times.

Why necessary: For a stable (sustained) interference pattern, the positions of bright and dark fringes must remain fixed on the screen. This is only possible if the phase difference between the two sources remains constant. If phase difference changes randomly (incoherent sources), bright and dark fringes shift rapidly and the pattern averages out — no visible fringes are seen.

In YDSE: The two slits S₁ and S₂ act as coherent sources because they are derived from the same single slit S (same source), so they always have a constant phase relationship.

🌀 Diffraction
Q6
Explain diffraction at a single slit. State the condition for minima. Draw the intensity distribution graph.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2023 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Diffraction at a single slit: When monochromatic light passes through a narrow slit of width 'a' (a ≈ λ), secondary wavelets from different parts of the same wavefront interfere to produce alternating bright and dark bands on a screen.

  Intensity pattern:
        |
  I_max |   ████
        |  ██████
        | █████████
  dim   |░░ ████████░░░
  ──────|────────────────→ position
        O  (central maximum — widest & brightest)
        secondary maxima are progressively dimmer

Condition for minima: The slit of width 'a' is divided into pairs of strips. Each pair has path difference = λ → destructive interference.

a sin θ = nλ (n = ±1, ±2, ±3 …) — condition for minima

Width of central maximum = 2λD/a (between 1st minima on both sides)

As slit width 'a' decreases → central maximum becomes wider → more diffraction occurs.

Difference — Interference vs Diffraction:

Interference: two separate coherent sources; all bright fringes equal intensity; all dark fringes zero intensity.

Diffraction: single slit; central max brightest; secondary maxima get progressively dimmer.

🔮 Polarisation
Q7
State Malus' Law. A plane polarised light of intensity I₀ passes through an analyser. Find the angle when transmitted intensity is (i) maximum (ii) zero (iii) I₀/2.
🔴 Every Year 3 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Malus' Law: When a beam of plane polarised light of intensity I₀ is incident on an analyser, the intensity of transmitted light is:

I = I₀ cos²θ

where θ = angle between plane of polarisation of incident light and transmission axis of the analyser.

(i) Maximum intensity: I = I₀ when cos²θ = 1

θ = 0° (analyser axis parallel to polariser)

(ii) Zero intensity: I = 0 when cos²θ = 0

θ = 90° (analyser ⊥ polariser — "crossed polaroids")

(iii) Half intensity: I = I₀/2 → cos²θ = 1/2 → cosθ = 1/√2

θ = 45°
Q8
State Brewster's Law. Derive the relation tan i_B = n. What is the significance of Brewster's angle?
🔴 High Freq 3–4 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Brewster's Law: When unpolarised light is incident at a specific angle called Brewster's angle (i_B), the reflected ray is completely plane polarised. The vibrations in the reflected ray are perpendicular to the plane of incidence.

Derivation of tan i_B = n:

At Brewster's angle, the reflected ray and refracted ray are perpendicular to each other:

i_B + r = 90° → r = 90° − i_B

Applying Snell's Law (air to medium of refractive index n):

1 × sin i_B = n × sin r = n × sin(90° − i_B) = n cos i_B sin i_B / cos i_B = n
tan i_B = n ✓

Significance:

At i_B, the reflected ray is 100% plane polarised. The refracted ray is only partially polarised. Used in: photography (Polaroid filters to reduce glare), LCD screens, anti-glare sunglasses, optical instruments.

Example: For glass (n = 1.5): i_B = tan⁻¹(1.5) ≈ 56.3°

Q9
What is polarisation of light? How does it prove the transverse nature of light? Name two methods of producing plane polarised light.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2023 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Polarisation: The phenomenon of restricting the vibration of a light wave to a single plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation is called polarisation.

Unpolarised light — vibrations in all planes. Plane polarised light — vibrations in one plane only.

Proof of transverse nature: Polarisation is only possible in transverse waves (displacement ⊥ direction of propagation). Sound waves are longitudinal — cannot be polarised. Since light can be polarised, it must be a transverse wave.

Two methods of producing plane polarised light:

1. Reflection (Brewster's method): Unpolarised light incident at Brewster's angle → reflected ray is completely plane polarised.

2. By Polaroids: Sheet of aligned molecular crystals (e.g., iodoquinine sulphate). Transmits vibrations in only one direction. Widely used method.

Q11
What is a wavefront? State its types. Draw diagrams for each type of wavefront.
🔴 High Freq 2–3 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Wavefront: The locus of all points in a medium which are vibrating in the same phase at a given instant of time is called a wavefront. All points on a wavefront have the same phase and the same amplitude.

Ray of light is always perpendicular to the wavefront — it represents the direction of propagation.

Types of Wavefronts:

1. Spherical wavefront: Produced by a point source of light. All points are at equal distance from the source. Wavefronts are concentric spheres.

Point source → Spherical wavefronts (3D)

2. Cylindrical wavefront: Produced by a line source of light (e.g., a long narrow slit). Wavefronts are coaxial cylinders.

Line source → Cylindrical wavefronts

3. Plane wavefront: When the source is at a very large (infinite) distance, the spherical wavefront becomes flat — a plane wavefront. Used in most optical experiments.

Source at infinity → Plane wavefronts (parallel rays)
Q12
What happens when Young's double slit experiment is performed with white light? Describe the pattern observed.
🔴 High Freq 2 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Pattern with white light:

Central fringe (n=0): At centre, path difference = 0 for all colours → all colours reinforce → central fringe is white.

Other fringes: Since fringe width β = λD/d, different colours have different fringe widths. Violet (smallest λ) has narrowest fringes; Red (largest λ) has widest fringes.

On either side of the white central fringe:

Violet (closest to centre) → Indigo → Blue → Green → Yellow → Orange → Red (farthest)

After a few orders, different colours overlap and the pattern becomes indistinct (white).

Conclusion: With white light — central fringe = white; first few fringes show coloured edges (violet inner, red outer); beyond a few orders — no distinct fringes.

Q13
What is resolving power of an optical instrument? State Rayleigh's criterion. Write the formula for resolving power of a telescope.
🔴 High Freq 2–3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Resolving Power: The ability of an optical instrument to distinctly separate (resolve) two nearby objects or points is called its resolving power.

Rayleigh's Criterion: Two nearby point sources are said to be just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one source falls exactly on the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other source.

Limit of Resolution of Telescope:

θ_min = 1.22λ / D

where λ = wavelength of light, D = diameter of objective lens/mirror.

Resolving Power of Telescope = 1/θ_min = D / (1.22λ)

To increase resolving power:

1. Use larger objective aperture D — that is why large telescopes have large mirrors/lenses

2. Use smaller wavelength λ — blue/UV light gives better resolution than red light

Resolving Power of Microscope: RP = 2μ sinθ / λ (where μ = refractive index of medium)

Q14
Explain with a diagram how the intensity varies in Young's double slit experiment. What is the intensity at a point where path difference is λ/2?
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2023 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Intensity formula: For two sources of equal intensity I₀, the resultant intensity at any point is:

I = 4I₀ cos²(δ/2)

where δ = phase difference = (2π/λ) × path difference (Δ)

Intensity distribution:

I ↑ 4I₀| ▌ ▌ ▌ ▌ ▌ ▌ ▌ | 0 |──────────────────────→ position dark bright dark bright

At Bright fringes (Δ = nλ, δ = 2nπ): I = 4I₀ cos²(0) = 4I₀ (maximum)

At Dark fringes (Δ = (2n−1)λ/2, δ = (2n−1)π): I = 4I₀ cos²(π/2) = 0 (minimum)

At path difference λ/2: δ = π → I = 4I₀ cos²(π/2) = 4I₀ × 0 = 0 (dark fringe)

Total energy is conserved — energy from dark regions is redistributed to bright regions.

Q15
Why are two independent sources of light not coherent? How are coherent sources produced in practice?
🟠 Medium Freq 2 Marks 2023 · 2022 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Why independent sources are NOT coherent: Ordinary light sources (bulbs, candles) emit light due to random atomic transitions. Each atom emits a wave train of duration ~10⁻⁸ s independently. The phase difference between waves from two separate sources changes randomly and rapidly — there is no constant phase relationship. Hence, they are incoherent and cannot produce sustained interference.

How coherent sources are produced:

1. By division of wavefront (e.g., Young's double slit): A single wavefront from one source is split into two parts by passing through two closely-spaced slits S₁ and S₂. Both slits act as secondary sources derived from the same original source — always in phase.

2. By division of amplitude (e.g., Lloyd's mirror, thin film): Light from one source is split by partial reflection/refraction into two beams which then interfere.

3. Using LASER: Laser produces highly coherent, monochromatic light — all photons have the same phase, frequency and direction.

Q16
What is meant by plane polarised light and unpolarised light? Draw diagrams to show the difference. What does polarisation prove about the nature of light?
🔴 High Freq 2–3 Marks 2024 · 2023 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Unpolarised light: Ordinary light in which the electric field vector vibrates in all planes perpendicular to the direction of propagation with equal probability. No preferred direction of vibration.

→ →→→ (vibrations in ALL directions ⊥ to propagation)

Plane polarised light: Light in which the electric field vector vibrates in only ONE specific plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation. All vibrations are confined to a single plane.

→ | | | (vibrations in ONE direction only)

What polarisation proves: Polarisation is only possible in transverse waves (where displacement is perpendicular to the direction of propagation). Sound waves are longitudinal — they CANNOT be polarised. Since light CAN be polarised, it conclusively proves that light is a transverse wave.

Q17
State the condition for sustained interference. Explain why the sky appears blue and the sun appears red at sunset/sunrise.
🟠 Medium Freq 2 Marks 2023 · 2021 · 2020
✅ Model Answer

Conditions for Sustained (Stable) Interference:

1. The two sources must be coherent — same frequency, same wavelength, constant phase difference

2. The two sources must emit light of the same wavelength (monochromatic)

3. The amplitudes of the two waves should be equal or nearly equal for good contrast

4. The two sources must be very close together and the screen must be at a sufficient distance

Blue Sky: Sunlight (white light) passes through atmosphere. Air molecules scatter light — shorter wavelengths (blue, violet) are scattered much more than longer wavelengths (Rayleigh scattering: I ∝ 1/λ⁴). Blue light is scattered in all directions reaching our eyes → sky appears blue.

Red sunset/sunrise: When sun is near the horizon, light travels through a very thick layer of atmosphere. Blue light is scattered away. Only the longer wavelengths — red and orange — survive and reach the observer's eye → sun appears red/orange.

Q18
Derive the condition for minima in single slit diffraction. What is the width of the central maximum?
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks 2024 · 2022 · 2021
✅ Model Answer

Consider a single slit of width 'a'. When monochromatic light of wavelength λ falls on it, secondary wavelets from the slit interfere to produce a diffraction pattern.

Condition for minima: Divide the slit into pairs of strips. Each strip in the upper half has a corresponding strip in the lower half. If path difference between waves from each pair = λ/2, they cancel by destructive interference.

For the first minimum, divide slit into 2 halves. Path difference between top and bottom of slit:

a sinθ₁ = λ (1st minimum)

For higher order minima:

a sinθ = nλ (n = ±1, ±2, ±3 …) — condition for minima

Width of central maximum:

1st minima on both sides are at angle θ where sinθ = λ/a. For small θ: sinθ ≈ tanθ = y/D

y₁ = λD/a (position of 1st minimum from centre)
Width of central maximum = 2y₁ = 2λD/a

As slit width 'a' decreases → central maximum becomes wider → more diffraction. If a >> λ, diffraction is negligible (geometric optics applies).

N1. In YDSE, two slits are 0.5 mm apart and the screen is 1.5 m away. If fringe width is 1.5 mm, find the wavelength of light. [2024]
d = 0.5 mm = 5×10⁻⁴ m | D = 1.5 m | β = 1.5 mm = 1.5×10⁻³ m | λ = ?
β = λD/d → λ = βd/D
λ = (1.5×10⁻³ × 5×10⁻⁴) / 1.5
λ = 7.5×10⁻⁷ / 1.5 = 5×10⁻⁷ m
∴ λ = 5×10⁻⁷ m = 500 nm ✓
N2. In YDSE, slits are 1 mm apart, screen is 1 m away, λ = 589 nm. Find: (i) fringe width, (ii) number of fringes in 1 cm. [2023]
d = 1×10⁻³ m | D = 1 m | λ = 589×10⁻⁹ m | width = 1 cm = 10⁻² m
(i) β = λD/d = (589×10⁻⁹ × 1) / 10⁻³ = 589×10⁻⁶ m = 0.589 mm
(ii) N = 10⁻² / 589×10⁻⁶ = 10⁻² / 5.89×10⁻⁴ ≈ 16.98 ≈ 17
∴ β = 0.589 mm; N ≈ 17 fringes ✓
N3. The 10th bright fringe is 2 cm from centre. Slits are 1 mm apart, screen is 80 cm away. Find λ. [2022]
n = 10 | y₁₀ = 2×10⁻² m | d = 10⁻³ m | D = 0.80 m
y_n = nλD/d → λ = y_n·d / (n·D)
λ = (2×10⁻² × 10⁻³) / (10 × 0.80)
λ = 2×10⁻⁵ / 8 = 2.5×10⁻⁶ m
∴ λ = 2.5×10⁻⁶ m = 2500 nm ✓
N4. In YDSE with λ = 4500 Å, 20 fringes are seen in a region. If λ is changed to 6000 Å, how many fringes in same region? [2020]
λ₁ = 4500 Å, n₁ = 20 | λ₂ = 6000 Å, n₂ = ?
Same region width: n₁β₁ = n₂β₂
n₁(λ₁D/d) = n₂(λ₂D/d)
n₂ = n₁λ₁/λ₂ = 20 × 4500/6000 = 20 × 3/4 = 15
∴ 15 fringes ✓
N5. Plane polarised light of intensity 32 W/m² passes through analyser. Intensity becomes 8 W/m². Find angle between polariser and analyser. [2024]
I₀ = 32 W/m² | I = 8 W/m² | θ = ?
I = I₀cos²θ
cos²θ = 8/32 = 1/4
cosθ = 1/2
θ = 60°
∴ θ = 60° ✓
N6. Unpolarised light passes through two polaroids. Transmitted intensity = I₀/4. Find angle between their axes. [2022 · 2021]
After 1st polaroid: I₀/2 (always) | After 2nd: I₀/4
I₀/4 = (I₀/2) cos²θ
cos²θ = 1/2
cosθ = 1/√2
θ = 45°
∴ θ = 45° ✓
N7. Critical angle of glass is 42°. Find: (i) refractive index, (ii) Brewster's angle. [2023]
θ_c = 42° | find n and i_B
(i) n = 1/sin θ_c = 1/sin 42° = 1/0.6691 ≈ 1.5
(ii) tan i_B = n = 1.5
i_B = tan⁻¹(1.5) ≈ 56.3°
∴ n ≈ 1.5; i_B ≈ 56.3° ✓
