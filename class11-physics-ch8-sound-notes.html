8.1 Introduction — Types of Waves

Wave: A periodic and regular disturbance in a medium which does not cause any flow of material but causes the flow of energy and momentum from one point to another.
Mechanical EM Waves Matter Waves
TypeDefinitionMedium needed?Examples
MechanicalNeeds material medium for propagation✅ YesSound, water waves, seismic, string waves
EM WavesPeriodic vibrations in electric & magnetic fields❌ NoLight, radio, X-rays, UV rays
Matter WavesWave associated with any moving object❌ NoElectron waves (quantum mechanics)
Travelling (Progressive) Wave: A disturbance created at one place travels to distant points. Energy is transferred from one point to another. Example: water ripples, sound in air.
📐

8.2 Common Properties of All Waves

1. Amplitude (A): Largest displacement of a particle of the medium from its rest (mean) position.
Unit: metre (m)
2. Wavelength (λ): Distance between two successive particles which are in the same state of vibration (same phase).
Unit: metre (m)
3. Period (T): Time required to complete one vibration by a particle of the medium.
Unit: seconds (s)
4. Double Periodicity: Wave motion is periodic in both time and space.
  • Periodic in time: at every location, motion repeats at equal time intervals
  • Periodic in space: at any instant, the wave pattern repeats at equal distances
5. Frequency (n or f): Number of vibrations performed by a particle per second.
Unit: Hertz (Hz)
n = 1/T
Frequency = 1 / Time Period
6. Velocity of wave (v): Distance travelled by the wave per unit time.
v = nλ = λ/T
Wave velocity = frequency × wavelength
Remember: v = nλ is the most important relation in wave motion. It connects velocity, frequency and wavelength.

8.4 Speed of Sound — Newton's Formula & Laplace's Correction

Newton's Formula: Newton assumed sound propagation in air is an isothermal process (constant temperature). He used Boyle's law: PV = constant → bulk modulus B = P
v = √(P/ρ)
Newton's formula for speed of sound | P = pressure, ρ = density
Limitation of Newton's Formula: At STP, v = √(101325/1.293) ≈ 280 m/s. But experimental value is 332 m/s. Error ≈ 16% — too low!
Laplace's Correction: Laplace argued sound propagation is an adiabatic process (no heat exchange — happens too fast). He replaced P with γP (γ = Cp/Cv).
v = √(γP/ρ)
Laplace's corrected formula | γ = 1.4 for air
With Laplace: v = √(1.4 × 101325/1.293) ≈ 332 m/s ✅ — matches experimental value!
Factors affecting speed of sound:
  • Temperature: v ∝ √T (increases with temperature). v_T = v₀ + 0.61t (m/s per °C)
  • Humidity: Moist air → lower density → higher speed (sound faster in humid air)
  • Pressure: No effect at constant temperature (P/ρ = constant)
  • Wind: Increases speed in direction of wind, decreases against wind
v_T / v₀ = √(T/273)
Variation of speed with absolute temperature T

8.5 Principle of Superposition of Waves

Statement: When two or more waves travel simultaneously through the same medium, the resultant displacement of any particle at any instant is equal to the algebraic sum of the displacements due to individual waves.
y = y₁ + y₂ + y₃ + ...
Resultant displacement = sum of individual displacements
Applications of Superposition:
  • Interference — constructive (loud) and destructive (silent)
  • Beats — periodic loud and soft sounds
  • Stationary waves — standing waves in strings and pipes
🥁

8.6 Beats

Beats: The periodic variation in intensity (loudness) of sound when two sound waves of slightly different frequencies are superimposed. The alternating loud and soft sounds are called beats.
Beat frequency = n₁ − n₂
Number of beats per second = difference in frequencies (n₁ > n₂)
  • One beat = one loud + one soft sound
  • Beats are audible only if the frequency difference is less than about 10 Hz
  • Time between beats = 1/(n₁ − n₂) seconds
  • Application: Tuning musical instruments
Example: Tuning forks of 450 Hz and 454 Hz → Beat frequency = 454 − 450 = 4 beats/second. Time between beats = 1/4 = 0.25 s.
🚑

8.7 Doppler Effect

Doppler Effect: The apparent change in frequency (pitch) of sound heard by an observer due to relative motion between the source of sound and the observer.
Key Rule:
  • When source and observer move towards each other → apparent frequency increases (higher pitch)
  • When source and observer move away from each other → apparent frequency decreases (lower pitch)
General Doppler Formula:
n' = n × (v ± v_L) / (v ∓ v_S)
n' = apparent frequency | n = original frequency | v = speed of sound | v_L = speed of listener | v_S = speed of source
Sign Convention:
  • Numerator: + if listener moves towards source; − if moves away
  • Denominator: − if source moves towards listener; + if moves away
SituationFormula
Source moves towards stationary listenern' = n × v/(v − v_S)
Source moves away from stationary listenern' = n × v/(v + v_S)
Listener moves towards stationary sourcen' = n × (v + v_L)/v
Listener moves away from stationary sourcen' = n × (v − v_L)/v
Both moving towards each othern' = n × (v + v_L)/(v − v_S)
Both moving away from each othern' = n × (v − v_L)/(v + v_S)
Applications: Radar, sonar, ultrasound (medical), police speed gun, astronomical measurements (redshift/blueshift).
🏔️

8.8 Echo and Reverberation

Echo: Repetition of sound due to reflection from a distant surface. For echo to be heard separately, the reflecting surface must be at least 17 m away (at 20°C) so that the reflected sound reaches after 0.1 s (persistence of hearing).
d = v × t / 2
d = distance to reflecting surface | v = speed of sound | t = time to hear echo
Reverberation: Multiple reflections of sound that persist after the original sound has stopped. Causes prolonged sound in halls and large rooms.
  • Echo requires minimum distance of 17 m
  • Persistence of hearing = 0.1 second
  • Reverberation is undesirable in concert halls — controlled by acoustic design (soft materials, curtains, carpets)
  • Application of echo: SONAR (sound navigation and ranging) — used in finding depth of sea, detecting submarines

Short & Long Answer Questions

1. What type of wave is a sound wave? ▼
Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave. It is mechanical because it requires a material medium for propagation. It is longitudinal because particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation of the wave.
2. Can sound travel in vacuum? Give reason. ▼
No, sound cannot travel in vacuum. Sound is a mechanical wave and requires a material medium (solid, liquid, or gas) for its propagation. In vacuum, there are no particles to vibrate and transmit the wave energy.
3. Distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves. ▼
Transverse wave: Particles vibrate perpendicular (⊥) to direction of propagation. Forms crests and troughs. Example: light waves, waves on string.

Longitudinal wave: Particles vibrate parallel (‖) to direction of propagation. Forms compressions and rarefactions. Example: sound waves. Only longitudinal waves can travel through gases.
4. Explain Newton's formula for speed of sound. What is its limitation? ▼
Newton's formula: v = √(P/ρ). Newton assumed sound travels isothermally (constant temperature) using Boyle's law, so bulk modulus = P.
At STP: v = √(101325/1.293) ≈ 280 m/s

Limitation: Experimental value is 332 m/s. Newton's formula gives a value about 16% lower than the actual value. This is because Newton's assumption of isothermal process is incorrect.
5. Explain Laplace's correction to Newton's formula. ▼
Laplace argued that sound propagation is an adiabatic process, not isothermal. Sound travels very fast — there is no time for heat exchange with surroundings.
For adiabatic process: Bulk modulus = γP (where γ = Cp/Cv)
Laplace's formula: v = √(γP/ρ)
For air: γ = 1.4, so v = √(1.4 × 101325/1.293) ≈ 332 m/s ✅ — matches experimental value!
6. State and explain the principle of superposition of waves. ▼
Statement: When two or more waves travel simultaneously through the same medium, the resultant displacement of any particle at any instant is equal to the algebraic sum of the displacements due to individual waves.
y = y₁ + y₂ + y₃ + ...

Applications: Interference (constructive/destructive), Beats, Stationary waves.
7. What are beats? State the formula for beat frequency. ▼
Beats: When two sound waves of slightly different frequencies travel in the same direction, the resultant wave shows periodic variation in amplitude (loudness). This periodic variation is called beats. One beat = one loud + one soft sound.

Beat frequency = n₁ − n₂ (where n₁ > n₂)
Beats are audible only if frequency difference is less than 10 Hz.
Application: Tuning musical instruments.
8. Define Doppler effect. State its formula and applications. ▼
Definition: The apparent change in frequency (pitch) of sound heard by an observer due to relative motion between the source and the observer is called the Doppler effect.

Formula: n' = n × (v ± v_L)/(v ∓ v_S)
where: n' = apparent frequency, n = original frequency, v = speed of sound, v_L = speed of listener, v_S = speed of source.

Applications: Radar, SONAR, ultrasound (medical imaging), speed guns (police), astronomical redshift/blueshift.
9. What are the factors affecting speed of sound in air? ▼
1. Temperature: v ∝ √T — speed increases with temperature. For every 1°C rise, speed increases by 0.61 m/s.
2. Humidity: Moist air (water vapour) has lower density than dry air → speed is higher in humid air.
3. Pressure: No effect — when pressure increases, density also increases proportionally, keeping P/ρ constant.
4. Wind: Increases speed in the direction of wind, decreases speed against wind.
10. What is echo? What is reverberation? Give the condition for echo. ▼
Echo: Repetition of sound due to reflection from a distant surface. The reflected sound must reach the listener at least 0.1 s after the original sound (persistence of hearing).
Condition for echo: Minimum distance of reflecting surface = 17 m (at 20°C, v ≈ 340 m/s).
d = vt/2 = 340 × 0.1/2 = 17 m

Reverberation: Persistence of sound in an enclosed space due to multiple reflections. Undesirable in concert halls — controlled by acoustic design using sound-absorbing materials.
11. Define: Amplitude, Wavelength, Frequency, Time Period. ▼
Amplitude (A): Maximum displacement of a particle from its mean position. Unit: metre.
Wavelength (λ): Distance between two successive particles in the same phase. Unit: metre.
Frequency (n): Number of vibrations per second. Unit: Hz (hertz).
Time Period (T): Time for one complete vibration. Unit: second. T = 1/n.
12. What is meant by double periodicity of waves? ▼
Wave motion is doubly periodic because it is periodic in both time and space:
Periodic in time: At every location, the wave motion repeats itself at equal intervals of time (period T).
Periodic in space: At any given instant, the form of the wave repeats at equal distances (wavelength λ).

Solved Numericals (from Textbook)

1. A certain sound wave in air has speed 340 m/s and wavelength 1.7 m. Calculate: (a) frequency (b) period.
Given: v = 340 m/s, λ = 1.7 m
(a) n = v/λ = 340/1.7 = 200 Hz
(b) T = 1/n = 1/200 = 0.005 s
n = 200 Hz, T = 0.005 s
2. A tuning fork of frequency 170 Hz produces sound waves of wavelength 2 m. Calculate speed of sound.
Given: n = 170 Hz, λ = 2 m
v = nλ = 170 × 2 = 340 m/s
v = 340 m/s
3. An echo-sounder in a fishing boat receives an echo from a shoal of fish 0.45 s after it was sent. If speed of sound in water is 1500 m/s, how deep is the shoal?
Given: t = 0.45 s, v = 1500 m/s
d = vt/2 = 1500 × 0.45 / 2
d = 675/2 = 337.5 m
Depth = 337.5 m
4. A girl stands 170 m away from a high wall and claps at steady rate so each clap coincides with the echo. If she makes 60 claps in 1 minute: (a) speed of sound? (b) If she makes 45 claps/min at new position, find new distance.
(a) Distance d = 170 m, 60 claps in 60 s → time between claps = 60/60 = 1 s
(b) 45 claps in 60 s → time between claps = 60/45 = 4/3 s
(a) Total distance = 2d = 2 × 170 = 340 m in 1 s
v = 340/1 = 340 m/s
(b) d₂ = v × t/2 = 340 × (4/3)/2 = 340 × 2/3 = 226.67 ≈ 255 m
(a) v = 340 m/s  |  (b) d = 255 m
5. Sound wave A has period 0.015 s, sound wave B has period 0.025 s. Which has greater frequency?
Given: T_A = 0.015 s, T_B = 0.025 s
n_A = 1/T_A = 1/0.015 = 66.67 Hz
n_B = 1/T_B = 1/0.025 = 40 Hz
Since n_A > n_B → Wave A has greater frequency
Wave A has greater frequency
6. At what temperature will the speed of sound in air be 1.75 times its speed at NTP?
Given: v_T = 1.75 v₀, T₀ = 273 K
v_T/v₀ = √(T/T₀)
1.75 = √(T/273)
(1.75)² = T/273
3.0625 = T/273
T = 3.0625 × 273 = 836.06 K
t = 836.06 − 273 = 563.06°C
T = 836.06 K = 563.06°C
7. A man standing between 2 parallel cliffs fires a gun. He hears two echoes: one after 3 s and other after 5 s. The separation between cliffs is 1360 m. Find speed of sound.
Given: t₁ = 3 s, t₂ = 5 s, total separation = 1360 m
Let man be at distance d₁ from cliff 1, d₂ from cliff 2.
d₁ = v × t₁/2 = v × 3/2 = 1.5v
d₂ = v × t₂/2 = v × 5/2 = 2.5v
d₁ + d₂ = 1360
1.5v + 2.5v = 1360
4v = 1360
v = 340 m/s
v = 340 m/s
8. A police car travels towards a stationary observer at 15 m/s. Siren emits 250 Hz. Speed of sound = 340 m/s. Calculate recorded frequency.
Given: n = 250 Hz, v_S = 15 m/s, v_L = 0, v = 340 m/s. Source moving towards listener.
n' = n × v/(v − v_S)
n' = 250 × 340/(340 − 15)
n' = 250 × 340/325
n' = 85000/325 = 261.54 Hz
n' = 261.54 Hz
9. Ambulance siren: 1500 Hz, v = 340 m/s, ambulance speed = 30 m/s. Find difference in frequencies when ambulance moves towards and away from a stationary observer.
Given: n = 1500 Hz, v = 340 m/s, v_S = 30 m/s, v_L = 0
When approaching: n₁' = 1500 × 340/(340 − 30) = 1500 × 340/310 = 1645.16 Hz
When receding: n₂' = 1500 × 340/(340 + 30) = 1500 × 340/370 = 1378.38 Hz
Difference = 1645.16 − 1378.38 = 266.78 Hz
Δn = 266.79 Hz
