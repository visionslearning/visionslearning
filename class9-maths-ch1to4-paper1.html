Choose the correct alternative. (MCQ)
5 Marks
Ch1 i. If A = {1,2,3} and B = {2,3,4}, then A ∩ B = ________.
Answer A ∩ B = {2, 3} Common elements of A and B are 2 and 3.
Ch2 ii. Which of the following is an irrational number?
Answer √2 is an irrational number. √2 = 1.41421356... (non-terminating, non-repeating decimal) → irrational. Note: 0.5, 1/3, and −4 are all rational numbers.
Ch3 iii. The degree of the polynomial 5x³ − 2x² + x − 7 is ________.
Answer Degree = 3 The highest power of x in the polynomial is 3 (in the term 5x³).
Ch4 iv. If a : b = 3 : 4, then b : a = ________.
Answer b : a = 4 : 3 Inverse ratio of 3:4 is 4:3.
Ch2 v. Every integer is a ________ number.
Answer Every integer is a rational number. Every integer n can be written as n/1, which is of the form p/q (q ≠ 0) → rational.

TRUE ✓ — Empty set (∅) is a subset of every set by definition.

FALSE ✗ — Correct: x² − 4 = (x−2)(x+2)   [∵ a²−b² = (a−b)(a+b)]

FALSE ✗ — Correct: √4 = 2, which is a rational number (integer).
Ch4 iv. If a/b = c/d then ad = bc.
TRUE ✓ — Cross multiplication: a/b = c/d ⟹ ad = bc (fundamental property of proportion).

Ch2 B. Represent √5 on the number line.
Step 1 Draw a number line. Mark O (origin) and A at point 2 (so OA = 2 units). Step 2 Draw AB ⊥ OA at A, with AB = 1 unit. Step 3 Join OB. By Pythagoras: OB = √(OA² + AB²) = √(4+1) = √5. Step 4 With O as centre and OB as radius, draw an arc cutting the number line at point P. P represents √5 on the number line. ✓
Ch3 C. Find the zeros of the polynomial p(x) = x² − 5x + 6.
Step 1 Factorise: x² − 5x + 6 = 0 Step 2 Find two numbers whose product = 6 and sum = −5: they are −2 and −3. Step 3 x² − 2x − 3x + 6 = x(x−2) − 3(x−2) = (x−2)(x−3) = 0 Step 4 x − 2 = 0 ⟹ x = 2    OR    x − 3 = 0 ⟹ x = 3 Zeros of p(x) = 2 and 3
Ch4 D. Divide ₹800 in the ratio 3 : 5.
Step 1 Total parts = 3 + 5 = 8 parts Step 2 Value of 1 part = 800 ÷ 8 = ₹100 Step 3 First share = 3 × 100 = ₹300 Step 4 Second share = 5 × 100 = ₹500 ₹300 and ₹500
Ch3 E. Expand using identity: (2x + 3)²
Identity (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² Step 1 Here a = 2x, b = 3 Step 2 = (2x)² + 2(2x)(3) + (3)² Step 3 = 4x² + 12x + 9 (2x + 3)² = 4x² + 12x + 9

Ch1 A. In a group of 60 students, 35 study Maths, 25 study Science, and 15 study both. Using sets, find: (i) students who study only Maths (ii) students who study only Science (iii) students who study neither.
Given n(M) = 35, n(S) = 25, n(M∩S) = 15, Total = 60 Step 1 Only Maths = n(M) − n(M∩S) = 35 − 15 = 20 Step 2 Only Science = n(S) − n(M∩S) = 25 − 15 = 10 Step 3 n(M∪S) = 35 + 25 − 15 = 45 Step 4 Neither = Total − n(M∪S) = 60 − 45 = 15 (i) Only Maths = 20   (ii) Only Science = 10   (iii) Neither = 15
Ch3 B. Divide: (x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6) by (x − 1) and find the quotient and remainder.
Remainder Theorem p(1) = (1)³ − 6(1)² + 11(1) − 6 = 1−6+11−6 = 0 So (x−1) is a factor. Perform long division: Step 1 x³ ÷ x = x²   →   x²(x−1) = x³−x²   Remainder: −5x²+11x−6 Step 2 −5x² ÷ x = −5x   →   −5x(x−1) = −5x²+5x   Remainder: 6x−6 Step 3 6x ÷ x = 6   →   6(x−1) = 6x−6   Remainder: 0 Quotient = x² − 5x + 6,   Remainder = 0 Further: x²−5x+6 = (x−2)(x−3)   ∴ All zeros: x = 1, 2, 3
Ch4 C. If 4 : x = x : 16, find x. Also verify that the terms are in proportion.
Given 4 : x :: x : 16 (means proportion) Formula Mean × Mean = Extreme × Extreme: x² = 4 × 16 Step 1 x² = 64 Step 2 x = √64 = 8 Verify 4:8 = 1:2  &&  8:16 = 1:2 ✓ (both equal) x = 8   (8 is the geometric mean / mean proportional of 4 and 16)

Ch2 A. (i) Define rational and irrational numbers. Prove that √2 is irrational. (4M)
Rational Number: A number that can be expressed as p/q where p, q are integers and q ≠ 0. Decimal expansion: terminating or non-terminating repeating. E.g., 1/2, 0.333..., −5. Irrational Number: Cannot be expressed as p/q. Decimal expansion: non-terminating and non-repeating. E.g., √2, π, √3. Proof that √2 is irrational (by contradiction): Assume √2 is rational. Then √2 = p/q where p, q ∈ Z, q ≠ 0, and p/q is in lowest terms (HCF of p,q = 1). Step 1 Squaring both sides: 2 = p²/q²  ⟹  p² = 2q² Step 2 So p² is even  ⟹  p is even. Let p = 2k. Step 3 (2k)² = 2q²  ⟹  4k² = 2q²  ⟹  q² = 2k² Step 4 So q² is even  ⟹  q is even. Step 5 Both p and q are even → HCF ≥ 2 → contradicts HCF(p,q) = 1. ∴ Our assumption is wrong. √2 is irrational. ✓ Proved.
Ch3 B. Using algebraic identities, expand and simplify: (i) (x+2)(x+3)   (ii) (3a+2b)³ (3M + 4M)
(i) (x+2)(x+3): Identity (x+a)(x+b) = x² + (a+b)x + ab Step 1 a = 2, b = 3   →   a+b = 5, ab = 6 (x+2)(x+3) = x² + 5x + 6 (ii) (3a+2b)³: Identity (x+y)³ = x³ + 3x²y + 3xy² + y³ Step 1 x = 3a, y = 2b Step 2 = (3a)³ + 3(3a)²(2b) + 3(3a)(2b)² + (2b)³ Step 3 = 27a³ + 3(9a²)(2b) + 3(3a)(4b²) + 8b³ Step 4 = 27a³ + 54a²b + 36ab² + 8b³ (3a+2b)³ = 27a³ + 54a²b + 36ab² + 8b³
