Q 1Multiple Choice Questions — 1 mark each7 Marks
(i) Ch1 The binomial nomenclature system was given by ________.
Answer (b) Carolus Linnaeus — introduced the two-name (genus + species) system in "Systema Naturae" (1758).
(ii) Ch2 Which of the following is the correct hierarchy of taxonomic categories?
Answer (c) Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species Mnemonic: King Phillip Came Over For Good Soup
(iii) Ch5 The most abundant protein in the world is ________.
Answer (a) RuBisCO (Ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase) — enzyme involved in carbon fixation in photosynthesis.
(iv) Ch7 During which phase of mitosis do chromosomes align at the equatorial plate?
Answer (b) Metaphase — chromosomes are maximally condensed and align at the metaphase plate (cell equator).
(v) Cell Structure The powerhouse of the cell is ________.
Answer (c) Mitochondria — site of aerobic respiration and ATP production.
(vi) Ch4 Flame cells are the excretory organs of ________.
Answer (b) Platyhelminthes (flatworms) — flame cells are protonephridia that regulate water and excrete wastes.
(vii) Ch5 DNA is made up of monomers called ________.
Answer (d) Deoxyribonucleotides — each nucleotide has deoxyribose sugar + phosphate group + nitrogenous base (A, T, G or C).
Q 2Very Short Answer Questions — 1 mark each7 Marks
(i) Ch1 Define metabolism.
Metabolism is the sum total of all chemical reactions occurring in a living organism — it includes anabolism (building up molecules, e.g., photosynthesis) and catabolism (breaking down molecules, e.g., respiration).
(ii) Ch2 What is a species?
A species is the basic unit of classification — a group of organisms that share common characteristics, can interbreed naturally, and produce fertile offspring. E.g., Homo sapiens (humans).
(iii) Ch5 Name two types of nucleic acids.
DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid) — carries genetic information; RNA (Ribonucleic acid) — involved in protein synthesis (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA).
(iv) Ch7 What is cytokinesis?
Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm that follows karyokinesis (nuclear division), resulting in two separate daughter cells. In animals it occurs by cleavage furrow; in plants by cell plate formation.
(v) Cell Structure What is the function of the ribosome?
Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis (translation). They read mRNA codons and assemble amino acids into polypeptide chains. 80S in eukaryotes; 70S in prokaryotes.
(vi) Ch4 Name the phylum to which earthworm belongs.
Earthworm (Pheretima) belongs to Phylum Annelida. Features: segmented body, true coelom, closed circulatory system, nephridia for excretion.
(vii) Ch1 What is the significance of the scientific name?
Scientific names (binomial nomenclature) are universally accepted, written in Latin/Greek, unique for each organism, and understood by scientists worldwide regardless of language — avoiding confusion caused by local common names.

Q 3Answer in Short. (Attempt any 8 out of 10) — 2 marks each16 Marks
Ch1 1. What are the two defining characteristics of all living organisms?
Answer The two defining characteristics of ALL living organisms are: 1. Metabolism — all living organisms carry out chemical reactions (anabolism + catabolism). This is the most reliable criterion of life. 2. Cellular organisation — all living things are made of one or more cells (cell theory). Viruses are an exception — they have metabolic reactions only inside host cells.
Ch2 2. What is the Five Kingdom Classification? Who proposed it?
Answer R.H. Whittaker (1969) proposed the Five Kingdom Classification: 1. Monera — prokaryotes (bacteria, cyanobacteria) 2. Protista — unicellular eukaryotes (Amoeba, Paramecium) 3. Fungi — heterotrophic, absorptive nutrition (mushroom, yeast) 4. Plantae — autotrophic multicellular eukaryotes 5. Animalia — heterotrophic multicellular eukaryotes
Ch5 3. What are enzymes? Give two properties.
Answer Enzymes are biological catalysts — proteins that speed up biochemical reactions without being consumed. Properties: 1. Highly specific — each enzyme acts on a specific substrate (lock and key model) 2. Reusable — not consumed in the reaction; can catalyse the same reaction repeatedly 3. Affected by pH and temperature — denatured at extreme conditions

Ch4 6. What are the main features of Phylum Porifera?
Answer Phylum Porifera (sponges) — characteristics: • Simplest multicellular animals — no tissues, no organs • Body has pores (ostia) and channels for water circulation • Choanocytes (collar cells) line the inner body and create water current • Sessile (attached to substrate), mostly marine • Skeleton of spicules (CaCO₃ or SiO₂) or spongin fibres • Examples: Sycon, Spongilla, Euspongia
Ch5 7. What are carbohydrates? Classify them with examples.
Answer Carbohydrates are organic compounds made of C, H, O with general formula (CH₂O)n. They are the primary energy source for cells. Classification: • Monosaccharides — simplest; glucose, fructose, galactose • Disaccharides — 2 monosaccharides; sucrose (glucose+fructose), lactose, maltose • Polysaccharides — many units; starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin
Ch7 8. Name and explain the stages of interphase.
Answer Interphase is the period between two divisions — the cell prepares for division. Three stages: 1. G₁ phase (Gap 1): Cell grows in size; RNA and proteins synthesised; intense metabolic activity 2. S phase (Synthesis): DNA replication occurs; chromosome number doubles (2C → 4C) 3. G₂ phase (Gap 2): Cell continues to grow; organelles duplicated; spindle proteins synthesised; cell prepares for division
Ch4 9. What are the distinguishing features of Phylum Annelida?
Answer Phylum Annelida (segmented worms) — features: • Segmented body (metameric segmentation) — each segment (metamere) is similar • True coelom (eucoelomate) • Closed circulatory system — blood remains in vessels • Excretion by nephridia • Locomotion by setae (chaetae) • Examples: Earthworm (Pheretima), Leech (Hirudinaria), Nereis
Cell Structure 10. What is the role of the Golgi apparatus?
Answer The Golgi apparatus (Golgi complex/body) — functions: • Post-translational modification — modifies proteins received from ER (glycosylation, phosphorylation) • Sorting and packaging — packages proteins into vesicles for secretion or delivery to organelles • Forms lysosomes — packages digestive enzymes • Called the "traffic police" or "post office" of the cell

Q 4Answer in Brief. (Attempt any 4 out of 6) — 3 marks each12 Marks
Ch1 1. Explain the properties of living organisms. (Any 3)
Answer Properties of living organisms: 1. Growth: Increase in mass and number of cells. In organisms: intrinsic (from inside); in non-living: extrinsic (added from outside). Both can show growth but method differs. 2. Reproduction: Ability to produce offspring, ensuring continuation of species. Not a universal property (sterile mules, worker bees cannot reproduce) but characteristic of living things. 3. Metabolism: Sum of all chemical reactions. Catabolism (breakdown) + Anabolism (synthesis). ONLY living organisms have this; viruses only inside host. 4. Cellular organisation: All living things made of cells — from unicellular bacteria to multicellular humans. 5. Response to stimuli: All organisms respond to environmental changes (tropisms in plants, behaviour in animals).

Ch5 3. Describe the structure of a DNA molecule (Watson-Crick model).
Answer Watson and Crick (1953) proposed the double helix model of DNA: • Two polynucleotide chains wound around each other in a right-handed double helix • Sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside (alternating deoxyribose + phosphate) • Nitrogenous bases face inward and pair by hydrogen bonds: • Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) — 2 hydrogen bonds • Guanine (G) pairs with Cytosine (C) — 3 hydrogen bonds • Anti-parallel strands — one runs 5'→3', the other 3'→5' • Pitch = 3.4 nm; 10 base pairs per turn; diameter = 2 nm

Ch7 4. Explain the phases of mitosis with diagrams.
Answer Mitosis — 4 phases (PMAT): 1. Prophase: Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes; nuclear envelope breaks down; spindle fibres form from centrioles (in animals) 2. Metaphase: Chromosomes align at the equatorial plate (metaphase plate); spindle fibres attach to centromeres; chromosomes are most condensed (best for karyotyping) 3. Anaphase: Centromeres split; sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles by spindle fibres; cell elongates 4. Telophase: Chromosomes reach poles and decondense; nuclear envelope reforms; nucleolus reappears; followed by cytokinesis Result: 2 daughter cells, each with same chromosome number as parent (2n→2n)
📐 Draw 4 diagrams: Prophase (condensed chromosomes), Metaphase (aligned at equator), Anaphase (separating), Telophase (two nuclei forming).
Cell Structure 5. Describe the structure and functions of the cell membrane (plasma membrane).
Answer Structure (Fluid Mosaic Model — Singer & Nicolson, 1972): • Phospholipid bilayer — two layers of phospholipids with hydrophilic heads (outside) and hydrophobic tails (inside) • Proteins — integral (transmembrane) and peripheral proteins embedded/attached • Cholesterol — regulates fluidity • Carbohydrates — as glycoproteins and glycolipids on outer surface (cell recognition) Functions: • Selectively permeable barrier — controls what enters/exits cell • Cell recognition and signalling (receptors) • Endocytosis (taking in) and exocytosis (secretion)
📐 Draw Fluid Mosaic Model: bilayer with heads facing out, tails inside, integral proteins, glycoproteins on outer surface, cholesterol between lipids.
Ch4 6. Describe the general characters of Phylum Arthropoda. Why is it the most successful phylum?
Answer Phylum Arthropoda — largest phylum in animal kingdom (>80% of all animal species): General Characters: • Jointed appendages (arthro = joint, poda = leg) — for locomotion, feeding, reproduction • Exoskeleton of chitin — protection + support; moulted (ecdysis) for growth • Segmented body — head, thorax, abdomen (fused in different classes) • Open circulatory system — blood (haemolymph) in body cavity (haemocoel) • Respiration by gills (aquatic), book lungs, tracheae (terrestrial) • Examples: Cockroach (Periplaneta), Prawn (Palaemon), Butterfly, Scorpion, Crab Why most successful: Jointed legs enable diverse locomotion; exoskeleton provides protection; diverse respiration allows land colonisation; rapid reproduction; small size reduces resource needs.

Ch2 1. Explain the classification of Kingdom Animalia with phyla Non-Chordata and their key features. Describe any three phyla with examples.
Answer Kingdom Animalia — Non-Chordata (invertebrates — no notochord/backbone): 1. Phylum Porifera: • Simplest animals; no true tissues; body full of pores • Choanocytes; sessile; mostly marine; asexual by budding/gemmules • Examples: Sycon, Spongilla (freshwater sponge), Euspongia (bath sponge) 2. Phylum Cnidaria (Coelenterata): • Two body forms: polyp (sessile) and medusa (free-swimming) • Stinging cells — cnidocytes/nematocysts for defence and prey capture • Diploblastic (ectoderm + endoderm); gastrovascular cavity • Examples: Hydra, Aurelia (jellyfish), Corals, Obelia 3. Phylum Platyhelminthes: • Flat, ribbon-like body (platys = flat); triploblastic, acoelomate • Excretion by flame cells (protonephridia) • Many are parasitic (Taenia, Fasciola); free-living Planaria • Examples: Planaria, Tapeworm (Taenia solium), Liver fluke (Fasciola) 4. Phylum Nematoda: • Round/thread-like worms; pseudocoelomate • Parasitic — cause diseases: Ascaris (roundworm), Wuchereria (filariasis) • Bilateral symmetry; complete digestive system
Ch5 2. Describe the structure and types of proteins. What is the significance of proteins in living organisms?
Answer Proteins: Polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. 20 types of amino acids. Most abundant biomolecule in dry weight of cells. Structure (levels of organisation): • Primary: Linear sequence of amino acids (polypeptide chain) • Secondary: α-helix or β-pleated sheet formed by hydrogen bonds • Tertiary: 3D folding — functional shape; stabilised by disulphide bonds, hydrophobic interactions • Quaternary: Two or more polypeptide chains together. E.g., haemoglobin (4 subunits) Types of Proteins: • Structural: Collagen (skin, tendons), Keratin (hair, nails) • Enzymatic: Pepsin, Amylase, RuBisCO • Transport: Haemoglobin (O₂), Albumin (fatty acids in blood) • Hormonal: Insulin, Growth hormone • Immunological: Antibodies (immunoglobulins) Significance: Catalyse reactions, provide structure, transport molecules, regulate processes, defend against pathogens, enable movement (actin, myosin in muscles).
Cell Structure 3. Describe the structure and functions of mitochondria. Why is it called the 'powerhouse of the cell'?
Answer Structure of Mitochondria: • Double membrane organelle — outer membrane (smooth) and inner membrane (folded) • Cristae — folds of inner membrane; increase surface area for ATP synthesis • Matrix — fluid-filled inner space; contains mitochondrial DNA (circular), ribosomes (70S), enzymes of Krebs cycle • Intermembrane space — between outer and inner membranes; site of proton gradient (chemiosmosis) • ATP synthase (F₀F₁ particles) on inner membrane — synthesises ATP Functions: • Site of aerobic respiration: — Krebs cycle in matrix — Electron transport chain (ETC) on inner membrane — ATP synthesis by oxidative phosphorylation • Produces 36–38 ATP per glucose molecule • Involved in calcium signalling, apoptosis (programmed cell death) Why 'powerhouse': Generates most of the cell's ATP (energy currency) through oxidative phosphorylation — hence called the powerhouse or energy factory of the cell. Semi-autonomous: Has own DNA and ribosomes → supports endosymbiotic theory (mitochondria evolved from ancient bacteria).
