Section B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
Q.2 (i)
Define pseudo first-order reaction.
DefinitionA pseudo first-order reaction is a second-order reaction that appears to follow first-order kinetics because one of the reactants is present in such large excess that its concentration remains essentially constant.
ExampleHydrolysis of ester: CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O → CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH
Water is in excess → rate = k'[ester] where k' = k[H₂O] = pseudo first-order rate constant
Q.2 (ii)
What is electro-osmosis?
DefinitionElectro-osmosis is the movement of the dispersion medium (solvent) through a porous membrane toward the electrode with the same charge as the colloidal particles, when an electric field is applied.
NoteOpposite to electrophoresis: in electrophoresis, colloidal particles move; in electro-osmosis, the medium moves.
Q.2 (iii)
What is van't Hoff factor (i)? When is i > 1?
Definitioni = (observed colligative property)/(calculated colligative property for non-electrolyte) = (total moles of solute particles in solution)/(moles of solute dissolved)
i > 1When solute dissociates (electrolytes): NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ (i = 2), CaCl₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ (i = 3)
i < 1When solute associates: CH₃COOH (dimerises in benzene, i = 0.5)
Q.2 (iv)
Define nucleophile. Give two examples.
DefinitionA nucleophile is an electron-rich species (anion or neutral molecule with lone pair) that donates electrons to an electron-deficient carbon (electrophile) during a reaction.
Examples(1) OH⁻ (hydroxide)   (2) CN⁻ (cyanide)   (3) NH₃ (ammonia)   (4) H₂O (water)
Q.2 (v)
What is denaturation of protein?
DefinitionDenaturation is the disruption of the secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure of a protein by physical (heat, UV) or chemical (acid, alkali, heavy metals) agents, causing loss of biological activity.
ExampleCooking egg (albumin protein → denatured, irreversible). Curdling of milk by acid.
Q.2 (vi)
What is an isotactic polymer?
DefinitionIsotactic polymer: all substituents are on the same side of the main polymer chain in a regular arrangement.
ExampleIsotactic polypropylene — crystalline, high melting point, used in fibres
Q.2 (vii)
Define crystal field theory (in brief).
DefinitionCrystal Field Theory (CFT) explains the electronic structure of coordination compounds by treating the interaction between the central metal ion and ligands as purely electrostatic (ionic). Ligand lone pairs repel metal d-orbitals, causing d-orbital splitting.
Q.2 (viii)
What is photosynthesis? Give the overall equation.
DefinitionPhotosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy, using CO₂ and H₂O to synthesise glucose and oxygen.
Equation6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Q.2 (ix)
Define vulcanisation of rubber.
DefinitionVulcanisation is the process of treating natural rubber with sulphur at 150°C to introduce sulphur cross-links (disulfide bridges, –S–S–) between polymer chains, increasing strength, elasticity, and resistance to temperature and solvents.
EffectVulcanised rubber (hard): very high cross-linking. Soft rubber (tyres): moderate cross-linking.
Q.2 (x)
What is ebullioscopy?
DefinitionEbullioscopy is the method of determining the molar mass of a non-volatile solute by measuring the elevation of boiling point of the solvent.
FormulaM_B = (K_b × w_B × 1000)/(ΔT_b × w_A)
Q.2 (xi)
Define linkage isomerism.
DefinitionLinkage isomerism occurs when an ambidentate ligand (one that can bond through two different donor atoms) bonds through different atoms in two complexes, giving different structures with the same formula.
Example[Co(NH₃)₅(NO₂)]²⁺ (N-bonded, nitro) and [Co(NH₃)₅(ONO)]²⁺ (O-bonded, nitrito)
Q.2 (xii)
What is the refractive index of a solution related to?
AnswerThe refractive index of a solution is related to its concentration. It increases with increasing concentration of solute. Used in refractometry to determine concentration of solutions (e.g., sugar solutions, urine analysis).

Q.3 (i)
Derive the rate law for opposing reactions at equilibrium.
Forward/ReverseA ⇌ B   Rate_f = k_f[A], Rate_r = k_r[B]
At EquilibriumRate_f = Rate_r → k_f[A]_eq = k_r[B]_eq → k_f/k_r = [B]_eq/[A]_eq = K_c
Result
K_c = k_f/k_r — equilibrium constant = ratio of forward to reverse rate constants
Q.3 (ii)

Chemical MethodReduction: AuCl₃ + formaldehyde → Au sol

Q.3 (iii)
Explain positive and negative deviations from Raoult's law with examples.
Positive Deviationp_obs > p_Raoult. Solute-solvent interactions weaker than solvent-solvent.
ΔH_mix > 0 (endothermic), ΔV_mix > 0. Form minimum boiling azeotrope.
Example: Ethanol + Water, Acetone + CS₂
Negative Deviationp_obs < p_Raoult. Solute-solvent interactions stronger than solvent-solvent.
ΔH_mix < 0 (exothermic), ΔV_mix < 0. Form maximum boiling azeotrope.
Example: Chloroform + Acetone (H-bond formation)
Q.3 (iv)
Explain the SN2 mechanism with an example.
SN2 MechanismBimolecular, one-step (concerted). Nucleophile attacks from back side while leaving group departs. Rate = k[substrate][Nu⁻]
StepsNu⁻ + R–X → [Nu...C...X]⁻ (transition state) → Nu–R + X⁻
Inversion of configuration (Walden inversion) at chiral centre
ExampleOH⁻ + CH₃Br → CH₃OH + Br⁻ (backside attack → inversion)
Q.3 (v)

ReagentConc. HNO₃ + Conc. H₂SO₄ (mixed acid), ~55°C
Step 1 — Electrophile formationHNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂NO₃⁺ + HSO₄⁻ → NO₂⁺ + H₂O (nitronium ion)

Q.3 (vi)
Explain mutarotation of glucose.
DefinitionMutarotation is the spontaneous change in optical rotation of a freshly prepared solution of α-D-glucose or β-D-glucose to an equilibrium value of +52.7° (mixture of α and β forms).
ExplanationIn solution, glucose opens its ring (open chain aldehyde form) and recycles to give both α-glucose (+112°) and β-glucose (+18.7°). At equilibrium: 37% α, 63% β form.
Q.3 (vii)
Explain how temperature affects conductance of a solution.
EffectAs temperature increases, conductance of an electrolyte solution increases because: (1) ions move faster (viscosity decreases), (2) degree of dissociation of weak electrolytes increases, (3) both ion mobility and concentration increase
Formulaκ_T = κ_0 × [1 + α(T − T₀)] (linear increase for ionic solutions)

Bonding4 NH₃ donate lone pairs to Cu²⁺ → 4 coordinate (dative) bonds → square planar complex
HybridisationCu²⁺: dsp² hybridisation. [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ is square planar, bright blue colour.

Section E — Long Answer Questions 4 × 4 = 16 Marks
Q.5 (i)
(a) Electrophoresis and coagulation of colloids. (b) Peptisation and dialysis.
ElectrophoresisMigration of charged colloidal particles toward opposite electrode when electric field applied.
Application: Cottrell precipitator (removes charged dust/smoke from factories).
CoagulationAggregation and settling of colloidal particles, caused by adding electrolyte. Explained by Hardy-Schulze rule: higher valence ions have greater coagulating power.
Example: river water coagulates with alum (Al³⁺) — flocculation in water treatment.
PeptisationConverting a freshly prepared precipitate into colloid by adding a peptising agent (electrolyte). e.g., Fe(OH)₃ ppt + FeCl₃ → Fe(OH)₃ sol (Fe³⁺ adsorbed)
DialysisPurification of colloid by placing it in a semipermeable membrane bag in pure water. Small ions/molecules diffuse out; colloidal particles remain inside.

Features(1) Metals above H (E° < 0) displace H₂ from acid

(3) Predicts spontaneity: reaction is spontaneous if E°_cell > 0
