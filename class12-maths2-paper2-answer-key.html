SECTION A — MCQ Answers 8 × 1 = 8
(i) Mutually exclusive events A,B: P(A∪B) = ?
Answer: P(A) + P(B)
Mutually exclusive: P(A∩B)=0 → P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B)+P(A∩B) = P(A)+P(B)
(ii) For regression lines b_yx = 0.8 and b_xy = 0.5, find r
Answer: r = 0.632
r = ±√(b_yx × b_xy) = √(0.8 × 0.5) = √0.4 ≈ 0.632
(iii) Number of constraints in LPP can be
Answer: any finite number
LPP has objective function + constraints (inequalities). Constraints can be any finite number.
(iv) Fisher's Ideal Index formula
Answer: √(L × P) — geometric mean of Laspeyre's and Paasche's
Fisher's Index = √(Laspeyre's × Paasche's)
(v) Normal distribution is
Answer: symmetric, bell-shaped, mean=median=mode
Normal: symmetric about mean, total area = 1, extends to ±∞, highest at mean
(vi) Trend in time series represents
Answer: long-term movement in data over years
Trend (T) = long-term direction  |  Seasonal (S) = within year  |  Cyclical (C) = multi-year waves  |  Irregular (I) = random
(vii) Binomial distribution mean = 4, variance = 3: find n and p
Answer: n=16, p=1/4
np = 4 and npq = 3 → q = 3/4 → p = 1/4 → n = 4/(1/4) = 16
(viii) If A and B are complementary: P(A) + P(B) = ?
Answer: 1
P(A) + P(Ā) = 1 → complementary events always sum to 1

(i) Bayes theorem — statement
P(Aᵢ|B) = P(B|Aᵢ)·P(Aᵢ) / Σⱼ P(B|Aⱼ)·P(Aⱼ) Allows updating probability given new information (posterior from prior and likelihood)
(iii) Spearman's rank correlation
r_s = 1 − 6ΣD²/[n(n²−1)] where D = difference in ranks, n = number of pairs Range: −1 ≤ r_s ≤ +1  |  r_s = +1: perfect positive rank agreement  |  r_s = −1: perfect negative
(v) Distinguish Laspeyre's and Paasche's
Laspeyre's (L): uses base year quantities q₀ → tends to overestimate price rise Paasche's (P): uses current year quantities q₁ → tends to underestimate price rise Fisher's = √(L×P) satisfies time reversal and factor reversal tests
(vii) Distinction between parameter and statistic
Parameter: Numerical measure that describes a population (e.g., population mean μ, σ). Fixed, usually unknown. Statistic: Numerical measure computed from a sample (e.g., sample mean x̄, s). Varies from sample to sample.

(x) z-test: n=100, x̄=68, s=5, μ=70
H₀: μ=70   H₁: μ≠70 (two-tailed) z = (x̄−μ)/(s/√n) = (68−70)/(5/10) = −2/0.5 = −4 |z| = 4 > 1.96 (z₀.₀₅ critical value) Reject H₀. Sample mean is significantly different from 70 at 5% level.
