2.1

🌊 Pressure in Fluids

🔑 Key Points
  • Pressure: P = F/A; force per unit area; unit: N/m² = Pascal (Pa)
  • Pressure in a liquid at depth h: P = ρgh (hydrostatic pressure)
  • Total pressure at depth h: P = P₀ + ρgh (P₀ = atmospheric pressure)
  • Pressure is a scalar quantity; acts equally in all directions in a static fluid
  • Pascal's Law: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished to every part of the fluid and the walls of the container
  • Application of Pascal's Law: Hydraulic lift, hydraulic brakes, hydraulic press
  • Hydraulic lift: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂; small force on small piston → large force on large piston
  • Atmospheric pressure: P₀ = 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa = 1 atm = 76 cm of Hg

2.2

🏊 Archimedes' Principle & Buoyancy

🔑 Key Points
  • Buoyant force: upward force exerted by a fluid on an object submerged in it
  • Archimedes' Principle: When a body is partially or fully immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced
  • Buoyant force = Weight of displaced fluid = ρ_fluid × V_submerged × g
  • Floats if: buoyant force ≥ weight → ρ_object ≤ ρ_fluid
  • Sinks if: buoyant force < weight → ρ_object > ρ_fluid
  • Apparent weight = True weight − Buoyant force = mg − ρ_fluid·V·g
  • Relative density = density of substance / density of water = weight in air / loss of weight in water

🌀 Fluid Flow — Equation of Continuity

🔑 Key Points
  • Steady flow: velocity of fluid at any point remains constant with time
  • Streamline: curve whose tangent gives direction of fluid velocity at that point
  • Laminar flow: smooth, parallel layers; Turbulent flow: irregular, chaotic motion
  • Equation of Continuity: for incompressible fluid (ρ = constant): A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
  • Where A = cross-sectional area, v = velocity — based on conservation of mass
  • Narrow pipe → faster flow; wider pipe → slower flow
  • Reynolds number: Re = ρvd/η; Re < 1000: laminar; Re > 2000: turbulent

2.4

⚡ Bernoulli's Equation

🔑 Key Points
  • Bernoulli's Principle: For steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow — sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy and potential energy per unit volume is constant
  • Bernoulli's Equation: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant
  • Based on conservation of energy
  • Where pressure is high, velocity is low and vice versa
  • Applications:
  • → Venturi meter: measures flow speed using pressure difference
  • → Atomiser / spray gun: fast air creates low pressure, sucks liquid up
  • → Aerofoil (aircraft wing): air moves faster over curved top → lower pressure on top → lift force upward
  • → Magnus effect: spinning ball curves due to pressure difference
  • → Blowing between papers: papers come together (high v → low P between them)
  • Speed of efflux (Torricelli's theorem): v = √(2gh) for a hole at depth h in a tank

2.5

🌿 Viscosity & Stokes' Law

🔑 Key Points
  • Viscosity: internal friction between adjacent layers of a moving fluid; resists relative motion
  • Coefficient of viscosity (η): η = F·d / (A·v); unit: N·s/m² = Pa·s = Poise (CGS)
  • Liquids: viscosity decreases with temperature (honey flows easily when hot)
  • Gases: viscosity increases with temperature
  • Stokes' Law: viscous force on a sphere F = 6πηrv
  • Terminal velocity: constant velocity attained when viscous drag + buoyancy = weight
  • At terminal velocity: 6πηrv_T = (4/3)πr³(ρ−σ)g
  • Terminal velocity formula: v_T = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η
  • v_T ∝ r² (larger object → higher terminal velocity)

2.6

🔮 Surface Tension

🔑 Key Points
  • Surface tension (T): force per unit length acting tangentially along the surface of a liquid; T = F/L
  • Unit: N/m; arises due to cohesive forces between molecules at surface
  • Surface acts like a stretched elastic membrane
  • Surface energy: Work done to increase surface area; W = T × ΔA
  • Excess pressure inside a drop: ΔP = 2T/r (one surface)
  • Excess pressure inside a soap bubble: ΔP = 4T/r (two surfaces)
  • Capillarity: rise or fall of liquid in a narrow tube due to surface tension
  • Capillary rise formula: h = 2T cosθ / (ρgr)
  • Angle of contact (θ): angle between tangent to liquid surface at contact point and solid surface
  • θ < 90°: liquid wets solid (water-glass); θ > 90°: liquid doesn't wet (mercury-glass)
  • Surface tension decreases with increase in temperature; zero at critical temperature

⚡ Bernoulli's Principle
Q1
State Bernoulli's principle. Derive Bernoulli's equation for fluid flow. Give two applications.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Statement: For steady, incompressible, non-viscous fluid flow, the sum of pressure, kinetic energy per unit volume, and potential energy per unit volume remains constant along a streamline.

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

Derivation (Work-Energy theorem):

Consider fluid flowing from section 1 (area A₁, height h₁, speed v₁) to section 2 (A₂, h₂, v₂).

Work done by pressure forces: W = P₁A₁v₁dt − P₂A₂v₂dt

By continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ = Q (volume flow rate)

W_pressure = (P₁ − P₂)Q·dt

Change in KE = ½ρQ·dt(v₂² − v₁²); Change in PE = ρQ·dt·g(h₂ − h₁)

By work-energy theorem: W = ΔKE + ΔPE

(P₁−P₂) = ½ρ(v₂²−v₁²) + ρg(h₂−h₁)
P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂ ✓

Applications:

1. Aircraft lift (Aerofoil): Curved top surface → air moves faster over top → lower pressure on top → net upward lift force (Bernoulli's effect).

2. Atomiser/spray gun: Fast-moving air over narrow tube opening creates low pressure → liquid sucked up and sprayed as fine mist.

Q2
Derive an expression for the speed of efflux (Torricelli's theorem).
🔴 High Freq3 Marks2023·2022·2020
✅ Model Answer

Consider a large tank with a small hole at depth h below the free surface. At the free surface: P = P₀ (atmospheric), v₁ ≈ 0 (large tank). At the hole: P = P₀ (open to atmosphere), v₂ = v (efflux speed).

Applying Bernoulli's equation between surface (1) and hole (2):

P₀ + ½ρ(0)² + ρgh = P₀ + ½ρv² + ρg(0) ρgh = ½ρv²
v = √(2gh) — Torricelli's theorem ✓

This is the same as the speed of a body falling freely through height h — the fluid behaves as if the liquid fell freely from the surface to the hole level.

🌿 Surface Tension
Q3
Derive an expression for excess pressure inside a soap bubble and a liquid drop.
🔴 Every Year3–4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Excess pressure inside a liquid drop:

Consider a spherical drop of radius r. Imagine it splitting into two halves. Surface tension acts along the circular rim of area 2πr trying to pull halves together.

Force due to surface tension = T × 2πr (along circumference) Force due to excess pressure = ΔP × πr² (over cross-section) At equilibrium: ΔP × πr² = T × 2πr
ΔP = 2T/r (liquid drop — one surface)

Excess pressure inside a soap bubble:

A soap bubble has TWO surfaces (inner and outer). Each contributes excess pressure of 2T/r:

ΔP = 2 × (2T/r) = 4T/r (soap bubble — two surfaces)

Soap bubble has greater excess pressure than a liquid drop of the same radius.

Q4
What is capillarity? Derive an expression for capillary rise: h = 2T cosθ / ρgr
🔴 Every Year3–4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Capillarity: The phenomenon of rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube (capillary) due to surface tension is called capillarity (or capillary action).

Rise: for liquids wetting the tube (θ < 90°) — e.g., water in glass

Fall: for liquids not wetting the tube (θ > 90°) — e.g., mercury in glass

Derivation:

For a capillary tube of radius r, the liquid surface is concave (meniscus). Surface tension T acts along the glass surface at angle θ.

Upward force due to surface tension along inner circumference:

F_up = T × 2πr × cosθ

Weight of liquid column of height h:

W = ρ × πr²h × g

At equilibrium: F_up = W

T × 2πr cosθ = ρπr²hg
h = 2T cosθ / (ρgr) ✓

Thinner tube (smaller r) → greater capillary rise. This explains water transport in plants through narrow xylem vessels.

🌿 Viscosity & Stokes' Law
Q5
State Stokes' Law. Derive an expression for terminal velocity of a sphere falling through a viscous fluid.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Stokes' Law: When a sphere of radius r moves with velocity v through a fluid of viscosity η, the viscous drag force on the sphere is:

F = 6πηrv

Terminal Velocity Derivation:

Forces on a sphere (density ρ, radius r) falling through fluid (density σ):

(i) Weight W = (4/3)πr³ρg (downward)

(ii) Buoyant force F_B = (4/3)πr³σg (upward)

(iii) Viscous drag F_v = 6πηrv (upward, increases with velocity)

At terminal velocity v_T, net force = 0:

W = F_B + F_v (4/3)πr³ρg = (4/3)πr³σg + 6πηrv_T 6πηrv_T = (4/3)πr³(ρ−σ)g
v_T = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η

v_T ∝ r² — larger sphere reaches higher terminal velocity.

v_T ∝ 1/η — more viscous fluid → lower terminal velocity.

Q6
State Archimedes' Principle. How is it used to determine the relative density of a solid?
🔴 High Freq3 Marks2023·2022·2021
✅ Model Answer

Archimedes' Principle: When a body is partially or fully immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

F_B = ρ_fluid × V_submerged × g

Relative density determination:

Let W₁ = weight of solid in air, W₂ = weight of solid in water (apparent weight)

Loss of weight in water = W₁ − W₂ = weight of water displaced = buoyant force

Relative density = density of solid / density of water = (mass of solid) / (mass of equal volume of water) = (weight of solid in air) / (loss of weight in water)
Relative density = W₁ / (W₁ − W₂)
N1. A spherical ball of radius 2×10⁻³ m and density 1500 kg/m³ falls through glycerin of viscosity 0.9 Pa·s and density 1260 kg/m³. Find terminal velocity. (g = 10 m/s²)
r = 2×10⁻³ m | ρ = 1500 kg/m³ | σ = 1260 kg/m³ | η = 0.9 Pa·s | g = 10 m/s²
v_T = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η
v_T = 2×(2×10⁻³)²×(1500−1260)×10 / (9×0.9)
v_T = 2×4×10⁻⁶×240×10 / 8.1
v_T = 19200×10⁻⁶ / 8.1 = 2.37×10⁻³ m/s
∴ v_T ≈ 2.37×10⁻³ m/s = 2.37 mm/s ✓
N2. A water tank has a hole at 5 m below the water surface. Find the speed of efflux. (g = 10 m/s²)
h = 5 m | g = 10 m/s²
By Torricelli's theorem:
v = √(2gh) = √(2×10×5) = √100 = 10 m/s
∴ Speed of efflux = 10 m/s ✓
N3. A soap bubble has radius 1.5×10⁻² m. Surface tension of soap solution = 0.03 N/m. Find excess pressure inside the bubble and the surface energy.
r = 1.5×10⁻² m | T = 0.03 N/m
Excess pressure (soap bubble, 2 surfaces):
ΔP = 4T/r = 4×0.03/(1.5×10⁻²) = 0.12/0.015 = 8 Pa

Surface area = 2 × 4πr² (two surfaces)
= 8π×(1.5×10⁻²)² = 8π×2.25×10⁻⁴ = 5.655×10⁻³ m²
Surface energy = T × area = 0.03×5.655×10⁻³ = 1.7×10⁻⁴ J
∴ ΔP = 8 Pa; Surface energy = 1.7×10⁻⁴ J ✓
N4. Water rises to 3 cm in a capillary tube. Find the radius of the tube. (T = 0.073 N/m, θ = 0°, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)
h = 3×10⁻² m | T = 0.073 N/m | θ = 0° | ρ = 1000 kg/m³ | g = 9.8 m/s²
h = 2T cosθ / (ρgr)
r = 2T cosθ / (ρgh)
r = 2×0.073×cos0° / (1000×9.8×3×10⁻²)
r = 0.146 / 294 = 4.97×10⁻⁴ m ≈ 0.5 mm
∴ r ≈ 4.97×10⁻⁴ m ≈ 0.5 mm ✓
N5. A hydraulic lift has piston areas A₁ = 5 cm² and A₂ = 100 cm². If force on small piston = 20 N, find force on large piston and pressure.
A₁ = 5×10⁻⁴ m² | A₂ = 100×10⁻⁴ m² | F₁ = 20 N
Pascal's Law: P₁ = P₂
F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂
F₂ = F₁ × A₂/A₁ = 20 × (100/5) = 20 × 20 = 400 N
Pressure P = F₁/A₁ = 20/(5×10⁻⁴) = 4×10⁴ Pa
∴ F₂ = 400 N; P = 4×10⁴ Pa ✓
