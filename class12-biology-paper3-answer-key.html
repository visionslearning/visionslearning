Q.1 (i)
Acrosome in sperm is derived from:
✔ Answer: (B) Golgi body
Explanation Acrosome = cap-like structure on the sperm head, covering the anterior 2/3 of the nucleus. Formed from Golgi body during spermiogenesis. Contains hydrolytic enzymes (hyaluronidase, acrosin) for penetrating the zona pellucida during fertilisation.
Q.1 (ii)
Haemophilia is:
✔ Answer: (D) X-linked recessive
Explanation Haemophilia A (clotting factor VIII) and B (factor IX) are X-linked recessive disorders. Males (XY) have only one X chromosome → if X carries defective gene, they express the disease (hemizygous). Females need two copies of defective allele (X^h X^h) to show disease; X^H X^h = carrier.
Q.1 (iii)
In translation, mRNA is read in direction:
✔ Answer: (A) 5' to 3'
Explanation mRNA is read in the 5'→3' direction (ribosome moves along mRNA 5'→3') New polypeptide is synthesised N-terminus to C-terminus.
Q.1 (iv)
Vector for cloning large DNA fragments:
✔ Answer: (C) BAC (Bacterial Artificial Chromosome)
Explanation BAC can carry inserts of 100–300 kb (very large). Used in Human Genome Project. Plasmid: up to ~10 kb | Lambda phage: ~15–20 kb | Cosmid: ~45 kb | BAC: 100–300 kb | YAC: up to 1000 kb
Q.1 (v)
Photorespiration occurs predominantly in:
✔ Answer: (B) C₃ plants
Explanation Photorespiration: RuBisCO acts as oxygenase instead of carboxylase (when O₂ is high, CO₂ is low). Wastes fixed carbon and energy. C₃ plants suffer from photorespiration. C₄ plants avoid it by concentrating CO₂ in bundle sheath. CAM plants also minimise it.
Q.1 (vi)
Ammonification is carried out by:
✔ Answer: (B) Decomposers (bacteria and fungi)
Explanation Ammonification: decomposition of organic nitrogen (proteins, urea) from dead organisms → NH₃/NH₄⁺ by putrefying bacteria and fungi. Examples: Bacillus, Pseudomonas (bacteria); various soil fungi.
Q.1 (vii)
First genetically modified food approved for human consumption:
✔ Answer: (A) Flavr Savr tomato
Explanation Flavr Savr tomato (1994, Calgen Inc.) — first GM food approved by FDA, USA. Gene for polygalacturonase (softening enzyme) antisense was introduced → tomato ripens but stays firm longer → extended shelf life.
Q.1 (viii)
The full form of IUCN is:
✔ Answer: International Union for Conservation of Nature
Explanation IUCN maintains the Red List — a classification of species by their risk of extinction. Categories: Extinct → Extinct in Wild → Critically Endangered → Endangered → Vulnerable → Near Threatened → Least Concern
Q.1 (ix)
Which of the following is a C₄ plant?
✔ Answer: Maize (corn) / Sugarcane / Sorghum
Explanation C₄ plants: Maize, Sugarcane, Sorghum, Amaranthus, Bermuda grass C₃ plants: Rice, Wheat, Cotton, Soybean, Sunflower, most trees C₄ advantage: efficient in hot, sunny, arid conditions; no photorespiration due to Kranz anatomy
Q.1 (x)
PCR was invented by:
✔ Answer: Kary Mullis (1983)
Explanation PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) invented by Kary Mullis in 1983 — awarded Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1993. PCR amplifies a specific DNA sequence exponentially. Uses Taq polymerase (from Thermus aquaticus, thermostable).

Key Definitions
SpermatogoniaDiploid (2n) germinal cells in the seminiferous tubules that undergo mitosis and meiosis to form spermatozoa.
CodominanceBoth alleles express themselves simultaneously in the heterozygous condition. Neither is dominant. Example: Blood group AB.
mRNAMessenger RNA — carries genetic information from DNA (in nucleus) to ribosomes (in cytoplasm) for protein synthesis.
Restriction enzymeRestriction endonuclease — bacterial enzyme that cuts double-stranded DNA at specific palindromic sequences (restriction sites), creating sticky or blunt ends.
TranspirationLoss of water vapour from aerial parts of plants (mainly through stomata). Drives the ascent of sap (water column) up the plant.
Nitrogen fixationConversion of atmospheric N₂ gas into ammonia (NH₃) by nitrogen-fixing organisms (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Cyanobacteria). Enzyme: nitrogenase.
ProbeA labelled (radioactive/fluorescent) single-stranded DNA or RNA fragment used to detect complementary sequences by hybridisation (in Southern/Northern blotting, FISH).
Genetic diversityVariation in the genetic makeup (allele frequencies, genotypes) within a species or population. Basis for evolution and adaptation.

Q.3 (iv)
Explain the SN2 reaction mechanism for nucleophilic substitution.
Restriction Enzyme Mechanism (Biology context) Restriction endonucleases recognise specific palindromic sequences (same reading on both strands 5'→3'). EcoRI: 5'—G↓AATTC—3'   /   3'—CTTAA↑G—5' Creates 5' overhanging "sticky ends" (AATT). These can hydrogen bond to complementary sequences. DNA ligase re-seals the cut ends when the sticky ends of foreign DNA and vector anneal.

Q.3 (vii)
Explain Bt toxin mode of action in pest control.
Bt Toxin Bacillus thuringiensis produces Cry proteins (crystal proteins) encoded by cry genes. Protoxin (inactive) → insect ingests it → alkaline midgut activates toxin → active Cry protein Active toxin binds to receptors on gut epithelial cells → forms pores → cells swell and lyse → insect dies Species-specific: cry1Ac targets bollworm; cry1Ab targets corn borer. Safe for humans (we lack the receptor).

Q.4 (ix)
What is biopiracy? Give two examples. How can it be prevented?
BiopiracyThe commercial exploitation (patenting, commercialisation) of biological resources and traditional knowledge by corporations or researchers without adequate compensation or acknowledgement to the countries/communities of origin.
Examples (1) Neem: European Patent Office granted patent on neem's antifungal properties to W.R. Grace (US company) without recognising Indian traditional knowledge. Challenged and revoked.
(2) Turmeric: US patent granted for healing wounds with turmeric. India challenged successfully — patent revoked as it was prior art in Ayurveda.
Prevention (1) Strict implementation of CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity)
(2) Documentation of traditional knowledge (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library — TKDL in India)
(3) Benefit-sharing agreements with local communities

Q.5 (ii)
(a) Mendel's dihybrid cross. (b) Linkage and recombination.
Dihybrid Cross P: YYRR (yellow round) × yyrr (green wrinkled) F₁: all YyRr (yellow round) F₂: YyRr × YyRr → 16 combinations Phenotypic ratio: 9 Yellow Round : 3 Yellow Wrinkled : 3 Green Round : 1 Green Wrinkled Proves Law of Independent Assortment: genes on different chromosomes assort independently
Linkage Genes on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together (linked). They do NOT assort independently. Tight linkage → fewer recombinants. T.H. Morgan discovered linkage in Drosophila.
Recombination During meiosis I prophase, homologous chromosomes undergo crossing over at chiasmata → exchange of alleles → new combinations of traits. Recombination frequency = (number of recombinants/total offspring) × 100. Used to construct genetic maps.
Q.5 (iv)
(a) Photorespiration. (b) Mineral nutrition in plants.
Photorespiration When O₂ concentration is high and CO₂ is low (on hot sunny days when stomata close), RuBisCO acts as oxygenase instead of carboxylase. RuBP + O₂ → phosphoglycolate (2C) + PGA (3C). Phosphoglycolate cannot enter Calvin cycle directly. Phosphoglycolate metabolised in peroxisomes and mitochondria → CO₂ released (no ATP or NADPH produced) Effect: Wastes 25–30% of fixed carbon. Reduces productivity. Absent/minimal in C₄ plants (CO₂ pump prevents it).
Mineral Nutrition Macronutrients (needed in large amounts): N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S Micronutrients (needed in traces): Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl N — chlorophyll, amino acids, nucleic acids; deficiency: yellowing (chlorosis) P — ATP, nucleic acids, phospholipids; deficiency: purple/dark green leaves K — osmotic regulation, enzyme activation; deficiency: scorching of leaf margins Fe — chlorophyll synthesis; deficiency: interveinal chlorosis
