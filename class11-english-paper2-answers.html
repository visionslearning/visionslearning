Q.1A — Seen Extract: "On to the Summit" — Tenzing Norgay12 Marks
📖 EXTRACT (reference):

One foot, then another, one step at a time. I was breathing very fast — perhaps eleven or twelve breaths to each step. The slope was very steep and I had to cut every step very carefully... Finally, we were on a ridge with nothing above us but sky. I felt a great surge of joy within me. We were on top of the world. Hillary stepped up beside me and we shook hands... I was too happy to say anything. I looked down at the valley below and thought of home, of my people, of Chomolungma — the Goddess Mother of the World.

A1Global Understanding (MCQ)2 Marks
❓ Question

i. Tenzing was breathing very fast because — (A) air was warm (B) slope was steep and climb exhausting (C) Hillary moving too fast (D) afraid of height

ii. "We were on top of the world" means — (A) flying in helicopter (B) standing on Everest summit (C) looking at map (D) climbing small hill

✅ Answer
i. (B) The slope was very steep and the climb was exhausting
ii. (B) Standing on the highest mountain summit — Mount Everest
1 mark each = 2 marks
A2Complex Factual2 Marks
❓ Question

i. What did Tenzing experience each time he thought he had reached the top?
ii. What did Tenzing think of when he looked down from the summit?

✅ Answer
i. Each time Tenzing thought he had reached the top, he found another slope above him — the true summit kept appearing to be further away.
ii. When he looked down from the summit, Tenzing thought of home, of his people, and of Chomolungma — the Goddess Mother of the World.
1 mark each for factually correct answer
A3Interpretation2 Marks
❓ Question

"Two men who had been through so much together." What does this reveal about the relationship between Tenzing and Hillary? What qualities did their partnership show?

✅ Sample Answer
This phrase reveals that Tenzing and Hillary shared a deep bond built on shared hardship, trust and mutual dependence. They had faced the same dangers — freezing temperatures, exhaustion and uncertainty — and had supported each other throughout. Their partnership showed qualities of teamwork, loyalty, and the understanding that individual achievement is most meaningful when shared with someone who was part of the journey.
1 mark: relationship identified correctly + 1 mark: qualities explained

A5Do as directed — Grammar (any TWO, 1 mark each)2 Marks
❓ Question

1. Change the Voice: "Hillary stepped up beside me and we shook hands."
2. Change the Narration: Tenzing said, "I was too happy to say anything."
3. Rewrite using 'so…that': "The slope was very steep. I had to cut every step carefully."

✅ Answers
1. Hillary stepped up beside me and hands were shaken by us. (Passive Voice)
2. Tenzing said that he had been too happy to say anything. (Indirect Speech — tense shifts, "I"→"he")
3. The slope was so steep that I had to cut every step carefully.
1 mark each for any TWO correct

B1Do as directed — any THREE (1 mark each)3 Marks
❓ Questions

1. Past Perfect: "By the time they _____ (reach) the summit, the weather had changed."
2. Question tag: "They had climbed Mount Everest together, _____?"
3. Identify clause: When the summit was reached, Tenzing felt an overwhelming joy.
4. Positive degree: "No mountain is higher than Mount Everest."
5. not only…but also: "Tenzing was brave. He was determined."

✅ Answers
1. had reached (Past Perfect)
2. hadn't they?
3. Adverb Clause of Time (subordinate clause introduced by "when")
4. Mount Everest is as high as any other mountain. OR "Mount Everest is the highest mountain." (Superlative also accepted)
5. Tenzing was not only brave but also determined.
1 mark each for any THREE correct

Q.2A — Seen Poem: "Cherry Tree" — Ruskin Bond10 Marks
📖 POEM (reference):
A cherry tree I planted once — / Just a twig with two leaves on it; / But it grew, and it grew / And it grew. // I watched it grow through the years, / Through the rain and the sun and the wind. / A little tree, fighting for its life / Against the world that wanted it to end. // But a cherry tree I planted once / Has grown now to a tree so strong, / And birds come nesting in its branches now / And beneath it, children play the whole day long.
A1Global Understanding (MCQ)2 Marks
❓ Question

i. The poet first planted the cherry tree as — (A) fully grown sapling (B) small twig with two leaves (C) seed in ground (D) branch of another tree

ii. "A little tree, fighting for its life against the world" suggests — (A) attacked by animals (B) struggled against harsh weather to survive (C) poet tried to cut it (D) afraid of birds

✅ Answer
i. (B) A small twig with two leaves
ii. (B) The tree struggled against harsh weather and difficult conditions to survive
1 mark each = 2 marks
A2Interpretation2 Marks
❓ Question

What does the final image — "birds come nesting" and "children play beneath it" — tell us about the result of the poet's patient care?

✅ Sample Answer
The final image tells us that the poet's years of patient nurturing have transformed a fragile twig into a source of life and shelter for others. The tree now gives a home to birds and provides shade for children to play under. This shows that whatever we care for with patience and love will grow to benefit not just ourselves but the world around us.
1 mark: understanding the image + 1 mark: connecting it to patience/nurturing

Write 2–3 sentences about something small you started or nurtured that grew into something meaningful over time.

✅ Sample Answer
I once started reading just one page of a book every night before sleeping. It felt like very little, but within a year I had finished fifteen books and developed a deep love for literature. Something that began as a small, quiet habit had quietly changed the way I think and speak.

A4Poetic Devices2 Marks
❓ Question

1. "A little tree, fighting for its life against the world" — Figure + Explanation
2. "It grew, and it grew, and it grew" — Figure + Explanation
3. "Through the rain and the sun and the wind" — Figure
4. Find an example of Visual Imagery

✅ Answers
LineFigure of SpeechExplanation
"fighting for its life"PersonificationThe tree is given the human quality of "fighting" — trees cannot actually fight
"it grew, and it grew and it grew"Repetition / Anaphora"grew" repeated 3 times — emphasises the continuous, unstoppable growth
"Through the rain and the sun and the wind"PolysyndetonRepeated "and" creates rhythm and lists forces of nature
Visual Imagery example"Birds come nesting in its branches" / "children play the whole day long"
½ mark each = 2 marks. Award flexibly for valid answers.

Sample — Cherry Tree (Ruskin Bond):
"Cherry Tree" is a beautiful poem by the Indian author Ruskin Bond. The poem describes how the poet planted a small cherry twig and watched it grow into a strong, sheltering tree over many years. The central theme is the power of patience and nurturing — small beginnings lead to great outcomes. The poem uses Personification ("a little tree, fighting for its life") and Repetition ("it grew, and it grew") very effectively. The tone is warm and celebratory. The message is that whatever we nurture with love and patience will grow and give back to the world. I find this poem deeply inspiring — it reminds me never to give up on small beginnings.

❓ Question

✅ Sample — "Travel Broadens the Mind"
When we travel to new places, we encounter different cultures, languages, foods and ways of life. We learn that the world is far richer and more diverse than our familiar surroundings suggest. A person who has only read about mountains never truly understands their majesty until standing before one. Travel teaches us tolerance, empathy and humility. We return home not just with photographs but with wider thinking and a deeper understanding of humanity. Truly, travel is the best education the world can offer.

Respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends — Good morning!

Q.4B — Subjective: Chapter 4.2 The Rising of the Moon (Lady Gregory)4 Marks
❓ Question 1 (2 Marks)

Why does the Sergeant misguide his officers? What does this action reveal about his character?

✅ Sample Answer
The Sergeant misguides his officers because, during his long conversation with the ragged man, he recognises a shared humanity — perhaps even a shared history of struggle. He sends them in the wrong direction, allowing the wanted man to escape. This action reveals that beneath his official duty, the Sergeant has a conscience and compassion. He is a man torn between his role as an enforcer of law and his deeper sense of justice and human feeling.
2 marks: 1 — reason clearly stated + 1 — character insight
❓ Question 2 (2 Marks)

The Sergeant's reaction at the end surprises the audience. Describe this reaction and explain why it is dramatically effective.

✅ Sample Answer
At the end of the play, the Sergeant — who has been chasing the wanted man all along — deliberately allows him to escape by sending his officers in the wrong direction. The audience expects him to make the arrest. His decision not to do so is a dramatic reversal. It is effective because it forces the audience to reconsider everything they assumed about authority and loyalty. It transforms the play from a simple chase story into a deep examination of conscience, justice and the meaning of patriotism.
2 marks: 1 — reaction correctly described + 1 — dramatic effect explained
