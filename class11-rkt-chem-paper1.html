Ch1 i. Define empirical formula. ⭐ IMP
The empirical formula of a compound is the simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element present in the compound. E.g., empirical formula of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is CH₂O.
Ch4 ii. State Pauli's Exclusion Principle. ⭐ IMP
No two electrons in the same atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers (n, l, m, s). An orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons with opposite spins (↑↓).
Ch14 iii. What is a nucleophile? Give one example. ⭐ IMP
A nucleophile is an electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to an electrophile to form a new bond. Examples: OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O.
Ch15 iv. Write the IUPAC name of CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃. ⭐ IMP
  • Longest chain = 4 carbons (butane); branch = CH₃ at C2
  • IUPAC name: 2-methylbutane (common: isopentane)

Ch1 Q3. Calculate the molecular formula of a compound with empirical formula CH₂O and molar mass 180 g/mol. ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
Empirical formula: CH₂O; M(empirical) = 12+2+16 = 30 g/mol
n = M(molecular)/M(empirical) = 180/30 = 6
Molecular formula = (CH₂O)₆
Molecular formula = C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose)
Ch1 Q4. A compound contains 75% carbon and 25% hydrogen by mass. Find its empirical formula. If its molar mass is 16 g/mol, find molecular formula. (C=12, H=1) ⭐ IMP (3M)
C: 75/12 = 6.25 mol; H: 25/1 = 25 mol
Ratio — divide by 6.25: C=1, H=4
Empirical formula = CH₄ (M = 12+4 = 16 g/mol)
n = 16/16 = 1
Molecular formula = CH₄ (Methane)
Ch2 Q5. Calculate the molarity of a solution containing 9.8 g of H₂SO₄ in 500 mL solution. (H=1, S=32, O=16) ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
M(H₂SO₄) = 2+32+64 = 98 g/mol
n = 9.8/98 = 0.1 mol
V = 500 mL = 0.5 L
M = n/V = 0.1/0.5
Molarity = 0.2 M
Ch2 Q6. For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: if 14 g of N₂ reacts with excess H₂, find: (i) moles of N₂ (ii) moles of NH₃ formed (iii) mass of NH₃. (N=14, H=1) ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
(i) M(N₂) = 28 g/mol; n(N₂) = 14/28 = 0.5 mol
(ii) Stoichiometry: 1 mol N₂ → 2 mol NH₃; 0.5 mol N₂ → 1 mol NH₃
(iii) M(NH₃) = 14+3 = 17 g/mol; mass = 1 × 17 = 17 g
0.5 mol N₂ → 1 mol NH₃ = 17 g
Ch4 Q7. State and explain Aufbau principle. Write the filling order of orbitals. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Aufbau Principle ("building up"): Electrons are added to orbitals in order of increasing energy — the lowest energy orbital is filled first before moving to the next higher energy orbital.
  • (n+l) rule: Orbital with lower (n+l) value has lower energy. If two orbitals have the same (n+l), the one with lower n has lower energy.
  • Filling order: 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p → 5s → 4d → 5p...
  • Example: n+l values: 4s (4+0=4) < 3d (3+2=5), so 4s fills before 3d.
  • This explains why K (Z=19) has config [Ar] 4s¹ not [Ar] 3d¹.

Ch4 Q9. Explain Hund's Rule. Show the orbital diagram for N (Z=7) and O (Z=8). ⭐ IMP (3M)
Hund's Rule: In a set of degenerate (equal energy) orbitals, electrons are distributed with one electron per orbital with parallel spin before any orbital gets a second (paired) electron. This maximises total spin multiplicity and minimises electron-electron repulsion.
  • N (Z=7): 1s² 2s² 2p³ → each of the 3 p-orbitals gets one ↑ electron: 2p: ↑ ↑ ↑
  • O (Z=8): 1s² 2s² 2p⁴ → one p-orbital gets a paired electron: 2p: ↑↓ ↑ ↑
📐 Draw box diagrams: N: [1s: ↑↓] [2s: ↑↓] [2p: ↑|↑|↑]; O: [1s: ↑↓] [2s: ↑↓] [2p: ↑↓|↑|↑]
Ch4 Q10. Calculate the wavelength of an electron moving with velocity 3×10⁶ m/s. (mass of e⁻ = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s) ⭐ IMP Numerical (3M)
de Broglie's formula:
λ = h/(mv)
λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 3×10⁶)
λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (27.33×10⁻²⁵)
λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (2.733×10⁻²⁴)
λ ≈ 2.42 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 2.42 Å
This is in the X-ray range — confirms electrons have significant wave character.

Ch2 Q12. Define: (i) Molality (ii) Mole fraction. Calculate the mole fraction of NaCl in a solution containing 2 mol NaCl and 8 mol H₂O. ⭐ IMP (3M)
(i) Molality: Moles of solute per kg of solvent. m = n/W_solvent(kg) Unit: mol/kg (temperature independent).
(ii) Mole fraction: Ratio of moles of one component to total moles of all components.
Total moles = 2 + 8 = 10 mol
X(NaCl) = 2/10 = 0.2
X(H₂O) = 8/10 = 0.8
Mole fraction of NaCl = 0.2; H₂O = 0.8 (sum = 1 ✓)
Ch1 Q13. State the law of conservation of mass and law of multiple proportions. Give one example each. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Law of Conservation of Mass: In a chemical reaction, total mass of reactants = total mass of products. Matter cannot be created or destroyed.
  • Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O; 4g + 32g = 36g ✓
Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton): When two elements combine to form two or more different compounds, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other element bear a simple whole number ratio.
  • Example: CO and CO₂ — oxygen combining with 12g C: CO has 16g O; CO₂ has 32g O; ratio = 16:32 = 1:2 (simple whole number ratio) ✓

Ch4 Q14. State Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle. Calculate the uncertainty in velocity of an electron if uncertainty in position is 1×10⁻¹⁰ m. (m_e = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s) ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
Statement: It is impossible to simultaneously measure the exact position and exact momentum of a subatomic particle. Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π  →  Δx · mΔv ≥ h/4π
Δv ≥ h/(4π × m × Δx)
Δv ≥ 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (4 × 3.1416 × 9.11×10⁻³¹ × 1×10⁻¹⁰)
Δv ≥ 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (1.143×10⁻³⁹)
Δv ≥ 5.79 × 10⁵ m/s
This enormous uncertainty proves we cannot track electron position — justifying orbital model.

Ch14 Q16. Explain the inductive effect with –I and +I examples. How does it affect acidity? ⭐ IMP (3M)
Inductive Effect: Permanent displacement of electron density along σ-bonds due to electronegativity differences.
  • –I effect (EW groups): –Cl, –Br, –NO₂, –CN, –OH pull electrons toward themselves. Increase acidity (stabilise conjugate base).
    Order of –I: –F > –OH > –Cl > –Br > –I
  • +I effect (ED groups): Alkyl groups (–CH₃, –C₂H₅) push electrons away from themselves. Decrease acidity (destabilise conjugate base).
    Order: –C(CH₃)₃ > –CH(CH₃)₂ > –C₂H₅ > –CH₃
  • Effect on acidity: More –I groups on α-carbon → more stable carboxylate anion → stronger acid.
    Example: CHCl₂COOH > CH₂ClCOOH > CH₃COOH (acidity decreases)
Ch14 Q17. What is resonance? Explain resonance in benzene (C₆H₆). ⭐ IMP (3M)
Resonance: When a molecule cannot be represented by a single Lewis structure, it is represented by two or more structures (resonance structures) and the actual molecule is a resonance hybrid of all of them.
  • Resonance structures differ only in the positions of π-electrons or lone pairs, not in atom positions.
  • The actual molecule has properties intermediate between all resonance structures — this is delocalisation of electrons.
Resonance in Benzene:
  • Benzene (C₆H₆) has two Kekulé structures — alternating C=C and C–C bonds (with C=C at different positions).
  • The actual structure is a resonance hybrid with all C–C bonds equivalent (bond length = 139 pm, between C–C 154 pm and C=C 134 pm).
  • The 6 π-electrons are completely delocalised over the ring → represented as a circle inside the hexagon.
  • This delocalisation gives benzene extra stability (resonance energy ≈ 150 kJ/mol).
📐 Draw two Kekulé structures with double-headed arrow ↔ between them, and the resonance hybrid (hexagon with circle). Label C–C bond length = 139 pm.
Ch14 Q18. Explain chain isomerism and position isomerism with examples for C₄H₁₀ and C₃H₇Cl. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Chain Isomerism (C₄H₁₀ — Butane):
  • n-butane: CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃ (straight chain, bp = –0.5°C)
  • Isobutane (2-methylpropane): CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₃ (branched chain, bp = –11.7°C)
  • Same molecular formula C₄H₁₀; different carbon skeleton → different physical properties.
Position Isomerism (C₃H₇Cl — Propyl chloride):
  • 1-chloropropane: CH₃–CH₂–CH₂Cl (Cl at C1)
  • 2-chloropropane: CH₃–CHCl–CH₃ (Cl at C2)
  • Same molecular formula C₃H₇Cl; same carbon skeleton; Cl at different positions.
Ch15 Q19. Explain the halogenation of methane (free radical mechanism) with all three steps. ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
Halogenation of alkanes occurs by free radical substitution mechanism with UV light (hν):
  • Step 1 — Initiation: UV light breaks Cl–Cl bond homolytically → 2 Cl• radicals. Cl₂ → (hν) → 2Cl•
  • Step 2 — Propagation (chain reaction):
    • Cl• attacks methane: CH₄ + Cl• → CH₃• + HCl
    • CH₃• attacks Cl₂: CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl• (regenerates Cl• → chain continues)
  • Step 3 — Termination: Two radicals combine → chain ends.
    • Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂
    • CH₃• + Cl• → CH₃Cl
    • CH₃• + CH₃• → C₂H₆
Products: CH₃Cl (chloromethane) as main product; CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, CCl₄ also formed.

Ch4 Q20. Describe Bohr's atomic model for hydrogen. Write all postulates and derive the expression for energy of the nth orbit. Calculate energy of electron in ground state. ⭐ Most IMP Long Answer (4M)
Bohr's Postulates:
  • 1. Electron revolves in fixed circular orbits (stationary states) without emitting energy.
  • 2. Angular momentum is quantised: mvr = nh/2π
  • 3. Energy is emitted/absorbed only during transitions: ΔE = hν = E₂ – E₁
  • 4. The innermost orbit (n=1) has lowest energy = ground state.
Energy of nth orbit — derivation:
  • Total energy = KE + PE = ½mv² – Ze²/4πε₀r
  • From force balance and angular momentum: v = Ze²/2ε₀nh
  • Substituting gives: Eₙ = –Z²e⁴m / 8ε₀²n²h² = –13.6Z²/n² eV
  • For hydrogen (Z=1): Eₙ = –13.6/n² eV
Ground state (n=1):
E₁ = –13.6/1² = –13.6 eV
E₂ = –13.6/4 = –3.4 eV; E₃ = –13.6/9 = –1.51 eV
Ground state energy of H = –13.6 eV = –2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J
Negative sign = electron is bound; ionisation energy = +13.6 eV.
Ch14 Q21. Explain IUPAC nomenclature of organic compounds. Name the following: (i) CH₃–CH₂–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃ (ii) CH₃–CH=CH₂ (iii) HC≡CH (iv) CH₃–CH₂–OH ⭐ Most IMP (4M)
IUPAC Nomenclature Rules:
  • 1. Select the longest continuous carbon chain as the parent chain → gives base name (meth, eth, prop, but, pent...).
  • 2. Number the chain from the end nearest to the substituent/functional group (lowest locant rule).
  • 3. Name substituents as prefixes with their position number.
  • 4. Suffix: alkane (–ane), alkene (–ene), alkyne (–yne), alcohol (–ol).
Naming:
  • (i) CH₃–CH₂–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃: Longest chain = 5C (pentane); CH₃ branch at C3. IUPAC: 3-methylpentane
  • (ii) CH₃–CH=CH₂: 3 carbons, one C=C. IUPAC: propene (prop-1-ene)
  • (iii) HC≡CH: 2 carbons, one C≡C. IUPAC: ethyne (acetylene)
  • (iv) CH₃–CH₂–OH: 2 carbons, –OH group. IUPAC: ethanol
Ch15 Q22. Explain physical and chemical properties of alkanes with equations. Describe cracking and isomerisation. ⭐ IMP Long Answer (4M)
Physical Properties:
  • State: CH₄–C₄H₁₀ are gases; C₅–C₁₇ are liquids; C₁₈+ are solids.
  • BP/MP increases with molecular mass; branched isomers have lower BP.
  • Insoluble in water; soluble in organic solvents; density less than water.
Chemical Properties:
  • 1. Combustion: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + 890 kJ/mol Complete combustion → CO₂ + H₂O; incomplete → CO + soot.
  • 2. Halogenation (Free radical substitution): CH₄ + Cl₂ → (hν) → CH₃Cl + HCl
  • 3. Cracking (Pyrolysis): High alkanes break into smaller molecules at high temperature with catalyst: C₈H₁₈ → (500°C, catalyst) → C₄H₁₀ + C₄H₈ Used in petroleum industry to increase petrol yield.
  • 4. Isomerisation: Straight chain alkanes convert to branched with AlCl₃/HCl: n-butane → (AlCl₃/HCl, Δ) → isobutane (2-methylpropane) Increases octane number of petrol.
Ch4 Q23. Explain the quantum mechanical model of the atom. What are atomic orbitals? Draw and explain s, p and d orbital shapes. ⭐ IMP Long Answer (4M)
Quantum Mechanical Model:
  • Based on Schrödinger equation: Ĥψ = Eψ
  • ψ (wave function) — gives information about electron's state.
  • |ψ|² = probability density — the probability of finding an electron in a small region of space.
  • Electrons do not have fixed paths; they exist in orbitals (probability regions).
  • An orbital is the region where the probability of finding the electron is ≥90%.
Shapes of Orbitals:
  • s orbital (l=0): Spherically symmetric. Non-directional. Size: 1s < 2s < 3s. One s orbital per shell. Capacity: 2e⁻.
  • p orbital (l=1): Dumbbell shaped with two lobes, separated by a nodal plane through the nucleus. Three orientations: pₓ, p_y, p_z (mutually perpendicular, 90° apart). Capacity: 6e⁻ per subshell.
  • d orbital (l=2): Five d-orbitals: d_xy, d_xz, d_yz (four lobes between axes), d_x²-y² (four lobes along axes), d_z² (two lobes along z + ring in xy-plane). Capacity: 10e⁻ per subshell.
📐 Draw: (1) s — sphere around nucleus; (2) p — two-lobed dumbbell in 3 directions; (3) d_xy — four lobes between x and y axes. Label all clearly.
