Ch1 i. What is Avogadro's number? State its value. ⭐ IMP
Avogadro's number (Nₐ) is the number of particles (atoms, molecules, ions) present in one mole of any substance. Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
Ch4 ii. Define an atomic orbital. ⭐ IMP
An atomic orbital is a three-dimensional region of space around the nucleus of an atom where the probability of finding an electron is maximum (≥ 90%). It is defined by the wave function ψ and has a specific energy, shape, size and orientation.
Ch14 iii. What is an electrophile? Give two examples. ⭐ IMP
An electrophile is an electron-deficient species that accepts an electron pair from a nucleophile. Examples: H⁺, Cl⁺, NO₂⁺ (nitronium ion), BF₃, AlCl₃, carbocations (R⁺).
Ch15 iv. What is the Wurtz reaction? Write a general equation. ⭐ IMP
Wurtz reaction: Two moles of alkyl halide react with sodium metal in dry ether to give a higher symmetric alkane. 2R–X + 2Na → (dry ether) → R–R + 2NaX Example: 2CH₃Br + 2Na → C₂H₆ + 2NaBr

Ch1 Q4. Explain Law of Conservation of Mass and Law of Definite Proportions with experimental evidence. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier, 1789):
  • Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass of reactants = total mass of products.
  • Evidence: Landolt (1908) carefully weighed reactants and products in sealed tubes — mass was always equal before and after reaction.
  • Example: AgNO₃ + HCl → AgCl↓ + HNO₃; total mass unchanged.
Law of Definite Proportions (Proust, 1799):
  • A pure compound always contains elements in same fixed mass ratio regardless of source.
  • Evidence: Water prepared by any method — H:O by mass always 1:8. CO₂ from any source always C:O = 3:8.

Ch4 Q8. Explain photoelectric effect. How did Einstein explain it using quantum theory? ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
Photoelectric Effect: Emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency is shone on it.
  • Observations: (1) No emission below threshold frequency ν₀. (2) KE of photoelectrons depends on frequency, not intensity. (3) Number of electrons ∝ intensity. (4) Emission is instantaneous.
Einstein's Explanation (1905):
  • Light consists of discrete packets of energy called photons. Energy of one photon = hν.
  • When a photon hits metal: hν = φ + ½mv²_max
  • φ = hν₀ = work function (minimum energy to remove electron from metal surface).
  • If hν < φ → no emission; if hν > φ → electron emitted with KE = hν – hν₀.
  • This proved particle nature of light and won Einstein the Nobel Prize (1921).
Ch4 Q9. Explain de Broglie's dual nature of matter. Derive the de Broglie wavelength formula. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Dual Nature: In 1924, de Broglie proposed that all matter (not just light) exhibits both particle and wave nature. The wave nature becomes significant for very small particles like electrons.

Derivation:
  • From Einstein's mass-energy relation and Planck's equation:
    E = mc² (energy of photon); E = hν = hc/λ (wave nature)
    ∴ mc = h/λ → λ = h/mc (for photon)
  • For matter particle with velocity v: replacing c with v and using p = mv: λ = h/mv = h/p
  • Larger mass → smaller λ (negligible for macroscopic objects).
  • For electrons (very small mass) → λ is significant and measurable.
  • Verified by Davisson and Germer (1927) — electron diffraction confirmed wave nature.
Ch4 Q10. What is atomic emission spectrum? Explain hydrogen spectrum with energy levels. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Atomic Emission Spectrum: When atoms are given energy (heat/electricity), electrons are excited to higher energy levels. On returning to lower levels, they emit light of specific frequencies → a series of coloured lines at fixed positions. This is the atomic emission spectrum (line spectrum) — unique for each element (like a fingerprint).

Hydrogen Spectrum (Bohr's explanation):
  • When electron jumps from n₂ to n₁ (n₂ > n₁), it emits a photon: hν = E₂ – E₁ = 13.6(1/n₁² – 1/n₂²) eV

Ch4 Q14. State and explain Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. Why is it not applicable to macroscopic objects? ⭐ Most IMP (3M)
Statement: It is fundamentally impossible to simultaneously determine with precision both the position (Δx) and momentum (Δp) of a subatomic particle. Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π  →  Δx · mΔv ≥ h/4π
  • Measuring position of electron requires shining light on it — the photon used disturbs the electron's momentum → uncertainty introduced.
  • The more precisely we know position (smaller Δx) → the less precisely we know momentum (larger Δp) and vice versa.
Not applicable to macroscopic objects:
  • h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s is extremely small. For a macroscopic object (e.g., ball, m = 0.1 kg), the uncertainties in position and velocity are:
    Δv ≥ h/(4πmΔx) ≈ 10⁻³³ m/s — negligibly small.
  • For an electron (m = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg), uncertainties become significant and measurable.

Ch14 Q16. Explain the resonance effect (+M and –M) with examples for each. How does +M affect reactivity of benzene? ⭐ IMP (3M)
Resonance Effect (Mesomeric Effect): Displacement of π-electrons or lone pairs through a conjugated system.
  • +M effect (electron donating): Groups with lone pairs push electrons into the π-system. Increases electron density on ring.
    Groups: –OH, –OR, –NH₂, –NR₂, –Cl, –Br (halogens donate lone pair despite –I)
    Example: In phenol, –OH donates lone pair into benzene ring → ortho and para positions become electron-rich.
  • –M effect (electron withdrawing): Groups with multiple bonds pull electrons away from the π-system.
    Groups: –NO₂, –CHO, –COOH, –C≡N, –CO–
    Example: In nitrobenzene, –NO₂ withdraws electrons → meta position less affected.
  • Effect on benzene reactivity: +M groups activate the ring toward electrophilic substitution (EAS) and direct substituents to ortho/para positions. –M groups deactivate the ring and direct to meta.
Ch14 Q17. Explain functional group isomerism and metamerism with examples for C₂H₆O and C₄H₁₀O. ⭐ IMP (3M)
Functional Group Isomerism (C₂H₆O):
  • Same molecular formula, different functional groups → different chemical properties.
  • Ethanol: CH₃–CH₂–OH (alcohol, –OH group, bp 78°C)
  • Dimethyl ether: CH₃–O–CH₃ (ether, –O– group, bp –24°C)
  • Both have C₂H₆O but completely different functional groups → very different properties.
Metamerism (C₄H₁₀O — ethers):
  • Same functional group, different alkyl groups on either side.
  • Diethyl ether: C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅
  • Methyl propyl ether: CH₃–O–C₃H₇
  • Both have –O– (ether) but different alkyl groups → slightly different properties.
Ch15 Q18. Prepare propane by: (i) Kolbe's electrolysis (ii) Sabatier-Senderens reduction. Write equations. (15.1.3) ⭐ IMP (3M)
(i) Kolbe's Electrolysis:

Ch15 Q19. Explain conformations of ethane (staggered and eclipsed). Which is more stable and why? (15.1.5) ⭐ IMP (3M)
Conformations of Ethane (C₂H₆): Different spatial arrangements of atoms arising due to free rotation about C–C single bond.
  • Staggered conformation: H atoms on one carbon are exactly between H atoms on the other carbon (dihedral angle = 60°). Maximum separation between H–H → minimum torsional strain → most stable.
  • Eclipsed conformation: H atoms on both carbons directly overlap when viewed along C–C axis (dihedral angle = 0°). H atoms on different carbons are as close as possible → maximum torsional (steric) strain → least stable.
Stability: Staggered > Eclipsed
  • Difference in energy ≈ 12.5 kJ/mol (torsional strain of eclipsed).
  • At room temperature, both forms rapidly interconvert (free rotation) → ethane exists mostly in staggered form.

Ch4 Q20. Explain de Broglie's hypothesis in detail. What is its significance? Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through a potential difference of 100 V. (m_e = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) ⭐ Most IMP Long (4M)
de Broglie's Hypothesis:
  • All moving particles have an associated wavelength: λ = h/mv = h/p
  • This predicts that particles exhibit wave nature just as light shows both wave and particle nature.
  • The wavelength is significant only for particles of very small mass (electrons, neutrons).
Significance:
  • Explains Bohr's quantisation condition: mvr = nh/2π → 2πr = nλ → electron orbit is a standing wave.
  • Foundation of quantum mechanics and led to Schrödinger equation.
  • Used in electron microscopy (electrons used instead of light for much higher resolution).
Numerical:
KE of electron = eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 100 = 1.6×10⁻¹⁷ J
KE = ½mv² → v = √(2×KE/m) = √(2×1.6×10⁻¹⁷/9.11×10⁻³¹)
v = √(3.512×10¹³) = 5.93×10⁶ m/s
λ = h/mv = 6.626×10⁻³⁴/(9.11×10⁻³¹ × 5.93×10⁶)
λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴/5.402×10⁻²⁴
λ ≈ 1.23 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 1.23 Å
This wavelength is of the order of X-rays → confirms electrons have significant wave nature.
Ch14 Q21. Explain the different types of organic reactions: addition, substitution, elimination and rearrangement. Give one example and equation for each. ⭐ Most IMP Long (4M)
1. Addition Reaction:
  • A molecule adds across a multiple bond (C=C or C≡C) → single bond product.
  • Example: CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂Br–CH₂Br (bromine addition to ethene)
  • Types: electrophilic addition (HX to alkene), nucleophilic addition (CN⁻ to carbonyl).
2. Substitution Reaction:
  • One atom/group is replaced by another. Atom or group leaves, another enters.
  • Example: CH₄ + Cl₂ → (hν) → CH₃Cl + HCl (free radical substitution)
  • Also: CH₃Br + OH⁻ → CH₃OH + Br⁻ (nucleophilic substitution SN2)
3. Elimination Reaction:
  • Atoms/groups from adjacent carbons are removed → double bond forms.
  • Example: CH₃–CH₂Br + KOH(alc) → CH₂=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O
4. Rearrangement Reaction:
  • Atoms or groups migrate within a molecule → structural isomer formed.
  • Example: 1-butene → 2-butene under acid catalyst (C=C position shifts).
  • Also: n-butane → isobutane under AlCl₃ catalyst.
Ch15 Q22. Describe the preparation of alkanes by all important methods with equations: (i) From alkyl halides (Wurtz) (ii) Grignard reagent + water (iii) Kolbe's electrolysis (iv) Catalytic hydrogenation. ⭐ Most IMP Long (4M)
(i) Wurtz Reaction:
  • 2R–X + 2Na → (dry ether) → R–R + 2NaX
  • Example: 2CH₃Br + 2Na → C₂H₆ + 2NaBr (ethane from methyl bromide)
  • Use: Preparation of symmetric alkanes with higher carbon number.
(ii) Grignard Reagent + Water:
  • Grignard reagent (R–Mg–X) reacts with water/dilute acid to give alkane.
  • Step 1: R–X + Mg → (dry ether) → R–Mg–X
  • Step 2: R–Mg–X + H₂O → R–H + Mg(OH)X
  • Example: CH₃MgBr + H₂O → CH₄ + Mg(OH)Br
(iii) Kolbe's Electrolysis:
  • Electrolysis of sodium salt of carboxylic acid → alkane.
  • 2RCOONa + 2H₂O → (electrolysis) → R–R + 2CO₂ + H₂ + 2NaOH
  • Example: 2CH₃COONa → C₂H₆ + 2CO₂ (ethane from sodium acetate)
(iv) Catalytic Hydrogenation (Sabatier-Senderens):
  • Alkene or alkyne + H₂ with Ni/Pt catalyst at 300°C:
  • CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → (Ni, Δ) → CH₃–CH₃ (ethane)
  • CH≡CH + 2H₂ → (Ni, Δ) → CH₃–CH₃ (from acetylene)
Ch4 Q23. Explain the concept of quantum numbers in detail. Give all four quantum numbers, their symbols, values and significance. What are the quantum numbers for the electrons in 3d subshell? ⭐ Most IMP Long (4M)
Quantum Numbers: A set of 4 numbers that completely describe the state of an electron in an atom (its energy, shape, orientation and spin).
Q. NumberSymbolAllowed ValuesSignificance
Principaln1, 2, 3, 4…Shell number; determines energy (main) and size of orbital. Higher n = higher energy = larger orbital.
Azimuthal (Angular momentum)l0 to (n–1)Subshell; shape of orbital. l=0:s, l=1:p, l=2:d, l=3:f. Number of orbitals in subshell = 2l+1.

Quantum numbers for 3d subshell electrons:
  • n = 3, l = 2 (d subshell)
  • mₗ values: –2, –1, 0, +1, +2 (5 orbitals)
  • mₛ = +½ or –½ for each orbital (2 electrons per orbital)
  • Total capacity = 5 × 2 = 10 electrons
Example for one electron in 3d: n=3, l=2, mₗ=0, mₛ=+½ → completely specified.
