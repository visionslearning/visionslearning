Conjunctions
TypeWordsUse
Co-ordinatingand, but, or, nor, for, yet, soJoins two equal clauses. She sings and dances.

⏰ 5. Tenses — All 12 Class 9
Present Tenses
TenseStructureSignal WordsExample

✍️ 6. Types of Sentences Class 9
TypeMeaningExample
Assertive / DeclarativeStates a fact (positive or negative)The sun rises in the east. / She did not come.
InterrogativeAsks a questionWhere are you going? / Did he come?
ImperativeGives order, request or advicePlease sit down. / Don't make noise.
ExclamatoryExpresses strong emotionWhat a beautiful day! / How brave he is!
OptativeExpresses wish or prayerMay you live long! / God bless you!
Based on Structure:
Simple Sentence — one subject + one predicate (one clause). She went home.
Compound Sentence — two or more main clauses joined by co-ordinating conjunction (and/but/or/so). She studied hard so she passed.
Complex Sentence — one main clause + one or more subordinate clauses. Although it rained, the match continued.
🔄 7. Active & Passive Voice Class 9
4-Step Method to Change Active → Passive
Step 1: Object of active sentence → New Subject of passive sentence.
Step 2: Choose the correct form of be (is/am/are/was/were/has been/have been/will be…) based on tense.
Step 3: Change the main verb → V3 (past participle).
Step 4: Subject of active → "by + object pronoun" at the end.
Passive Voice — All Tenses
TenseActivePassive
Simple PresentShe writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
Simple PastHe ate the food.The food was eaten by him.
Simple FutureThey will do the work.The work will be done by them.
Present ContinuousShe is writing a letter.A letter is being written by her.
Past ContinuousHe was eating food.Food was being eaten by him.
Present PerfectShe has written a letter.A letter has been written by her.
Past PerfectHe had finished the work.The work had been finished by him.
ImperativeOpen the door.Let the door be opened. / You are requested to open the door.
Pronoun Changes — Active to Passive
Active Subject→ Passive "by ___"Active Subject→ Passive "by ___"
Iby meWeby us
Heby himSheby her
Theyby themYouby you
⚠️ Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be made passive. Intransitive verbs like run, sleep, arrive, die CANNOT be made passive.
🗣️ 8. Direct & Indirect Speech Class 9
Rules for Changing Direct → Indirect (Narration)
Step 1: Remove inverted commas. Use the correct joining word (that / to / if / whether / wh- word).
Step 2: Change pronouns — 1st person acc. to subject of reporting verb; 2nd person acc. to object; 3rd person stays same.
Step 3: Backshift the tense (if reporting verb is past).
Step 4: Change time and place words.

⭐ Board Exam Tips — Class 9: In Tenses, always find the signal word first. In Passive, follow the 4 steps. In Narration, identify the sentence type before changing. Capital letter rule: proper nouns, start of sentence, pronoun 'I' — always capitals.

Pronoun Change Rule:
📌 1st person (I/we/me/us/my/our) → changes acc. to subject of reporting verb.

🔗 4. Types of Clauses Class 10
Clause TypeFunctionIntroduced byExample
Noun ClauseWorks as subject or object of main verbthat, what, who, which, when, where, how, whether, ifI know that she is honest. / What he said was wrong.
Adjective Clause (Relative)Describes / modifies a noun or pronounwho, whom, whose, which, thatThe girl who scored highest won. / The book that I bought is torn.
Adverb Clause of TimeTells when the action happenedwhen, while, after, before, since, until, as soon asShe left before he arrived.
Adverb Clause of CauseTells reason / causebecause, since, asHe failed because he was careless.
Adverb Clause of ConditionTells under what conditionif, unless, provided that, in caseI will come if you invite me.
Adverb Clause of ContrastShows contrast / concessionalthough, though, even though, whereasShe smiled although she was sad.
Adverb Clause of ResultShows result / effectso…that, such…thatHe was so tired that he slept at once.
⭐ Quick Test: Noun clause → can replace with "it/this". Adjective clause → identifies which noun? Adverb clause → answers when/where/why/how/under what condition?
✂️ 5. Transformation of Sentences Class 10
1. Affirmative ↔ Negative (same meaning)
He is always late. → He is never early.
All students passed. → Not a single student failed.
She is too weak to run. → She is so weak that she cannot run.
As soon as he came, she left. → No sooner had he come than she left.
Both Ravi and Raj came. → Neither Ravi nor Raj was absent.
2. Assertive ↔ Interrogative (same meaning)
Everyone loves their motherland. → Who does not love their motherland?
Nobody can deny this truth. → Can anybody deny this truth?
He is the best player. → Is he not the best player?
3. Simple ↔ Compound ↔ Complex
Simple: Despite being sick, she attended school.
Compound: She was sick but she attended school.
Complex: Although she was sick, she attended school.
4. Exclamatory ↔ Assertive
Exclamatory: What a beautiful garden! → Assertive: It is a very beautiful garden.
Exclamatory: How bravely he fought! → Assertive: He fought very bravely.
Rule: What + a/an + adj + noun → It is a very + adj + noun. | How + adj/adv → S + V + very + adj/adv.
5. Too…to → So…that
He is too weak to walk. → He is so weak that he cannot walk.
She is too young to drive. → She is so young that she cannot drive.

Transforming Degrees — Same Meaning:
Superlative: Sachin is the greatest batsman in India.
Comparative: Sachin is greater than any other batsman in India.
Positive: No other batsman in India is as great as Sachin.

Pattern: Superlative → "more/___er than any other [noun]" | Positive → "No other [noun] is as ___ as [subject]"
🎯 8. Conditional Sentences Class 10
TypeMeaningIf-clause (condition)Main clause (result)Example
Type 0 (Zero)Universal truth / Scientific factSimple PresentSimple PresentIf you heat water to 100°C, it boils.
Type 1 (Real)Possible future condition (likely)Simple Presentwill + V1If it rains, we will stay home.
Type 2 (Unreal Present)Imaginary / unlikely present situationSimple Pastwould + V1If I were rich, I would help the poor.
Type 3 (Unreal Past)Imaginary past — expressing regretPast Perfectwould have + V3If he had studied, he would have passed.
⚠️ In Type 2, always use "were" (not "was") for all persons in formal/exam writing. If I were you… / If she were here… | Never use "will" in the if-clause of Type 1.
❌ 9. Common Errors to Avoid Class 10
❌ WrongHe didn't went.
✅ RightHe didn't go. (use V1 after did not)
❌ WrongShe is more taller.
✅ RightShe is taller. (never double comparative)
❌ WrongI have seen him yesterday.
✅ RightI saw him yesterday. (yesterday = simple past)
❌ WrongHe suggested me to go.
✅ RightHe suggested that I should go.
❌ WrongAlthough he was tired, but he worked.
✅ RightAlthough he was tired, he worked.
❌ WrongOne of my friend.
✅ RightOne of my friends. (use plural after "one of")
❌ WrongThe news are good.
✅ RightThe news is good. (news is always singular)
❌ WrongHe is more intelligent than any student.
✅ RightHe is more intelligent than any other student.
