✍️ 6. Types of Sentences Class 9
TypeMeaningExample
Assertive / DeclarativeStates a fact (positive or negative)The sun rises in the east. / She did not come.
InterrogativeAsks a questionWhere are you going? / Did he come?
ImperativeGives order, request or advicePlease sit down. / Don't make noise.
ExclamatoryExpresses strong emotionWhat a beautiful day! / How brave he is!
OptativeExpresses wish or prayerMay you live long! / God bless you!
Based on Structure:
Simple Sentence — one subject + one predicate (one clause). She went home.
Compound Sentence — two or more main clauses joined by co-ordinating conjunction (and/but/or/so). She studied hard so she passed.
Complex Sentence — one main clause + one or more subordinate clauses. Although it rained, the match continued.
🔄 7. Active & Passive Voice Class 9
4-Step Method to Change Active → Passive
Step 1: Object of active sentence → New Subject of passive sentence.
Step 2: Choose the correct form of be (is/am/are/was/were/has been/have been/will be…) based on tense.
Step 3: Change the main verb → V3 (past participle).
Step 4: Subject of active → "by + object pronoun" at the end.
Passive Voice — All Tenses

Simple PastHe ate the food.The food was eaten by him.
Simple FutureThey will do the work.The work will be done by them.
Present ContinuousShe is writing a letter.A letter is being written by her.
Past ContinuousHe was eating food.Food was being eaten by him.
Present PerfectShe has written a letter.A letter has been written by her.
Past PerfectHe had finished the work.The work had been finished by him.
ImperativeOpen the door.Let the door be opened. / You are requested to open the door.

⚠️ Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be made passive. Intransitive verbs like run, sleep, arrive, die CANNOT be made passive.
🗣️ 8. Direct & Indirect Speech Class 9
Rules for Changing Direct → Indirect (Narration)
Step 1: Remove inverted commas. Use the correct joining word (that / to / if / whether / wh- word).
Step 2: Change pronouns — 1st person acc. to subject of reporting verb; 2nd person acc. to object; 3rd person stays same.
Step 3: Backshift the tense (if reporting verb is past).
Step 4: Change time and place words.

✂️ 5. Transformation of Sentences Class 10
1. Affirmative ↔ Negative (same meaning)
He is always late. → He is never early.
All students passed. → Not a single student failed.
She is too weak to run. → She is so weak that she cannot run.
As soon as he came, she left. → No sooner had he come than she left.
Both Ravi and Raj came. → Neither Ravi nor Raj was absent.
2. Assertive ↔ Interrogative (same meaning)
Everyone loves their motherland. → Who does not love their motherland?
Nobody can deny this truth. → Can anybody deny this truth?
He is the best player. → Is he not the best player?
3. Simple ↔ Compound ↔ Complex
Simple: Despite being sick, she attended school.
Compound: She was sick but she attended school.
Complex: Although she was sick, she attended school.
4. Exclamatory ↔ Assertive
Exclamatory: What a beautiful garden! → Assertive: It is a very beautiful garden.
Exclamatory: How bravely he fought! → Assertive: He fought very bravely.
Rule: What + a/an + adj + noun → It is a very + adj + noun. | How + adj/adv → S + V + very + adj/adv.
5. Too…to → So…that
He is too weak to walk. → He is so weak that he cannot walk.
She is too young to drive. → She is so young that she cannot drive.

📋 All Figures of Speech — Quick Reference

⭐ Board Exam Tip: When asked to identify a figure of speech — first look for the key signal: like/as = Simile | is/are (comparison) = Metaphor | human quality to non-human = Personification | same start sound = Alliteration | exaggeration = Hyperbole | opposites together = Oxymoron | sound word = Onomatopoeia.
1. Simile Class 9 & 10
Definition: A figure of speech that makes a comparison between two unlike things using the words like or as.
Purpose: Makes descriptions vivid and imaginative.
ExampleWhat is compared
"Her cheeks are like roses."Cheeks compared to roses (rosy, beautiful)
"He is as brave as a lion."His bravery compared to a lion's
"The stars shone like diamonds."Stars compared to diamonds (bright, sparkling)
"She sings like a nightingale."Her singing compared to a nightingale
"Life is like a race."Life compared to a race
⚠️ Simile vs Metaphor: "She is like a rose." = Simile (uses like). "She is a rose." = Metaphor (direct, no like/as).
2. Metaphor Class 9 & 10
Definition: A direct comparison between two unlike things, stating one thing IS another — without using like or as.
Purpose: Creates a strong, vivid image; more powerful than a simile.
ExampleMeaning
"Life is a journey."Life has stages, ups/downs, a destination — like a journey
"The world is a stage."People play roles in life like actors on stage
"Time is money."Time is as precious and valuable as money
"He has a heart of stone."He is cold, unfeeling and hard-hearted
"Books are mirrors of the soul."Books reflect inner thoughts and feelings
"The mind is an ocean."The mind is vast, deep and full of hidden things
3. Personification Class 9 & 10
Definition: Giving human qualities, feelings or actions to non-human things (animals, objects, ideas, nature).
Key signal: A non-human thing is doing something only humans do.
ExampleHuman quality given to
"The sun smiled down on us."Sun — smiling (human expression)
"The wind whispered through the trees."Wind — whispering (human speech)
"Opportunity knocked at the door."Opportunity — knocking (human action)
"The flowers nodded their heads."Flowers — nodding (human gesture)
"Death crept up on him silently."Death — creeping (human movement)
"The sea roared in anger."Sea — anger (human emotion)
4. Alliteration Class 9 & 10
Definition: Repetition of the same initial consonant sound in two or more nearby words.
Purpose: Creates a musical rhythm; used in poetry for sound effect.
ExampleRepeated sound
"She sells sea shells by the sea shore."S sound
"Peter Piper picked a peck."P sound
"The fair breeze flew, the foam flew."F sound
"Big black bugs bleed blue blood."B sound
⭐ Note: Alliteration is about sound, not spelling. "King" and "Cat" alliterate (both 'k' sound), but "Cellar" and "Ship" do not (different sounds despite C/S start).
5. Hyperbole Class 9 & 10
Definition: A figure of speech that uses deliberate exaggeration (overstatement) for emphasis or effect. Not meant to be taken literally.
Purpose: Creates strong emphasis or a humorous effect.
ExampleWhat it really means
"I've told you a million times."I've told you many times (not literally a million)
"I'm so hungry I could eat a horse."I'm very hungry
"He ran faster than the wind."He ran very fast
"Her smile could light up the world."She has a very bright, beautiful smile
"I have a mountain of homework."I have a lot of homework
6. Oxymoron Class 9 & 10
Definition: A figure of speech in which two contradictory or opposite terms are placed together to create a special effect.
Purpose: Highlights paradox; shows complexity of an idea.
OxymoronMeaning / Effect
"Deafening silence"A silence so total it feels overwhelming
"Sweet sorrow"Parting that is both painful and tender
"Living death"A state of existence that feels like death
"Bitter sweet"Something that is both sad and pleasant
"Cruel kindness"Harsh treatment given out of good intention
"Dark light"A light that illuminates darkness but feels grim
7. Onomatopoeia Class 9 & 10
Definition: Words that imitate or suggest the natural sound of the thing they describe.
Purpose: Makes writing lively; the reader can almost hear the sound.
Example SentenceSound Word
"The bees buzzed around the flowers."buzzed
"The clock ticked loudly."ticked
"The fire crackled in the fireplace."crackled
"The stream murmured gently."murmured
"The thunder rumbled in the distance."rumbled
"The snake hissed."hissed
8. Apostrophe (Figure of Speech) Class 9 & 10
Definition: Directly addressing an absent or dead person, an abstract idea, or an inanimate object as if it could hear and respond.
Key signal: Often starts with "O" or the thing being addressed is non-present.
ExampleWhat is addressed
"O Death, where is thy sting?"Death (abstract, personified)
"O Solitude! where are the charms?"Solitude (abstract idea)
"Milton! thou shouldst be living at this hour."Milton — dead poet
"O Nature! I do not aspire to be the highest."Nature (inanimate)
⚠️ Do not confuse with punctuation apostrophe ('). In grammar, apostrophe (') is a punctuation mark. As a figure of speech, Apostrophe means addressing something/someone absent.
9. Irony Class 9 & 10
Definition: A figure of speech in which the literal meaning is opposite to the intended meaning. The words say one thing but mean another.
Types: Verbal Irony (saying opposite of what you mean) | Dramatic Irony (audience knows what character doesn't) | Situational Irony (outcome opposite to what is expected).
ExampleActual Meaning
"What lovely weather!" (said during a storm)The weather is terrible
"Oh great, I failed again." It is not great at all — it is bad
"As clear as mud."Very confusing, not clear at all
"A fire station burns down."Situational irony — the expected protector is destroyed
10. Antithesis Class 9 & 10
Definition: A figure of speech in which two contrasting or opposite ideas are placed side by side in a grammatically parallel structure.
Purpose: Creates emphasis through contrast; makes a statement memorable.
ExampleContrast
"To err is human, to forgive divine."human vs divine
"Man proposes, God disposes."man's plan vs God's will
"Give every man thy ear, but few thy voice."listening vs speaking
"It was the best of times, it was the worst of times."best vs worst
"Speech is silver, silence is golden."silver vs golden, speech vs silence
11. Repetition / Anaphora Class 9 & 10
Repetition: Deliberate use of the same word or phrase more than once for emphasis or effect.
Anaphora: Repetition of a word/phrase at the beginning of successive lines or clauses.
Epistrophe: Repetition at the end of successive clauses.
ExampleType
"We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the seas, we shall fight in the air."Anaphora — "we shall fight" repeated at start
"The horror, the horror."Simple Repetition for emphasis
"Government of the people, by the people, for the people."Epistrophe — "the people" repeated at end
12. Rhyme & Rhythm Class 9 & 10
Rhyme: Repetition of similar end sounds in lines of poetry.
End Rhyme: Last words of lines rhyme. Ex: "Twinkle twinkle little star / How I wonder what you are."
Internal Rhyme: Words within the same line rhyme. Ex: "Once upon a midnight dreary, while I pondered weak and weary."

Rhythm: The pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of poetry — gives it a musical beat.
Common meters: Iambic (da-DUM), Trochaic (DUM-da), Anapestic (da-da-DUM)
⭐ Rhyme Scheme: Pattern labeled with letters. Ex: ABAB (alternate rhyme) | AABB (couplet) | ABBA (enclosed rhyme). In board exams you may be asked: "What is the rhyme scheme of the poem?"

1. Formal Letter Class 9 Class 10
Types: Letter of complaint · Letter of request · Letter to editor · Letter to authority (Principal, Municipal Officer, etc.) · Application for job/leave
Format of a Formal Letter
Sender's Address:   House No., Street, City – Pin code
Date:                  DD Month YYYY

Receiver's Address:  The Principal / The Editor / The Officer
                             Name of school / Newspaper / Office
                             Address

Subject:               Regarding ___________________

Salutation:            Respected Sir / Dear Sir or Madam

Body:
  Para 1 — Introduction / Purpose of the letter
  Para 2 — Main content / Details / Complaint / Request
  Para 3 — Conclusion / What you expect / Hope for action

Closing:             Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely
Signature:            (Your full name)
⭐ Yours faithfully — when salutation is "Dear Sir/Madam" (name not known). Yours sincerely — when you address by name (Dear Mr. Sharma). Never use "Yours lovingly" in formal letters.
Sample Opening Lines:
• "I beg to state that…" / "I am writing to draw your attention to…"
• "I wish to bring to your kind notice that…"
• "Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to…" (Letter to Editor)
Sample Closing Lines:
• "I hope you will take necessary action at the earliest."
• "I shall be highly obliged for the same."
2. Informal Letter Class 9 Class 10
Written to: Friend, relative, classmate, cousin, pen-pal — people you know personally.
Tone: Friendly, warm, conversational — not too formal.
Format of an Informal Letter
Sender's Address:   Your Address
Date:                  DD Month YYYY

Salutation:            Dear [Name] / My dear [Name]

Body:
  Para 1 — Opening / Hope you are well
  Para 2 — Main content / What you want to say
  Para 3 — Closing / Best wishes / Regards

Closing:             Yours lovingly / With love / Yours affectionately
Signature:            Your name (first name only, usually)
⭐ No receiver's address in informal letter. No subject line. Use Yours lovingly / affectionately / sincerely — choose based on relationship. Never "Yours faithfully" for friends.
3. Essay Writing Class 9 Class 10
Types of Essays:
• Descriptive — describes a person, place or thing in detail
• Narrative — tells a story or describes an event
• Expository / Informative — gives information and explains a topic
• Argumentative / Persuasive — argues for or against a point of view
Structure of an Essay
PartWhat to WriteLength
IntroductionDefine the topic; state your main idea; hook the reader with a quote or question1 paragraph
BodyDevelop each main point in a separate paragraph; use examples and facts; connect ideas with linking words2–3 paragraphs
ConclusionSummarise the main points; give your final opinion; end with a strong closing sentence1 paragraph
Useful Linking Words:
To add: Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Also, Besides
To contrast: However, On the other hand, Nevertheless, Although, Despite
To conclude: In conclusion, To sum up, Finally, Therefore, Thus
To give examples: For instance, For example, Such as, To illustrate
⭐ Board Exam Tips: Write in paragraphs — never as bullet points. Aim for 200–250 words (Class 9) / 250–300 words (Class 10). Use a variety of sentence structures. Avoid repetition of same word — use synonyms.
4. Notice Writing Class 9 Class 10
Purpose: To announce an event, inform about a change, issue a warning, or give instructions to a specific group of people.
Key features: Short, clear, formal, specific. Written in third person. No personal pronouns (I/we).
Format of a Notice
NAME OF SCHOOL / ORGANISATION
NOTICE
Date: DD/MM/YYYY

HEADING / TITLE IN CAPITALS

Body of the notice — Who, What, When, Where, Why.
Keep it brief. All essential details must be mentioned.

Name of Issuing Authority
Designation (Secretary / Head Boy / Principal)
⭐ Must include: Who (issuing authority) · What (event/information) · When (date & time) · Where (venue) · Why (purpose if needed). Box the notice in board exams. Word limit: 50–80 words.
5. Report Writing Class 10
Purpose: To give a factual account of an event that has already taken place (past event). Written in third person, passive voice, past tense.
Types: Newspaper Report · School Event Report
Format of a Report
HEADLINE (in bold/capitals — summarises the event)
By [Your Name] / Our Staff Reporter
Place, Date — (City, DD Month YYYY)

Opening paragraph: What happened? When? Where? Who was involved?
Middle paragraphs: Details — How did it happen? Key highlights. Quotes from participants/witnesses.
Closing paragraph: Outcome/conclusion. Future plans if any.
Language to use in reports:
"The event was inaugurated by…" / "A large number of students participated in…"
"It was reported that…" / "According to the sources…"
"The programme concluded with…" / "Prizes were distributed by…"
⭐ Use passive voice and past tense throughout. Never use "I" — use "this reporter" if needed. Include 5 W's + H: Who, What, When, Where, Why, How. Word limit: 100–150 words.
6. Story Writing Class 9 Class 10
Types in board exam:
• Story from a given beginning / outline / hints
• Story based on a moral (proverb given)
Structure of a Story
PartContent
TitleGive a creative, relevant title
IntroductionIntroduce characters, setting (time & place), situation
Rising ActionBuild the problem or conflict; keep reader engaged
ClimaxThe turning point — most exciting/tense moment
ResolutionHow the problem is solved
Moral (if asked)Write clearly: "Moral: Honesty is the best policy."
⭐ Tips: Use vivid, descriptive language. Give characters names. Use dialogues to make the story lively. Write in past tense. Stick to word limit (150–200 words). End with a clear moral if asked. Avoid abrupt or unfinished endings.
7. Speech Writing Class 9 Class 10
Purpose: To write a speech to be delivered on a specific occasion — school assembly, debate, prize distribution, etc.
Tone: Formal but engaging. Address the audience directly.
Format of a Speech
Title / Occasion

Salutation: Respected Principal, dear teachers and my fellow students…

Introduction: Good morning everyone. Today I stand before you to speak on… / It is my honour to…

Body:
  Main points — organised in paragraphs
  Use facts, examples, quotes
  Address audience: "As you all know…" / "Friends, let us consider…"

Conclusion: I would like to conclude by saying… / Let us all pledge to…
Thank you.
⭐ Always start with a greeting. Use rhetorical questions to engage audience: "Have you ever wondered…?" Use "we" and "our" to include audience. End with a call to action or inspiring thought. Word limit: 150–200 words.
8. Email & Telegram Class 9 Class 10
Format of an Email
To: recipient@email.com
From: sender@email.com
Subject: Brief description of purpose

Salutation: Dear Sir / Dear [Name]

Body: (same as formal/informal letter — keep concise)

Closing: Regards / Yours sincerely
Name + Designation
Format of a Telegram
Telegrams are brief, urgent messages. No articles (a/an/the), no conjunctions, minimum words — charged per word.
Write in CAPITAL LETTERS.

Example: "REACHED SAFELY. TRAIN DELAYED BY TWO HOURS. WILL CALL TONIGHT. — RAVI"
Compare with: "I have reached safely. The train was delayed by two hours. I will call tonight." → Condensed to 10 words from 19.
⭐ Telegram tips: Remove all articles, auxiliary verbs, conjunctions possible. Keep only key words. Should be clear despite brevity. Word limit usually given (10–15 words).
