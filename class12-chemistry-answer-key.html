SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10
(i) Unit of rate constant for 2nd order reaction
Answer: (B) L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
Rate = k[A]² → k = Rate/[A]² = (mol L⁻¹s⁻¹)/(mol L⁻¹)² k = L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
(ii) Lyophilic colloid
Answer: (C) Starch sol
Lyophilic = solvent-loving. Starch, gelatin, gum, proteins form lyophilic colloids. Gold sol, AS₂S₃, AgI are lyophobic (solvent-hating) colloids.
(iii) Standard electrode potential of SHE
Answer: (C) 0 V
Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is the reference electrode. Its potential is arbitrarily taken as exactly 0 V at 25°C.
(iv) IUPAC name of (CH₃)₂CHCHO
Answer: (A) 2-methylpropanal
Structure: CH₃−CH(CH₃)−CHO Longest chain with CHO = 3 carbons (propan-1-al) Methyl group at C-2 IUPAC name: 2-methylpropanal
(v) Bulletproof glass polymer
Answer: (B) Lexan
Lexan is polycarbonate resin — tough, transparent, used in bulletproof glass.
(vi) p-Aminobenzene sulphonic acid = ?
Answer: (A) Sulphanilic acid
p-Aminobenzene sulphonic acid is commonly called sulphanilic acid. Used in dye manufacture.
(vii) Moles of electrons per Faraday
Answer: (C) 1
1 Faraday = 96500 C = charge of 1 mole of electrons = 1 mole of electrons
(viii) NOT a reducing sugar
Answer: (C) Sucrose
Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar — it has no free aldehyde or ketone group (both anomeric carbons are involved in glycosidic bond). Glucose, maltose, lactose are reducing sugars (have free −CHO group).
(ix) Isomerism between [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Cl
Answer: (B) Ionisation isomerism
These two compounds have same formula but give different ions in solution. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ → gives SO₄²⁻ in solution; [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Cl → gives Cl⁻ in solution. This is ionisation isomerism
(x) Gas from Na + ethanol
Answer: (B) H₂
2Na + 2C₂H₅OH → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑ Gas evolved: Hydrogen (H₂)
SECTION B — Very Short Answers 1 Mark Each
(i) Define mole fraction.
Mole fraction of a component is the ratio of the number of moles of that component to the total number of moles of all components in the solution. xA = nA/(nA + nB) Sum of all mole fractions = 1. It is dimensionless.
(ii) Activation energy and effect of catalyst
Activation energy (Ea) is the minimum extra energy that reactant molecules must possess to overcome the energy barrier and form products. A catalyst provides an alternate reaction pathway with lower activation energy. Catalyst lowers Ea → increases rate of reaction
(iii) Henry's law
Henry's Law: The mass (or mole fraction) of a gas dissolved in a liquid at constant temperature is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid. p = KH × x where KH = Henry's law constant (increases with temperature), x = mole fraction of dissolved gas.
(iv) Tyndall effect
The scattering of a beam of light by colloidal particles is called the Tyndall effect. When a beam of light passes through a colloid, the colloidal particles scatter light in all directions, making the path visible. Observed in: colloids (not in true solutions). Example: beam of light in dusty room.
(v) Standard electrode potential
Standard electrode potential is the potential of an electrode measured under standard conditions — temperature 25°C, ion concentration 1 M, pressure 1 atm — with respect to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (E° = 0 V). Unit: Volts (V). More positive E° → stronger oxidising agent.
(vi) Order of reaction
The order of a reaction is the sum of the powers of the concentration terms in the rate law expression. For: Rate = k[A]^m [B]^n → Order = m + n It is experimentally determined and can be 0, 1, 2, or a fraction.
(vii) IUPAC name of CH₃COCH₂CH₃
CH₃−CO−CH₂−CH₃ = butanone (methylethylketone) Longest chain = 4 carbons; keto group at C-2 IUPAC name: Butan-2-one
(viii) Define saponification
Saponification is the alkaline hydrolysis of an ester to form a soap (sodium or potassium salt of a fatty acid) and glycerol. Fat/Oil + NaOH → Soap (sodium salt of fatty acid) + Glycerol RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH
(ix) Biodegradable polymers
Biodegradable polymers are those which can be broken down by microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) in the environment. Examples: PHBV (Poly-β-hydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate), Nylon-2-Nylon-6, PLA (polylactic acid)
(x) Coordination number
Coordination number is the total number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal atom/ion in a complex compound. Example: In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, each CN⁻ donates 1 electron pair → coordination number = 6
(xi) Glycoside linkage
Glycoside linkage (glycosidic bond) is the acetal linkage formed between two monosaccharide units when the —OH of the anomeric carbon of one sugar reacts with an —OH of another sugar with the elimination of water. Example: In sucrose, C₁ of glucose is linked to C₂ of fructose by an α-1,2-glycosidic bond.
(xii) Kohlrausch's law
Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions: At infinite dilution, each ion migrates independently and contributes a fixed value to the molar conductivity regardless of the nature of the other ion. Λ°m = ν₊λ°₊ + ν₋λ°₋ where λ°₊ and λ°₋ are limiting molar conductivities of cation and anion.
SECTION C — Short Answer I (2 marks each) Any 8 of 12
(i) Integrated rate law for first-order reaction
For a first-order reaction: A → Products Rate = −d[A]/dt = k[A] Rearranging: d[A]/[A] = −k dt Integrating both sides: ∫ d[A]/[A] = −k ∫ dt ln[A] = −kt + C At t=0, [A]=[A]₀, so C = ln[A]₀ ln([A]₀/[A]) = kt or k = (2.303/t) log([A]₀/[A]) This is the integrated rate law for first-order reaction.
(iii) Raoult's law
Raoult's Law: The partial vapour pressure of each component in an ideal solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction. pA = p°A × xA where p°A = vapour pressure of pure component A, xA = mole fraction of A Total pressure: P = pA + pB = p°AxA + p°BxB Ideal solution obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations. ΔHmix = 0, ΔVmix = 0
(xii) Elevation of boiling point — ΔTb = Kb × m
When a non-volatile solute is dissolved in a solvent, the boiling point of the solution is higher than that of the pure solvent. This rise is called elevation of boiling point. The elevation is proportional to molality m: ΔTb = Kb × m where Kb = ebullioscopic constant (boiling point elevation constant of solvent) m = molality = moles of solute / kg of solvent Kb for water = 0.52 K·kg/mol
SECTION D — Short Answer II (3 marks each) Any 6 of 10
(i) First-order rate constant k = (2.303/t) log(a/a−x) + half-life
For first-order: A → Products; initial conc. = a; conc. at time t = (a−x) k = (2.303/t) × log(a/(a−x)) Half-life (t₁/₂): when (a−x) = a/2 k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log(a/(a/2)) = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log 2 k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × 0.3010 t₁/₂ = 0.693/k Half-life of first-order reaction is independent of initial concentration — characteristic property
(iii) Molar conductivity, equivalent conductivity, specific conductivity
Specific Conductivity (κ): Conductance of 1 cm³ of solution placed between electrodes 1 cm apart, area 1 cm². κ = G × l/A   Unit: S·cm⁻¹ Molar Conductivity (Λm): Conductance of all ions produced by one mole of electrolyte in V cm³ of solution. Λm = κ × 1000/M   Unit: S·cm²·mol⁻¹ Equivalent Conductivity (Λeq): Conductance of one gram equivalent of electrolyte in V cm³. Λeq = κ × 1000/N   Unit: S·cm²·eq⁻¹
(vi) Depression in freezing point — ΔTf = Kf × m
When a non-volatile solute is dissolved in a solvent, the freezing point of the solution is lower than the pure solvent. This lowering is called depression of freezing point. ΔTf = Kf × m Kf = cryoscopic constant (Kf for water = 1.86 K·kg/mol) Determination of molecular mass: m = ΔTf/Kf = (w₂ × 1000)/(M₂ × w₁) M₂ = (Kf × w₂ × 1000) / (ΔTf × w₁)
SECTION E — Long Answers (4 marks each) Any 4 of 6
(ii) Electrolysis + Faraday's laws + Numerical (Cu deposition)
Faraday's First Law: The mass of substance deposited/dissolved at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed. m = ZQ = ZIt Faraday's Second Law: When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes, masses deposited are in the ratio of their chemical equivalents. m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂ Numerical: CuSO₄ electrolysis, Q = 0.5 F At cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu 2 Faraday deposits 63.5 g of Cu 0.5 F deposits = (63.5 × 0.5) / 2 = 63.5/4 Mass of Cu = 15.875 g ≈ 15.9 g
(vi) Osmosis + Van't Hoff's law + Numerical
Osmosis: The spontaneous flow of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to higher solute concentration. Osmotic Pressure (π): The minimum pressure that must be applied to stop osmosis. Van't Hoff's Law: π = CRT = (n/V)RT π = CRT (for dilute solutions) Numerical: 6 g urea (M=60), 100 g water Moles of urea = 6/60 = 0.1 mol Moles of water = 100/18 = 5.556 mol (1) Mole fraction of urea = 0.1 / (0.1 + 5.556) = 0.1/5.656 xᵤᵣₑₐ = 0.0177 (2) Molality = moles of solute / kg of solvent = 0.1 / 0.1 m = 1 mol/kg
