What is a choropleth map?
1
∴ A choropleth map is a distributional map that uses different shades or colours to represent the intensity or density of a phenomenon within administrative boundaries.
3.2
What is a volcano?
1
∴ A volcano is an opening or vent in the Earth's crust through which molten rock (lava), gases, ash, and steam escape from the interior of the Earth to its surface.
3.3
What is weathering?
1
∴ Weathering is the process of breaking down and decomposition of rocks at or near the Earth's surface by physical, chemical, or biological agents without transportation of material.
3.4
What is the hydrological cycle?
1
∴ The hydrological cycle (water cycle) is the continuous circulation of water between the ocean, atmosphere, and land through processes of evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff.
3.5
What are tides?
1
∴ Tides are the periodic rise and fall of sea water level caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on the Earth's water bodies.
Q.4 — Short note (any ONE)[4 Marks]
4A
Short note: Exogenetic Processes / Work of Glaciers
4
Definition
Glaciers are large masses of moving ice that erode, transport, and deposit material, creating distinctive landforms.
Erosion features
U-shaped valley: Glacier widens and deepens a river valley into broad U-shape. Cirque: Hollow/armchair-shaped depression where glacier originates. Arête: Sharp ridge between two cirques.
Deposition features
Moraine: Ridge of rocks/debris deposited by glacier. Types: lateral (sides), terminal (front), medial (middle). Drumlin: Smooth, elongated hill of glacial deposit.
Examples
Himalayan glaciers — Gangotri, Siachen. Swiss Alps glaciers. Fjords of Norway.
∴ Glacier = erosion (U-valley, Cirque) + deposition (Moraine, Drumlin)
4B
Short note: Ocean Currents (Alternative)
4
Definition
Ocean currents are large-scale horizontal movements of sea water in a definite direction, driven by wind, temperature, salinity differences, and Earth's rotation.
Types
Warm currents: Flow from tropics towards poles (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio). Cold currents: Flow from poles towards tropics (Labrador current, Benguela current).
Effects
Warm currents → mild climate on coasts | Cold currents → fog, dry conditions | Meeting of currents → ideal fishing grounds (e.g. Grand Banks, Dogger Bank).
Examples
Gulf Stream (Atlantic) — keeps Western Europe warm. Benguela Current (off Africa) — brings cold upwelling, rich fishing.
Q.5 — Give the Geographical Reason (any 2)[6 Marks — 3 each]
5.1
Rivers flow faster in their upper course than in the lower course.
3
Reason 1
In the upper course, rivers flow through mountains and highlands with a steep gradient (high slope). This steep slope gives water more gravitational energy.
Reason 2
The channel is narrow with irregular, rocky bed causing turbulence. The river has high energy for erosion — cutting gorges, waterfalls, and V-shaped valleys.
Lower course
In the lower course, the gradient becomes gentle, river flows across flat plains, channel widens, velocity decreases → deposition occurs forming meanders and deltas.
∴ Upper course: steep gradient → fast flow → erosion. Lower course: gentle slope → slow flow → deposition.
5.2
Coastal areas near warm ocean currents have mild winters.
3
Reason
Warm ocean currents carry warm tropical water towards higher latitudes (poles). As warm water passes near coastal areas, it releases heat into the atmosphere.
Effect
This warms the coastal air even in winter months, preventing extreme cold temperatures. Onshore winds pick up this warmth and bring it inland.
Example
Gulf Stream keeps ports of Norway and UK ice-free even in winter despite their high latitude. London (52°N) has milder winters than Moscow (55°N) due to Gulf Stream influence.
∴ Warm current → warms coastal air → mild winters. e.g. Gulf Stream keeps Western Europe warm.
5.3
Antarctica receives very little rainfall despite having the world's largest ice sheet. (Alternative)
3
Reason 1
Antarctica is an extremely cold polar region. The air is very cold and holds very little moisture — cold air has very low capacity for water vapour.
Reason 2
Cold, dense polar air sinks (high pressure zone) rather than rising, so there is no condensation and cloud formation needed for precipitation.
Ice sheet
The existing ice sheet accumulated over millions of years from very light snowfall. It is preserved because temperatures never rise to melt it significantly.
∴ Cold air = low moisture + sinking air = no clouds = no rain. Ice = accumulated over millions of years.
Q.6 — Answer the following briefly (any 2)[8 Marks — 4 each]
6.1
Explain the landforms formed by rivers (erosion + deposition).
4
Erosion Landforms
  • V-shaped valley: River cuts downward through rock, forming steep-sided narrow valley
  • Gorge/Canyon: Very deep, narrow V-valley in hard rock regions. e.g. Grand Canyon
  • Waterfall: River flows over resistant rock band and plunges down. e.g. Niagara Falls
  • Pot holes: Circular holes drilled in river bed by swirling pebbles
Deposition Landforms
  • Floodplain: Flat fertile land alongside lower course river, built of alluvium
  • Meander: Large S-shaped curves in lower course on flat plains
  • Oxbow Lake: Cut-off meander that becomes an isolated crescent lake
  • Delta: Fan/triangular land at river mouth where deposition occurs. e.g. Ganga delta
∴ Upper course erosion → V-valley, waterfall. Lower course deposition → meander, delta.
6.2
Explain the types of rainfall with causes.
4
1. Convectional
Cause: Intense heating of land → warm moist air rises rapidly → cools at high altitude → heavy thunder rain. Where: Equatorial belt (Amazon, Congo, SE Asia). When: Afternoon. Heavy short duration.
2. Orographic
Cause: Moist winds hit mountains → forced to rise → cool → condensation → rain on windward side. Leeward side = rain shadow. Example: Western Ghats — Mumbai gets 2200mm; Pune 700mm.
3. Cyclonic/Frontal
Cause: Cold and warm air masses meet at weather fronts. Warm air rises over cold → cools → rainfall. Where: Temperate regions. Moderate, prolonged rainfall over large area.

Q.8a
What causes water vapour to rise in Stage 1–2?
Answer: Solar heating warms the land/sea causing water to evaporate. The warm, moist air is lighter than surrounding air and rises upward.
Q.8b
What is the dew point mentioned in Stage 3?
Answer: Dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated and water vapour condenses into tiny water droplets to form clouds.
Q.8c
At which stage does actual rainfall occur?
Answer: At Stage 4 — when tiny cloud droplets combine to form larger, heavier drops that fall to Earth's surface as rain or snow.
Q.8d
What happens to rainfall water after it reaches the ground? (Stage 5)
Answer: After reaching the ground, water either flows as surface runoff into rivers and lakes, infiltrates into the soil as groundwater, or evaporates again — continuing the water cycle.
∴ Rainfall formation: Solar heating → Evaporation → Rising air → Cooling → Condensation → Rain → Collection → Cycle repeats.
