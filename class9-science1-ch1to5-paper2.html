Ch1 A. Define inertia. What are its types? Give one example of each.
Inertia: The tendency of a body to resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion is called inertia. It is a property of matter.
  • Inertia of rest: A body at rest remains at rest. E.g., A passenger falls backward when a bus starts suddenly.
  • Inertia of motion: A body in motion stays in motion. E.g., A passenger falls forward when a bus stops suddenly.
  • Inertia of direction: A body moving in a straight line tends to continue in the same direction. E.g., Mud flies off tangentially from a rotating cycle tyre.
Ch2 B. What is power? Give its formula, SI unit and CGS unit.
Power: The rate at which work is done is called power.
P = W/t
where W = work done (J), t = time (s).
  • SI unit: Watt (W) = 1 Joule/second = 1 J/s
  • CGS unit: erg/second
  • Other unit: Horsepower (hp); 1 hp = 746 W
  • 1 kW = 1000 W; 1 MW = 10⁶ W
Ch3 C. What is electric potential difference? Give its formula and SI unit.
Electric Potential Difference (V): The work done per unit charge in moving a positive charge from one point to another in an electric field is called potential difference.
V = W/Q
where W = work done (Joule), Q = charge (Coulomb).
  • SI unit: Volt (V) = 1 Joule/Coulomb = 1 J/C
  • It is measured using a voltmeter connected in parallel.
  • Potential difference drives current through a circuit.
Ch4 D. What is a chemical formula? Calculate the number of moles in 36 g of water.
Chemical formula: A symbolic representation showing the types and numbers of atoms present in a molecule of a substance. E.g., H₂O (2 H atoms, 1 O atom).

Calculation:
  • Molar mass of H₂O = (2×1) + 16 = 18 g/mol
  • n = m/M = 36/18 = 2 moles
  • 36 g of water = 2 moles of water
Ch5 E. What is neutralisation reaction? Give one example with equation.
Neutralisation: The reaction between an acid and a base to form a salt and water is called neutralisation reaction. It is an exothermic reaction (heat is released).
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Example:
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
(Hydrochloric acid + Sodium hydroxide → Sodium chloride + Water)
  • H⁺ ions from acid combine with OH⁻ ions from base to form water.
  • The remaining ions (Na⁺ and Cl⁻) form the salt NaCl.

Ch2 A. A rocket moves forward when gases are ejected backward.
By Newton's Third Law of Motion, when the rocket engine burns fuel and ejects gases downward/backward with great force (action), an equal and opposite reaction force acts on the rocket in the upward/forward direction. This reaction force propels the rocket forward. Also, momentum is conserved — the backward momentum of gases = forward momentum of rocket.
Ch4 B. The molar mass of oxygen (O₂) is 32 g/mol and not 16 g/mol.
Oxygen exists as a diatomic molecule (O₂) in nature, meaning each molecule contains 2 oxygen atoms. The atomic mass of one oxygen atom = 16 u. Therefore, the molar mass of O₂ = 2 × 16 = 32 g/mol. The value 16 g/mol is the molar mass of a single oxygen atom (O), not the molecule.
Ch5 C. Acids conduct electricity when dissolved in water but not in solid state.
In solid state, the ions of an acid are held in a fixed position in the crystal lattice and cannot move freely — so they cannot conduct electricity. When dissolved in water, acids ionise (release H⁺ and anions), producing free mobile ions. These mobile ions carry electric charge through the solution, enabling conduction of electricity.

Ch1 A. Explain the law of conservation of momentum. Derive it for a collision between two bodies.
Law of Conservation of Momentum: In the absence of an external force, the total linear momentum of a system remains constant (conserved).

Derivation for two-body collision:
  • Let body A (mass m₁, velocity u₁) collide with body B (mass m₂, velocity u₂).
  • After collision: A has velocity v₁, B has velocity v₂.
  • Force by A on B = F_AB; Force by B on A = F_BA = -F_AB (Newton's 3rd Law)
  • For body B: F_AB = m₂(v₂-u₂)/t
  • For body A: F_BA = m₁(v₁-u₁)/t
  • F_AB + F_BA = 0 → m₂(v₂-u₂)/t + m₁(v₁-u₁)/t = 0
  • m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
  • Total initial momentum = Total final momentum ✓
Ch3 B. Explain Joule's law of heating. Write the formula and three applications of heating effect of current.
Joule's Law of Heating: The heat produced in a conductor is:
  • Directly proportional to the square of the current: H ∝ I²
  • Directly proportional to the resistance: H ∝ R
  • Directly proportional to the time: H ∝ t
H = I²Rt (Joules)
Also: H = VIt = V²t/R

Applications of Heating Effect:
  • 1. Electric heater and iron — Nichrome wire resists current and produces heat.
  • 2. Electric bulb (filament lamp) — Tungsten filament heats to ~3000°C and emits light.
  • 3. Electric fuse — Thin wire melts on excessive current, protecting the circuit.

Ch5 A. Explain the pH scale. How is pH related to acidity and basicity? Give pH values of common substances.
pH Scale:
  • pH is a scale from 0 to 14 that measures the concentration of H⁺ ions in a solution.
  • Lower pH → higher H⁺ concentration → more acidic.
  • Higher pH → lower H⁺ concentration → more basic.
pH RangeNatureExamples
0 – 3Strong acidHCl (pH≈1), H₂SO₄ (pH≈2)
3 – 6Weak acidVinegar (pH≈3), lemon (pH≈2.5)
7NeutralPure water
8 – 10Weak baseBaking soda (pH≈8.3), sea water
11 – 14Strong baseNaOH (pH≈13), bleach
  • Human blood has pH ≈ 7.4 (slightly basic); stomach acid has pH ≈ 2.
  • pH is measured using pH paper, universal indicator, or pH meter.
Ch2 B. Derive the formula for kinetic energy. A car of mass 1000 kg moves at 72 km/h. Find its kinetic energy.
Derivation of Kinetic Energy (KE):
  • A body of mass m starts from rest (u=0) and reaches velocity v under force F over distance s.
  • Work done = F × s = ma × s (by Newton's 2nd Law)
  • Using v² = u² + 2as → v² = 2as → as = v²/2
  • W = m × (v²/2)
  • KE = ½mv²
Numerical:
  • m = 1000 kg, v = 72 km/h = 72×(1000/3600) = 20 m/s
  • KE = ½ × 1000 × (20)²
  • KE = ½ × 1000 × 400
  • KE = 2,00,000 J = 2 × 10⁵ J

Ch3 A. Explain characteristics of parallel combination. Solve: Two resistors 4 Ω and 6 Ω are first connected in series, then in parallel across a 12 V battery. Find: (i) equivalent resistance, (ii) total current, (iii) power consumed in each case.
Characteristics of Parallel Combination:
  • Voltage across each resistor is the same (= supply voltage).
  • Total current = sum of branch currents: I = I₁ + I₂
  • Equivalent resistance is less than the smallest individual resistance.
  • 1/R_p = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂
(i) Series:
R_s = 4 + 6 = 10 Ω
I = V/R = 12/10 = 1.2 A
P = VI = 12 × 1.2 = 14.4 W
(ii) Parallel:
1/R_p = 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12
R_p = 12/5 = 2.4 Ω
I = V/R = 12/2.4 = 5 A
P = VI = 12 × 5 = 60 W
Ch2 B. (i) Define potential energy and kinetic energy with SI units. (ii) State the relationship between Joule and erg.
(i) Potential Energy: Energy due to position/state. PE = mgh. SI unit: Joule (J). CGS: erg.
Kinetic Energy: Energy due to motion. KE = ½mv². SI unit: Joule (J). CGS: erg.

(ii) Joule and Erg:
  • 1 Joule = 1 kg·m²/s²
  • Converting: 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 m = 100 cm
  • 1 J = 1000 g × (100)² cm²/s² = 1000 × 10000 g·cm²/s²
  • ∴ 1 Joule = 10⁷ erg
