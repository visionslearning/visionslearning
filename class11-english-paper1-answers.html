A₄ — Grammar (any TWO, 1 mark each)2 Marks
1. Change the Voice:
"He is saying, in effect, 'I recognise that you are not my servant.'"
→ It is being said by him, in effect, "You are not my servant, but my equal."
2. Change the Narration:
The writer said, "Civility costs nothing but buys everything."
→ The writer said that civility costs nothing but buys everything.
3. Rewrite using 'so…that':
"He was very polite. Everyone respected him."
→ He was so polite that everyone respected him.
4. Question Tag:
"The bus conductor treated with respect gives his best service, doesn't he?"
1 mark each for any TWO correct answers.

Q.2A — Passage: The Importance of Farming[10 Marks]
A₁ — Understanding (MCQ)2 Marks
i. People called Sunita 'the silent woman' because —
✅ (B) she rarely spoke unless necessary (1 mark)
ii. Sunita's garden survived the drought because —
✅ (C) she had dug an underground rainwater tank (1 mark)
A₂ — Interpretation2 Marks
📝 Sample Answer:
Sunita means that her garden's growth depends not just on her effort, but on nature's contributions — the sun, rain and soil. This shows her humility and gratitude. She does not consider herself the sole owner or creator; she sees herself as a caretaker who worked with nature, not against it.
1 mark for explaining the meaning + 1 mark for identifying the character quality (humility/gratitude).
2 Marks

📝 Sample Answer:
Preparing in advance is very important because difficulties can arrive without warning. Just as Sunita stored rainwater before the drought came, we too should save money, study regularly and maintain our health before problems arise. Those who prepare face hardship with confidence rather than panic.
1 mark for relevant reason + 1 mark for personal/general example.

Q.2B — Summary Writing[2 Marks]
Title (suggested): "The Silent Woman Who Prepared" OR "Sunita's Gift to the Village"
📝 Sample Summary (about 90 words):
Sunita was a quiet, self-sufficient woman who lived alone on the edge of a village and tended a colourful garden. When a severe drought struck the village, Sunita's garden survived because she had wisely dug an underground water tank years earlier. She generously opened her garden to the entire village, sharing vegetables and shade during the crisis. When the headman thanked her, Sunita humbly said the garden belonged to nature as much as to her. Her story teaches us the value of foresight, humility and sharing.
1 mark for content covering main points + 1 mark for suitable title and language.

📝 Sample Critical Appreciation — "Song of the Open Road" (Whitman):
The poem "Song of the Open Road" is written by the American poet Walt Whitman. The central theme is freedom — the joy of living life on one's own terms, unburdened by complaints or negativity. The poet declares himself "good-fortune" itself, showing deep self-confidence and positivity. The poem uses vivid imagery such as "the long brown path before me" and repetition of "Henceforth" to emphasise new beginnings. The tone is joyful and empowering. The message is that true happiness lies within oneself and that life is an open road full of possibility. I personally find this poem very inspiring, especially when facing difficulties.

a) "Travel Broadens the Mind"
📝 Sample Expansion (approx. 90 words):
When we travel to new places, we encounter different cultures, languages, foods and lifestyles. We learn that there are many ways to live and think. Travel breaks the narrow walls of our familiar world and opens our eyes to the vast diversity of human experience. A person who has only read about mountains will never truly understand their majesty until they stand before one. Meeting new people teaches us tolerance and empathy. Thus, travel does not just move our body from one place to another — it expands our thinking, making us wiser and more compassionate human beings.

Q.5B — Brief Questions from Lesson 4.2[4 Marks]
📌 Based on the drama from Lesson 4.2 (the one-act play about a man and a sergeant). Award 1 mark each.
i. Reason of the man for staying at the place:
📝 Sample Answer:
The man stays at the place because he has nowhere else to go. He is homeless and desperate. The place provides him with some shelter and safety. He is not a criminal but a helpless person caught in difficult circumstances, and he fears being moved on by the authorities.
ii. The Sergeant misguides his officers to support the man — views:
📝 Sample Answer:
The sergeant's action of misguiding his officers shows his humane side. Rather than enforcing rules mechanically, he uses his authority with compassion. Though it may seem irregular, it reveals that he understands the man's helplessness. I feel that true justice sometimes requires us to bend rules for genuine human need.
iii. Opinion about the man and the sergeant:
📝 Sample Answer:
The man represents the silent suffering of the poor who are pushed aside by society. The sergeant represents authority with a conscience. Together they show that kindness can exist even within rigid systems. In my opinion, both characters deserve respect — one for surviving with dignity, the other for serving with humanity.
iv. The sergeant's reaction surprises the audience — views:
📝 Sample Answer:
The audience expects the sergeant to follow orders and move the man away. His unexpected decision to protect the man instead creates a dramatic surprise. This surprise makes the audience question their assumptions about authority figures. It shows that not all people in power are heartless, and this twist gives the play a powerful, hopeful ending.
1 mark each for any relevant answer (2–3 sentences, covering the key point). Accept valid variations.
