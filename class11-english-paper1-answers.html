Q.1 — Seen Prose: "On to the Summit" — Tenzing Norgay12 Marks
Q.1A — Extract from: 1.2 On to the Summit[10 Marks]
📖 EXTRACT (for reference):

One foot, then another, one step at a time. I was breathing very fast — perhaps eleven or twelve breaths to each step. The slope was very steep and I had to cut every step very carefully. Hillary was behind me and I could hear his axe cutting into the snow and ice. We were going very slowly.

I could see the top — or at least what I thought was the top — but every time I reached it, there was still another slope above. Finally, however, we were on a ridge with nothing above us but sky. I felt a great surge of joy within me. We were on top of the world.

Hillary stepped up beside me and we shook hands. Then we hugged each other, two men who had been through so much together, standing on the highest point on earth. I was too happy to say anything. I looked down at the valley below and thought of home, of my people, of Chomolungma — the Goddess Mother of the World.

Q.2 — Unseen Passage: "The Importance of Farming"12 Marks
Q.2A — All Activities[10 Marks]
📖 PASSAGE (for reference):

Farming is one of the oldest and most important occupations on earth. For thousands of years, human civilisations have depended on the farmer to grow food and sustain life. Yet today, in many parts of the world, farming is seen as a backward profession — something to escape from, rather than something to be proud of.

The reality is very different. A modern farmer is a scientist, an economist and an ecologist all in one. He must understand the soil, the climate, the seeds and the water. He must know when to plant, when to water and when to harvest. He must also know the market — when to sell, and at what price.

In India, the farmer feeds over 1.4 billion people. Despite this, the average Indian farmer earns very little. Droughts, floods, falling prices and rising costs make farming a difficult and often heartbreaking profession. Yet millions of farmers continue to work their land with patience and hope, believing that the earth will not betray those who care for it.

"If a nation truly respects its people, it must begin by respecting the hands that feed them."

✅ Sample Appreciation 1 — 2.1 Cherry Tree (Ruskin Bond)4 Marks
Step 1 — Title & Poet
The poem is titled "Cherry Tree", written by Ruskin Bond, a celebrated Indian author known for his love of nature and the hills of Mussoorie.
Step 2 — Theme / Central Idea
The central theme of the poem is the power of patience, nurturing and time. The poet planted a cherry twig and watched it struggle and slowly grow into a strong, beautiful tree that now shelters birds and children. The poem shows that small beginnings, with care and time, can lead to great outcomes. It is also a poem about the bond between humans and nature.
Step 3 — Figures of Speech (minimum 2)
Line from poemFigure of SpeechExplanation
"A little tree, fighting for its life against the world"PersonificationThe tree is given the human quality of "fighting" — trees cannot fight. This makes the tree's struggle feel alive and emotional.
"It grew, and it grew, and it grew"Repetition / AnaphoraThe word "grew" is repeated three times to emphasise the continuous, steady growth of the tree over many years.
"Through the rain and the sun and the wind"Polysyndeton (also Imagery)Repeated use of "and" creates a rhythmic effect and lists the forces of nature the tree endured.
"Birds come nesting in its branches now"Visual ImageryCreates a vivid picture of the tree now providing a home to birds — showing the tree's maturity and value.
Step 4 — Mood / Tone
The mood of the poem is warm, hopeful and celebratory. It begins with a simple act (planting a twig) and moves towards a feeling of deep joy and wonder as the tree grows and becomes a source of life for others. The tone is gentle and reflective — like a person looking back with satisfaction at something they nurtured.
Step 5 — Message / Moral
The message of the poem is: "Whatever we nurture with love and patience will grow and give back to others." The cherry tree is a symbol of life itself — it started as a tiny fragile twig and became a tree of strength and shelter. The poem teaches us that we must not give up on things that are small and struggling, for they may become our greatest source of joy.
Step 6 — Personal Opinion
I personally find this poem deeply moving. It reminds me that growth takes time and that we must be patient. In our fast-moving world, we often want instant results. But this poem teaches us that the most meaningful things in life — like a tree, a friendship, or a skill — grow slowly and last long. I love how Ruskin Bond connects the tree's growth to his own life, making it feel personal and real.

✅ Sample Appreciation 2 — 2.2 The Sower (Victor Hugo, tr. Toru Dutt)4 Marks
📖 POEM TEXT (for reference):
Sitting in a porchway cool,
Sunlight, I see, dying fast,
Twilight hastens on to rule,
Night will soon be overcast.

Shadows shoot across the lands;
But one Sower lingers still,
Old, in rags, he patient stands,
Looking on, I feel a thrill.

Black and high his silhouette
Dominates the furrows deep!
Now to sow the task is set,
Soon shall come a time to reap.
Step 1 — Title & Poet
The poem is titled "The Sower", written by the French poet Victor Hugo (translated into English by the Indian poet Toru Dutt). Victor Hugo was a 19th-century Romantic poet, novelist and playwright — one of the greatest writers in French literature. The original poem is titled "Saison des Semailles: Le Soir" (Sowing Season: The Evening).
Step 2 — Theme / Central Idea
The central theme of the poem is the dignity of labour, perseverance and hope. The poet observes an old, ragged farmer still sowing seeds in his field as evening falls and darkness approaches. The sower works selflessly — not for himself, but for others who will one day benefit from the harvest. The poem also carries a deeper symbolic meaning: the sower represents all those who work tirelessly in the hope of a better future, even without seeing the results immediately.
Step 3 — Figures of Speech (minimum 2)
Line from poemFigure of SpeechExplanation
"Black and high his silhouette dominates the furrows deep"Imagery / MetaphorThe dark, towering outline of the sower against the sky creates a powerful visual image, making him appear grand and heroic despite his rags.
"Twilight hastens on to rule"PersonificationTwilight is given the human quality of hastening (hurrying) and ruling — as if night is a king taking over the land.
"Now to sow the task is set, / Soon shall come a time to reap"Contrast / JuxtapositionPresent action (sowing) is contrasted with future reward (reaping), symbolising that hard work today leads to results tomorrow.
"Old, in rags, he patient stands"AlliterationThe repetition of the 'p' sound in "patient" and the visual description together emphasise the sower's quiet endurance.
Step 4 — Mood / Tone
The mood of the poem is solemn, admiring and inspiring. The poet watches the sower with deep respect — "looking on, I feel a thrill." The overall atmosphere is one of evening stillness, with shadows and darkness gathering, yet the sower's figure is steady and strong. The tone moves from quiet observation to profound admiration.
Step 5 — Message / Moral
The message of the poem is: "Patient, selfless work done today will bring results tomorrow." The sower does not work for himself — he sows seeds for a harvest he may never see. This is a lesson in selflessness and dedication. The poem also honours the dignity of farmers and agricultural workers, reminding us that those who feed the world deserve our deepest respect.
Step 6 — Personal Opinion
I find this poem deeply moving. The image of an old man in rags still working as darkness falls is unforgettable. In a world that values speed and rewards, the sower's patience is a powerful reminder that the most important work is often the most silent and unrecognised. This poem makes me think of all the farmers and workers in India who feed millions without recognition. I believe every student should read this poem.

✅ Sample Appreciation 3 — 2.3 There is Another Sky (Emily Dickinson)4 Marks
📖 POEM TEXT (for reference):
There is another sky,
Ever serene and fair,
And there is another sunshine,
Though it be darkness there;
Never mind faded forests, Austin,
Never mind silent fields —
Here is a little forest,
Whose leaf is ever green;
Here is a brighter garden,
Where not a frost has been;
In its unfading flowers
I hear the bright bee hum:
Prithee, my brother,
Into my garden come!
Step 1 — Title & Poet
The poem is titled "There is Another Sky", written by the great American poet Emily Dickinson (1830–1886). Dickinson was known for her short, intensely personal poems about nature, death, love and immortality. This poem was written as a letter-poem to her brother Austin, inviting him to come home to her world of poetry and imagination.
Step 2 — Theme / Central Idea
The central theme of the poem is hope, inner peace and the healing power of imagination and poetry. The poet contrasts the real, fading external world (faded forests, silent fields, darkness) with an inner world (her poetry / imagination) which is always serene, fair, bright and full of life. She invites her brother Austin, who seems to be going through sadness or difficulty, to find comfort in this inner world. The poem is also about the eternal quality of art — unlike the real world, art never fades.
Step 3 — Figures of Speech (minimum 2)
Line from poemFigure of SpeechExplanation
"There is another sky" / "another sunshine"MetaphorThe "other sky" and "other sunshine" are metaphors for the poet's inner world — her poetry and imagination — which is always bright even when the outside world is dark.
"Here is a little forest, whose leaf is ever green"Metaphor / SymbolismThe "forest with ever-green leaves" symbolises poetry or art, which never fades unlike real forests that lose leaves in winter.
"Here is a brighter garden, where not a frost has been"Imagery / ContrastVivid image of an eternal garden that never experiences frost (hardship or decay), contrasted with the real world's faded forests and silent fields.
"Never mind faded forests, Austin / Never mind silent fields"Repetition / Apostrophe"Never mind" is repeated, creating emphasis. Addressing "Austin" directly is apostrophe — a direct address to a person.
"I hear the bright bee hum"Alliteration + OnomatopoeiaThe 'b' sound in "bright bee" is alliteration. "Hum" is onomatopoeia — it imitates the sound of a bee.
Step 4 — Mood / Tone
The mood of the poem is gentle, warm, inviting and hopeful. The poem begins with a quiet contrast between the dark outside world and a serene inner world. The tone is tender and loving — like a sister speaking softly to a worried brother. The final line, "Prithee, my brother, into my garden come!" is warm and affectionate, almost like an embrace through words. The overall atmosphere is one of comfort and reassurance.
Step 5 — Message / Moral
The message of the poem is: "When the real world feels dark and fading, find comfort and beauty in the world of imagination, poetry and art — it never fades." The poem also tells us that when a loved one is suffering, the best gift we can give them is not material — it is an invitation into a world of beauty, peace and hope. Dickinson's deeper message is that poetry is eternal — it outlives all seasons.
Step 6 — Personal Opinion
This is one of my favourite poems from the textbook. Dickinson's idea that we all carry an inner world — a "garden" where nothing ever fades — is beautiful and comforting. As a student who sometimes feels overwhelmed by studies and pressure, I find this poem a reminder that books, poetry and imagination are always a safe place to return to. The simplicity of the language makes the message all the more powerful.

Q.5B — Brief Questions: The Rising of the Moon (Lady Gregory)[4 Marks]
i. Why did the wanted man stay at the quay?1 Mark
❓ Question
Find the reason why the wanted man chose to stay at the quay (dock). Write your views.
✅ Sample Answer
The wanted man stayed at the quay because he was waiting for a boat to escape across the water. The quay was his only route to freedom. He was desperate — a fugitive with no other option — and the darkness of the night gave him some cover. He also recognised that disguising himself as a ballad singer would help him blend in and avoid detection by the police.
Award 1 mark for relevant answer covering the key reason
ii. The Sergeant misguides his officers — your opinion1 Mark
❓ Question
The Sergeant misguides his officers to protect the ragged man. Write your opinion about this action.
✅ Sample Answer
The Sergeant's action of misguiding his officers shows that he is not merely a machine of authority but a human being with a conscience. He chose humanity over duty. Though it may seem like a betrayal of his role, I believe it reflects the highest form of justice — recognising when a rule causes more harm than good. His action shows that even those who enforce the law can sometimes question its fairness.
Award 1 mark for clear opinion with reason. Accept any thoughtful perspective.
iii. Your opinion about the man and the Sergeant1 Mark
❓ Question
Write your opinion about the two main characters — the ragged man and the Sergeant.
✅ Sample Answer
The ragged man represents the spirit of those who fight for freedom against an unjust system. He is brave, resourceful and dignified despite his desperate situation. The Sergeant represents authority with a hidden conscience — torn between his duty and his humanity. Together they show that ordinary people, even on opposite sides of the law, can find a moment of shared understanding. Both characters deserve respect for their honesty in that final moment.
Award 1 mark for characterising both figures with a reason. Accept valid views.
iv. The Sergeant's reaction surprises the audience — your views1 Mark
❓ Question
The Sergeant's unexpected reaction at the end surprises the audience. Write your views.
✅ Sample Answer
The audience expects the Sergeant to arrest the wanted man — that is the entire tension of the play. His decision not to do so is a dramatic reversal that shocks and moves the audience at the same time. It forces us to ask: what truly makes someone a good person? The surprise ending transforms the play from a simple chase story into a deep reflection on loyalty, justice and the meaning of duty. It is Lady Gregory's most powerful moment in the play.
Award 1 mark for explaining the dramatic effect of the surprise and its meaning.
