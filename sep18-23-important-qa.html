Heat — Conduction, Convection, Radiation & Numericals

Science 1 ▼
1 What are the three modes of heat transfer? Explain each briefly. 3 Marks ▼
  • Conduction: Transfer of heat through a medium without movement of the medium itself. Particles vibrate and pass energy to neighbours. E.g. heating one end of a metal rod — the other end gets hot.
  • Convection: Transfer of heat through actual movement of fluid (liquid or gas) particles. Hotter, lighter fluid rises and cooler fluid comes down forming a convection current. E.g. boiling water, sea breeze.
  • Radiation: Transfer of heat without any medium. Energy travels as electromagnetic waves. E.g. heat from the Sun reaching Earth.
2 Define Specific Heat Capacity. Write its SI unit. 2 Marks ▼

Specific Heat Capacity (c) is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C (or 1 K).

Q = m × c × ΔT
  • Q = Heat absorbed (in Joules)
  • m = Mass (in kg)
  • c = Specific heat capacity
  • ΔT = Change in temperature (in °C or K)

SI unit: J / (kg·K)  or  J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹

📌 Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/(kg·K) — highest among common substances, which is why water is used as a coolant.
3 Numerical: How much heat energy is required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water from 20°C to 70°C? (Specific heat of water = 4200 J/kg°C) 3 Marks ▼

Given:

  • m = 2 kg
  • c = 4200 J/kg°C
  • ΔT = 70 – 20 = 50°C

Formula:

Q = m × c × ΔT

Solution:

Q = 2 × 4200 × 50 = 4,20,000 J = 420 kJ

Answer: 4,20,000 J (420 kJ)

4 Why is the bottom of cooking vessels made of copper but the handles are made of plastic or wood? 2 Marks ▼
  • Bottom (Copper): Copper is a very good conductor of heat, so it quickly transfers heat from the flame to the food being cooked.
  • Handle (Plastic/Wood): Plastic and wood are poor conductors of heat (insulators), so they stay cool and prevent burns when held.
5 What is the difference between heat and temperature? 2 Marks ▼
  • Heat: Form of energy that flows from a hotter body to a cooler body. Measured in Joules (J).
  • Temperature: Measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. Measured in °C or K.
📌 A large body may have more heat than a small body even if the small body has a higher temperature.

Arithmetic Progression — Word Problems & Practice

Maths 1 ▼
1 Write the key formulas of Arithmetic Progression (AP). 2 Marks ▼
  • nth term: tₙ = a + (n – 1)d
  • Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n/2 × [2a + (n–1)d]   or   Sₙ = n/2 × (a + l)
  • a = first term, d = common difference, l = last term, n = number of terms
📌 Common difference d = any term − previous term
2 The sum of three consecutive terms of an AP is 27 and their product is 504. Find the terms. 4 Marks ▼

Let the three consecutive terms be (a – d), a, (a + d)

Sum: (a – d) + a + (a + d) = 27 → 3a = 27 → a = 9

Product: (9 – d) × 9 × (9 + d) = 504

9 × (81 – d²) = 504 → 81 – d² = 56 → d² = 25 → d = ±5

When d = 5: Terms are 4, 9, 14

When d = –5: Terms are 14, 9, 4

Answer: The three terms are 4, 9, 14.

3 A contractor has to pay a fine for delay. He pays ₹200 for the 1st day, ₹250 for the 2nd day, ₹300 for the 3rd day and so on. How much does he pay in all for 30 days? 4 Marks ▼

a = 200, d = 50, n = 30

Sₙ = n/2 × [2a + (n–1)d]
S₃₀ = 30/2 × [2×200 + 29×50]
= 15 × [400 + 1450] = 15 × 1850 = 27,750

Answer: ₹27,750

4 How many terms of the AP 9, 17, 25… must be taken to give a sum of 636? 3 Marks ▼

a = 9, d = 8, Sₙ = 636

n/2 × [2×9 + (n–1)×8] = 636
n × [18 + 8n – 8] = 1272
n(8n + 10) = 1272 → 8n² + 10n – 1272 = 0 → 4n² + 5n – 636 = 0
(n – 12)(4n + 53) = 0 → n = 12

Answer: 12 terms

Life Processes Part 2 — Control, Coordination & Reproduction

Science 2 ▼
1 Distinguish between nervous system and endocrine system (hormonal system). 4 Marks ▼
Nervous System Endocrine System
Uses electrical impulses (nerve impulses)Uses chemical messengers (hormones)
Response is very fastResponse is slow
Effect is short-livedEffect lasts longer
Controlled by brain and spinal cordControlled by endocrine glands
2 What is a reflex action? Describe the reflex arc with a diagram description. 3 Marks ▼

Reflex Action: An automatic and rapid involuntary response to a stimulus without the involvement of the brain. E.g. withdrawing hand from a hot object.

Reflex Arc path:

Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory nerve → Spinal cord → Motor nerve → Effector → Response
📌 Reflex arc bypasses the brain for speed — the spinal cord processes the response directly.
3 Name the male and female sex hormones. What are their functions? 2 Marks ▼
  • Testosterone (Male — secreted by testes): Controls development of secondary sexual characters in males — facial hair, deepening of voice, muscle development.
  • Oestrogen (Female — secreted by ovaries): Controls development of secondary sexual characters in females — breast development, widening of hips, menstrual cycle.
4 What is the role of the following hormones: (a) Insulin (b) Adrenaline (c) Thyroxine? 3 Marks ▼
  • Insulin (Pancreas): Regulates blood sugar levels — converts excess glucose to glycogen for storage. Deficiency causes diabetes.
  • Adrenaline (Adrenal gland): "Fight or flight" hormone — increases heart rate, dilates airways, boosts blood glucose during emergency/stress.
  • Thyroxine (Thyroid gland): Regulates metabolism, growth, and development. Requires iodine for production. Deficiency causes goitre.
5 Distinguish between asexual and sexual reproduction. 4 Marks ▼
Asexual Reproduction Sexual Reproduction
Single parent involvedTwo parents (male & female) involved
No formation of gametesGametes (sperm & egg) are formed
Offspring are genetically identicalOffspring have genetic variation
E.g. budding in yeast, binary fission in amoebaE.g. reproduction in humans, flowering plants

Probability — Coins, Cards, Dice

Maths 1 ▼
1 State the formula for probability. What are the possible values of probability? 2 Marks ▼
P(Event) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of outcomes
  • Probability always lies between 0 and 1 (inclusive).
  • P = 0 → impossible event; P = 1 → certain event.
2 A coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of getting: (a) Both heads (b) At least one tail (c) No tail 3 Marks ▼

Sample space = {HH, HT, TH, TT} → Total outcomes = 4

  • (a) Both heads: {HH} → P = 1/4
  • (b) At least one tail: {HT, TH, TT} → P = 3/4
  • (c) No tail (= both heads): {HH} → P = 1/4
3 A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability that the card is: (a) a red card (b) a king (c) a face card (d) a spade 4 Marks ▼

Total cards = 52

  • (a) Red cards = 26 → P = 26/52 = 1/2
  • (b) Kings = 4 → P = 4/52 = 1/13
  • (c) Face cards (J, Q, K) = 12 → P = 12/52 = 3/13
  • (d) Spades = 13 → P = 13/52 = 1/4
4 A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting: (a) a number greater than 4 (b) an even number (c) a multiple of 3 3 Marks ▼

Sample space = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} → Total outcomes = 6

  • (a) Greater than 4: {5, 6} → P = 2/6 = 1/3
  • (b) Even number: {2, 4, 6} → P = 3/6 = 1/2
  • (c) Multiple of 3: {3, 6} → P = 2/6 = 1/3

Refraction of Light — Laws, Refractive Index, TIR

Science 1 ▼
1 State the laws of refraction of light. 3 Marks ▼
  1. The incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
  2. The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media and for a given colour of light (Snell's Law):
sin i / sin r = n = constant (refractive index)
2 What is refractive index? If the speed of light in glass is 2 × 10⁸ m/s and in vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s, find the refractive index of glass. 3 Marks ▼

Refractive Index (n): Ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (c) to the speed of light in the medium (v).

n = c / v = 3×10⁸ / 2×10⁸ = 1.5

Answer: Refractive index of glass = 1.5

3 What is Total Internal Reflection (TIR)? State its two conditions. Give two applications. 4 Marks ▼

Total Internal Reflection: When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, light is completely reflected back into the denser medium.

Conditions:

  1. Light must travel from a denser medium to a rarer medium.
  2. The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.

Applications:

  • Optical fibres — used in internet cables and medical endoscopes.
  • Sparkling of diamonds — diamonds are cut so that TIR makes them sparkle brilliantly.
4 Why does a ray of light bend when it passes from one medium to another? 2 Marks ▼

Light bends (refracts) when it passes from one medium to another because the speed of light changes in different media. When light enters a denser medium, it slows down and bends towards the normal. When it enters a rarer medium, it speeds up and bends away from the normal.

Lenses — Ray Diagrams, Lens Formula, Power

Science 1 ▼
1 State the lens formula. Define magnification for lenses. 2 Marks ▼
Lens Formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
  • v = image distance, u = object distance, f = focal length
Magnification: m = v / u = h' / h
  • h' = image height, h = object height
  • m > 0 → virtual, erect image; m < 0 → real, inverted image
2 Numerical: An object is placed 15 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the image distance and magnification. 4 Marks ▼

Given: u = –15 cm (object on left), f = +10 cm (convex lens)

1/v − 1/u = 1/f
1/v − 1/(−15) = 1/10
1/v + 1/15 = 1/10
1/v = 1/10 − 1/15 = 3/30 − 2/30 = 1/30
v = +30 cm

Image distance = 30 cm (real, on opposite side)

m = v/u = 30/(−15) = −2

Magnification = −2 (real, inverted, magnified)

3 What is the power of a lens? A convex lens has a focal length of 25 cm. What is its power? 2 Marks ▼

Power of a Lens: The reciprocal of the focal length in metres.

P = 1/f(in metres)
f = 25 cm = 0.25 m → P = 1/0.25 = +4 D

Answer: Power = +4 Dioptre (D)

📌 Convex lens → positive power; Concave lens → negative power

Positive focal lengthNegative focal length
Used to correct hypermetropia (long-sightedness)Used to correct myopia (short-sightedness)

Environmental Management — Ecosystem, Pollution, Conservation

Science 2 ▼
1 What is an ecosystem? Name its two main components. 2 Marks ▼

An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with their physical (non-living) environment.

  • Biotic components — all living things: producers, consumers, decomposers.
  • Abiotic components — non-living factors: sunlight, water, temperature, soil, air.
2 What is a food chain? Explain with an example and state the 10% energy law. 3 Marks ▼

A food chain is the sequence in which energy and nutrients pass from one organism to another through feeding.

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

10% Energy Law (Lindeman's Law): Only 10% of the energy stored at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level. The remaining 90% is lost as heat during metabolism.

3 Define biodiversity. What are the major threats to biodiversity? 3 Marks ▼

Biodiversity: The variety of living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) found in a given area including their genetic diversity and the ecosystems they form.

Major Threats:

  • Habitat destruction (deforestation, urbanisation)
  • Overexploitation of natural resources
  • Pollution (air, water, soil)
  • Introduction of invasive (foreign) species
  • Climate change and global warming
4 What is the greenhouse effect? Name the main greenhouse gases and write its consequences. 4 Marks ▼

The greenhouse effect is the warming of Earth's surface due to trapping of heat by certain gases in the atmosphere that allow sunlight in but prevent heat from escaping.

Main greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄ (methane), N₂O, water vapour, CFCs.

Consequences:

  • Global warming → rise in average temperature
  • Melting of polar ice caps → rise in sea level
  • Extreme weather events — floods, droughts
  • Extinction of species

Linear & Quadratic Equations — Revision

Maths 1 ▼
1 Solve the following system of linear equations: 2x + 3y = 13 and 5x – 2y = 4. 3 Marks ▼

Multiply eq1 × 2 and eq2 × 3:

4x + 6y = 26 ... (i)
15x − 6y = 12 ... (ii)

Add (i) + (ii): 19x = 38 → x = 2

Substitute x = 2 in eq1: 4 + 3y = 13 → 3y = 9 → y = 3

Answer: x = 2, y = 3

2 Write the quadratic formula. When does a quadratic equation have: (a) two real roots (b) one root (c) no real root? 3 Marks ▼
x = [–b ± √(b² – 4ac)] / 2a

Discriminant: D = b² – 4ac

  • (a) D > 0 → two distinct real roots
  • (b) D = 0 → two equal real roots (one repeated root)
  • (c) D < 0 → no real roots (complex roots)
3 The sum of two numbers is 27 and their product is 182. Find the numbers. 4 Marks ▼

Let the numbers be x and (27 – x).

x(27 – x) = 182
27x – x² = 182
x² – 27x + 182 = 0
(x – 13)(x – 14) = 0

x = 13 or x = 14

Answer: The two numbers are 13 and 14.

4 A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less. Find the speed of the train. 4 Marks ▼

Let speed = x km/h

360/x − 360/(x+5) = 1
360(x+5) − 360x = x(x+5)
1800 = x² + 5x
x² + 5x − 1800 = 0
(x − 40)(x + 45) = 0 → x = 40

Answer: Speed of the train = 40 km/h
