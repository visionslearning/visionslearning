Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
Ch1 i. What is escape velocity?
Escape velocity is the minimum velocity required for an object to escape from the gravitational field of a planet without further propulsion; for Earth it is approximately 11.2 km/s.
Ch2 ii. State Mendeleev's Periodic Law.
Mendeleev's Periodic Law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses.
Ch3 iii. What is a displacement reaction? Give one example.
A displacement reaction is one in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound; e.g., Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu.
Ch5 iv. What is latent heat of vaporisation?
Latent heat of vaporisation is the amount of heat required to convert 1 kg of a liquid into vapour at its boiling point without any change in temperature.
Ch6 v. What is lateral displacement of light through a glass slab?
When a ray of light passes through a glass slab, the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but shifted sideways; this perpendicular distance between the incident ray and the emergent ray is called lateral displacement.

Ch1 A. Circular motion and centripetal force (2 marks)
  • When an object moves in a circular path, it undergoes continuous change in direction even if speed is constant — this is circular motion.
  • The acceleration directed towards the centre of the circle is called centripetal acceleration.
  • The force required to keep an object moving in a circular path (directed towards the centre) is called centripetal force.
  • F = mv²/r where m = mass, v = velocity, r = radius of circular path.
  • Example: A satellite revolving around Earth needs centripetal force, which is provided by gravity.
Ch2 B. Modern Periodic Table — Groups and Periods (2 marks)
  • The Modern Periodic Table (Moseley, 1913) has 18 groups (vertical columns) and 7 periods (horizontal rows).
  • Groups: Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons and similar chemical properties. E.g., Group 1 (alkali metals): Li, Na, K — all have 1 valence electron.
  • Periods: Elements in the same period have the same number of electron shells. E.g., Period 2: Li to Ne — all have 2 shells.
  • Period 1 has 2 elements; Periods 2 and 3 have 8 elements each; Periods 4 and 5 have 18 elements each.
  • Trends across a period: Atomic radius decreases; electronegativity increases; metallic character decreases.
Ch3 C. Corrosion and its prevention (2 marks)
  • Corrosion: The gradual eating away of a metal due to attack by atmospheric gases (oxygen, moisture, acids) is called corrosion.
  • Example: Rusting of iron — 4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (brown rust)
  • Corrosion is an oxidation reaction at the surface of the metal.
  • Prevention methods:
    • Painting or varnishing (barrier coating)
    • Galvanising — coating iron with zinc
    • Electroplating — depositing a protective metal layer
    • Alloying — mixing metals (e.g., stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni)
    • Greasing / oiling (for machine parts)
Ch4 D. Ohm's Law and resistance (2 marks)
  • Ohm's Law: At constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across it. V = IR where R = resistance in Ohms (Ω)
  • Resistance (R): The opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current is called resistance.
  • Factors affecting resistance: length (R ∝ L), cross-sectional area (R ∝ 1/A), material (resistivity), temperature.
  • R = ρL/A where ρ = resistivity of the material.
  • Resistors in series: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
  • Resistors in parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
Ch5 E. Modes of Heat Transfer (2 marks)
  • 1. Conduction: Heat transfer through a solid without actual movement of particles. E.g., heat flowing through an iron rod. Requires physical contact. Best conductors: metals (copper, silver).
  • 2. Convection: Heat transfer through liquids and gases by actual movement of particles (currents). E.g., water heated in a pot — hot water rises, cool water sinks. Does not work in vacuum.
  • 3. Radiation: Heat transfer in the form of electromagnetic waves (infrared) without any medium. E.g., heat from the Sun reaching Earth through space. Works in vacuum.
  • All three modes may occur simultaneously.

Ch2 A. Explain the Modern Periodic Law. State the drawbacks of Mendeleev's table and how they were resolved. (4 marks)
Modern Periodic Law (Moseley, 1913):
  • The physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers (not atomic masses).
  • When arranged by atomic number, properties repeat at regular intervals.
Drawbacks of Mendeleev's Table:
  • 1. Position of isotopes: Isotopes of the same element have different atomic masses — could not be placed properly.
  • 2. Wrong order: Some elements placed in wrong order (e.g., Ar before K despite Ar having higher atomic mass).
  • 3. Dissimilar elements grouped together: Some groups had elements with very different properties.
  • 4. Position of hydrogen: Not clear whether to place it in Group 1 (alkali metals) or Group 17 (halogens).
  • 5. No place for lanthanides and actinides.
How Modern Periodic Table resolved these:
  • Isotopes have the same atomic number → occupy same position correctly.
  • Arrangement by atomic number avoids anomalies of Ar/K type.
  • Lanthanides and actinides placed separately in f-block.
  • Hydrogen still placed in Group 1 (by convention) but its unique properties noted.
Ch4 B. Explain the chemical effect of electric current. Describe electroplating with a diagram. (4 marks)
Chemical Effect of Electric Current:
  • When electric current is passed through a conducting liquid (electrolyte), it causes a chemical reaction — decomposition of the solution. This is called the chemical effect of electric current (electrolysis).
  • The conducting liquid is called an electrolyte.
  • The electrodes dipped in the electrolyte are called anode (+) and cathode (–).
  • At the cathode: positive ions (cations) gain electrons → deposited as metal.
  • At the anode: negative ions (anions) lose electrons → dissolved/released as gas.
Electroplating:
  • The process of depositing a thin layer of one metal over another using electrolysis is called electroplating.
  • Purpose: to improve appearance, prevent corrosion, increase hardness.
  • Setup: Object to be plated = cathode; Plating metal = anode; Solution of metal salt = electrolyte.
  • Example: Gold plating of jewellery — gold anode, jewellery = cathode, gold salt solution = electrolyte.
  • When current flows → gold ions deposit on jewellery (cathode) → thin gold layer forms.

Ch5 C. What is specific latent heat? Distinguish between latent heat of fusion and latent heat of vaporisation with examples. (4 marks)
Specific Latent Heat:
  • The amount of heat required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance at its melting/boiling point without change in temperature is called specific latent heat.
  • Q = mL where m = mass (kg), L = specific latent heat (J/kg).
PropertyLatent Heat of FusionLatent Heat of Vaporisation
DefinitionHeat needed to melt 1 kg of solid to liquid at melting pointHeat needed to convert 1 kg of liquid to gas at boiling point
State changeSolid → LiquidLiquid → Gas
For water3.36 × 10⁵ J/kg (at 0°C)22.6 × 10⁵ J/kg (at 100°C)
ExampleMelting of iceBoiling of water into steam
  • Latent heat of vaporisation is always much greater than latent heat of fusion for the same substance.
  • During a change of state, temperature remains constant even though heat is being added/removed.

Ch1 A. The mass of Earth is 6 × 10²⁴ kg and its radius is 6.4 × 10⁶ m. Find the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of Earth. (G = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²) (2 marks)
Given: M = 6 × 10²⁴ kg, R = 6.4 × 10⁶ m, G = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹
Formula: g = GM/R²
g = (6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6 × 10²⁴) / (6.4 × 10⁶)²
g = (4.004 × 10¹⁴) / (4.096 × 10¹³)
∴ g ≈ 9.77 m/s² ≈ 9.8 m/s²
Ch1 B. An object is thrown vertically upward with a velocity of 49 m/s. Find the maximum height reached. (g = 9.8 m/s²) (2 marks)
Given: u = 49 m/s, v = 0 (at max height), g = 9.8 m/s²
Formula: v² = u² – 2gs (deceleration upward)
0 = (49)² – 2 × 9.8 × s
s = 2401 / 19.6
∴ Maximum height = 122.5 m
Ch4 C. Two resistors of 6 Ω and 12 Ω are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance. (2 marks)
Given: R₁ = 6 Ω, R₂ = 12 Ω
Formula: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂
1/R = 1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12
R = 12/3
∴ Equivalent resistance = 4 Ω
Ch5 D. Calculate the heat required to melt 500 g of ice at 0°C. (Latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.36 × 10⁵ J/kg) (2 marks)
Given: m = 500 g = 0.5 kg, L = 3.36 × 10⁵ J/kg
Formula: Q = mL
Q = 0.5 × 3.36 × 10⁵
∴ Q = 1.68 × 10⁵ J = 168000 J
Ch6 E. A ray of light travels from water (n = 1.33) to glass (n = 1.5). The angle of incidence is 30°. Find the angle of refraction. (sin 30° = 0.5) (2 marks)
Given: n₁ = 1.33 (water), n₂ = 1.5 (glass), i = 30°
Formula (Snell's Law): n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
1.33 × 0.5 = 1.5 × sin r
0.665 = 1.5 × sin r
sin r = 0.665 / 1.5 = 0.4433
r = sin⁻¹(0.4433) ≈ 26.3°
∴ Angle of refraction ≈ 26.3°
