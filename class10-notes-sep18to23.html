Day 8 · Sep 18

Heat — Science 1

Science 1

🔑 Key Notes

★ Core Concepts
  • Heat is a form of energy that flows from a body at higher temperature to one at lower temperature.
  • Temperature is the measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. SI unit: Kelvin (K). Common unit: °C.
  • Relation: K = °C + 273
  • Specific Heat Capacity (s): Heat required to raise temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C (or 1 K). Unit: J/kg·°C
  • Water has the highest specific heat capacity: 4200 J/kg·°C

🌡️ Modes of Heat Transfer

ModeMedium Required?How it WorksExample
ConductionYes (solid)Particle-to-particle transfer without movement of particlesMetal spoon in hot tea
ConvectionYes (liquid/gas)Bulk movement of fluid particlesBoiling water, sea breeze
RadiationNo (vacuum also)Electromagnetic waves (infrared)Sun's heat reaching Earth

📐 Important Formula

Q = m × s × ΔT
Q = Heat gained/lost (Joules)
m = mass (kg)
s = specific heat capacity (J/kg·°C)
ΔT = change in temperature (T₂ − T₁)
Also Remember
  • Good conductors (metals: copper, iron) transfer heat quickly. Bad conductors / Insulators (wood, plastic, air) transfer heat slowly.
  • Black/rough surfaces absorb and radiate heat better than white/smooth surfaces.
  • Calorimetry principle: Heat lost by hot body = Heat gained by cold body (when no heat is lost to surroundings).
  • Latent heat: Heat absorbed/released during change of state without change in temperature.

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. What is specific heat capacity? Give the value for water.
Ans: Specific heat capacity is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/kg·°C, which is the highest among common substances. This is why water is used as a coolant.
Q2. Differentiate between conduction, convection and radiation with examples.
Ans: Conduction: Transfer of heat through a solid without movement of particles. Ex: metal rod heated at one end gets hot at other end. Convection: Transfer through movement of fluid particles. Ex: warm air rises, cool air takes its place (room heater). Radiation: Transfer through electromagnetic waves, no medium needed. Ex: heat from the Sun.
Q3 (Numerical). How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of 5 kg of water from 20°C to 70°C? (s of water = 4200 J/kg·°C)
Ans: Q = m × s × ΔT = 5 × 4200 × (70 − 20) = 5 × 4200 × 50 = 10,50,000 J = 1.05 × 10⁶ J
Q4. Why is cooking utensil made of metal but its handle is made of plastic/wood?
Ans: Metals are good conductors of heat so the utensil body conducts heat quickly to cook food. Wood/plastic are bad conductors (insulators), so the handle remains cool and safe to hold — this prevents burning of hands.
Q5. State the principle of calorimetry.
Ans: When two bodies at different temperatures are mixed (in an insulated system), the heat lost by the hotter body = heat gained by the colder body. That is: m₁s₁ΔT₁ = m₂s₂ΔT₂
Q6. Why does the Earth not become too hot during the day or too cold at night compared to the Moon?
Ans: The Earth has an atmosphere and large amounts of water. Water has high specific heat capacity, so it absorbs a lot of heat during the day without a large temperature rise, and releases heat slowly at night. The atmosphere also traps some heat. The Moon has no atmosphere or water, so it experiences extreme temperature changes.

Arithmetic Progression (AP) — Maths 1

Maths 1

🔑 Key Notes

★ Definitions
  • AP: A sequence in which the difference between any two consecutive terms is constant.
  • Common Difference (d) = any term − previous term = a₂ − a₁ = a₃ − a₂ …
  • First term = a (or a₁)
  • General AP: a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, …
  • If d > 0 → increasing AP  |  d < 0 → decreasing AP  |  d = 0 → constant sequence

📐 Key Formulas

nth term: tₙ = a + (n−1)d

Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n/2 × [2a + (n−1)d]
OR Sₙ = n/2 × (a + l) [l = last term]

Middle term (odd n): t_{(n+1)/2}
If tₙ is known: n = (tₙ − a)/d + 1
★ Important Tips
  • To check if a sequence is AP: check if d is constant throughout.
  • Three terms in AP: take them as (a−d), a, (a+d) — their sum = 3a.
  • Four terms in AP: take them as (a−3d), (a−d), (a+d), (a+3d).
  • Sum of n terms: Sₙ − S_{n−1} = tₙ

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. Find the 20th term of the AP: 3, 7, 11, 15, …
Ans: a = 3, d = 4, n = 20.
t₂₀ = a + (n−1)d = 3 + 19 × 4 = 3 + 76 = 79
Q2. Find the sum of first 25 terms of the AP: 5, 8, 11, 14, …
Ans: a = 5, d = 3, n = 25.
S₂₅ = 25/2 × [2×5 + 24×3] = 25/2 × [10 + 72] = 25/2 × 82 = 25 × 41 = 1025
Q3. Which term of the AP 4, 9, 14, 19, … is 124?
Ans: a = 4, d = 5, tₙ = 124.
124 = 4 + (n−1)×5 → 120 = (n−1)×5 → n−1 = 24 → n = 25. So 124 is the 25th term.
Q4 (Word Problem). A contractor pays ₹200 for the first day, ₹225 for the second day, ₹250 for the third day and so on. What will he pay on the 15th day?
Ans: a = 200, d = 25, n = 15.
t₁₅ = 200 + 14×25 = 200 + 350 = ₹550
Q5. The sum of three consecutive terms of an AP is 27 and their product is 504. Find the terms.
Ans: Let terms be (a−d), a, (a+d).
Sum: 3a = 27 → a = 9.
Product: (9−d) × 9 × (9+d) = 504 → 9(81−d²) = 504 → 81−d² = 56 → d² = 25 → d = ±5.
Terms: 4, 9, 14 or 14, 9, 4.
Q6. Find the sum of all multiples of 7 between 1 and 200.
Ans: AP: 7, 14, 21, … 196. a = 7, d = 7, l = 196.
n = (196−7)/7 + 1 = 28.
S = 28/2 × (7 + 196) = 14 × 203 = 2842

Life Processes Part 2 — Science 2

Science 2

Topics: Control & Coordination (Nervous + Hormonal) · Reproduction

🧠 Control & Coordination

★ Nervous System
  • Neurons are the basic structural and functional units of the nervous system.
  • Parts of neuron: Dendrite → Cell body (Cyton) → Axon → Nerve endings
  • CNS (Central Nervous System) = Brain + Spinal cord
  • PNS (Peripheral Nervous System) = Cranial nerves + Spinal nerves
  • Reflex action: Automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus. Path = Reflex arc.
  • Reflex arc: Receptor → Afferent nerve → Spinal cord → Efferent nerve → Effector
★ Endocrine System (Chemical Coordination)
GlandHormoneFunction
Pituitary (master gland)GH, TSH, FSH, LHGrowth, controls other glands
ThyroidThyroxineControls metabolism
Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans)Insulin, GlucagonRegulates blood sugar
AdrenalAdrenalineEmergency hormone (fight or flight)
Testes/OvariesTestosterone/EstrogenSecondary sexual characteristics

🌱 Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

  • Auxin – promotes cell elongation, phototropism
  • Gibberellin – promotes stem elongation, seed germination
  • Cytokinin – promotes cell division
  • Abscisic acid (ABA) – inhibits growth, promotes dormancy (stress hormone)
  • Ethylene – promotes fruit ripening

🔬 Reproduction

★ Types
  • Asexual reproduction: Single parent, no gametes. Types: Fission (Amoeba, Paramecium), Budding (Hydra, Yeast), Fragmentation (Spirogyra), Spore formation (Rhizopus), Vegetative propagation (plants).
  • Sexual reproduction: Two parents, involves gametes (male + female). Results in genetic variation.
  • In humans: Male gamete = sperm; Female gamete = ovum. Fertilisation occurs in fallopian tube.
  • Pollination in plants: Self-pollination (same flower) or Cross-pollination (different flower/plant).

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. What is a reflex action? Give one example and explain the reflex arc.
Ans: A reflex action is a sudden, automatic and involuntary response of the body to a stimulus, without conscious thought. Example: Withdrawing hand on touching a hot object. Reflex arc: Stimulus → Receptor (skin) → Sensory/Afferent nerve → Spinal cord (nerve centre) → Motor/Efferent nerve → Effector (muscle) → Response.
Q2. What is the role of adrenaline hormone? Why is it called the emergency hormone?
Ans: Adrenaline is secreted by the adrenal glands in emergency situations (fear, anger, excitement). It: increases heart rate, raises blood pressure, dilates pupils, increases blood sugar, prepares the body for 'fight or flight'. Because it prepares the body for immediate response to danger, it is called the emergency hormone.
Q3. Why is the pituitary gland called the 'master gland'?
Ans: The pituitary gland is called the master gland because it secretes hormones (like TSH, FSH, LH) that control and regulate the secretion of hormones from other endocrine glands such as the thyroid, gonads and adrenal glands.
Q4. What is the function of insulin? What happens when it is not produced sufficiently?
Ans: Insulin (secreted by beta cells of islets of Langerhans in pancreas) lowers blood glucose levels by promoting the conversion of glucose to glycogen in the liver. When insulin is insufficient, blood glucose levels rise, causing Diabetes mellitus.
Q5. Name and explain any two asexual reproduction methods with examples.
Ans: 1. Binary Fission: The parent organism splits into two equal daughter cells. Example: Amoeba, Paramecium. 2. Budding: A small outgrowth (bud) develops on the parent body, grows, and detaches to form a new organism. Example: Hydra, Yeast.
Q6. What is phototropism? How do auxins explain it?
Ans: Phototropism is the growth movement of a plant in response to light. When light falls from one side, auxin migrates to the shaded side, causing greater cell elongation on that side, making the shoot bend towards light (positive phototropism). Roots show negative phototropism.

Probability — Maths 1

Maths 1

🔑 Key Notes

★ Basic Definitions
  • Experiment: An action with a well-defined outcome (e.g., tossing a coin).
  • Sample Space (S): Set of all possible outcomes.
  • Event (E): Any subset of the sample space.
  • Probability of event E: P(E) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of outcomes
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1 always.   P(E) + P(E') = 1
  • Impossible event: P = 0.   Sure event: P = 1.

🎯 Quick Reference — Sample Spaces

Coin (1): S = {H, T} → n(S) = 2
Coin (2): S = {HH, HT, TH, TT} → n(S) = 4
Die (1): S = {1,2,3,4,5,6} → n(S) = 6
Two Dice: n(S) = 36
Cards (1 pack): n(S) = 52 (4 suits × 13 cards each)
→ Suits: Hearts♥, Diamonds♦ (Red) | Spades♠, Clubs♣ (Black)
→ Face cards: Jack, Queen, King (3 per suit = 12 total)
→ Ace cards: 4 total (1 per suit)

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. A die is thrown once. Find P(getting an even number) and P(getting a prime number).
Ans: S = {1,2,3,4,5,6}, n(S) = 6.
Even numbers: {2,4,6} → P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2
Prime numbers: {2,3,5} → P(prime) = 3/6 = 1/2
Q2. Two coins are tossed simultaneously. Find P(at least one head).
Ans: S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}, n(S) = 4.
At least one head: {HH, HT, TH} → 3 outcomes.
P(at least one H) = 3/4   OR   P = 1 − P(no head) = 1 − 1/4 = 3/4
Q3. A card is drawn at random from a pack of 52 cards. Find P(a king), P(a red card), P(a face card).
Ans: n(S) = 52.
Kings = 4 → P(king) = 4/52 = 1/13
Red cards = 26 → P(red) = 26/52 = 1/2
Face cards = 12 → P(face card) = 12/52 = 3/13
Q4. Two dice are thrown together. Find P(sum = 7) and P(sum = 11).
Ans: n(S) = 36.
Sum = 7: {(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)} → 6 outcomes → P = 6/36 = 1/6
Sum = 11: {(5,6),(6,5)} → 2 outcomes → P = 2/36 = 1/18
Q5. A bag contains 3 red, 5 blue and 2 green balls. A ball is drawn at random. Find P(red), P(not green), P(blue or green).
Ans: Total = 10.
P(red) = 3/10  |  P(not green) = 8/10 = 4/5  |  P(blue or green) = 7/10
Q6. The probability of winning a game is 0.4. What is the probability of losing it?
Ans: P(lose) = 1 − P(win) = 1 − 0.4 = 0.6

Refraction of Light & Lenses — Science 1

Science 1

💡 Refraction of Light — Key Notes

★ Definition & Laws
  • Refraction: Bending of light when it passes from one medium to another due to change in speed.
  • Laws of Refraction:
    1. Incident ray, refracted ray and normal — all lie in the same plane.
    2. Snell's Law: sin i / sin r = constant = n (refractive index)
  • Light bends towards normal when going from rarer to denser medium (speed decreases).
  • Light bends away from normal when going from denser to rarer medium (speed increases).

📐 Refractive Index

n = sin i / sin r (Snell's Law)
n = Speed of light in vacuum (c) / Speed of light in medium (v)
n = c / v
n of water ≈ 1.33 | n of glass ≈ 1.5 | n of diamond ≈ 2.42
★ Total Internal Reflection (TIR)
  • Occurs when light travels from denser to rarer medium AND angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle.
  • Examples: Optical fibres, Sparkling of diamond, Mirage.
  • Critical angle of glass ≈ 42°, Diamond ≈ 24° (high refractive index → small critical angle → more TIR → more sparkle).

🔭 Lenses — Key Notes

★ Types & Key Terms
  • Convex lens: Converging lens — thicker at centre. Used in cameras, magnifying glasses, eye correction (hypermetropia).
  • Concave lens: Diverging lens — thinner at centre. Used in eye correction (myopia).
  • Optical centre (O): Centre of the lens.
  • Principal focus (F): Point where parallel rays converge (convex) or appear to diverge from (concave).
  • Focal length (f): Distance from optical centre to principal focus. +ve for convex, −ve for concave.

📐 Lens Formulas

Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
Magnification: m = v/u = h'/h
Power of lens: P = 1/f (f in metres, P in Dioptre D)

Sign convention: distances measured from optical centre;
distances in direction of incident light = +ve; opposite = −ve

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. State Snell's Law of refraction. Define refractive index.
Ans: Snell's Law: The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media. This constant is called the refractive index (n): n = sin i / sin r. Refractive index is also equal to the ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in the medium: n = c/v.
Q2. What is total internal reflection? State its two conditions and two applications.
Ans: TIR occurs when all light is reflected back into the same medium without any refraction. Conditions: (1) Light must travel from denser to rarer medium. (2) Angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle. Applications: Optical fibres (internet/communication), sparkling of diamond, mirage formation.
Q3 (Numerical). A convex lens has focal length 10 cm. Object is placed 30 cm from lens. Find image distance and magnification.
Ans: f = +10 cm, u = −30 cm.
1/v − 1/u = 1/f → 1/v − 1/(−30) = 1/10 → 1/v = 1/10 − 1/30 = 2/30 → v = +15 cm (real, on other side).
m = v/u = 15/(−30) = −0.5 (real, inverted, diminished).
Q4. What is the power of a lens? A doctor prescribes −2.5 D lens. What type is it and what is its focal length?
Ans: Power of a lens (P) = 1/f (f in metres), unit = Dioptre (D). Positive power = convex; Negative power = concave. Here P = −2.5 D → Concave lens. f = 1/P = 1/(−2.5) = −0.4 m = −40 cm.
Q5. A pencil placed in water appears bent at the surface. Why?
Ans: When light travels from water (denser medium) to air (rarer medium) it bends away from the normal. The eye traces back these rays in a straight line, so the submerged part of the pencil appears bent/displaced at the water surface. This is due to refraction of light.

Circle — Maths 2

Maths 2

🔑 Key Terms

★ Definitions
  • Circle: Locus of all points equidistant from a fixed point (centre).
  • Chord: Line segment joining any two points on a circle. Diameter is the longest chord.
  • Tangent: A line that touches the circle at exactly one point (point of tangency). It is always perpendicular to the radius at that point.
  • Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two points.
  • Arc: Part of the circumference. Minor arc (smaller) and Major arc (larger).
  • Sector: Region between two radii and the arc.

📜 Important Theorems

★ Theorems to Know
  • Theorem 1: The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency. (∠OAT = 90°)
  • Theorem 2: Two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal in length. (PA = PB if P is external point)
  • Theorem 3: The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
  • Theorem 4: Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.
  • Theorem 5: The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended at any point on the remaining part of the circle. (∠AOB = 2∠ACB)
  • Theorem 6: Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.
  • Theorem 7: Angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  • Theorem 8 (Cyclic quadrilateral): Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary (sum = 180°).

📐 Formulas

Circumference = 2πr
Area of circle = πr²
Area of sector = (θ/360) × πr²
Length of arc = (θ/360) × 2πr
Tangent–Radius angle = 90°
PA² = PO² − r² (length of tangent from external point P)

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. Prove that the tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
Ans: Let O be the centre of a circle and T be the point of tangency of tangent XY. Take any point P on the tangent (other than T). Since P lies outside the circle, OP > OT (radius). This is true for every point on the tangent. Thus OT is the shortest distance from O to line XY. The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular. Therefore OT ⊥ XY, i.e., radius ⊥ tangent at point of contact. ✓
Q2. Two tangents PA and PB are drawn from external point P. Prove PA = PB.
Ans: In △OAP and △OBP: OA = OB (radii), OP = OP (common), ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius). By RHS congruence: △OAP ≅ △OBP. Therefore PA = PB. ✓
Q3. If PA and PB are tangents from P and ∠APB = 60°, find ∠OAP and ∠AOB.
Ans: ∠OAP = 90° (tangent–radius). In quadrilateral OAPB: ∠AOB + ∠OAP + ∠APB + ∠OBP = 360° → ∠AOB + 90° + 60° + 90° = 360° → ∠AOB = 120°. Also ∠OAP = 90°.
Q4. State and verify: angle in a semicircle is 90°.
Ans: Theorem: The angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circle is 90°. If AB is a diameter and C is any point on the circle, then ∠ACB = 90°. This follows from the theorem that the central angle (180°) is double the inscribed angle, so ∠ACB = 180°/2 = 90°.
Q5. The distance from external point P to the centre O is 10 cm and radius = 6 cm. Find the length of the tangent from P.
Ans: PA² = PO² − r² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64. PA = 8 cm.
Q6. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral where ∠A = 80°. Find ∠C.
Ans: In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles are supplementary. ∠A + ∠C = 180° → 80° + ∠C = 180° → ∠C = 100°.

Environmental Management — Science 2

Science 2

🌍 Ecosystem — Key Notes

★ Core Concepts
  • Ecosystem: A system formed by the interaction of living organisms (biotic) with their non-living environment (abiotic).
  • Producers: Green plants — make their own food by photosynthesis.
  • Consumers: Animals — Primary (herbivores), Secondary (carnivores), Tertiary.
  • Decomposers: Bacteria, fungi — break down dead matter, recycle nutrients.
  • Food chain: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk
  • Food web: Interconnected network of food chains.
  • 10% energy law: Only 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next.

⚠️ Pollution

TypeMain CausesEffectsControl
Air PollutionVehicles, industries, burning fossil fuelsRespiratory diseases, acid rain, global warmingCNG vehicles, scrubbers, afforestation

🌿 Conservation

★ Key Points
  • Biodiversity: Variety of living organisms in a region. Essential for ecosystem stability.
  • Deforestation: Removal of forests → loss of habitat, soil erosion, reduced rainfall, global warming.
  • Afforestation: Planting trees — best solution to many environmental problems.
  • Wildlife sanctuaries & National parks: Protected areas for wildlife conservation.
  • 3 R's: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle — for waste management.
  • Global Warming: Rise in Earth's average temperature due to increase in greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O). Leads to melting of glaciers, rise in sea level, climate change.
  • Ozone layer depletion: CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) destroy ozone (O₃) layer → harmful UV rays reach Earth's surface → skin cancer, eye damage.

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1. What is an ecosystem? Explain its biotic and abiotic components.
Ans: An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with their physical environment. Biotic components: all living organisms — producers (plants), consumers (animals), decomposers (bacteria, fungi). Abiotic components: non-living factors — sunlight, temperature, water, soil, air, minerals.
Q2. Explain the 10% energy law with a food chain example.
Ans: Only 10% of energy at one trophic level is available to the next level; 90% is lost as heat. Example: If grass has 10,000 J → grasshopper gets 1000 J → frog gets 100 J → snake gets 10 J → hawk gets 1 J. This explains why food chains rarely have more than 4–5 levels.
Q3. What is acid rain? How is it formed and what are its effects?
Ans: Acid rain is precipitation (rain, snow) with pH below 5.6. It forms when sulphur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from burning fossil fuels react with water vapour in the atmosphere to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃. Effects: damages forests, aquatic life, corrodes buildings and monuments, makes soil acidic.
Q4. What is global warming? What are its causes and effects?
Ans: Global warming is the gradual increase in Earth's average surface temperature. Causes: Increased CO₂, CH₄, N₂O (greenhouse gases) from burning fossil fuels, deforestation, industries trap more heat in the atmosphere. Effects: Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers, rise in sea levels, flooding of coastal areas, extreme weather events, extinction of species.
Q5. What is the importance of the ozone layer? What causes its depletion?
Ans: The ozone layer (in the stratosphere) absorbs harmful UV radiation from the sun, protecting life on Earth. Depletion: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — used in refrigerators, air conditioners, aerosol sprays — release chlorine atoms that react with and destroy ozone molecules. Effects: increased UV radiation causes skin cancer, cataracts, weakens immune system, harms crops.
Q6. State any four measures to conserve biodiversity.
Ans: (1) Establishing wildlife sanctuaries and national parks to protect habitats. (2) Afforestation — planting trees to restore forests. (3) Banning hunting/poaching of endangered species. (4) Reducing pollution and use of pesticides. (5) Practicing sustainable agriculture. (6) Public awareness and education about conservation.
