5D
(Ch.9) Short note: Homologous series in carbon compounds.
3
Definition
A homologous series is a group of organic compounds with same general formula, same functional group, similar chemical properties, and differing by CH₂ (14 mass units) successively.
Characteristics
  • Same general formula (e.g. Alkanes: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂)
  • Each member differs by −CH₂− group
  • Similar chemical properties, gradually varying physical properties
  • Can be prepared by same general method
Example
Alkane series: Methane (CH₄), Ethane (C₂H₆), Propane (C₃H₈), Butane (C₄H₁₀). Each differs by CH₂ = 14 mass units.
∴ Homologous series: same formula type, same functional group, differ by CH₂ (14u).
Q.6 — Answer the following questions in detail (Attempt any TWO) [2 × 4 = 8 Marks]
6A
(Ch.1 & 10) Explain Kepler's laws of planetary motion. How did it help in space exploration?
4
Law 1 — Orbit
Law of Ellipses: Each planet revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one of the two foci.
Law 2 — Area
Law of Equal Areas: The line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal time intervals. Planet moves faster near the Sun (perihelion) and slower when farther (aphelion).
Law 3 — Period
Law of Periods: Square of the period of revolution (T²) is proportional to cube of the mean distance from Sun (r³). T² ∝ r³ or T²/r³ = constant
Role in Space
  • Kepler's laws helped calculate orbits of artificial satellites
  • Geostationary orbit (36,000 km) calculated using T² ∝ r³
  • Trajectory of space probes like Mangalyaan, Chandrayaan planned using these laws
  • Transfer orbits (Hohmann transfer) between planets use Law 2 and 3
∴ Kepler: Ellipse + Equal areas + T²∝r³. Foundation of satellite orbit calculations.
6B
(Ch.3 & 8) Explain types of chemical reactions with examples from metallurgy.
4
1. Oxidation
Addition of oxygen / removal of hydrogen. In metallurgy: Roasting converts sulphide ores to oxides by oxidation. 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂
2. Reduction
Removal of oxygen / addition of hydrogen. In smelting: oxide ore reduced by coke. ZnO + C → Zn + CO (Carbon acts as reducing agent)
3. Displacement
More reactive metal displaces less reactive. e.g. Thermite reaction: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe (Al displaces Fe — used in welding)
4. Decomposition
Electrolytic decomposition in refining. Al₂O₃ → 2Al + 3O (electrolysis). Also: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ in cement manufacture.
5. Redox
Oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously. In metallurgy — smelting involves redox reactions where carbon is oxidised (C→CO₂) and metal oxide is reduced (MO→M).
∴ Roasting=oxidation; Smelting=reduction; Thermite=displacement; Refining=electrolysis/decomposition.
6C
(Ch.5 & 6) Explain the phenomenon of total internal reflection and its applications.
4
Definition
When light travels from denser medium to rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, the light is completely reflected back into the denser medium — Total Internal Reflection (TIR).
Conditions
  • Light must travel from denser → rarer medium
  • Angle of incidence must be > critical angle
Critical angle
Angle at which refracted ray goes along the surface (r=90°). For glass: ~42°. sin C = 1/n where n = refractive index.
Applications
  • Optical fibres: Light signals travel long distances with negligible loss (internet, medical endoscopy)
  • Mirages: Due to TIR in hot air layers near ground (desert/road illusion)
  • Sparkling of diamond: Very small critical angle (~24°) causes multiple TIR → brilliant sparkle
  • Periscope, binoculars: Use prisms based on TIR principle
∴ TIR: denser→rarer, angle > critical angle. Used in optical fibre, mirages, diamond sparkle.
6D
(Ch.9) Describe ethanol (C₂H₅OH) — preparation, properties and uses.
4
Preparation
  • Fermentation: Yeast acts on glucose. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (Zymase enzyme, 30°C)
  • Hydration of ethene: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (H₂SO₄, 300°C, high pressure)
Physical Properties
Colourless liquid | Pleasant smell | BP = 78.4°C | Miscible with water in all proportions | Good solvent.
Chemical Properties
  • Burns: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O (blue flame, exothermic)
  • Dehydration with H₂SO₄ at 170°C → ethene (C₂H₄)
  • Oxidation with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ → acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
Uses
Alcoholic beverages | Solvent (medicines, perfumes) | Antiseptic (70% solution) | Fuel (ethanol blending with petrol) | Manufacture of vinegar, ether, esters.
∴ Ethanol: fermentation of glucose. Burns cleanly. Used as fuel, solvent, antiseptic.

3

F = Gm₁m₂/d²

= (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 50000) / 100

7D
(Ch.6 & 7) A concave mirror has a focal length of 20 cm. An object is placed 60 cm in front of it. Find the image distance, magnification and nature of image using mirror formula.
3
Given
f = −20 cm (concave), u = −60 cm (sign convention)
Mirror formula
1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Solution
1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−20) − 1/(−60) = −1/20 + 1/60 = −3/60 + 1/60 = −2/60
v = −60/2 = −30 cm
Magnification
m = −v/u = −(−30)/(−60) = −0.5
|m| = 0.5 < 1 → diminished. m is negative → inverted
Nature of image
v is negative → image is in front of mirror → Real, Inverted, Diminished
∴ v = −30 cm (real), m = −0.5 (inverted, diminished)
