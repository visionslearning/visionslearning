Extract — From: 1.6 Tiger Hills — General Ved Prakash Malik (Adapted)

The soldiers were exhausted. For days they had climbed steep ridges, crossed frozen streams and endured temperatures that would stop most men in their tracks. Their boots were torn, their rations were almost finished, and the cold cut through their jackets like a knife. But not one of them spoke of turning back.

The Tiger Hills loomed above them — a strategic peak that both sides wanted desperately. Whoever held the high ground, held the advantage. The commanding officer had given them a simple instruction: "Reach the top. Hold it. Come back alive."

Private Suresh, barely nineteen years old, was the first to plant the flag on the summit. When the radio crackled with news of their success, a cheer went up from the troops far below. The men on the summit looked at each other, too tired to celebrate properly, but in every face was the same quiet, fierce pride. They had done it.

Wars are not won only by weapons. They are won by the will of ordinary people who, in extraordinary circumstances, discover that they are capable of more than they ever imagined.

Q.2 — Comprehension: Unseen Passage12 Marks
Q.2A — Read the following passage carefully and do all activities.[10 Marks]
Unseen Passage

There is a kind of courage that does not announce itself with drums and flags. It is the courage of a schoolteacher who walks four kilometres every day in the rain to reach her students. It is the courage of a young doctor who chooses to work in a remote village when she could earn three times as much in a city. It is the courage of a farmer who replants his field after three failed harvests, still believing that the next season will be better.

This kind of courage is rarely celebrated. No medal is given for it. No speech is made. But without it, ordinary life as we know it would not function. Schools would close. Hospitals would empty. Fields would lie barren.

Courage, in its deepest form, is not the absence of fear. It is the decision to continue despite fear — to show up, day after day, for the people and the work you believe in. It does not require a battlefield. It only requires a reason worth fighting for.

The world does not need more heroes. It needs more people who quietly do what needs to be done.

(A) brave people never feel fear(B) courage means ignoring danger (C) real courage is acting even when afraid(D) fear and courage cannot coexist

Poem: Upon Westminster Bridge — William Wordsworth (Textbook 2.4)
Earth has not anything to show more fair:
Dull would he be of soul who could pass by
A sight so touching in its majesty:
This City now doth, like a garment, wear
The beauty of the morning; silent, bare,
Ships, towers, domes, theatres, and temples lie
Open unto the fields, and to the sky;
All bright and glittering in the smokeless air.

Never did sun more beautifully steep
In his first splendour, valley, rock, or hill;
Ne'er saw I, never felt, a calm so deep!
The river glideth at his own sweet will:
Dear God! the very houses seem asleep;
And all that mighty heart is lying still!
