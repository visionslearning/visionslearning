⏰ 1. Tenses Class 11 & 12
All 12 Tenses — Structure & Examples
TenseStructureExample

⭐ Time words: Yesterday/ago/last = Past | Since/for/already/just/yet = Perfect | Tomorrow/next = Future | Now/at present = Continuous
🔄 2. Active and Passive Voice Class 11 & 12
Rules for Changing Voice
Active: Subject + Verb + Object
Passive: Object + be-verb + V3 + by + Subject

Steps:
1. Object of active → Subject of passive
2. Subject of active → goes after "by" at the end
3. Change main verb → V3 (past participle)
4. Add appropriate form of "be" (is/am/are/was/were/has been/have been/will be etc.)
TenseActivePassive
Simple PresentShe writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
Simple PastShe wrote a letter.A letter was written by her.
Simple FutureShe will write a letter.A letter will be written by her.
Present ContinuousShe is writing a letter.A letter is being written by her.
Past ContinuousShe was writing a letter.A letter was being written by her.
Present PerfectShe has written a letter.A letter has been written by her.
Past PerfectShe had written a letter.A letter had been written by her.
⭐ Pronoun changes: I→me, he→him, she→her, they→them, we→us, you→you | For subject in passive: me→I, him→he, her→she, them→they, us→we
Imperative: "Open the door." → "Let the door be opened."
Question: "Did she finish the work?" → "Was the work finished by her?"
If no object: Cannot be changed to passive. (e.g., "She laughed.")
🗣️ 3. Direct and Indirect Speech (Narration) Class 11 & 12
Rules for Changing Narration
Tense Changes (Backshift):
Simple Present → Simple Past
Present Continuous → Past Continuous
Present Perfect → Past Perfect
Simple Past → Past Perfect
Past Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous
will → would | can → could | may → might | shall → should
Pronoun Changes: I→he/she | we→they | my→his/her | our→their | you→he/she/they (depends on context)

Time/Place Changes: now→then | today→that day | yesterday→the previous day | tomorrow→the next day | here→there | this→that | these→those | ago→before | last night→the previous night
Reporting VerbDirectIndirect
said / told"I am happy," he said.He said that he was happy.
asked (question)"Are you well?" she asked.She asked if/whether he was well.
asked (wh-question)"Where do you live?" he asked.He asked where he lived.
ordered/commanded"Sit down!" the teacher said.The teacher ordered them to sit down.
requested"Please help me," she said.She requested him to help her.
advised"You should study hard," he said.He advised me to study hard.
exclaimed (joy)"What a beautiful day!" she said.She exclaimed with joy that it was a very beautiful day.
exclaimed (sorrow)"Alas! I have failed," he said.He exclaimed with sorrow that he had failed.

🔗 5. Clauses Class 11 & 12
Types of Clauses
  • Main/Independent Clause: Complete thought, can stand alone. Example: She went home.
  • Subordinate/Dependent Clause: Cannot stand alone, depends on main clause.
Types of Subordinate Clauses
TypeFunctionConjunctionsExample
Noun ClauseActs as noun (subject/object)that, what, whether, ifI know that he is honest.
Adjective ClauseModifies a nounwho, whom, which, that, whoseThe boy who won the race is my friend.
Adverb Clause of TimeTells whenwhen, while, after, before, since, untilShe left when I arrived.
Adverb Clause of ConditionTells conditionif, unless, provided thatI will come if you invite me.
Adverb Clause of CauseTells reasonbecause, since, asHe failed because he did not study.
Adverb Clause of ResultTells resultso...that, such...thatShe was so tired that she slept at once.
Adverb Clause of ComparisonComparesas...as, thanShe is taller than I am.
✂️ 6. Transformation of Sentences Class 11 & 12
Types of Transformation
1. Affirmative ↔ Negative
Remove/add "not" and change verb if needed.
Aff: Everyone was present. → Neg: No one was absent.
Aff: She is always late. → Neg: She is never on time.
2. Assertive ↔ Interrogative
Assert: He is a good student. → Inter: Is he not a good student? (or) Isn't he a good student?
Assert: Nobody can deny this. → Inter: Who can deny this?
3. Simple ↔ Compound ↔ Complex
Simple: Despite being tired, she worked hard.
Compound: She was tired but she worked hard.
Complex: Although she was tired, she worked hard.
4. Degrees of Comparison
Positive: No other boy in the class is as tall as Ravi.
Comparative: Ravi is taller than any other boy in the class.
Superlative: Ravi is the tallest boy in the class.
5. Exclamatory ↔ Assertive
Exclamatory: What a beautiful flower this is!
Assertive: This is a very beautiful flower.
Exclamatory: How clever she is!
Assertive: She is very clever.
6. Too...to → So...that
He is too weak to walk. → He is so weak that he cannot walk.
She is too young to drive. → She is so young that she cannot drive.
📝 7. Articles (a, an, the) Class 11 & 12
Rules for Using Articles
ArticleUseExample
aBefore consonant sound, first mention, one of manya dog, a university, a European
anBefore vowel soundan apple, an hour, an honest man, an MBA
theSpecific/known thing; superlatives; unique things; names of seas/rivers/rangesthe Sun, the best, the Himalayas, the Amazon
No article used with:
• Names of people, cities, countries (generally): India, Rahul, Mumbai
• Languages: English, Hindi, French
• Sports: He plays cricket.
• Meals (generally): We have breakfast at 8.
• Abstract nouns used generally: Knowledge is power.
Tricky cases:
a uniform (starts with 'yu' sound) | an hour (h is silent) | an MLA (M sounds like 'em')
the United States | the Netherlands | the UAE (countries with 'United/States/Republic' → use the)

🔗 9. Conjunctions Class 11 & 12
Types of Conjunctions
TypeExamplesUse
Coordinatingand, but, or, nor, so, yet, forJoins equal parts: She sings and dances.
Subordinatingbecause, although, if, when, since, unless, until, while, as, that, thoughJoins unequal parts: I will come if you invite me.
Correlativeboth...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also, whether...or, as...asUsed in pairs: Either she or her sister will come.
Common mistakes:
❌ Although he was tired, but he worked. → ✅ Although he was tired, he worked. (Don't use 'but' with 'although')
❌ Because he was late, so he missed the bus. → ✅ Because he was late, he missed the bus. (Don't use 'so' with 'because')
🔢 10. Determiners Class 11 & 12
Types of Determiners
TypeWordsExample
Articlesa, an, theThe dog is loyal.
Demonstrativethis, that, these, thoseThis book is mine.
Possessivemy, your, his, her, its, our, theirHer answer was correct.
Quantifierssome, any, many, much, few, little, a lot of, enough, all, both, half, several, noFew students passed.
Numeralsone, two, first, second, double, twiceThree students were absent.
Interrogativewhich, what, whoseWhich book do you want?
Distributiveeach, every, either, neitherEach student got a prize.
few / a few / the few:
few = negative (almost none): Few students came. (bad)
a few = positive (some): A few students came. (good)
the few = specific small group: The few students who came worked hard.

little / a little / the little: Same pattern for uncountable nouns.
✏️ 11. Punctuation Class 11 & 12
Rules for Punctuation
MarkUseExample
Full stop (.)End of sentence; abbreviationsShe went home. / Mr. Sharma
Comma (,)List; pause; after intro phrase; before conjunctions in compound sentencesI bought apples, mangoes, and bananas.
Question mark (?)Direct questionWhere do you live?
Exclamation (!)Strong feeling, exclamationsWhat a beautiful day!
Colon (:)Introduce list/explanationHe has three subjects: Maths, Science, English.
Semicolon (;)Connect related clausesShe studied hard; she passed.
Apostrophe (')Possession; contractionsRavi's book. / don't = do not
Inverted commas ("")Direct speech; titles; special wordsShe said, "Come in."
Hyphen (-)Compound words; word divisionwell-known, twenty-one
Dash (—)Strong break; explanationHe had one goal — success.

Common Errors — Wrong vs Right
❌ WrongShe is more better than him.
✅ RightShe is better than him.
❌ WrongHe went to buy some furnitures.
✅ RightHe went to buy some furniture. (uncountable)
❌ WrongI am knowing him since years.
✅ RightI have known him for years.
❌ WrongShe told that she was hungry.
✅ RightShe said that she was hungry.
❌ WrongEach of the boys have a book.
✅ RightEach of the boys has a book.
❌ WrongHe is senior than me.
✅ RightHe is senior to me. (Latin comparatives: senior/junior/superior/inferior/prior + to)
❌ WrongBetween you and I, it is wrong.
✅ RightBetween you and me, it is wrong. (use objective pronoun after preposition)
❌ WrongOne should love his country.
✅ RightOne should love one's country.
