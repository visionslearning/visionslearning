State and Explain the Laws
Q1a
Law of Definite Proportion
Statement
A pure chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the same fixed ratio by mass, regardless of source or method of preparation.
Example
Water (H₂O) always contains H:O = 1:8 by mass, whether made in lab or found in nature.
Proposed by
Joseph Louis Proust (1799)
∴ Compound = Fixed ratio of elements by mass, always.
Q1b
Law of Conservation of Mass
Statement
Matter (mass) can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. The total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products.
Example
C + O₂ → CO₂   12g C + 32g O₂ = 44g CO₂
Proposed by
Antoine Lavoisier (1789)
∴ Mass of reactants = Mass of products (always).
Q1c
Avogadro's Law
Statement
Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of molecules.
Mathematically
V ∝ n (at constant T and P)   →   V/n = constant
Avogadro's Number
1 mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
Example
1 mole H₂ and 1 mole O₂ at STP both occupy 22.4 L.
∴ Equal volumes of gases (same T, P) = same number of molecules.
2
Distinguish Between
Q2a
Aromatic vs Aliphatic Compounds
Aromatic
  • Contain benzene ring (cyclic, planar)
  • Have alternating double bonds (delocalized π electrons)
  • Undergo substitution reactions
  • Example: Benzene, Toluene, Naphthalene
Aliphatic
  • Open-chain (straight or branched) structures
  • No benzene ring
  • Undergo addition reactions (if unsaturated)
  • Example: Methane, Ethane, Propene
∴ Key difference: Benzene ring (aromatic) vs open chain (aliphatic).
Q2b
Homolysis vs Heterolysis
Homolysis
  • Bond breaks equally — each atom gets one electron
  • Forms free radicals (neutral, with unpaired electron)
  • Occurs in non-polar conditions, UV light
  • A:B → A• + B•
Heterolysis
  • Bond breaks unequally — one atom gets both electrons
  • Forms ions (carbocation or carbanion)
  • Occurs in polar solvents
  • A:B → A⁺ + B⁻
∴ Homolysis → free radicals; Heterolysis → ions.
Q2c
Electrophiles vs Nucleophiles
Electrophile
  • Electron-deficient species — loves electrons
  • Accepts electron pair
  • Attacks electron-rich centres
  • Examples: H⁺, Cl⁺, NO₂⁺, BF₃, AlCl₃, carbocations (R⁺)
Nucleophile
  • Electron-rich species — loves nucleus (positive centre)
  • Donates electron pair
  • Attacks electron-deficient centres
  • Examples: OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O, carbanions (R⁻)
∴ Electrophile = electron seeker; Nucleophile = nucleus seeker.
Q2d
Carbocation vs Carbanion
Carbocation
  • Carbon bears a positive charge (C⁺)
  • Carbon has only 6 electrons (electron-deficient)
  • sp² hybridized, planar structure
  • Acts as electrophile
  • Example: CH₃⁺ (methyl carbocation)
Carbanion
  • Carbon bears a negative charge (C⁻)
  • Carbon has 8 electrons (electron-rich, lone pair)
  • sp³ hybridized, pyramidal
  • Acts as nucleophile
  • Example: CH₃⁻ (methyl carbanion)
∴ Carbocation = C⁺ (electrophile); Carbanion = C⁻ (nucleophile).

Structural Formulae of Functional Groups
Q3
Write structural formula for: Cyanide, Ketone, Carboxylic Acid, Alcohol, Ester
a) Cyanide
Functional group: −C≡N   Example: CH₃−C≡N (Methyl cyanide / Acetonitrile)
b) Ketone
Functional group: C=O (between two carbon atoms)   Example: CH₃−C(=O)−CH₃ (Acetone/Propanone)
c) Carboxylic Acid
Functional group: −COOH   Example: CH₃−COOH (Acetic acid / Ethanoic acid)
d) Alcohol
Functional group: −OH   Example: CH₃−OH (Methanol) ; C₂H₅−OH (Ethanol)
e) Ester
Functional group: −COO−   Example: CH₃−COO−C₂H₅ (Ethyl acetate)
∴ Functional groups determine chemical properties of organic compounds.
4
Define the Terms
Q4.1
Molarity
Definition
Number of moles of solute dissolved per litre (dm³) of solution. M = moles of solute / volume of solution (L) Unit: mol/L or M
∴ M = n/V(L)   e.g. 1M NaCl = 1 mole NaCl in 1 litre solution.
Q4.2
Normality
Definition
Number of gram equivalents of solute dissolved per litre of solution. N = gram equivalents / volume (L) Unit: N (equivalents/L)
∴ N = Molarity × n-factor (valence factor).
Q4.3
Mole Fraction
Definition
Ratio of moles of one component to the total moles of all components in the mixture. χA = nA / (nA + nB) Dimensionless. Sum of all mole fractions = 1.
∴ Mole fraction = moles of component / total moles. Always between 0 and 1.
Q4.4
Crystallization
Definition
A purification technique where an impure solid is dissolved in a hot solvent and then allowed to cool slowly so that pure crystals separate out. Used to purify substances like sugar, salt, alum.
∴ Crystallization = dissolve in hot solvent → cool → pure crystals form.
Q4.5
One AMU (Atomic Mass Unit)
Definition
One amu is defined as exactly 1/12th of the mass of one atom of Carbon-12 isotope. 1 amu = 1.66054 × 10⁻²⁴ g = 1.66054 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
∴ 1 amu = 1/12 × mass of one ¹²C atom.
Q4.6
Molality
Definition
Number of moles of solute dissolved per kilogram of solvent (not solution). m = moles of solute / mass of solvent (kg) Unit: mol/kg. Independent of temperature.
∴ m = n / mass of solvent (kg). Does NOT change with temperature.
Q4.7
Formula Mass
Definition
Sum of atomic masses of all atoms present in the empirical or molecular formula of a compound. Used for ionic compounds. Example: NaCl → 23+35.5 = 58.5 g/mol
∴ Formula mass = sum of atomic masses of all atoms in formula.
Q4.8
Wavelength (λ)
Definition
The distance between two consecutive crests (or troughs) of a wave. Symbol: λ (lambda). Unit: metres (m), nm, or Å. Related to frequency: c = νλ where c = speed of light.
∴ λ = c/ν   Shorter wavelength = higher frequency = higher energy.
5
Explain the Following
Q5.1
Quantum Numbers
n (Principal)
Shell number. Indicates energy level and size. n = 1,2,3... Maximum electrons = 2n².
l (Azimuthal)
Subshell shape. l = 0 to (n-1). l=0(s), l=1(p), l=2(d), l=3(f).
m (Magnetic)
Orientation of orbital in space. m = −l to +l. Indicates number of orbitals in subshell.
s (Spin)
Electron spin direction. s = +½ (↑) or −½ (↓). Max 2 electrons per orbital.
∴ 4 quantum numbers (n, l, m, s) uniquely describe each electron in an atom.
Q5.2
Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity
Rule
Electrons fill orbitals of the same energy level (degenerate orbitals) one by one before pairing. All unpaired electrons have parallel spins.
Example
Carbon (Z=6): 1s² 2s² 2p² → two 2p electrons occupy different p orbitals with parallel spins: 2p: ↑_ ↑_ __
Nitrogen
N (Z=7): 2p³ → each p orbital has one electron: ↑_ ↑_ ↑_
∴ Fill each orbital singly first (maximum spin) before doubling up.
Q5.3
Crystallization (with example)
Process
Impure solid dissolved in minimum hot solvent → hot solution filtered → filtrate cooled slowly → pure crystals form → filtered and dried.
Example
Purification of copper sulphate: Dissolve in hot water → filter → cool → blue CuSO₄·5H₂O crystals form.
Principle
Solubility of most solids increases with temperature. On cooling, pure substance crystallizes first.
Q5.4
Simple Distillation (with example)
Definition
Separation of a liquid from non-volatile dissolved solids or separation of two liquids with a large difference in boiling points (>25°C).
Process
Liquid heated → vapours condense in condenser → distillate collected. Residue stays in flask.
Example
Separating water from common salt (NaCl); Distilling acetone from water.
Q5.5
Adsorption Chromatography
Principle
Different components of a mixture have different affinities for the stationary phase (adsorbent). More strongly adsorbed components move slower.
Types
Column chromatography (silica gel column) and TLC (Thin Layer Chromatography on alumina plate).
Uses
Separating plant pigments, purifying organic compounds, testing purity.
Q5.6
Geometrical Isomerism in Alkenes
Definition
Isomers having same molecular formula but different spatial arrangement of atoms/groups around C=C double bond (restricted rotation around C=C).
Types
cis-isomer: same groups on same side of double bond.   trans-isomer: same groups on opposite sides.
Example
2-Butene (CH₃CH=CHCH₃): cis-2-butene (both CH₃ on same side) and trans-2-butene (CH₃ on opposite sides).
Condition
Each carbon of C=C must have two different substituents.
Q5.7
Ionic Bond
Definition
Electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions (cation and anion) formed by complete transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal.
Formation
Metal loses electrons → cation. Non-metal gains electrons → anion. Strong electrostatic attraction between them.
Example
NaCl: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ ;   Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ ;   Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl
Properties
High melting point | Soluble in water | Conducts electricity in molten/solution state | Crystalline solids.
Q5.8
Covalent Bond
Definition
A chemical bond formed by the sharing of electron pairs between two atoms (usually non-metals).
Types
Single bond (1 shared pair), Double bond (2 shared pairs), Triple bond (3 shared pairs).
Example
H₂ (H−H), O₂ (O=O), N₂ (N≡N), H₂O, CO₂
Properties
Low melting point | Generally insoluble in water | Poor conductors | Exist as molecules.
Q5.9
Dalton's Atomic Theory
Postulates
  • All matter is made of indivisible particles called atoms.
  • Atoms of the same element are identical in mass and properties.
  • Atoms cannot be created or destroyed in chemical reactions.
  • Atoms combine in simple whole number ratios to form compounds.
  • Chemical reactions are rearrangements of atoms.
Limitations
Atom is divisible (electrons, protons, neutrons). Isotopes exist. Atomic mass is not always whole number.
Q5.10
Postulates of Bohr's Atomic Theory
Postulates
  • Electrons revolve in fixed circular orbits (shells) around nucleus without losing energy.
  • Each orbit has fixed energy — called energy levels or stationary states.
  • Energy is absorbed when electron jumps to higher orbit; emitted when it falls to lower orbit.
  • Angular momentum of electron is quantized: mvr = nh/2π
  • Only certain orbits are allowed where angular momentum = integer multiple of h/2π.
Limitations
Cannot explain spectra of multi-electron atoms | Does not explain Zeeman/Stark effect | Ignores wave nature of electron.
Q5.11
Analgesics
Definition
Medicines that relieve pain without causing loss of consciousness. Also called pain killers.
Types
  • Non-narcotic (mild): Aspirin, Paracetamol, Ibuprofen — for headache, fever, mild pain
  • Narcotic (strong/opioid): Morphine, Codeine — for severe pain (post-surgery). Can cause addiction.
∴ Analgesics = pain relievers. Non-narcotic (mild) or Narcotic (strong, addictive).
Q5.12
Saponification
Definition
Hydrolysis of an ester (fat/oil) with a strong alkali (NaOH or KOH) to give soap (sodium/potassium salt of fatty acid) and glycerol.
Reaction
Fat/Oil + NaOH → Soap (RCOONa) + Glycerol
Example
Sodium stearate (C₁₇H₃₅COONa) is a soap made from stearic acid and NaOH.
∴ Saponification = Fat + NaOH → Soap + Glycerol
Q5.13
Types of Cleansing Agents
1. Soaps
Sodium/Potassium salts of higher fatty acids. Made by saponification. Ineffective in hard water (forms scum). Biodegradable.
2. Synthetic Detergents
Sulphonates of long chain hydrocarbons. Three types: Anionic (SLS), Cationic (fabric softeners), Non-ionic (dishwash). Effective in hard water.
Action
Both have hydrophilic (water-loving) head and hydrophobic (oil-loving) tail. They form micelles that trap grease and remove dirt.
Q5.14
Limitations of Bohr's Atomic Model
Limitations
  • Explains spectrum of hydrogen only — fails for multi-electron atoms.
  • Cannot explain Zeeman effect (splitting of spectral lines in magnetic field).
  • Cannot explain Stark effect (splitting in electric field).
  • Does not consider wave nature of electron (de Broglie).
  • Ignores Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.
  • Cannot explain fine structure of spectral lines.
Q5.15
Browning of Cut Fruits
Cause
Enzymatic oxidation. When fruit is cut, enzyme polyphenol oxidase (PPO) is exposed to oxygen in air. It oxidizes phenolic compounds (colourless) to brown-coloured quinones.
Prevention
Lemon juice (ascorbic acid acts as antioxidant) | Store in cold water | Blanching | Airtight packaging.
∴ Cut fruit + O₂ → enzyme (PPO) oxidizes phenols → brown quinones.
Q5.16
Antiseptics
Definition
Chemical agents applied to living tissues (skin, wounds) to kill or inhibit the growth of microorganisms and prevent infection.
Examples
Dettol (chloroxylenol + terpineol) | Savlon | Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) | Iodine tincture (2% I₂ in alcohol) | Boric acid (eye wash)
vs Disinfectants
Antiseptics = safe for living tissue. Disinfectants = used on non-living surfaces only (floors, instruments). e.g. Phenol 1% = antiseptic; 5% = disinfectant.

Physical Properties and Uses of Hydrocarbons
Q6.1
Alkanes — Physical Properties and Uses
Physical Properties
  • Colourless, odourless (lower members — petrol smell)
  • C₁–C₄ = gases; C₅–C₁₇ = liquids; C₁₈+ = solids at room temp
  • Insoluble in water; soluble in organic solvents
  • Boiling point increases with molecular weight
  • Density < 1 g/cm³ (lighter than water)
Uses
  • Methane: fuel (natural gas), manufacture of H₂ and carbon black
  • Propane/Butane: LPG fuel for cooking
  • Petrol, diesel, kerosene: transport fuels
  • Paraffin wax (long chain alkanes): candles, lubricants
  • Dry cleaning solvent (petroleum ether)
Q6.2
Alkenes — Physical Properties and Uses
Physical Properties
  • Ethene, propene = gases; higher alkenes = liquids
  • Slightly soluble in water; soluble in organic solvents
  • BP higher than corresponding alkanes
  • Colourless with slight sweet odour
  • Undergo addition reactions (due to C=C double bond)
Uses
  • Ethene: manufacture of polyethylene (plastic bags, containers)
  • Ripening agent for fruits (ethylene gas)
  • Propene → polypropylene (fibres, plastics)
  • Manufacture of alcohols (ethanol from ethene)
  • Synthesis of PVC, polystyrene
Q6.3
Acetylene (Ethyne) — Physical Properties and Uses
Physical Properties
  • Colourless gas with garlic-like odour
  • Slightly soluble in water; very soluble in acetone
  • BP = −84°C; lighter than air
  • Highly flammable; burns with luminous sooty flame
  • Triple bond (C≡C) — most reactive hydrocarbon
Uses
  • Oxyacetylene flame: welding and cutting metals (temp ~3500°C)
  • Starting material for synthesis of vinyl chloride (PVC)
  • Manufacture of acetic acid, acetaldehyde
  • Artificial ripening of fruits (calcium carbide)

Explain with Reactions
Q7.1
Dehalogenation of Vicinal Dihalide to Prepare Alkene
Reagent
Zinc (Zn) dust in ethanol
Reaction
CH₂Br−CH₂Br + Zn → CH₂=CH₂ + ZnBr₂
(1,2-dibromoethane + Zn → Ethene + Zinc bromide)
Mechanism
Zn removes one Br from each adjacent carbon (vicinal = neighbouring carbons), forming double bond.
∴ Vicinal dihalide + Zn → Alkene + ZnX₂
Q7.2
Reduction of Alkyl Halide to Prepare Methane
Method
Using Zinc (Zn) and HCl or Zn-Cu couple, or catalytic hydrogenation, or reaction with Mg in dry ether (Grignard) then water
Reaction (Zn+HCl)
CH₃Cl + H₂ → CH₄ + HCl (using Ni catalyst, 200°C)
Grignard Method
CH₃Br + Mg (dry ether) → CH₃MgBr → + H₂O → CH₄ + Mg(OH)Br
∴ CH₃X + H₂ (Ni catalyst) → CH₄ + HX
Q7.3
Dehydration of Alcohols to Prepare Alkene
Reagent
Concentrated H₂SO₄ at 170°C (or Al₂O₃ at 350°C)
Reaction
CH₃CH₂OH → (conc. H₂SO₄, 170°C) → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
Ethanol → Ethene + Water
Mechanism
H₂SO₄ protonates OH group → forms carbocation → loses H⁺ from adjacent carbon → alkene forms.
Note
At 140°C with excess alcohol → ether (C₂H₅OC₂H₅) forms instead.
∴ Alcohol → (conc. H₂SO₄, 170°C) → Alkene + H₂O
Q7.4
Friedel-Crafts Alkylation Reaction
Definition
Introduction of an alkyl group into benzene ring using alkyl halide in presence of Lewis acid catalyst (AlCl₃).
Reaction
C₆H₆ + CH₃Cl → (AlCl₃) → C₆H₅CH₃ + HCl
Benzene + Chloromethane → Toluene + HCl
Mechanism
AlCl₃ activates alkyl halide → forms carbocation (CH₃⁺) → electrophilic attack on benzene ring → substitution occurs.
Type
Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)
∴ Benzene + RX → (AlCl₃) → Alkylbenzene + HX
Q7.5
Addition of HBr to Ethene (Markovnikov's Rule)
Reaction
CH₂=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃−CH₂Br
Ethene + HBr → Bromoethane (ethyl bromide)
Mechanism
Step 1: H⁺ (electrophile) attacks C=C → carbocation forms. Step 2: Br⁻ (nucleophile) attacks carbocation → product.
For Propene
CH₃−CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃−CHBr−CH₃ (2-bromopropane, major product per Markovnikov's rule: H adds to C with more H)
Type
Electrophilic Addition reaction (characteristic of alkenes)
∴ Alkene + HBr → alkyl bromide (addition reaction)

Medicinal Properties of Plants
Q8
Name the medicinal property of: Turmeric, Clove, Wintergreen, Cinnamon
1. Turmeric
Active compound: Curcumin. Properties: Anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, antibacterial, antioxidant. Used to heal wounds, treat skin infections, reduce inflammation.
2. Clove
Active compound: Eugenol. Properties: Analgesic (pain relief), antiseptic, antimicrobial. Used as dental anaesthetic (toothache), clove oil for sore gums, digestive problems.
3. Wintergreen
Active compound: Methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen). Properties: Analgesic, anti-inflammatory, counterirritant. Used in pain relief balms/ointments (Deep Heat, Tiger Balm) for muscle pain.
4. Cinnamon
Active compound: Cinnamaldehyde. Properties: Antimicrobial, antidiabetic, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory. Used to control blood sugar, digestive aid, antibacterial food preservative.
∴ Turmeric=curcumin (antiseptic); Clove=eugenol (analgesic); Wintergreen=methyl salicylate (pain relief); Cinnamon=cinnamaldehyde (antimicrobial).
