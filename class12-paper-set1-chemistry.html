SECTION A – MCQ Solutions
(i) (C) Decrease in [reactant]/time
Rate of reaction = −Δ[Reactant]/Δt = +Δ[Product]/Δt. Decrease in reactant concentration per unit time.
(ii) (C) Gold sol
Tyndall effect occurs in colloidal solutions (particle size 1–1000 nm). Gold sol is a colloid; NaCl and sugar are true solutions.
(iii) (C) 4
DoU = (2C + 2 + N − H − X) / 2 = (2×6 + 2 − 6) / 2 = (12+2−6)/2 = 8/2 = 4 Benzene has 3 C=C double bonds + 1 ring = 4 degrees of unsaturation.
(iv) (C) CH₃CH₂OH
Primary alcohol: −OH attached to carbon bearing one alkyl group. CH₃CH₂OH → −OH on C1 with one C chain. (Note: CH₃OH/methanol is also primary but the option C is the most common answer accepted.)
(v) (C) 6
In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, Fe is surrounded by 6 CN⁻ ligands. Coordination number = number of ligand atoms directly bonded to central metal = 6.
(vi) (B) Raoult's law
Raoult's law: pA = xA · p°A, where xA is mole fraction of solvent.
(vii) (C) Bessemerisation
The Bessemer process converts pig iron (high carbon) to steel by blowing air through molten pig iron to oxidise excess carbon and impurities.
(viii) (A) Caprolactam
Nylon-6 is produced by ring-opening polymerisation of caprolactam (ε-caprolactam), a cyclic amide (lactam) with 6 carbons.
(ix) (D) Vitamin C
Water-soluble vitamins: B-group and C. Fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E, K. Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is water-soluble.
(x) (B) Prop-1-ene
CH₃−CH=CH₂: 3 carbons (prop-), double bond at C1 (−1-), alkene (−ene). IUPAC name: Prop-1-ene
SECTION B – Short Definitions (1 mark each)
(i) Rate of reaction:
Rate of reaction is the change in concentration of reactant or product per unit time. r = −d[A]/dt = +d[B]/dt; SI unit: mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
(ii) Brownian motion:
The continuous, random, zig-zag motion of colloidal particles due to unequal bombardment by molecules of the dispersion medium is called Brownian motion.
(iii) Molality:
Molality (m) = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg. Unit: mol kg⁻¹.
(iv) Saponification number:
The mass of KOH (in mg) required to saponify 1 g of fat/oil completely is called saponification number. Higher value → lower molecular weight of fat.
(v) Peptide bond:
The amide linkage (−CO−NH−) formed between the −COOH of one amino acid and −NH₂ of another, with loss of water, is called a peptide bond.
(vi) Degree of polymerisation:
The number of repeating monomer units present in one molecule of a polymer is called the degree of polymerisation (n).
(vii) Lanthanoid contraction:
The steady decrease in ionic radii of lanthanoid elements from La³⁺ to Lu³⁺ due to poor shielding by 4f electrons is called lanthanoid contraction.
(viii) Disproportionation:
A reaction in which the same substance undergoes both oxidation and reduction simultaneously (self-oxidation-reduction) is called disproportionation. e.g., 2Cu⁺ → Cu + Cu²⁺
(ix) Osmotic pressure:
The minimum external pressure that must be applied on the solution side to stop osmosis (flow of solvent from solvent to solution through a semi-permeable membrane) is called osmotic pressure (π).
(x) Inductive effect:
The permanent displacement of electrons along a chain of sigma bonds towards a more electronegative atom or group is called the inductive effect (−I or +I effect).
(xi) Specific conductivity:
The conductance of an electrolyte solution between two electrodes of unit area of cross-section separated by unit distance. κ = 1/ρ; SI unit: S m⁻¹.
(xii) Biodegradable polymer:
Polymers that can be decomposed by microorganisms into simple, harmless substances in a reasonable time are biodegradable polymers. Example: PHBV (polyhydroxybutyrate-co-hydroxyvalerate), Nylon-2-nylon-6.
SECTION C – 2-Mark Solutions
(i) First-order rate constant derivation:
For A → Products; Rate = −d[A]/dt = k[A] Let [A]₀ = a, [A] at time t = (a−x) dx/dt = k(a−x)  ⟹  dx/(a−x) = k dt Integrating: −ln(a−x) + ln(a) = kt ln[a/(a−x)] = kt k = (2.303/t) log[a/(a−x)]
(ii) Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm:
The empirical relationship between the extent of adsorption and pressure (or concentration) at constant temperature. x/m = k·P^(1/n)  (where x/m = mass adsorbed per gram adsorbent, P = pressure, n > 1) Log(x/m) = log k + (1/n)log P → straight line graph with slope 1/n and intercept log k. Valid at intermediate pressure; fails at very high P (saturation) and very low P.
(iii) Molality of H₂SO₄ solution:
Mass of H₂SO₄ = 9.8 g, M = 98 g/mol, Mass of solvent = 500 g = 0.5 kg
Moles of H₂SO₄ = 9.8 / 98 = 0.1 mol Molality m = moles / kg(solvent) = 0.1 / 0.5 m = 0.2 mol kg⁻¹
(iv) Electrophilic addition of HBr to ethylene:
Step 1 (Electrophilic attack): H⁺ from HBr attacks π electrons of C=C → forms carbocation: CH₃−CH₂⁺ (ethyl cation) Step 2 (Nucleophilic attack): Br⁻ attacks the carbocation → CH₃−CH₂−Br (bromoethane) Overall: CH₂=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CH₂Br Product: Bromoethane (by Markovnikov's rule for unsymmetrical alkenes)
(v) Aspirin – Preparation and uses:
Preparation: Salicylic acid + Acetic anhydride → Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) + Acetic acid C₆H₄(OH)(COOH) + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)(COOH) + CH₃COOH Uses: (1) Analgesic (pain reliever), (2) Antipyretic (reduces fever), (3) Anti-inflammatory, (4) Blood thinner (antiplatelet).
(vi) Enantiomers:
Stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other are called enantiomers. They require a chiral centre (asymmetric carbon). Example: (+)-lactic acid and (−)-lactic acid (2-hydroxypropanoic acid, C* = C-2).
(vii) Arrhenius equation and temperature effect:
k = A · e^(−Ea/RT)   (Arrhenius equation) A = frequency factor, Ea = activation energy, R = gas constant, T = absolute temperature. Log form: log k = log A − Ea/(2.303 RT) As T increases, e^(−Ea/RT) increases → k increases → rate increases. Rule of thumb: Rate doubles for every 10°C rise in temperature.
(viii) Peptide linkage formation:
R₁CH(NH₂)COOH + H₂N−CHR₂−COOH → R₁CH(NH₂)CO−NH−CHR₂−COOH + H₂O The −CO−NH− group formed is the peptide bond (amide linkage). Two amino acids joined → dipeptide; many → polypeptide/protein.
(ix) Bakelite – structure and uses:
Structure: 3D cross-linked thermosetting polymer of phenol and formaldehyde. Phenol reacts with formaldehyde in acidic/basic medium → methylene bridges form between phenol rings. Uses: (1) Electrical switches and sockets, (2) Radio cabinets, (3) Billiard balls, (4) Kitchenware handles.
(x) Extraction of iron – Blast furnace reactions:
Zone of combustion (bottom): C + O₂ → CO₂ ; CO₂ + C → 2CO Zone of reduction (middle): Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ Zone of slag formation: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ ; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag) Molten iron (pig iron) collects at bottom; slag floats on top.
(xi) Electrophoresis:
The movement of colloidal particles under the influence of an electric field towards the oppositely charged electrode is called electrophoresis. Uses: Separation of proteins in gel electrophoresis, water purification, rubber industry, DNA analysis.
(xii) Conversion reactions:
(a) Ethanol → Ethanal: CH₃CH₂OH → [Oxidation by K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ or Cu at 573K] → CH₃CHO (b) Methanol → Methanal: CH₃OH → [Oxidation by K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ or Cu at 573K] → HCHO (formaldehyde)
SECTION D – 3-Mark Solutions
(i) First-order t½ derivation and calculation:
At t = t½, x = a/2 (half the reactant has reacted) k = (2.303/t½) log[a/(a − a/2)] = (2.303/t½) log 2 t½ = 2.303 × 0.3010 / k = 0.693 / k t½ = 0.693 / k (independent of initial concentration) For k = 2.31×10⁻³ s⁻¹: t½ = 0.693 / 2.31×10⁻³ = 300 s t½ = 300 seconds = 5 minutes
(ii) Contact Process for H₂SO₄:
Step 1: S + O₂ → SO₂ (burning of sulphur) Step 2: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (catalyst: V₂O₅, 450°C, 1–2 atm) Step 3: SO₃ + H₂SO₄ (conc.) → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum / pyrosulphuric acid) Step 4: H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄ (SO₃ is NOT dissolved directly in water as it is too exothermic and produces acid mist.) Final product: Concentrated H₂SO₄ (98%)
(iii) Werner's coordination theory + EAN of [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻:
Werner's theory: (1) Metal shows two types of valency — primary (ionisable) and secondary (non-ionisable/coordination). (2) Primary valency = oxidation state; secondary = coordination number. (3) Secondary valencies are directed in space (geometry). EAN (Effective Atomic Number) = Atomic number of metal − electrons lost + electrons donated by ligands For [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻: Fe is Fe²⁺ (oxidation state = +2, 4 electrons donated by complex charge). Z(Fe) = 26; electrons in Fe²⁺ = 26−2 = 24; electrons from 6 CN⁻ = 6×2 = 12 EAN = 24 + 12 = 36 (= atomic number of Kr, stable noble gas)
(iv) Glycogen vs starch:
Glycogen: Storage polysaccharide in animals (liver, muscles). Monomer: glucose. Highly branched with α(1→4) linkages in chain and α(1→6) at branch points. Branching every 8–12 glucose units. Starch: Storage in plants. Has two components — amylose (unbranched, α(1→4), helical) and amylopectin (branched, α(1→4) with α(1→6) branches every 24–30 glucose units). ∴ Glycogen is more branched than amylopectin and has shorter branch chains.
(v) Aldol condensation mechanism:
Conditions: dilute NaOH (base catalyst), aldehydes/ketones with α-hydrogen. Step 1: Base abstracts α-H → enolate ion (carbanion). Step 2: Enolate attacks carbonyl carbon of another aldehyde molecule → alkoxide intermediate. Step 3: Protonation → β-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol product). Step 4 (on heating): Dehydration → α,β-unsaturated aldehyde. Example: 2 CH₃CHO → [OH⁻] → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO (3-hydroxybutanal) → CH₃CH=CHCHO (crotonaldehyde)
(vi) Boiling point elevation calculation:
m(glucose) = 1.8 g, M = 180 g/mol, mass of solvent = 100 g = 0.1 kg, Kb = 0.52 K·kg/mol
Moles of glucose = 1.8 / 180 = 0.01 mol Molality = 0.01 / 0.1 = 0.1 mol/kg ΔTb = Kb × m = 0.52 × 0.1 ΔTb = 0.052 K  (boiling point rises by 0.052°C)
(vii) Nucleophilic and Electrophilic substitution:
(a) Nucleophilic substitution (SN): Nucleophile (electron-rich species) attacks electron-deficient carbon. SN1: two-step via carbocation intermediate (unimolecular). SN2: one-step backside attack (bimolecular), inversion of configuration. (b) Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS): Electrophile attacks benzene ring. Steps: (1) Formation of electrophile, (2) Attack on π system → arenium ion (σ complex), (3) Loss of H⁺ → re-aromatisation. Example: Halogenation, nitration, sulphonation, Friedel-Crafts reaction.
(viii) Classification of polymers:
(a) Based on mode of polymerisation:   • Addition polymers: formed by addition of monomers with double bonds. e.g., polyethylene (CH₂=CH₂ → -[CH₂-CH₂]-n)   • Condensation polymers: formed by condensation reaction with loss of small molecule (H₂O, HCl). e.g., Nylon-6,6, Dacron. (b) Based on molecular forces:   • Elastomers (weak van der Waals): rubber. • Fibres (strong H-bonds): nylon, silk. • Thermoplastics (moderate forces): PVC. • Thermosetting (3D network): Bakelite.
SECTION E – 4-Mark Solutions
(i)(a) Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis:
First Law: The mass of substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed. m = Z × Q = Z × I × t (Z = electrochemical equivalent) Second Law: When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes, the masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent weights. m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂ (ratio of equivalent weights)
(i)(b) Time calculation for Cu deposition:
m = 1.27 g, I = 2 A, Cu = 63.5, F = 96500 C, n(electrons) = 2 (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu)
Moles of Cu = 1.27/63.5 = 0.02 mol Moles of electrons = 2 × 0.02 = 0.04 mol Charge Q = 0.04 × 96500 = 3860 C t = Q/I = 3860/2 = 1930 seconds ≈ 32.2 minutes
(ii)(a) Fractional distillation of petroleum:
Crude oil is heated to ~400°C and fed into a fractionating tower (tall vertical column, cooler at top, hotter at bottom). Fractions (bottom to top): Residue (asphalt) → Fuel oil (300–400°C) → Diesel (250–350°C) → Kerosene (175–275°C) → Gasoline (40–180°C) → Petroleum ether → LPG/gases. Each fraction condenses at its boiling point range on different trays.
(ii)(b) Cracking and Reforming:
Cracking: Breaking large hydrocarbon molecules into smaller, more useful ones (e.g., gasoline) by heat +/− catalyst. Thermal cracking: 450–750°C; Catalytic cracking: zeolite catalyst, ~500°C. Reforming: Converting low-octane straight-chain alkanes into branched/cyclic/aromatic hydrocarbons with higher octane number. e.g., n-hexane → cyclohexane → benzene (dehydrogenation).
(iii)(a) Free-radical addition polymerisation:
Initiation: R−R → 2R• (homolytic cleavage of initiator); R• + CH₂=CH₂ → R−CH₂−CH₂• Propagation: R−(CH₂−CH₂)n• + CH₂=CH₂ → R−(CH₂−CH₂)n+1• Termination: Two radicals combine → R−(CH₂CH₂)m+n−R (dead polymer chain)
(iii)(b) Nylon-6,6 and PVC:
Nylon-6,6: Hexamethylene diamine + Adipic acid → [−NH−(CH₂)₆−NH−CO−(CH₂)₄−CO−]n + nH₂O. Uses: ropes, parachutes, tyre cords, toothbrush bristles. PVC (polyvinyl chloride): CH₂=CHCl → free-radical poly → [−CH₂−CHCl−]n. Uses: water pipes, raincoats, shoe soles, insulation on wires.
(iv)(a) Protein structure:
Primary: Linear sequence of amino acids linked by peptide bonds (−CO−NH−). Secondary: Folding of polypeptide chain into α-helix (right-handed spiral, H-bonds within chain) or β-pleated sheet (H-bonds between chains). Tertiary: 3D folding of entire polypeptide into specific shape; stabilised by disulfide bonds, H-bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions. Quaternary: Association of two or more polypeptide subunits; e.g., hemoglobin (4 subunits).
(iv)(b) DNA vs RNA (4 points):
1. DNA: deoxyribose sugar; RNA: ribose sugar. 2. DNA: double-stranded helix; RNA: usually single-stranded. 3. DNA: bases A,T,G,C (thymine); RNA: bases A,U,G,C (uracil instead of thymine). 4. DNA: found mainly in nucleus (genetic material); RNA: found in nucleus and cytoplasm (functional roles in protein synthesis).
(v)(a) Osmosis and derivation of π = CRT:
Osmosis: Spontaneous flow of solvent from a dilute solution (or pure solvent) to a concentrated solution through a semi-permeable membrane. Van't Hoff showed (by analogy with ideal gas law PV = nRT): π V = n R T  ⟹  π = (n/V)RT = CRT where C = molar concentration = n/V (mol L⁻¹), R = 0.082 L·atm/mol·K, T = absolute temperature.
(v)(b) Osmotic pressure calculation:
m(urea) = 6 g, M = 60 g/mol, V = 500 mL = 0.5 L, T = 27+273 = 300 K, R = 0.082
C = n/V = (6/60)/0.5 = 0.1/0.5 = 0.2 mol/L π = CRT = 0.2 × 0.082 × 300 = 0.2 × 24.6 π = 4.92 atm
