Q.1 (i)
Zona pellucida is the protective coat of:
✔ Answer: (A) Ovum (egg cell)
Explanation Zona pellucida = thick glycoprotein layer surrounding the secondary oocyte/ovum. Functions: Protects the egg; has ZP3 protein that acts as receptor for sperm (species-specific). After fertilisation, zona pellucida reaction (cortical reaction) prevents polyspermy.
Q.1 (ii)
Cystic fibrosis is inherited as:
✔ Answer: (B) Autosomal recessive
Explanation Cystic fibrosis: mutation in CFTR gene (chromosome 7). Most common fatal genetic disorder in Caucasians. Autosomal recessive: both copies of the gene must be mutated (homozygous) to show the disease. Features: thick mucus in lungs, pancreas, intestines; repeated lung infections.
Q.1 (iii)
Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase charges tRNA by:
✔ Answer: (B) Attaching an amino acid to the 3' end of tRNA
Explanation Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase is called the "second genetic code" — it ensures each tRNA is loaded with the correct amino acid. Attaches amino acid to CCA sequence at 3' acceptor stem of tRNA using ATP. Amino acid + tRNA + ATP → Aminoacyl-tRNA + AMP + PPᵢ
Q.1 (iv)
Palindromic sequences in DNA are recognised and cut by:
✔ Answer: (C) Restriction endonucleases
Explanation Restriction endonucleases (Type II) recognise specific palindromic sequences (read the same 5'→3' on both strands) and cut both strands of DNA. Example: EcoRI recognises 5'-GAATTC-3' (palindrome).
Q.1 (v)
RuBisCO shows dual activity as:
✔ Answer: (B) Carboxylase and oxygenase
Explanation RuBisCO = Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase Carboxylase activity: CO₂ + RuBP → 2 × 3-PGA (Calvin cycle — normal) Oxygenase activity: O₂ + RuBP → 3-PGA + phosphoglycolate (photorespiration — wasteful)
Q.1 (vi)
Frankia fixes nitrogen in association with:
✔ Answer: (B) Non-leguminous trees (e.g., Alnus)
Explanation Frankia = actinomycete bacterium. Forms root nodules in non-leguminous trees (Alnus, Casuarina, Hippophae). Rhizobium forms nodules only in legumes. Anabaena and Nostoc (cyanobacteria) fix nitrogen in rice paddies and Azolla.
Q.1 (vii)
Bt-cotton is resistant to:
✔ Answer: (B) Cotton bollworm (Helicoverpa)
Explanation Bt-cotton (Bollgard) contains cry1Ac gene from Bacillus thuringiensis. Cry1Ac protein is toxic to cotton bollworm larvae (Helicoverpa armigera, Heliothis virescens) — pests that bore into cotton bolls. Reduces need for chemical insecticides by 70-80%.
Q.1 (viii)
Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species is:
✔ Answer: CITES — signed 1973
Explanation CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora) Controls international trade in endangered species. Three appendices: Appendix I (ban trade), II (regulate), III (country-specific).
Q.1 (ix)
Clonal selection theory was proposed by:
✔ Answer: Frank Macfarlane Burnet
Explanation Clonal selection theory: Each B-cell has unique antibody. Antigen selects and activates the specific B-cell that binds it → proliferation → plasma cells (antibody factories) + memory cells. Explains immunological memory, specificity, and diversity of immune response.
Q.1 (x)
The disease caused by Trypanosoma is:
✔ Answer: African sleeping sickness (Trypanosomiasis)
Explanation Trypanosoma brucei → African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness). Transmitted by tsetse fly. Trypanosoma cruzi → Chagas disease (American trypanosomiasis). Transmitted by triatomine bug.

Q.2 — Key Definitions
CleavageRapid mitotic divisions of the zygote without cell growth, producing smaller and smaller cells called blastomeres. The zygote develops into morula (16 cells) and then blastocyst.
Pedigree chartA diagram showing the occurrence and appearance of phenotypes of a particular gene or organism and its ancestors from one generation to the next. Used in genetic counselling.
IntronsNon-coding intervening sequences within a eukaryotic gene that are transcribed into pre-mRNA but removed (spliced out) during RNA processing to form mature mRNA. Exons remain and are translated.
Selectable markerA gene in a vector that confers a selectable characteristic (e.g., antibiotic resistance) to identify and select cells that have taken up the recombinant DNA. Example: ampicillin resistance gene in pBR322.
Transpiration pullThe negative pressure (tension) created in the xylem by evaporation of water from leaves (transpiration), which pulls water upward from roots. The main mechanism for water transport in tall trees.
NodulationThe formation of root nodules in leguminous plants through the symbiotic association with Rhizobium bacteria. The process involves recognition, infection thread formation, and differentiation of bacteroids that fix N₂.
PlasmidA small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecule in bacteria that replicates independently of the chromosomal DNA. Used as a vector in recombinant DNA technology. Contains ori, selectable marker, and multiple cloning site (MCS).
Species area relationshipAs the area of a habitat increases, the number of species also increases. Expressed as: log S = log C + z log A. (S = species, A = area, z = regression coefficient ≈ 0.1-0.2 for small islands, 0.6-1.2 for biogeographic regions).

Q.3 (ii)
Explain Mendel's law of dominance.
StatementWhen two plants differing in one pair of contrasting characters are crossed, the character that appears in the F₁ generation is called dominant, and the other (which does not appear in F₁) is called recessive.
Example Tall (TT) × Dwarf (tt) → F₁: all Tall (Tt) → Tallness is dominant, dwarfness is recessive F₂: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (dominant : recessive = 3:1)
Q.3 (xi)
Describe the Meselson-Stahl experiment.
Experiment Setup: E. coli grown for several generations in ¹⁵N (heavy nitrogen) medium → all DNA becomes heavy (¹⁵N/¹⁵N) Transfer: Shifted to ¹⁴N medium (light). After 1 and 2 generations, DNA extracted and centrifuged in CsCl density gradient. After 1 generation: ONE band at intermediate density (hybrid ¹⁵N/¹⁴N DNA) After 2 generations: TWO bands — one intermediate, one light (¹⁴N/¹⁴N)
Conclusion
DNA replication is SEMI-CONSERVATIVE — each new molecule has one original strand and one new strand.

Q.4 (i)
Describe the menstrual cycle with hormone level changes.
Overview28-day cycle. Hormones: GnRH (hypothalamus) → FSH, LH (anterior pituitary) → Estrogen, Progesterone (ovary)
4 Phases Menstrual (Day 1-5): FSH low; endometrium sheds. Low estrogen/progesterone. Follicular (Day 6-13): FSH rises → Graafian follicle matures → Estrogen rises → endometrium proliferates Ovulation (Day 14): LH surge → ovulation. Estrogen peak. Luteal (Day 15-28): LH → corpus luteum → progesterone + estrogen → endometrium secretory. If no fertilisation: corpus luteum regresses → hormones fall → menstruation.
Q.4 (iii)
Explain transcription in prokaryotes — initiation, elongation and termination.
InitiationRNA polymerase sigma (σ) subunit recognises and binds the promoter (Pribnow box: -10 and -35 regions). σ opens the DNA double helix (melting) to form transcription bubble.
Elongationσ factor released. Core enzyme moves along template strand (3'→5') and synthesises RNA (5'→3'). RNA is complementary to template; contains U instead of T.
Termination Rho-independent (intrinsic): Hairpin loop structure forms in mRNA + poly-U sequence → polymerase stalls → RNA released Rho-dependent: Rho protein follows RNA polymerase → at termination site, Rho unwinds RNA-DNA hybrid → polymerase + RNA released
Q.4 (v)
Draw the nitrogen cycle with a neat diagram.
Nitrogen Cycle Steps 1. Nitrogen Fixation: N₂ → NH₃ by Rhizobium (symbiotic), Azotobacter (free-living), lightning, industrial (Haber process) 2. Nitrification: NH₃ → NO₂⁻ (Nitrosomonas) → NO₃⁻ (Nitrobacter) 3. Assimilation: Plants absorb NO₃⁻/NH₄⁺ → amino acids → proteins 4. Ammonification: Decomposers break organic N → NH₃ when organisms die 5. Denitrification: NO₃⁻ → N₂ by Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus (anaerobic conditions) Cycle keeps N₂ cycling between atmosphere, soil and organisms.
Q.4 (ix)
Write a note on cancer — types, causes and detection methods.
Types Carcinoma: from epithelial cells (skin, lung, breast, colon cancer) Sarcoma: from connective tissue, bone, muscle Lymphoma: from lymphoid tissue (Hodgkin's lymphoma) Leukaemia: from blood cells (blood cancer)
Causes Oncogenes (mutated proto-oncogenes), loss of tumour suppressor genes (p53, Rb) Carcinogens: tobacco, UV radiation, X-rays, chemicals, asbestos Oncogenic viruses: HPV (cervical cancer), EBV (Burkitt lymphoma), Hepatitis B (liver cancer)
DetectionBiopsy, CT scan, MRI, PET scan, tumour markers (PSA for prostate, AFP for liver), Pap smear (cervical), mammography (breast)

Q.5 (iii)
(a) DNA structure and semi-conservative replication. (b) Mutations — types and examples.
DNA Structure Double helix (Watson & Crick, 1953). Two antiparallel polynucleotide strands. Sugar-phosphate backbone outside; bases inside. A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds). 3.4 Å/base pair, 34 Å/turn (10 bp/turn).
Semi-conservative Replication Each daughter DNA = 1 original + 1 new strand (proved by Meselson-Stahl, 1958) Enzymes: Helicase (unwind), Primase (RNA primer), DNA Pol III (synthesis), DNA Pol I (primer removal), DNA Ligase (join fragments)
Mutations Gene mutations (point mutations): Substitution: one base replaced. e.g., Sickle cell anaemia (A→T in 6th codon of β-globin: GAG→GTG = Glu→Val) Insertion/Deletion: frameshift mutations — alter entire reading frame downstream Chromosomal mutations: deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation of chromosome segments
Q.5 (vi)
(a) Ecosystem — energy flow and 10% law. (b) In situ vs ex situ conservation.
Energy Flow (Lindeman's 10% Law) Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next. 90% is lost as heat (respiration, excretion, decomposition). Example food chain: Grass (10,000 J) → Grasshopper (1000 J) → Frog (100 J) → Snake (10 J) → Eagle (1 J) Energy flows unidirectionally and decreases at each trophic level. Implications: short food chains are more energy efficient; more people can be fed if they eat grains directly (less trophic level loss).
