Ch1 A. State Newton's Second Law of Motion. Write its mathematical form.
Newton's Second Law: The rate of change of linear momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied external force, and the change takes place in the direction of the applied force.
  • Mathematically: F = ma
  • where F = net force (Newton), m = mass (kg), a = acceleration (m/s²)
  • Also written as: F = Δp/Δt = m(v-u)/t
  • 1 Newton = force needed to give 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s²
Ch2 B. Define work done. When is work done negative? Give an example.
Work Done: Work is done when a force causes displacement of a body in the direction of the force.
W = F × s × cosθ
where F = force, s = displacement, θ = angle between force and displacement.
  • Negative work: When force and displacement are in opposite directions (θ = 180°, cos180° = -1), work done is negative.
  • Example: When brakes are applied to a moving car — friction acts opposite to motion → negative work is done by friction, reducing KE of the car.
  • Also: When you carry a bag upstairs, gravity does negative work (acts down, displacement is up).
Ch3 C. What is electric current? Define 1 Ampere. Give its formula and SI unit.
Electric Current (I): The rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor is called electric current.
I = Q/t
where Q = charge in Coulombs (C), t = time in seconds (s).
  • SI unit: Ampere (A)
  • 1 Ampere: If 1 Coulomb of charge flows through a cross-section of a conductor in 1 second, the current is called 1 Ampere.
  • Current is measured using an ammeter connected in series.
  • Direction of conventional current: from positive terminal to negative terminal (opposite to electron flow).
Ch4 D. Calculate the number of moles in 88 g of CO₂. Also find the number of CO₂ molecules. (M of CO₂ = 44 g/mol)
Given: mass of CO₂ = 88 g, M = 44 g/mol
Moles: n = m/M = 88/44 = 2 moles
Number of molecules: N = n × Nₐ = 2 × 6.022 × 10²³
∴ 2 moles of CO₂; N = 1.2044 × 10²⁴ molecules
Ch5 E. What are strong acids and weak acids? Give two examples of each.
Strong Acids: Acids that completely ionise in water, releasing all their H⁺ ions.
  • Examples: Hydrochloric acid (HCl), Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄), Nitric acid (HNO₃)
Weak Acids: Acids that partially ionise in water, releasing only some of their H⁺ ions.
  • Examples: Acetic acid (CH₃COOH), Carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), Citric acid
  • Weak acids have higher pH (less acidic) than strong acids at same concentration.

Ch2 A. An object thrown vertically upward ultimately comes back to the ground.
When an object is thrown upward, gravitational force (mg) acts downward on it continuously. This causes deceleration — the object slows down, momentarily stops at maximum height (all KE converted to PE), and then gravitational force pulls it back down (PE converts back to KE). Since no other force sustains upward motion, the object returns to ground. This is consistent with the Law of Conservation of Energy and Newton's laws.
Ch3 B. The filament of an electric bulb is made of tungsten.
Tungsten is used for bulb filaments because: (1) It has a very high melting point (~3422°C) — it can withstand the high temperature produced by the electric current without melting. (2) It has high resistivity — it produces a large amount of heat and light for a given current. (3) It can be drawn into a thin wire (ductile). (4) It does not oxidise easily in the inert gas atmosphere inside the bulb.
Ch5 C. When excess CO₂ is passed through lime water, the white precipitate formed first disappears.
When CO₂ is passed through Ca(OH)₂ (lime water), white precipitate of CaCO₃ forms first:
Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃↓ (white) + H₂O
When excess CO₂ is passed, insoluble CaCO₃ reacts with extra CO₂ and water to form soluble calcium bicarbonate Ca(HCO₃)₂ which dissolves — so the white precipitate disappears:
CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O → Ca(HCO₃)₂ (soluble)

Ch1 A. Explain the concept of inertia and Newton's First Law. Why do we need seat belts in vehicles?
Inertia: The property of a body by virtue of which it resists any change in its state of rest or uniform motion is called inertia. It depends on mass — greater mass = greater inertia.

Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia):
  • A body at rest remains at rest, and a body in uniform motion continues in the same direction and speed, unless an unbalanced external force acts on it.
  • This is known as the law of inertia.
Why we need seat belts:
  • When a car stops suddenly (e.g., in a crash), the car decelerates rapidly.
  • Due to inertia of motion, the passengers' bodies tend to continue moving forward at the same speed.
  • Without a seat belt, passengers can be thrown forward violently into the dashboard/windshield — causing serious injury.
  • The seat belt applies a backward force on the passenger, causing them to decelerate with the car and preventing dangerous forward motion.
Ch4 B. Explain the concept of atomic mass unit. What is the gram atomic mass? Calculate gram atomic mass and moles in 56 g of Fe. (Atomic mass of Fe = 56 u)
Atomic Mass Unit (u):
  • 1 atomic mass unit (u) = 1/12 of the mass of one Carbon-12 atom.
  • 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g
  • Atomic masses of elements are expressed in u: H=1u, C=12u, O=16u, Fe=56u.
Gram Atomic Mass:
  • The atomic mass of an element expressed in grams is called its gram atomic mass (= molar mass).
  • 1 gram atomic mass of any element contains 6.022 × 10²³ atoms (1 mole).
Calculation for 56 g of Fe:
Gram atomic mass of Fe = 56 g/mol
n = m/M = 56/56 = 1 mole
Number of atoms = 1 × 6.022 × 10²³
∴ 56 g Fe = 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ atoms

Ch3 A. Explain Ohm's Law. Draw the V-I graph. Derive the formula for heat produced (Joule's Law).
Ohm's Law:
  • At constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends.
  • V ∝ I → V = IR
  • R = resistance of the conductor (Ohm, Ω). It is the slope of V-I graph.
📐 V-I Graph: Draw axes — Y-axis = Voltage (V), X-axis = Current (I). A straight line through origin. Slope = R = V/I. Steeper slope = higher resistance.
Joule's Law of Heating — Derivation:
  • When charge Q flows through resistance R under potential difference V in time t:
  • Work done W = VQ = V(It) = VIt (since Q = It)
  • By Ohm's Law: V = IR
  • So W = IR × It = I²Rt
  • This work appears as heat: H = I²Rt (Joules)
  • Joule's Law: H ∝ I², H ∝ R, H ∝ t (directly)
Ch5 B. Explain neutralisation reaction in detail. Describe the preparation of sodium chloride (NaCl) salt in the laboratory with its uses.
Neutralisation Reaction:
  • When an acid and a base react to form a salt and water, the reaction is called neutralisation.
  • The acidic and basic properties cancel each other out — the solution becomes neutral.
    • Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat
  • It is an exothermic reaction (heat is released).
  • H⁺ (from acid) + OH⁻ (from base) → H₂O
Preparation of NaCl in Laboratory:
  • NaCl is prepared by reacting dilute HCl with NaOH solution.
  • HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • Procedure: Take NaOH in a beaker; add few drops of phenolphthalein indicator (turns pink). Slowly add dilute HCl from a burette while stirring. When the pink colour just disappears → neutralisation complete → NaCl formed.
  • Evaporate water carefully → white crystals of NaCl obtained.
Uses of NaCl:
  • In food as table salt; food preservation; making soap; in chemical industry (raw material for NaOH, Na₂CO₃, HCl); used in medical saline solutions.

Ch3 · Current Electricity A. (7 Marks) — Explain the characteristics of series and parallel combinations of resistors. Solve the following numerical.
📌 Characteristics of Series Combination of Resistors
  • 1. The same current flows through each resistor.
  • 2. The total voltage = sum of voltages across each resistor: V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃
  • 3. Equivalent (total) resistance = sum of all resistances: R_series = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
  • 4. R_series is always greater than the largest individual resistance.
  • 5. If one resistor breaks, the entire circuit is broken (all components stop).
  • 6. Used in: Decorative series lights, fuse connections.
📌 Characteristics of Parallel Combination of Resistors
  • 1. The same voltage appears across each resistor (= supply voltage).
  • 2. The total current = sum of branch currents: I = I₁ + I₂ + I₃
  • 3. Equivalent resistance is found by: 1/R_parallel = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
  • 4. R_parallel is always less than the smallest individual resistance.
  • 5. If one resistor breaks, other branches continue to work independently.
  • 6. Used in: Domestic wiring — all appliances get full voltage and work independently.
📐 Numerical: Three resistors of 3 Ω, 4 Ω and 12 Ω are connected (i) in series, (ii) in parallel, across a 24 V battery. Find: (a) Equivalent resistance in each case (b) Total current drawn from battery (c) Voltage across each resistor in series (d) Current through each resistor in parallel
(i) Series Combination:
(a) R_series: R_s = R₁+R₂+R₃ = 3+4+12 = 19 Ω
(b) Total current: I = V/R = 24/19 ≈ 1.26 A
(c) Voltage across each resistor (same current I = 1.26 A flows through all):
V₁ = IR₁ = 1.26 × 3 = 3.78 V
V₂ = IR₂ = 1.26 × 4 = 5.04 V
V₃ = IR₃ = 1.26 × 12 = 15.12 V
Check: 3.78 + 5.04 + 15.12 ≈ 24 V ✓
(ii) Parallel Combination:
(a) R_parallel: 1/R_p = 1/3 + 1/4 + 1/12
= 4/12 + 3/12 + 1/12 = 8/12
R_p = 12/8 = 1.5 Ω
(b) Total current: I = V/R_p = 24/1.5 = 16 A
(d) Current through each resistor (same V = 24V across all):
I₁ = V/R₁ = 24/3 = 8 A
I₂ = V/R₂ = 24/4 = 6 A
I₃ = V/R₃ = 24/12 = 2 A
Check: I₁+I₂+I₃ = 8+6+2 = 16 A ✓
Ch2 · Work & Energy B. (3 Marks) — (i) Define kinetic energy and potential energy with their SI and CGS units. (ii) Derive the relationship between Joule and erg. (iii) Convert 500 J into erg.
(i) Definitions:
  • Kinetic Energy (KE): The energy possessed by a body due to its motion. KE = ½mv² (m = mass in kg, v = velocity in m/s)
    SI unit: Joule (J)  |  CGS unit: erg
  • Potential Energy (PE): The energy possessed by a body due to its position or state. PE = mgh (m = mass, g = acceleration due to gravity, h = height)
    SI unit: Joule (J)  |  CGS unit: erg
(ii) Relationship between Joule and Erg — Derivation:
Work = Force × displacement
In SI: 1 Joule = 1 Newton × 1 metre = 1 kg·m/s² × 1 m = 1 kg·m²·s⁻²
In CGS: 1 erg = 1 dyne × 1 cm = 1 g·cm/s² × 1 cm = 1 g·cm²·s⁻²
Now convert: 1 kg = 1000 g = 10³ g; 1 m = 100 cm = 10² cm
1 Joule = 1 kg·m²·s⁻² = 10³ g × (10² cm)²·s⁻²
= 10³ × 10⁴ g·cm²·s⁻²
∴ 1 Joule = 10⁷ erg
(iii) Convert 500 J into erg:
1 J = 10⁷ erg
500 J = 500 × 10⁷ erg
∴ 500 J = 5 × 10⁹ erg
