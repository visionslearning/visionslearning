Ch1 A. Define momentum. How is it related to force? Give its SI unit.
Momentum (p): The product of mass (m) and velocity (v) of a moving body is called momentum.
p = mv
  • SI unit: kg·m/s
  • It is a vector quantity (has direction same as velocity).
  • Relation to Force: Newton's 2nd Law → Force = rate of change of momentum: F = Δp/Δt = ma
  • Greater momentum → greater force needed to stop the body.
Ch2 B. A machine does 600 J of work in 10 seconds. Find its power. Also convert it to horsepower. (1 hp = 746 W)
Given: W = 600 J, t = 10 s
Formula: P = W/t
P = 600/10 = 60 W
In horsepower: 60/746 ≈ 0.08 hp
Ch3 C. What is resistance? State the factors on which resistance of a conductor depends.
Resistance (R): The opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current through it is called resistance. SI unit: Ohm (Ω).
R = V/I
Factors affecting resistance:
  • 1. Length (L): R ∝ L — longer wire → more resistance.
  • 2. Cross-sectional area (A): R ∝ 1/A — thicker wire → less resistance.
  • 3. Material (ρ): R depends on resistivity of material. R = ρL/A
  • 4. Temperature: For metals, resistance increases with temperature.
Ch4 D. Find the number of moles and number of molecules in 44 g of CO₂. (Molar mass of CO₂ = 44 g/mol)
Given: mass = 44 g, M = 44 g/mol
Moles: n = m/M = 44/44 = 1 mole
Number of molecules: N = n × Nₐ = 1 × 6.022 × 10²³
∴ 1 mole; 6.022 × 10²³ molecules

Ch1 A. It is easier to stop a bicycle than a truck moving at the same velocity.
According to Newton's First Law and concept of inertia, a heavier object (truck) has greater inertia (mass) than a bicycle. Since momentum = mass × velocity, at the same velocity the truck has much more momentum. A greater force is needed to change (stop) the momentum of the truck. Therefore, it is harder to stop a truck than a bicycle moving at the same speed.
Ch3 B. Copper wire is used for electrical connections, not iron wire.
Copper has a very low electrical resistivity (ρ = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m) compared to iron (ρ = 10 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m). Lower resistivity means less resistance for the same length and thickness — so less energy is wasted as heat and more current flows efficiently. Copper is also ductile (can be drawn into thin wires) and does not oxidise easily. Hence copper is preferred for electrical wiring.
Ch4 C. The mole is used as a unit in chemistry even though it represents a very large number.
Atoms and molecules are extremely small — a single atom weighs about 10⁻²³ grams. In any chemical reaction or laboratory experiment, we deal with billions of atoms. The mole (= 6.022 × 10²³ particles) provides a convenient way to count these particles in macroscopic (practical) quantities. Just as a "dozen" = 12 items simplifies counting eggs, a mole simplifies counting atoms/molecules in a way that directly links to measurable gram quantities.

Ch3 A. Draw a circuit diagram with a battery, switch, ammeter, voltmeter and a resistor. Explain how to verify Ohm's Law experimentally.
Circuit Setup:
  • Connect a resistor R, ammeter (in series), battery, and rheostat in a series circuit.
  • Connect a voltmeter across the resistor (in parallel).
  • Include a key/switch to complete the circuit.
📐 Circuit diagram: Battery (+/–) → Key → Ammeter (A) → Resistor (R) → back to battery. Voltmeter (V) connected across resistor in parallel.
Procedure:
  • Close the switch. Adjust the rheostat to set different voltages (V).
  • For each setting, note the voltmeter reading (V) and ammeter reading (I).
  • Calculate V/I for each reading.
Observation & Conclusion:
  • V/I = constant = R (resistance) for all readings.
  • A graph of V vs I is a straight line through origin → proves V ∝ I.
  • This verifies Ohm's Law: V = IR
Ch5 B. What are salts? How are they formed? Classify salts with examples (normal, acidic, basic, double, complex).
Salts: Salts are ionic compounds formed when the H⁺ of an acid is replaced by a metal ion (or NH₄⁺ ion) through neutralisation.
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Classification of Salts:
  • 1. Normal salt: All H⁺ of acid replaced. pH ≈ 7 (if strong acid + strong base). E.g., NaCl, KNO₃.
  • 2. Acidic salt: Only some H⁺ replaced. pH < 7. E.g., NaHSO₄, NaHCO₃ (when acid is excess). Formed from strong acid + weak base.
  • 3. Basic salt: Contains OH⁻ or O²⁻ groups. pH > 7. E.g., Mg(OH)Cl, Pb(OH)NO₃. Formed from weak acid + strong base.
  • 4. Double salt: Contains two different cations or anions. E.g., Potash alum K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O.
  • 5. Complex salt: Contains complex ions. E.g., K₄[Fe(CN)₆] — potassium ferrocyanide.

Ch2 A. What is energy? Explain different forms of energy with examples. State and explain the law of conservation of energy.
Energy: The capacity to do work is called energy. SI unit: Joule (J).

Forms of Energy:
  • Mechanical energy: KE + PE. E.g., moving ball (KE), stretched spring (PE).
  • Heat energy: Energy due to temperature. E.g., burning wood.
  • Light energy: Emitted by luminous objects. E.g., Sun, lamp.
  • Electrical energy: Energy due to flow of charge. E.g., electricity from battery.
  • Chemical energy: Stored in bonds. E.g., food, fuel, batteries.
  • Sound energy: Energy in sound waves. E.g., vibrating string.
  • Nuclear energy: Released from nucleus. E.g., nuclear reactor.
Law of Conservation of Energy:
  • Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be converted from one form to another.
  • Total energy in an isolated system remains constant.
  • Example: In a hydroelectric power plant: Water PE → KE of water → KE of turbine → Electrical energy.
  • In a candle: Chemical energy → Heat energy + Light energy.
Ch4 B. Explain Avogadro's number and the mole concept. Calculate the number of atoms in 12 g of carbon and 4 g of hydrogen.
Avogadro's Number (Nₐ):
  • The number of atoms/molecules in 1 mole of any substance = 6.022 × 10²³.
  • Named after Amedeo Avogadro.
Mole Concept:
  • 1 mole of a substance = its molar mass in grams = 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
  • n = m/M; Number of particles N = n × Nₐ
Calculation 1 — 12 g of Carbon (C):
Molar mass of C = 12 g/mol
n = 12/12 = 1 mole
N = 1 × 6.022 × 10²³
∴ Number of C atoms = 6.022 × 10²³
Calculation 2 — 4 g of Hydrogen (H):
Molar mass of H = 1 g/mol
n = 4/1 = 4 moles
N = 4 × 6.022 × 10²³
∴ Number of H atoms = 2.409 × 10²⁴

Ch2 B. (i) Define kinetic energy and potential energy with SI units and CGS units. (ii) State the relationship between Joule and erg with derivation.
Kinetic Energy: Energy due to motion of a body. KE = ½mv²
  • SI unit: Joule (J)  |  CGS unit: erg
Potential Energy: Energy due to position/state of a body. PE = mgh
  • SI unit: Joule (J)  |  CGS unit: erg
Relationship — Joule and Erg:
  • 1 Joule = 1 kg × (1 m/s)² = 1 kg·m²·s⁻²
  • 1 kg = 1000 g = 10³ g
  • 1 m = 100 cm = 10² cm
  • ∴ 1 J = 10³ g × (10² cm)² × s⁻² = 10³ × 10⁴ g·cm²·s⁻²
  • 1 Joule = 10⁷ erg
