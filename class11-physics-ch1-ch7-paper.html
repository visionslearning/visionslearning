SECTION A — Q2: Very Short Answer Questions

Q11
Ch11 Mark State the principle of homogeneity of dimensions.
▼ Answer
In a physically correct equation, the dimensions of all terms on both sides must be identical. This principle is used to check the correctness of physical equations and to derive relationships.
Q12
Ch11 Mark Define absolute error and write its formula.
▼ Answer
Absolute error = |True value − Measured value| = |ā − aₙ|.
Mean absolute error: Δā = (Σ|aₙ − ā|) / n.
Unit is the same as the measured quantity.
Q13
Ch11 Mark What is a screw gauge? State its least count formula.
▼ Answer
A screw gauge is a precision instrument that uses a calibrated screw mechanism to measure small lengths (up to 0.01 mm).
Least count = Pitch / Number of circular scale divisions.
Typical: LC = 0.5 mm / 50 = 0.01 mm.
Q14
Ch11 Mark Round off 15.654 to 3 significant figures.
▼ Answer
15.654 rounded to 3 significant figures = 15.7.
(The 4th digit is 5, so the 3rd significant figure 6 rounds up to 7.)
Q15
Ch71 Mark Define specific heat capacity. Write its SI unit.
▼ Answer
Specific heat capacity: The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C (or 1 K).
Formula: Q = msΔT.
SI unit: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
Q16
Ch71 Mark What is latent heat? Name its two types.
▼ Answer
Latent heat: Heat energy absorbed or released during a change of state at constant temperature.
Types:
(1) Latent heat of fusion — solid → liquid (e.g., ice → water).
(2) Latent heat of vaporisation — liquid → gas (e.g., water → steam).
Q17
Ch71 Mark State Newton's Law of Cooling.
▼ Answer
The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the difference in temperature between the body and its surroundings, provided the difference is small.
dQ/dt ∝ (T − T₀)
Q18
Ch71 Mark What is a black body? What is emissivity of a perfect black body?
▼ Answer
A black body is one that absorbs all incident radiation completely and emits maximum radiation at any temperature.
Emissivity of a perfect black body: e = 1.

SECTION B — Q3 to Q14: Short Answer I (Attempt any 8 of 12)

8 × 2 = 16 Marks
Q3
Ch12 Marks Explain the terms: (i) Accuracy (ii) Precision in measurements.
▼ Answer
(i) Accuracy — Closeness of a measured value to the true value. A small systematic error means high accuracy.

(ii) Precision — Closeness of repeated measurements to each other. A small random error means high precision.

Example: True value = 10.0 cm.
• Readings 9.9, 10.0, 10.1 → accurate but less precise.
• Readings 9.5, 9.5, 9.5 → precise but inaccurate.
Q4
Ch12 Marks State four uses of dimensional analysis.
▼ Answer
  • (1) Check correctness of a physical equation (principle of homogeneity).
  • (2) Derive relationships between physical quantities.
  • (3) Convert a quantity from one system of units to another.
  • (4) Find the dimensions of unknown physical quantities in a formula.
Q5
Ch12 Marks Define and distinguish between systematic error and random error.
▼ Answer
Systematic error — consistently in one direction (always too high or too low); due to faulty instrument or environmental effects; can be eliminated.

Random error — varies randomly in direction and magnitude; due to fluctuations in conditions or human reaction time; reduced by taking the mean of several readings.
Q6
Ch12 Marks The velocity of a particle is given by v = at + bt². Find the dimensions of a and b.
▼ Answer
By principle of homogeneity: [v] = [at] = [bt²] = [LT⁻¹].

For 'at': [a][T] = [LT⁻¹] → [a] = [LT⁻²] (acceleration)
For 'bt²': [b][T²] = [LT⁻¹] → [b] = [LT⁻³]
a → [LT⁻²], b → [LT⁻³]
Q7
Ch12 Marks Explain the parallax method of measuring very large distances.
▼ Answer
Two observers at points A and B (baseline d apart) observe the same distant object. The parallax angle θ is measured.

Distance D = d / θ  (θ in radians)
For astronomical distances, the baseline = diameter of Earth or Earth's orbit (1 AU). This method is used to find distances to nearby stars and planets.
Q8
Ch72 Marks Explain the anomalous expansion of water and its significance.
▼ Answer
Water contracts on cooling like normal liquids BUT between 4°C and 0°C it expands. Density is maximum at 4°C (1000 kg/m³).

Significance: In winter, as a lake cools, water at 4°C (maximum density) sinks to the bottom. Ice forms at the surface while the bottom stays at 4°C, protecting aquatic life from freezing.
Q9
Ch72 Marks Distinguish between linear, superficial and cubical thermal expansion.
▼ Answer
Linear expansion: ΔL = LαΔT → coefficient α (K⁻¹)
Superficial expansion: ΔA = AβΔT → coefficient β = 2α
Cubical expansion: ΔV = VγΔT → coefficient γ = 3α
Relation: α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3
Q10
Ch72 Marks What is calorimetry? State the principle of calorimetry.
▼ Answer
Calorimetry is the measurement of heat exchanged in physical or chemical processes using a calorimeter.

Principle (Law of Mixtures): In an insulated system,
Heat lost by hot body = Heat gained by cold body
m₁s₁(T₁ − T) = m₂s₂(T − T₂)
where T is the final equilibrium temperature.
Q11
Ch72 Marks State and explain Wien's displacement law.
▼ Answer
Wien's Displacement Law: The wavelength of maximum emission (λₘ) of a black body is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature T.
λₘ × T = b = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K  (Wien's constant)
As T increases, λₘ shifts to shorter wavelengths.
Example: Sun (T ≈ 6000 K) → λₘ ≈ 480 nm (yellow-green light).
Q12
Ch72 Marks Briefly explain conduction, convection and radiation. Which mode does not need a medium?
▼ Answer
Conduction — heat transfer through a solid by molecular vibrations without bulk flow; needs a medium.
Convection — heat transfer by actual movement of fluid molecules; needs a medium.
Radiation — heat transfer by electromagnetic waves (infrared); does NOT need a medium.

Sun's heat reaches Earth through vacuum by radiation.
Q13
Ch12 Marks Use dimensional analysis to derive the formula for the time period of a simple pendulum (depends on L and g).
▼ Answer
Let T = k × Lᵃgᵇ. Dimensions:
[T] = [L]ᵃ × [LT⁻²]ᵇ = [L^(a+b) · T^(−2b)]
Comparing powers of T: −2b = 1 → b = −½
Comparing powers of L: a + b = 0 → a = ½
∴ T = k√(L/g)
With k = 2π (from experiment): T = 2π√(L/g)
Q14
Ch72 Marks A copper rod of length 1 m is heated from 20°C to 120°C. If α = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ /°C, find the increase in length.
▼ Answer
Given: L = 1 m, ΔT = 120 − 20 = 100°C, α = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ /°C
ΔL = L × α × ΔT
ΔL = 1 × 1.7×10⁻⁵ × 100
ΔL = 1.7 × 10⁻³ m
ΔL = 1.7 × 10⁻³ m = 1.7 mm

SECTION C — Q15 to Q26: Short Answer II (Attempt any 8 of 12)

Q15
Ch13 Marks Explain absolute error, relative error, and percentage error with formulas and examples.
▼ Answer
Absolute error = |ā − aₙ|
Mean absolute error Δā = (Σ|Δaₙ|) / n — same unit as quantity
Example: true = 5.0 cm, measured = 5.2 cm → Δa = 0.2 cm
Relative error = Δā / ā = 0.2 / 5.0 = 0.04 (dimensionless)
Percentage error = (Δā / ā) × 100 = 0.04 × 100 = 4%
% error = (Δā / ā) × 100
Q16
Ch13 Marks What are significant figures? State the rules for counting them with examples.
▼ Answer
Significant figures = all certain digits + one uncertain digit.

Rules:
  • (1) Non-zero digits are significant: 243 → 3 s.f.
  • (2) Zeros between non-zero digits are significant: 2003 → 4 s.f.
  • (3) Leading zeros are NOT significant: 0.0023 → 2 s.f.
  • (4) Trailing zeros after decimal are significant: 2.300 → 4 s.f.
  • (5) Trailing zeros in whole numbers are ambiguous — use scientific notation. Example: 6.022 × 10²³ → 4 s.f.
Q17
Ch13 Marks Define dimensional formula. Find dimensional formula of (i) gravitational constant G and (ii) Planck's constant h.
▼ Answer
Dimensional formula expresses a quantity in terms of fundamental dimensions [M, L, T].

(i) G: from F = Gm₁m₂/r² → G = Fr²/(m₁m₂)
[G] = [MLT⁻²][L²] / [M²] = [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]
(ii) h: from E = hν → h = E/ν
[h] = [ML²T⁻²] / [T⁻¹] = [ML²T⁻¹] (same as angular momentum)
[G] = M⁻¹L³T⁻²  |  [h] = ML²T⁻¹
Q18
Ch13 Marks A wire has radius r = (0.52 ± 0.01) mm and length L = (5.0 ± 0.1) cm. Find the percentage error in the volume (V = πr²L).
▼ Answer
ΔV/V = 2(Δr/r) + ΔL/L
Δr/r = 0.01/0.52 = 0.01923
ΔL/L = 0.1/5.0 = 0.02
ΔV/V = 2 × 0.01923 + 0.02 = 0.03846 + 0.02 = 0.05846
Percentage error ≈ 5.8%
Q19
Ch13 Marks Explain the oil film experiment to estimate the size of a molecule.
▼ Answer
A drop of oleic acid solution of known concentration is placed on water sprinkled with lycopodium powder. The oil spreads into a thin circular film of area A (measured by counting squares on a grid).

Volume of oil V = volume of drop × concentration
Diameter of molecule = t = V / A
Typical result: t ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m = 1 Å, consistent with known molecular sizes.
Q20
Ch73 Marks Derive the expression ΔL = LαΔT. Define the coefficient of linear expansion.
▼ Answer
Experiment shows:
• ΔL ∝ original length L
• ΔL ∝ rise in temperature ΔT

∴ ΔL = α × L × ΔT
Definition: α = ΔL/(L × ΔT) = fractional increase in length per unit rise in temperature. Unit: K⁻¹.

Final length: L' = L(1 + αΔT).
Area: β = 2α, ΔA = AβΔT.  |  Volume: γ = 3α, ΔV = VγΔT.
Q21
Ch73 Marks State Stefan-Boltzmann law and derive the formula for net heat radiated by a body.
▼ Answer
Stefan-Boltzmann Law: Emissive power of a black body = E = σT⁴, where σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴.
For a real body with emissivity e: E = eσT⁴.

Net radiation: A body at T in surroundings at T₀:
Emits: eσAT⁴    Absorbs: eσAT₀⁴
Net rate of heat loss: dQ/dt = eσA(T⁴ − T₀⁴)
dQ/dt = eσA(T⁴ − T₀⁴)
Q22
Ch73 Marks Explain the construction and working of Fery's black body.
▼ Answer
Fery's Black Body is a double-walled hollow sphere with a small hole (aperture).

Construction: The inner surface is coated with lampblack (highly absorptive). The double wall contains water/steam to maintain constant temperature.

Working: Any radiation entering the small hole undergoes multiple internal reflections and is completely absorbed — no radiation escapes back. The radiation emitted from the hole represents perfect black body radiation at the temperature of the walls.

It is a perfect black body because absorptance = 1 for all wavelengths (emissivity e = 1).
Q23
Ch73 Marks 50 g of ice at 0°C is added to 200 g of water at 50°C. Find the final temperature. (L_ice = 336 J/g, s_water = 4.2 J/g°C)
▼ Answer
Heat to melt all ice: Q_melt = 50 × 336 = 16,800 J
Heat available from hot water if cooled to 0°C:
Q_avail = 200 × 4.2 × 50 = 42,000 J
Since Q_avail > Q_melt → all ice melts ✓
After melting: 50 g water at 0°C + 200 g water at 50°C
Heat balance (let final temp = T):
Heat lost by hot water = Heat gained by melted ice
200 × 4.2 × (50−T) = 50 × 4.2 × T + 16800
42000 − 840T = 210T + 16800
1050T = 25200
T = 24°C
Final temperature = 24°C
Q24
Ch73 Marks Explain Kirchhoff's law of radiation. How does it explain that a good absorber is a good emitter?
▼ Answer
Kirchhoff's Law: At thermal equilibrium, for any body, the ratio of emissive power to absorptive power equals the emissive power of a perfect black body at that temperature:
Eλ / aλ = Eλ° (constant at given T)
Explanation: If aλ is high (good absorber), then Eλ must also be high (good emitter) for the ratio to remain constant.

• Polished silver: low absorptance → low emittance.
• Rough black surface: high absorptance → high emittance.

Used in design of radiators, thermos flasks, etc.
Q25
Ch13 Marks A physical quantity P = a²b³/(c√d). If errors in a, b, c, d are 1%, 2%, 3%, 4% respectively, find the percentage error in P.
▼ Answer
Taking log: ln P = 2 ln a + 3 ln b − ln c − ½ ln d
Differentiating for maximum error:
ΔP/P = 2(Δa/a) + 3(Δb/b) + (Δc/c) + ½(Δd/d)
% error = 2×1% + 3×2% + 1×3% + ½×4%
% error = 2% + 6% + 3% + 2%
% error in P = 13%
Q26
Ch73 Marks Explain Newton's Law of Cooling. Write its mathematical form and state conditions for validity.
▼ Answer
Statement: The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the excess of its temperature over the surroundings, for small temperature differences.

dQ/dt = −k(T − T₀)
Since dQ = ms dT:
ms(dT/dt) = −k(T − T₀) → dT/dt = −K(T − T₀)
Integrating: T − T₀ = (T₁ − T₀)e^(−Kt)
Conditions for validity:
  • (i) Temperature difference must be small (≤ 30°C).
  • (ii) Heat loss mainly by convection and radiation.
  • (iii) Surrounding temperature T₀ must remain constant.

Q27
Ch14 Marks Describe a vernier caliper. How is it used to measure the diameter of a sphere? Derive the formula for least count.
▼ Answer
Vernier Caliper is a precision instrument for measuring lengths to 0.1 mm or 0.02 mm.

Parts: Main scale with jaws A (external) and C (depth rod); vernier scale with jaw B; locking screw.

Least Count Derivation:
If N VSD = (N−1) MSD
Then 1 VSD = (N−1)/N MSD
LC = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 1/N MSD
For N = 10, 1 MSD = 1 mm: LC = 0.1 mm
To measure sphere diameter:
  • (1) Check zero error (close jaws, see if 0 of vernier aligns with 0 of main scale).
  • (2) Place sphere between external jaws.
  • (3) MSR = main scale reading just before 0 of vernier scale.
  • (4) VSR = vernier division coinciding with any main scale line.
  • (5) Diameter = MSR + VSR × LC ± zero error correction.
  • (6) Take readings at 3 different orientations, find mean.
Q28
Ch14 Marks What is dimensional analysis? State its applications and limitations.
▼ Answer
Dimensional analysis studies physical equations using dimensions of quantities. Every quantity is expressed as powers of M, L, T (and other fundamentals).

Applications:
  • (1) Check correctness: principle of homogeneity — all terms must have same dimensions.
  • (2) Derive formulae: speed of sound v = k√(E/ρ) derived by matching dimensions.
  • (3) Unit conversion: N₁u₁ = N₂u₂; example: 1 J = 10⁷ erg.
  • (4) Find unknown powers in a formula.
Limitations:
  • (1) Cannot determine dimensionless constants (2π, ½).
  • (2) Cannot handle equations with more than 3 unknown indices.
  • (3) Cannot distinguish between quantities with same dimensions (work and torque).
  • (4) Fails for exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric equations (e.g. s = A sin(ωt)).
Q29
Ch74 Marks Derive Newton's law of cooling from Stefan-Boltzmann law. Explain factors affecting rate of cooling.
▼ Answer
Derivation: Body at temperature T, surroundings at T₀.
Net heat loss: dQ/dt = eσA(T⁴ − T₀⁴)
Write T = T₀ + ΔT where ΔT is small.
T⁴ − T₀⁴ = (T₀+ΔT)⁴ − T₀⁴ ≈ 4T₀³ΔT (binomial approx.)
∴ dQ/dt ≈ 4eσAT₀³(T − T₀) = k(T − T₀)
This is Newton's Law of Cooling.
For ms(dT/dt) = −k(T−T₀):
dT/dt = −K(T−T₀)

Factors affecting rate of cooling:
  • (1) Nature of surface — dull black surface cools faster than polished.
  • (2) Area exposed — larger area, faster cooling.
  • (3) Mass — smaller mass cools faster (less heat to lose).
  • (4) Temperature difference — higher ΔT, faster cooling.
  • (5) Nature of surrounding medium — forced convection speeds up cooling.
Q30
Ch74 Marks Explain latent heat of fusion and vaporisation. Find the heat needed to convert 5 kg of ice at −10°C to steam at 100°C. (s_ice = 2100 J/kg°C, Lf = 336,000 J/kg, s_water = 4200 J/kg°C, Lv = 2,260,000 J/kg)
▼ Answer
Latent Heat of Fusion (Lf): Heat per unit mass absorbed/released during solid ↔ liquid change. For ice: Lf = 336 kJ/kg.
Latent Heat of Vaporisation (Lv): Heat per unit mass during liquid ↔ vapour change. For water: Lv = 2260 kJ/kg. (Lv > Lf because molecules must be completely separated in vaporisation.)

Numerical Solution (5 kg of ice at −10°C → steam at 100°C):
Stage 1 — Ice: −10°C → 0°C:
Q₁ = 5 × 2100 × 10 = 105,000 J

Stage 2 — Melt ice at 0°C:
Q₂ = 5 × 336,000 = 1,680,000 J

Stage 3 — Water: 0°C → 100°C:
Q₃ = 5 × 4200 × 100 = 2,100,000 J

Stage 4 — Vaporise at 100°C:
Q₄ = 5 × 2,260,000 = 11,300,000 J

Total Q = 105,000 + 1,680,000 + 2,100,000 + 11,300,000
Total heat = 15,185,000 J = 15.185 MJ
Q31
Ch74 Marks Explain three modes of heat transfer — conduction, convection, radiation — with one example each. Derive Fourier's law of conduction.
▼ Answer
(1) Conduction: Heat transfer through a solid without bulk movement of matter; energy passes by molecular vibration.
Example: One end of an iron rod heated → other end becomes hot.

Fourier's Law:
Experimentally: dQ/dt ∝ A, ∝ ΔT, ∝ 1/d
∴ dQ/dt = kA(T₁−T₂)/d
k = thermal conductivity (W/mK), A = cross-sectional area,
d = thickness, (T₁−T₂) = temperature difference
(2) Convection: Heat transfer by actual movement of fluid particles.
• Natural convection — driven by density differences.
• Forced convection — by external agency (fan).
Example: Sea breeze — land heats faster, air rises, cool air rushes in from sea.

(3) Radiation: Heat by electromagnetic waves (mainly infrared), needs no medium.
Example: Sun's heat reaches Earth through vacuum. All bodies above 0 K radiate. Rate: E = eσAT⁴.
