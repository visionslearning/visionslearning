Accounting Concepts & Conventions

Fundamental assumptions that govern accounting records & reporting

📖 Meaning & Introduction
Accounting Concepts are basic assumptions or conditions upon which the science of accounting is based. They are the foundation rules that every accountant follows while recording transactions.

Accounting Conventions are customs or practices followed by accountants to ensure uniformity, comparability, and fairness in financial statements.

These concepts and conventions are not legally enforceable but are universally accepted guidelines. They ensure that financial statements are prepared consistently and can be compared across periods and organisations.

💡 Important Accounting Concepts

1. Business Entity Concept

The business is treated as a separate entity from its owner. Owner's personal transactions are not recorded in business books.
Example: Capital introduced by owner is shown as a liability of the business.

2. Money Measurement Concept

Only those transactions that can be expressed in monetary terms are recorded. Non-monetary events (e.g., employee skill, customer loyalty) are not recorded.
Currency used in India: Indian Rupees (₹)

3. Going Concern Concept

The business is assumed to continue its operations for the foreseeable future. Assets are recorded at cost, not at liquidation value. Long-term investments are justified under this concept.

4. Cost Concept (Historical Cost)

Assets are recorded at their original purchase price (cost), not at current market value. The cost is the basis for all future accounting entries for that asset.
Example: Land bought for ₹5 lakh is always shown at ₹5 lakh.

5. Dual Aspect Concept

Every business transaction has two aspects — a debit and a credit of equal amount. This forms the basis of Double Entry Bookkeeping.
Formula: Assets = Liabilities + Capital

6. Accrual Concept

Revenues and expenses are recognised when they are earned or incurred, NOT when cash is received or paid. This gives a true and fair view of profit.
Example: Outstanding salary is recorded even if not yet paid.

7. Matching Concept

Expenses of a period should be matched with the revenues of the same period. Profit = Revenues earned – Expenses incurred during that period.
Related to: Accrual concept.

8. Periodicity Concept

The life of a business is divided into equal time periods (usually 12 months) for accounting purposes. This helps stakeholders assess performance regularly.
Accounting year in India: 1 April – 31 March.

9. Realisation Concept

Revenue is recognised only when it is actually earned — i.e., when goods/services are delivered and the right to receive payment arises. Advance receipts are NOT treated as revenue.

10. Objective Evidence Concept

Every accounting entry should be supported by an objective and verifiable document (voucher, invoice, receipt). This ensures reliability and authenticity of records.

📏 Accounting Conventions

1. Convention of Consistency

The same accounting methods and policies should be followed from one period to another. This allows meaningful comparison of financial statements across years.
Example: If SLM depreciation is used, it should be used every year.

2. Convention of Conservatism (Prudence)

"Anticipate no profits but provide for all possible losses." Accountants follow a cautious approach — recognise losses early but delay recognition of gains.
Example: Creating provision for bad debts.

3. Convention of Full Disclosure

All material information relevant to users of financial statements must be disclosed. Nothing significant should be hidden.
Example: Contingent liabilities disclosed in notes to accounts.

4. Convention of Materiality

Only those items that are significant enough to affect the decision of a user need to be separately disclosed. Insignificant items can be combined or ignored.
Example: Small stationery expense need not be shown separately.

Accounting Standards

Guidelines issued by ICAI for uniform preparation of financial statements

📖 Meaning & Definition
Accounting Standards (AS) are written policy documents issued by expert accounting bodies that lay down the rules for recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure of accounting transactions in financial statements.

In India, AS are issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) through its Accounting Standards Board (ASB), established in 1977.
🎯 Objectives of Accounting Standards

Uniformity

Ensure that financial statements of different companies are prepared on a uniform basis, making them comparable.

Reliability

Improve the reliability of financial statements so that users (investors, creditors) can trust the information.

Transparency

Promote transparency by requiring adequate disclosure of all material information in financial statements.

Reduce Manipulation

Prevent window dressing and creative accounting by laying down specific rules for recording and reporting.

Global Comparability

Harmonise domestic standards with International Accounting Standards (IAS/IFRS) for global comparability.

Guidance

Provide guidance to accountants and auditors on how to treat specific items in financial statements.

✅ Benefits / Advantages of Accounting Standards
#BenefitExplanation
1Uniformity in ReportingAll companies follow the same rules, making inter-firm comparison easy.
2Better Decision MakingInvestors, banks, and management get reliable data for decisions.
3Builds Investor ConfidenceStandardised statements increase trust in published accounts.
4Reduces Fraud & ErrorsClear guidelines reduce scope for manipulation of accounts.
5Helps AuditorsAuditors can verify accounts against defined standards easily.
6International RecognitionAligned with IFRS, helping Indian companies attract foreign investment.
7Legal ComplianceCompanies Act requires compliance with AS, giving them legal standing.
⚠️ Limitations of Accounting Standards

Rigidity

Standards may be too rigid and may not suit every type of business or situation.

Frequent Changes

Standards keep getting revised; keeping up with changes is time-consuming.

Complex Language

Some standards are written in technical language, making implementation difficult for small businesses.

May Allow Alternatives

Certain standards allow multiple treatment options, reducing comparability.

Cost of Implementation

Complying with all standards involves significant time and cost, especially for SMEs.

Not a Substitute for Judgement

Standards cannot replace professional judgement; they only provide a framework.

🏛️ Role of ICAI & Accounting Standards Board (ASB)
ICAI (Institute of Chartered Accountants of India) was established in 1949 under The Chartered Accountants Act. It is the sole body in India authorized to issue Accounting Standards.

ASB (Accounting Standards Board) was set up by ICAI in 1977. It formulates and revises accounting standards keeping in view the international standards (IFRS/IAS) issued by IASB.

Functions of ASB

• Formulate Accounting Standards
• Review & revise existing standards
• Provide guidance notes
• Harmonise with IFRS

Process of Issuing AS

1. Identify topic
2. Form a study group
3. Draft the standard
4. Invite public comments
5. Finalize & issue the standard

Applicability

AS are applicable to all entities covered under Companies Act, 2013. Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) follow a simplified version.

2 Marks Questions
Q1. What is the Business Entity Concept?
Ans. The Business Entity Concept states that a business is treated as a separate and distinct entity from its owner. All business transactions are recorded in the books of the business and not in the name of the owner. The owner's personal transactions are kept separate from business transactions. For example, capital introduced by the owner is treated as a liability of the business, not as the owner's asset.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
बिज़नेस एन्टिटी कॉन्सेप्ट (व्यवसाय अलग इकाई): बिज़नेस और मालिक अलग हैं। बिज़नेस के सारे लेन-देन बिज़नेस की किताबों में लिखे जाते हैं, मालिक के नाम से नहीं। मालिक के निजी खर्च और बिज़नेस के खर्च अलग-अलग रखे जाते हैं। मालिक ने जो पैसा लगाया (Capital) वह बिज़नेस पर देनदारी (Liability) है — मालिक की संपत्ति नहीं।
Q2. What is the Money Measurement Concept?
Ans. According to the Money Measurement Concept, only those transactions and events that can be expressed in terms of money are recorded in the books of accounts. Non-monetary events like the quality of management, employee morale, or air pollution caused by a factory cannot be measured in money and hence are not recorded in accounting books. In India, all accounts use Indian Rupees (₹) as the unit of measurement.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
मनी मेज़रमेंट कॉन्सेप्ट (धन मापन): सिर्फ उन लेन-देन को हिसाब में लिया जाता है जिन्हें पैसे में नापा जा सके। जो बातें पैसे में नहीं नापी जा सकतीं — जैसे मैनेजमेंट की क्वालिटी, कर्मचारियों का मनोबल या फैक्ट्री से होने वाला प्रदूषण — उन्हें हिसाब की किताबों में नहीं लिखा जाता। भारत में सारा हिसाब रुपये (₹) में रखा जाता है।
Q3. What is the Going Concern Concept?
Ans. The Going Concern Concept assumes that a business will continue its operations for an indefinitely long period in the future. It is not expected to be closed down or liquidated in the near future. Because of this assumption, assets are recorded at historical cost and depreciated over their useful lives. Long-term investments and prepaid expenses are also justified under this concept.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
गोइंग कॉन्सर्न कॉन्सेप्ट (चालू उद्यम): माना जाता है कि बिज़नेस लंबे समय तक चलता रहेगा, जल्दी बंद या बिक्री नहीं होगी। इसी वजह से एसेट्स को खरीदी कीमत (historical cost) पर लिखा जाता है और उनका डेप्रिसिएशन उनकी उपयोगी ज़िंदगी पर लगाया जाता है। लंबी अवधि के निवेश और प्रीपेड एक्सपेंस भी इसी के तहत जायज़ हैं।
Q4. What is the Dual Aspect Concept?
Ans. The Dual Aspect Concept states that every business transaction has two equal and opposite effects — a debit and a credit. This is the foundation of the Double Entry System of bookkeeping. The accounting equation based on this concept is: Assets = Liabilities + Capital. For example, when a business buys goods for cash, one account (Purchases) is debited and another (Cash) is credited.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
डुअल एस्पेक्ट कॉन्सेप्ट (द्वैत पक्ष): हर लेन-देन के दो बराबर और उलटे असर होते हैं — एक डेबिट, एक क्रेडिट। यही डबल एंट्री सिस्टम की बुनियाद है। फॉर्मूला: Assets = Liabilities + Capital। उदाहरण: नकद से सामान खरीदने पर Purchases डेबिट और Cash क्रेडिट होता है।
Q5. What is the Periodicity (Accounting Period) Concept?
Ans. The Periodicity Concept states that the life of a business should be divided into equal and shorter time periods for the purpose of preparing financial statements. Usually, the accounting period is 12 months. In India, the financial year runs from 1st April to 31st March. This helps owners, investors, and government bodies to assess the financial performance and position of the business at regular intervals.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
पिरियॉडिसिटी कॉन्सेप्ट (लेखा अवधि): बिज़नेस की ज़िंदगी को बराबर और छोटे समय के हिस्सों में बाँटा जाता है ताकि फाइनेंशियल स्टेटमेंट बनाए जा सकें। आमतौर पर अवधि 12 महीने की होती है। भारत में फाइनेंशियल ईयर 1 अप्रैल से 31 मार्च तक चलता है। इससे मालिक, निवेशक और सरकार समय-समय पर प्रदर्शन देख सकते हैं।
Q6. What is the Convention of Conservatism?
Ans. The Convention of Conservatism (also called Prudence) states that an accountant should "anticipate no profits but provide for all possible losses." This means that expected losses must be recorded immediately, but expected profits should only be recorded when they are actually realised. Example: Creating a provision for bad debts even before they actually occur. This ensures that financial statements do not overstate profits or assets.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
कन्वेंशन ऑफ कंज़र्वेटिज़्म (रूढ़िवादिता / सावधानी): अकाउंटेंट का नियम — “मुनाफे की उम्मीद मत करो, लेकिन हर संभव नुकसान का इंतज़ाम करो।” उम्मीद का नुकसान तुरंत लिखो, उम्मीद का मुनाफा सिर्फ तब लिखो जब असल में मिले। उदाहरण: bad debts का प्रोविज़न पहले से बनाना। इससे मुनाफा या एसेट्स ज़्यादा नहीं दिखते।
5 Marks Questions
Q7. Explain the Accrual Concept and Matching Concept with examples.
Ans.
Accrual Concept: According to this concept, revenues and expenses are recognised in the accounting period in which they are earned or incurred, regardless of when cash is actually received or paid. It ensures a true and fair view of profit.
Example: If rent of ₹5,000 is due for March but not yet paid, it is still recorded as an expense in March (Outstanding Rent – Liability).

Matching Concept: This concept states that the expenses of a particular accounting period must be matched with the revenues of the same period to find the correct profit or loss. It is an extension of the accrual concept.
Example: Cost of goods sold should be matched with the revenue earned from selling those goods in the same period. Prepaid expenses (future benefit) and outstanding expenses (past benefit unpaid) are adjusted accordingly.

Relationship: Both concepts together ensure that the Profit & Loss Account reflects the true profit for the period — not just the cash inflows and outflows.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
एक्रूअल व मैचिंग कॉन्सेप्ट:
💰 Accrual (प्रोद्भव्): आमदनी और खर्च उस अवधि में लिखे जाते हैं जब कमाए/हुए, नकद मिलने/देने पर नहीं। उदाहरण: मार्च का किराया ₹5,000 बाकी है तो भी मार्च में खर्च (Outstanding Rent – Liability) दिखाओ।
⚖ Matching (मिलान): उसी अवधि के खर्च उसी अवधि की आमदनी से मिलाओ ताकि सही प्रॉफिट निकले — बेचे सामान का कॉस्ट उसी अवधि की सेल से मिलाओ।
ये दोनों मिलकर P&L में सही प्रॉफिट दिखाते हैं — सिर्फ नकद आने-जाने से नहीं।
Q8. Explain any four Accounting Conventions.
Ans.
1. Convention of Consistency: The same accounting policies and methods must be used consistently from one period to another. This makes financial statements comparable over time. Example: If Straight Line Method (SLM) of depreciation is used, it should be used every year.

2. Convention of Conservatism: "Anticipate no profits, provide for all possible losses." Losses are recorded as soon as they are probable, but profits are recorded only when realised. Example: Provision for doubtful debts.

3. Convention of Full Disclosure: All material information relevant to users should be fully disclosed in financial statements or notes. Nothing significant should be hidden. Example: Contingent liabilities must be shown in notes to accounts.

4. Convention of Materiality: Only items that are significant enough to influence the decisions of users need separate disclosure. Insignificant items may be merged with other items. Example: Postage expenses of ₹50 need not be shown separately.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
चार अकाउंटिंग कन्वेंशन (रूढ़ियाँ):
1️⃣ Consistency (एकरूपता): हर साल एक ही अकाउंटिंग तरीका अपनाओ ताकि सालों की तुलना हो सके। उदाहरण: डेप्रिसिएशन का SLM तरीका एक बार चुना तो हर साल वही।
2️⃣ Conservatism (सावधानी): “मुनाफे की उम्मीद मत करो, नुकसान का इंतज़ाम करो।” उदाहरण: doubtful debts का प्रोविज़न।
3️⃣ Full Disclosure (पूर्ण प्रकटीकरण): ज़रूरी सारी जानकारी दिखाओ, कुछ छिपाओ नहीं। उदाहरण: contingent liabilities नोट्स में दिखाओ।
4️⃣ Materiality (महत्त्व): सिर्फ वही आइटम अलग दिखाओ जो निर्णय बदल सकें; छोटे आइटम मिला दो। उदाहरण: ₹50 का पोस्टेज अलग दिखाने की ज़रूरत नहीं।

Chapter 2 — Accounting Standards

2 marks · 5 marks · 8 marks questions

2 Marks Questions
Q1. What are Accounting Standards?
Ans. Accounting Standards are written policy documents issued by expert accounting bodies that establish rules for recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure of accounting transactions in financial statements. In India, they are issued by ICAI (Institute of Chartered Accountants of India) through its Accounting Standards Board (ASB). Their main purpose is to bring uniformity and reliability in financial reporting.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
अकाउंटिंग स्टैंडर्ड्स (लेखांकन मानक): ये एक्सपर्ट बॉडी के लिखित नियम हैं जो तय करते हैं कि लेन-देन को कैसे रिकॉर्ड, नाप, दिखाओ और खोलो (disclose)। भारत में ये ICAI के ASB द्वारा जारी होते हैं। मकसद: फाइनेंशियल रिपोर्टिंग में एकरूपता और भरोसेमंदपन लाना।
Q2. What is ICAI? When was it established?
Ans. ICAI stands for Institute of Chartered Accountants of India. It was established in the year 1949 under The Chartered Accountants Act. It is the sole body authorised to issue Accounting Standards in India. It set up the Accounting Standards Board (ASB) in 1977 to formulate and revise accounting standards in line with international practices.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
ICAI (इंस्टीट्यूट ऑफ चार्टर्ड अकाउंटेंट्स ऑफ इंडिया): भारत में अकाउंटिंग स्टैंडर्ड जारी करने वाली इकलौती बॉडी। इसकी स्थापना 1949 में Chartered Accountants Act के तहत हुई। इसने 1977 में Accounting Standards Board (ASB) बनाई ताकि अंतरराष्ट्रीय प्रैक्टिस के मुताबिक स्टैंडर्ड बनाए और बदले जा सकें।
Q3. State the meaning of AS 1.
Ans. AS 1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies requires that all significant accounting policies adopted by a company in the preparation of financial statements must be disclosed. This helps users understand the basis on which financial statements are prepared. Policies once adopted should be applied consistently. Any change in policy must be disclosed with its financial effect. It is the most fundamental of all Accounting Standards.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
AS 1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies (लेखा नीतियों का प्रकटीकरण): कंपनी को अपनी सारी ज़रूरी अकाउंटिंग नीतियाँ (डेप्रिसिएशन तरीका, इन्वेंटरी वैल्यूएशन आदि) फाइनेंशियल स्टेटमेंट में खोलकर बतानी पड़ती हैं। नीति एक बार चुनी तो हर साल वही; कोई बदलाव करो तो उसका फाइनेंशियल असर भी बताओ। यह सबसे बुनियादी स्टैंडर्ड है।
Q4. What is AS 2? State the rule for valuation of inventory.
Ans. AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories deals with the accounting treatment for inventories (stock). According to AS 2, inventories should be valued at Cost or Net Realisable Value (NRV), whichever is lower. This follows the Convention of Conservatism. Cost includes purchase price, freight, duties, and other costs to bring the inventory to its present condition. NRV is the estimated selling price minus estimated costs of completion and selling.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories (इन्वेंटरी/स्टॉक का मूल्यांकन): इन्वेंटरी को Cost या Net Realisable Value (NRV), जो भी कम हो, उस पर वैल्यू करो (यह Conservatism रूढ़ि है)। Cost में खरीदी कीमत, फ्रेट, ड्यूटी और सामान को तैयार करने का खर्च आता है। NRV = अनुमानित बिक्री मूल्य − पूरा करने और बेचने का अनुमानित खर्च।
Q5. What is AS 9 — Revenue Recognition?
Ans. AS 9 — Revenue Recognition deals with the basis for recognising revenue in the Profit & Loss Account. Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when all significant risks and rewards of ownership have been transferred to the buyer. Revenue from services is recognised on the basis of completion of service. Interest income is recognised on a time proportion basis. Revenue is recognised when it is reasonable to expect that it will be collected.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
AS 9 — Revenue Recognition (आय पहचान): रेवेन्यू P&L में कब और कैसे दिखानी है यह तय करता है। सामान बेचने पर जब रिस्क और फ़ायदे खरीददार को ट्रांसफर हो जाएँ तब रेवेन्यू पहचानो। सर्विस में पूरा होने के हिसाब से। ब्याज समय के हिसाब से। और तब जब वसूली की उम्मीद हो।
5 Marks Questions
Q6. State the objectives of Accounting Standards.
Ans. The main objectives of Accounting Standards are:

1. Uniformity: AS ensure that all companies prepare financial statements on a uniform basis, making them comparable across organisations.

2. Reliability: They improve the reliability of financial information, helping investors and creditors make informed decisions.

3. Transparency: AS require disclosure of all material information, promoting transparency in financial reporting.

4. Reduce Manipulation: Clear accounting rules prevent window dressing and creative accounting, reducing the scope for fraud.

5. Global Comparability: By aligning with IFRS, Indian standards help attract foreign investment and enable global comparison of financial statements.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
अकाउंटिंग स्टैंडर्ड के उद्देश्य:
1️⃣ एकरूपता: सब कंपनियाँ एक जैसे तरीके से स्टेटमेंट बनाएँ ताकि तुलना हो सके।
2️⃣ भरोसेमंदपन: जानकारी पर निवेशक-क्रेडिटर भरोसा करें।
3️⃣ पारदर्शिता: ज़रूरी सारी जानकारी खुली रखो।
4️⃣ हेरफेर कम: साफ नियम से window-dressing रुके।
5️⃣ वैश्विक तुलना: IFRS से मिलाकर विदेशी निवेश आए और दुनिया से तुलना हो।
Q7. State the benefits of Accounting Standards.
Ans.
1. Uniformity in Reporting: All companies follow the same rules making inter-firm comparison easy.

2. Better Decision Making: Investors, banks, and management get reliable data for sound decisions.

3. Builds Investor Confidence: Standardised statements increase trust in published accounts and attract investment.

4. Reduces Fraud & Errors: Clear guidelines reduce scope for manipulation or misrepresentation of accounts.

5. Helps Auditors: Auditors can verify accounts against defined standards, making the audit process easier and more objective.

6. Legal Compliance: The Companies Act, 2013 requires compliance with Accounting Standards, giving them legal enforceability.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
अकाउंटिंग स्टैंडर्ड के फायदे:
1️⃣ एक जैसी रिपोर्टिंग से फर्मों की आपसी तुलना आसान।
2️⃣ भरोसेमंद डेटा से निवेशक-बैंक-मैनेजमेंट अच्छा निर्णय लें।
3️⃣ एक जैसी स्टेटमेंट से निवेशकों का भरोसा और निवेश बढ़े।
4️⃣ साफ नियमों से धोखाधड़ी व गलतियाँ कम।
5️⃣ ऑडिटर को तय मानक से जाँच आसान और वस्तुनिष्ठ।
6️⃣ Companies Act, 2013 इन्हें कानूनी ताकत देता है।

8 Marks Questions
Q9. Explain in detail the objectives, benefits, and limitations of Accounting Standards.
Ans.
A. Objectives:
1. To bring uniformity in preparation of financial statements.
2. To improve reliability and trustworthiness of financial information.
3. To ensure full and transparent disclosure of material information.
4. To reduce manipulation and fraudulent reporting.
5. To harmonise Indian accounting with International standards (IFRS).

B. Benefits:
1. Makes inter-company comparison possible due to uniform accounting methods.
2. Increases investor confidence in financial statements.
3. Provides a ready framework and guidance to accountants and auditors.
4. Helps the government in monitoring company performance and levying taxes.
5. Facilitates foreign investment by aligning with global standards.

C. Limitations:
1. Some standards are rigid and do not suit every type or size of business.
2. Frequent revisions make it difficult for businesses to keep up.
3. Technical language makes implementation difficult for small entities.
4. Certain standards offer multiple treatment options, reducing comparability.
5. High cost of compliance, especially for SMEs (Small & Medium Enterprises).

स्टैंडर्ड के उद्देश्य, फायदे व सीमाएँ:
🎯 उद्देश्य: एकरूपता, भरोसेमंदपन, पूर्ण प्रकटीकरण, हेरफेर कम, IFRS से मेल।
✅ फायदे: फर्मों की तुलना, निवेशक भरोसा, अकाउंटेंट-ऑडिटर को मार्गदर्शन, सरकार को निगरानी-टैक्स में मदद, विदेशी निवेश।

Q10. Write short notes on any four Accounting Standards.
Ans.
1. AS 1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies: All significant accounting policies (depreciation method, inventory valuation, etc.) must be disclosed in financial statements. Policies should be applied consistently. Any change and its effect must be disclosed.

2. AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories: Inventories must be valued at Cost or Net Realisable Value (NRV), whichever is lower. Cost includes purchase price, import duties, and other costs. Methods allowed: FIFO or Weighted Average Cost.

3. AS 9 — Revenue Recognition: Revenue from sale of goods is recognised when risks and rewards are transferred to the buyer. Revenue from services is recognised as per completion. Interest is recognised on time proportion basis.

4. AS 10 — Property, Plant & Equipment: Fixed assets are recorded at cost of acquisition. Cost includes purchase price + directly attributable expenses. Depreciation is charged systematically over the useful life of the asset. On disposal, profit/loss is recognised in the P&L account.
🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें
चार अकाउंटिंग स्टैंडर्ड (संक्षेप में):
1️⃣ AS 1 — सारी ज़रूरी अकाउंटिंग नीतियाँ खोलकर बताओ, हर साल एक जैसी रखो, बदलाव का असर दिखाओ।
2️⃣ AS 2 — इन्वेंटरी = Cost या NRV जो कम हो; FIFO या Weighted Average तरीके इजाज़त।
3️⃣ AS 9 — रेवेन्यू तब पहचानो जब रिस्क-फ़ायदे ट्रांसफर हों (सामान) या सर्विस पूरी हो; ब्याज समय-अनुपात पर।
4️⃣ AS 10 — फिक्स्ड एसेट खरीदी कीमत + सीधे खर्च पर; उपयोगी ज़िंदगी पर डेप्रिसिएशन; बेचने पर प्रॉफिट/लॉस P&L में।
