Q.5 — Answer in 2-3 sentences each 2 marks each | Total 10 marks
5.1
What is a Distributional Map? Name any two types.
2
Definition
A distributional map shows the geographical distribution or spread of a particular phenomenon over an area using symbols, shading, dots, or lines.
Types
1. Dot map — uses dots to show distribution (e.g., population distribution) 2. Choropleth map — uses shading to show density 3. Isopleth map — uses lines joining equal values.
5.2
Distinguish between Endogenetic and Exogenetic movements.
2
Endogenetic
Forces originating from inside the Earth. Build up the Earth's surface. e.g. Earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain building, folding, faulting.
Exogenetic
Forces originating from outside the Earth (external agents). Wear down the Earth's surface. e.g. Erosion by rivers, glaciers, wind, waves, weathering.
5.3
What is weathering? State its types.
2
Definition
Weathering is the process of breaking down of rocks in situ (at their original place) due to physical, chemical, or biological agents, without transportation.
Types
Physical (Mechanical): Temperature changes cause expansion/contraction, breaking rocks. Chemical: Oxidation, carbonation, hydration dissolve rocks. Biological: Roots of plants and organisms break rocks.
5.4
What is precipitation? Name its forms.
2
Definition
Precipitation is the process by which water droplets or ice crystals in clouds become heavy enough to fall to the Earth's surface.
Forms
Rain (liquid water), Snow (frozen crystals), Sleet (rain + snow), Hail (solid ice pellets), Frost (ice crystals on surfaces), Dew (water droplets).
5.5
What is salinity of sea water? State the factors affecting it.
2
Salinity
Amount of dissolved salts in sea water, expressed in parts per thousand (‰). Average ocean salinity = 35‰.
Factors
Increases salinity: High evaporation, low rainfall, enclosed seas. Decreases salinity: Heavy rainfall, river inflow, melting ice. e.g. Red Sea (high evaporation) = 40‰; Baltic Sea (river inflow) = 7‰.
Q.6 — Answer in detail (Long Answer) 3–4 marks each | Total 8 marks
6.1
Explain the types of rainfall with diagrams. (Any two types)
4
1. Convectional
Cause: Intense heating of Earth's surface → warm moist air rises rapidly → cools → heavy rainfall with thunder and lightning. Region: Equatorial/tropical areas (Amazon, Congo). Time: Afternoon. Heavy, short duration.
2. Orographic (Relief)
Cause: Moist winds blow towards mountains → forced to rise → cool → condense → rain on windward side. Leeward side gets very little rain (rain shadow area). Example: Western Ghats — heavy rain on western side; Deccan plateau = rain shadow.
3. Cyclonic (Frontal)
Cause: Warm and cold air masses meet → warm air rises over cold air → cools → rainfall. Region: Temperate zones. Example: Rainfall in North India during winter (western disturbances).
∴ 3 types: Convectional (tropical), Orographic (mountains), Cyclonic (frontal/temperate)
6.2
Explain the work of a river — erosion, transportation and deposition. Name one landform of each.
4
1. Erosion
River cuts and wears away rocks and soil. Methods: Hydraulic action (water pressure), Abrasion (rocks scraping bed), Corrosion (chemical dissolution), Attrition (fragments wearing each other down). Landform: Gorge, V-shaped valley, Waterfall.
2. Transportation
River carries eroded material downstream. Methods: Solution (dissolved), Suspension (fine particles carried), Saltation (bouncing), Traction (rolling large boulders).
3. Deposition
When river velocity decreases, it drops its load. Occurs in lower course, plains, and at mouth. Landforms: Floodplain, Meander, Oxbow lake, Delta (at river mouth).
∴ Upper course = erosion (V-valley). Middle = transport (meander). Lower = deposition (delta).

⭐ Important Definitions — Must Know (Exam Tips)
Key
One-line definitions — All chapters
Volcano
A vent or opening in Earth's crust through which molten rock (magma), gases and ash escape to the surface. e.g. Mt. Vesuvius, Mt. Fuji.
Earthquake
Sudden shaking/trembling of the Earth's surface caused by release of energy from within the Earth along fault lines.
Tsunami
Giant ocean waves caused by underwater earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides. Travel at 800+ km/h.
Fold Mountains
Mountains formed by folding of rock layers due to compressional forces at convergent plate boundaries. e.g. Himalayas, Alps, Rockies.
Meander
A large curve or loop in a river's course, formed in the middle and lower course where the river flows slowly across a wide floodplain.
Oxbow Lake
A cut-off meander loop that becomes an isolated lake when the river straightens its course by cutting through the neck of the meander.
Glacier
A slow-moving mass/body of ice formed in high mountain areas where snowfall exceeds melting. Carves U-shaped valleys.
Hydrological Cycle
The continuous circulation of water between the ocean, atmosphere and land through processes of evaporation, condensation, precipitation and runoff.
Ocean Currents
Large scale, continuous movements of ocean water in definite direction. Warm currents flow from tropics to poles; cold currents from poles to tropics.
Tide
Periodic rise (high tide) and fall (low tide) of sea level caused by gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on ocean water.
Insolation
Solar radiation received by the Earth's surface. Intensity varies by latitude, season, and time of day.
Rain Shadow
The dry area on the leeward (sheltered) side of a mountain that receives little rainfall because moisture was lost on the windward side.
