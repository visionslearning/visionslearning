Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
Ch6 i. What are bryophytes? Why are they called 'amphibians of the plant kingdom'?
Answer Bryophytes are small, non-vascular plants (mosses, liverworts, hornworts) that grow in moist, shaded places; they are called 'amphibians of the plant kingdom' because they need water for reproduction (for fertilisation) but can live on land, just like amphibians.
Ch7 ii. What is an ecological pyramid?
Answer An ecological pyramid is a graphical representation of the number, biomass, or energy at each trophic level in a food chain, with producers at the base and top predators at the apex; it is usually pyramid-shaped because the number and energy decrease at each level.
Ch8 iii. What is an antibiotic? Give two examples.
Answer An antibiotic is a chemical substance produced by microorganisms (bacteria or fungi) that inhibits or kills other harmful bacteria; examples: Penicillin (from Penicillium fungus) and Streptomycin (from Streptomyces bacteria).
Ch9 iv. What is soil erosion?
Answer Soil erosion is the removal of the topsoil (fertile layer) by wind, water, or human activities like deforestation and overgrazing; it leads to loss of soil fertility, desertification, and reduced agricultural productivity.
Ch10 v. What is the difference between hardware and software?
Answer Hardware refers to the physical, tangible components of a computer (e.g., keyboard, CPU, monitor, RAM), while software refers to the set of instructions/programs that tell the hardware what to do (e.g., Windows OS, MS Word, games); hardware is what you touch, software is what you run.

Ch7 A. Ecosystem — components and types. (4M)
Ecosystem: A self-sustaining unit where living organisms (biotic components) interact with each other and with the non-living environment (abiotic components). Biotic Components: • Producers: Green plants, algae — make food by photosynthesis • Consumers: Animals — eat plants or other animals (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary) • Decomposers: Bacteria and fungi — break down dead matter, return nutrients to soil Abiotic Components: Sunlight, temperature, water, air, soil, minerals Types of Ecosystem: 1. Natural: Forest, pond, ocean, grassland, desert 2. Artificial (man-made): Aquarium, crop field, garden
📐 Draw: Ecosystem diagram showing Sun → Producers → Consumers (1°, 2°, 3°) → Decomposers → Nutrients back to soil → Plants.
Ch8 B. Food preservation methods — explain any 4 methods with examples. (4M)
Food Preservation: Methods used to prevent food from spoilage caused by microbes, so it remains safe and nutritious for longer periods. 1. Pasteurisation: Heating milk at 72°C for 15 sec → kills pathogens. E.g., pasteurised milk, juices. 2. Refrigeration/Freezing: Low temperature slows microbial growth. E.g., storing vegetables, meat in refrigerator/freezer. 3. Adding salt/sugar: High concentration removes water from microbes (osmosis) → kills them. E.g., pickles (salt), jams (sugar). 4. Canning/Sterilisation: Food sealed in airtight cans after heating to high temp → no microbes. E.g., canned vegetables, fish. 5. Drying/Dehydration: Removing moisture prevents microbial growth. E.g., dried fish, sun-dried fruits, spices.
Ch9 C. Water pollution — causes, effects and prevention. (4M)
Water Pollution: Addition of harmful substances (pollutants) to water bodies making it unfit for use by living organisms. Causes: • Industrial effluents — chemicals, heavy metals (mercury, lead) into rivers • Agricultural runoff — pesticides, fertilisers (nitrates, phosphates) • Domestic sewage — untreated household waste • Religious activities — idol immersion, plastic waste in rivers/seas Effects: • Waterborne diseases — cholera, typhoid, dysentery • Death of aquatic organisms • Eutrophication — algal bloom, O₂ depletion Prevention: • Sewage treatment plants; industrial waste treatment before discharge • Ban on plastic; organic farming; Namami Gange programme
Ch10 D. Uses of ICT in education and daily life. (4M)
ICT (Information Communication Technology): Use of computers, internet, mobiles, and digital tools to manage, communicate and share information. Uses in Education: • E-learning platforms — video lectures, online courses (YouTube, Khan Academy) • Digital libraries — access to books and research papers • Online examinations and result checking • Interactive smart boards in classrooms Uses in Daily Life: • Online banking and digital payments (UPI, net banking) • Telemedicine — consult doctors online • E-commerce — shopping online (Amazon, Flipkart) • Social media for communication and news • GPS navigation and weather forecasting

Ch7 A. Explain the nitrogen cycle in nature. Why is nitrogen important for living organisms? (4M)
Nitrogen Cycle: The biogeochemical cycle by which nitrogen is converted between its various chemical forms as it circulates through the atmosphere, terrestrial, and aquatic ecosystems. Importance of Nitrogen: Nitrogen is essential for making proteins, DNA, RNA, and chlorophyll — vital for all living organisms. The atmosphere is 78% N₂ but most organisms cannot use it directly. Steps of Nitrogen Cycle: 1. Nitrogen Fixation: N₂ (gas) → NH₃ (ammonia) • By Rhizobium bacteria in legume root nodules • By free-living bacteria (Azotobacter) in soil • By lightning (converts N₂ to NO₃) 2. Nitrification: NH₃ → NO₂ → NO₃ (by Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter bacteria) → plants absorb nitrates 3. Assimilation: Plants absorb NO₃ → make proteins → animals eat plants → get nitrogen 4. Ammonification: Dead organisms decomposed by bacteria/fungi → NH₃ returns to soil 5. Denitrification: NO₃ → N₂ gas back to atmosphere (by Pseudomonas bacteria)
📐 Draw circular diagram: Atmosphere (N₂) → Nitrogen fixation → Soil nitrates → Plants → Animals → Dead matter → Decomposers → Soil → Denitrification → back to Atmosphere.
Ch9 B. What is the greenhouse effect? Explain global warming and its consequences. What are the solutions? (4M)
Greenhouse Effect: The natural process where greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O vapour) in the atmosphere trap heat (infrared radiation) from Earth's surface, keeping Earth warm enough to support life. Without it, Earth would be −18°C. Enhanced Greenhouse Effect (Global Warming): Human activities have increased greenhouse gases → more heat trapped → global temperature rises → this is global warming. Major Greenhouse Gases and Sources: • CO₂ — burning fossil fuels, deforestation • CH₄ — cattle, rice fields, landfills • N₂O — fertilisers, industrial processes Consequences of Global Warming: • Melting glaciers → rising sea levels → coastal flooding (Mumbai, Bangladesh) • Droughts, floods, extreme cyclones — increased frequency • Coral reef bleaching; extinction of species • Disruption of agriculture and food security Solutions: • Renewable energy (solar, wind, hydro) instead of fossil fuels • Afforestation — plant more trees • Energy-efficient appliances; electric vehicles • International agreements: Paris Agreement (2015), net-zero emissions target
Ch10 C. Explain the components of a computer. What are input, output and storage devices? Give two examples of each. (4M)
Computer Components: 1. CPU (Central Processing Unit): The brain of computer — performs all calculations and controls other components. Contains ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit) and CU (Control Unit). 2. Memory: RAM (temporary) — stores data in use; ROM (permanent) — stores boot instructions.

Ch9 A student visits a village and observes: (1) A factory discharging smoke into the air, (2) A farmer burning crop stubble in the field, (3) A river being used as a dumping ground for plastic waste, (4) A woman installing solar panels on her roof. Answer the following: (4M)
i. Which two observations are examples of environmental pollution? What type of pollution do they cause? (1) Factory smoke → Air pollution (releases CO₂, SO₂, particulate matter → respiratory diseases, acid rain) (3) Plastic in river → Water pollution (kills aquatic life, enters food chain, contaminates drinking water) ii. What are the harmful effects of burning crop stubble? • Releases CO₂, CO, and toxic smoke → severe air pollution (smog) • Destroys beneficial soil microorganisms and insects • Contributes to greenhouse effect and global warming • Better alternative: Use crop residue for biogas production or composting iii. How does the solar panel installation help the environment? Solar panels generate electricity using sunlight — a renewable, clean energy source that produces no CO₂, reduces use of fossil fuels, lowers electricity bills, and helps reduce global warming. iv. Name one law/principle related to environmental protection in India. Environment Protection Act (1986) — gives government power to take measures to protect and improve environmental quality. Also: 3R Principle (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle); Wildlife Protection Act (1972); Forest Conservation Act (1980).
