⚙️ Rotational Dynamics

🔑 Key Concepts
  • Moment of Inertia (I) — rotational analogue of mass; I = Σmr²
  • Radius of Gyration (K) — I = MK²; distance from axis where total mass is concentrated
  • Theorem of Parallel Axes: I = I_cm + Mh² (h = distance from CM to new axis)
  • Theorem of Perpendicular Axes: I_z = I_x + I_y (for lamina only)
  • Torque (τ) = r × F = Iα; SI unit: N·m
  • Angular Momentum (L) = Iω = r × p
  • Law of Conservation of Angular Momentum: If net torque = 0, then L = constant (Iω = constant)
  • Rolling Motion: Total KE = ½Iω² + ½mv²; for sphere: KErot = 2/7 × Total KE

🔮 Magnetic Materials

🔑 Key Concepts
  • Magnetisation (M): magnetic moment per unit volume; M = I·n (n = no. of dipoles)
  • Magnetic Intensity (H): H = B/μ₀ − M
  • Magnetic susceptibility (χ): M = χH; large χ → ferromagnetic
  • Diamagnetic: χ < 0; weakly repelled; examples: Cu, Bi, water
  • Paramagnetic: χ small positive; weakly attracted; examples: Al, O₂
  • Ferromagnetic: χ very large; strongly attracted; examples: Fe, Co, Ni
  • Curie's Law: χ = C/T (paramagnetic); susceptibility ∝ 1/T
  • Hysteresis: loss of energy in magnetisation cycle; retentivity and coercivity
  • Permanent magnet: high retentivity, high coercivity (steel)
  • Electromagnet core: high permeability, low retentivity (soft iron)

⚙️ Rotational Dynamics
Q1
State and explain the theorem of parallel axes for moment of inertia.
🔴 Very High Freq 3 Marks Ch 1
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The moment of inertia of a body about any axis is equal to the sum of the moment of inertia about a parallel axis passing through the centre of mass and the product of the mass of the body and the square of the perpendicular distance between the two axes.

I = Icm + Mh²

where Icm = MOI about CM axis, M = mass of body, h = perpendicular distance between the two parallel axes.

Example: For a thin rod of mass M, length L about one end: I = ML²/12 + M(L/2)² = ML²/12 + ML²/4 = ML²/3

Q2
State and prove the law of conservation of angular momentum. Give one example.
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Statement: If no external torque acts on a system, the total angular momentum of the system remains constant (conserved).

Proof: Torque τ = dL/dt. If τ = 0, then dL/dt = 0, so L = Iω = constant.

Therefore, if I decreases, ω increases proportionally to keep Iω constant.

Example: A figure skater pulling her arms in during a spin — reduces MOI (I), increases angular velocity (ω). Iω = constant. Same applies to a diver tucking in mid-air to rotate faster before entering water.

Q3
Derive an expression for the kinetic energy of a body rolling without slipping.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks
✅ Model Answer

For a body rolling without slipping, there are two components of KE:

Translational KE = ½mv²

Rotational KE = ½Iω²

Since v = Rω for rolling without slipping: ω = v/R

Total KE = ½mv² + ½Iω² = ½mv² + ½I(v/R)² = ½mv²(1 + I/mR²)

For a solid sphere: I = 2mR²/5, so Total KE = ½mv²(1 + 2/5) = 7/10 mv²

Rotational KE = 2/7 of total; Translational KE = 5/7 of total.

🌊 Wave Optics
Q4
State Huygens' principle. Using it, derive the laws of refraction of light (Snell's Law).
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks Ch 7
✅ Model Answer

Huygens' Principle: (i) Every point on a wavefront acts as a secondary source of light, emitting spherical secondary wavelets in all directions. (ii) The new wavefront at any instant is the common tangent (envelope) to all the secondary wavelets.

Derivation of Snell's Law:

Let AB be incident wavefront. Ray hits A at time t=0 and B at t=τ. Speed in medium 1 = v₁, medium 2 = v₂.

Distance BC = v₁τ (secondary wavelet from B in medium 1)

Distance AE = v₂τ (secondary wavelet from A in medium 2)

From triangles: sin i = BC/AC = v₁τ/AC and sin r = AE/AC = v₂τ/AC

sin i / sin r = v₁/v₂ = n₂/n₁ → n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r (Snell's Law)
Q5
In Young's double slit experiment, derive the formula for fringe width. What happens to fringe width if distance between slits is doubled?
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Path difference for point P at distance y from centre: Δ = yd/D

For bright fringe: Δ = nλ → yn = nλD/d

Fringe width β = yn+1 − yn

β = λD/d

where λ = wavelength, D = distance from slits to screen, d = slit separation.

If d is doubled: β = λD/(2d) → fringe width becomes half. Fringes become narrower.

Q6
State Malus' Law. What is Brewster's angle? Derive the relation tan i_B = n.
🟠 Medium Freq 3 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Malus' Law: When a plane polarised light beam of intensity I₀ is incident on an analyser, the intensity of the transmitted light is I = I₀ cos²θ, where θ is the angle between the polariser and analyser axes.

Brewster's Angle: When light is incident at Brewster's angle i_B, the reflected ray is completely plane polarised (parallel to surface). Refracted ray is partially polarised.

Derivation: At Brewster's angle, reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular: i_B + r = 90°, so r = 90° − i_B.

By Snell's Law: n = sin i_B / sin r = sin i_B / sin(90°−i_B) = sin i_B / cos i_B

∴ tan i_B = n
⚡ Electrostatics & Current Electricity
Q7
State and prove Gauss's Law in electrostatics. Apply it to find electric field due to an infinite line charge.
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks Ch 8
✅ Model Answer

Gauss's Law: The total electric flux through any closed surface (Gaussian surface) is equal to 1/ε₀ times the total charge enclosed.

φ = ∮ E·dA = Qenc/ε₀

Application — infinite line charge:

Consider a Gaussian cylindrical surface of radius r and length L around the line charge (linear charge density λ).

Flux through curved surface = E × 2πrL (E uniform and ⊥ to surface)

Qenc = λL

By Gauss's Law: E × 2πrL = λL/ε₀

∴ E = λ / (2πε₀r)
Q8
Derive an expression for energy stored in a parallel plate capacitor. What is energy density?
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks
✅ Model Answer

When a capacitor is charged from 0 to Q, small work is done against repulsion.

Work done = dW = V dq = (q/C) dq

Total energy stored:

U = ∫₀^Q (q/C) dq = Q²/2C = ½CV² = ½QV

Energy density (energy per unit volume between plates):

V = Ed, A·d = volume

u = U/(Ad) = ½ε₀E²
Q9
Explain the working of a potentiometer. How is it used to compare EMFs of two cells?
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Principle: When a steady current flows through a uniform resistance wire, the potential drop across any length is directly proportional to that length. V ∝ l

Construction: A long resistance wire AB connected to a battery (driver cell) through a rheostat. A jockey slides along the wire. The cell to be measured is connected between A and the jockey through a galvanometer.

Comparison of EMFs: Connect cell E₁; find balancing length l₁ (G reads zero). Then connect cell E₂; find balancing length l₂.

E₁/E₂ = l₁/l₂
🧲 Magnetism & Electromagnetic Induction
Q10
State and explain Faraday's laws of electromagnetic induction. State Lenz's law.
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks Ch 12
✅ Model Answer

Faraday's First Law: Whenever magnetic flux linked with a closed circuit changes, an EMF is induced in the circuit. The EMF lasts as long as the flux is changing.

Faraday's Second Law: The magnitude of the induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

ε = −dΦ/dt = −N · d(BA cosθ)/dt

Lenz's Law: The direction of induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that causes it. This is consistent with energy conservation — work must be done to change the flux.

Example: When N pole of a magnet approaches a coil, induced current in coil creates N pole facing the magnet (opposing approach).

Q11
Derive an expression for self-inductance of a solenoid.
🔴 High Freq 3 Marks
✅ Model Answer

For a solenoid of length l, N turns, cross-sectional area A, carrying current I:

Magnetic field inside: B = μ₀nI = μ₀(N/l)I

Flux through one turn: Φ₁ = BA = μ₀NIA/l

Total flux linkage: NΦ₁ = μ₀N²IA/l

By definition, L = NΦ/I:

L = μ₀N²A/l = μ₀n²Al

Where n = N/l is turns per unit length. L depends only on geometry and permeability of core material.

🔄 AC Circuits & Modern Physics
Q12
Explain resonance in a series LCR circuit. Derive the condition for resonance and expression for resonant frequency.
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks Ch 13
✅ Model Answer

In series LCR circuit, impedance Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²]

At resonance, impedance is minimum and current is maximum. This occurs when X_L = X_C:

ωL = 1/ωC → ω₀² = 1/LC → ω₀ = 1/√(LC)
f₀ = 1 / (2π√LC)

At resonance: Z = R (purely resistive), current I = V/R (maximum), phase difference φ = 0 (V and I in phase).

Sharpness of resonance / Q-factor: Q = ω₀L/R = 1/ω₀CR = (1/R)√(L/C). Higher Q = sharper resonance.

Q13
Explain Einstein's photoelectric equation. What are the conclusions drawn from the photoelectric effect experiment?
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks Ch 14
✅ Model Answer

Einstein's Photoelectric Equation: A photon of energy hν is absorbed by an electron in the metal. Part of this energy is used to do work against the work function φ (minimum energy to liberate electron), and remaining becomes KE of electron.

KEmax = hν − φ = hν − hν₀ = h(ν − ν₀)

Conclusions:

1. Threshold frequency ν₀: If ν < ν₀, no emission regardless of intensity.

2. KEmax depends on frequency, not on intensity — confirmed by equation.

3. Increasing intensity increases number of photons → more electrons emitted → more photocurrent.

4. Stopping potential V₀ = KEmax/e = h(ν−ν₀)/e

Q14
State Bohr's postulates for hydrogen atom. Derive an expression for the radius of nth orbit.
🔴 Very High Freq 4 Marks Ch 15
✅ Model Answer

Bohr's Postulates:

1. Electrons revolve around nucleus in certain allowed circular orbits without radiating energy.

2. Only those orbits are allowed where angular momentum is an integral multiple of h/2π: L = mvr = nh/2π

3. When electron jumps from higher to lower orbit, energy is emitted: hν = En₂ − En₁

Derivation of rn:

Centripetal force = Coulomb force: mv²/r = e²/4πε₀r²

Bohr condition: mvr = nh/2π → v = nh/2πmr

Substituting: m(nh/2πmr)²/r = e²/4πε₀r²

rn = n²h²ε₀ / πme² = n² × 0.529 Å = n² × a₀
Q15
What is a transistor? Explain how a transistor works as a switch (in CE configuration).
🟠 Medium Freq 3 Marks Ch 16
✅ Model Answer

Transistor: A three-terminal semiconductor device (Emitter, Base, Collector). NPN: two n-type layers with p-type base. PNP: reverse.

Transistor as Switch (CE configuration):

Cut-off (OFF) state: Vin = 0 V; base-emitter junction not forward biased; I_B = 0; I_C = 0; Vout = VCC (HIGH). Transistor is OFF.

Saturation (ON) state: Vin = high; base-emitter forward biased; I_B is large; I_C = β·I_B saturates; VCE ≈ 0; Vout ≈ 0 (LOW). Transistor is ON.

Thus it acts as ON/OFF switch — input HIGH → output LOW (inverted). This is also an NOT gate.
