Q. — Answer in ONE SENTENCE — All Chapters Textbook — Most Important
Chapter 1 — Distributional MapsOne Sentence
1
What is a distributional map?
∴ A distributional map is a type of map that shows the geographical spread or distribution of a particular phenomenon over an area using symbols, shading, dots, or lines.
2
What is a dot map?
∴ A dot map is a distributional map in which each dot represents a specific fixed quantity or value to show the distribution of a phenomenon.
3
What is a choropleth map?
∴ A choropleth map uses different shades or colours to represent the intensity or density of a phenomenon within administrative boundaries.
4
What is an isopleth map?
∴ An isopleth map uses lines (isolines) joining places of equal value of a phenomenon — e.g. isotherm (temperature), isohyet (rainfall), isobar (pressure), contour (elevation).
Chapter 2 — Endogenetic MovementsOne Sentence
5
What are endogenetic movements?
∴ Endogenetic movements are forces originating from within the Earth that build up the Earth's surface — e.g. earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, folding, and faulting.
6
What is an earthquake?
∴ An earthquake is the sudden shaking or trembling of the Earth's surface caused by the release of energy from within the Earth along fault lines.
7
What is a volcano?
∴ A volcano is an opening or vent in the Earth's crust through which molten rock (lava), gases, steam, and ash escape from the Earth's interior to its surface.
8
What is the focus of an earthquake?
∴ The focus (hypocentre) is the actual point of origin of earthquake energy deep inside the Earth from where seismic waves radiate outward in all directions.
9
What is the epicentre of an earthquake?
∴ The epicentre is the point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus — it experiences the greatest intensity of shaking and damage.
Chapter 3 & 4 — Exogenetic ProcessesOne Sentence
10
What is weathering?
∴ Weathering is the process of breaking down and decomposition of rocks at their original place (in situ) by physical, chemical, or biological agents without any transportation of material.
11
What is erosion?
∴ Erosion is the process by which rocks are broken down and the resulting material is transported away from its original place by agents like rivers, glaciers, wind, or waves.
12
What is a delta?
∴ A delta is a fan-shaped or triangular depositional landform formed at the mouth of a river where it meets the sea or lake and deposits its load of silt and sediment.
13
What is an oxbow lake?
∴ An oxbow lake is a crescent-shaped lake formed when a river cuts across the neck of a meander bend, isolating the loop to form a curved, horseshoe-shaped water body.
14
What is a glacier?
∴ A glacier is a slow-moving mass of thick ice formed in high mountain areas where snowfall exceeds melting, accumulating and compressing over hundreds of years.
15
What is a meander?
∴ A meander is a large S-shaped bend or curve formed in the lower course of a river where it flows slowly across a broad, flat floodplain due to reduced gradient.
Chapter 5 — PrecipitationOne Sentence
16
What is precipitation?
∴ Precipitation is the process by which condensed water vapour in clouds falls to the Earth's surface in various forms — rain, snow, sleet, hail, frost, or dew.
17
What is orographic rainfall?
∴ Orographic rainfall occurs when moist winds are forced to rise over mountains, cool, condense and fall as rain on the windward (facing wind) slope of the mountain.
18
What is convectional rainfall?
∴ Convectional rainfall occurs when intense solar heating causes warm moist air to rise rapidly, cool, condense and fall as heavy thundershowers — common in equatorial regions.
19
What is a rain shadow area?
∴ A rain shadow area is the dry region on the leeward (sheltered) side of a mountain that receives very little rainfall because moisture was already lost on the windward side.
20
What is the hydrological cycle?
∴ The hydrological (water) cycle is the continuous circulation of water between the ocean, atmosphere, and land through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff.
Chapter 6 — Properties of Sea WaterOne Sentence
21
What is salinity of sea water?
∴ Salinity is the total amount of dissolved salts in sea water expressed in parts per thousand (‰) — the average ocean salinity is 35‰.
22
What are tides?
∴ Tides are the periodic rise (high tide) and fall (low tide) of sea water level caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on the Earth's ocean water.
23
What are ocean currents?
∴ Ocean currents are large-scale, continuous, directional movements of sea water driven by wind, temperature differences, Earth's rotation (Coriolis effect) and salinity differences.
24
Why is the sea water blue in colour?
∴ Sea water appears blue because it absorbs red and yellow wavelengths of sunlight and reflects and scatters the blue wavelength, making it appear blue to our eyes.
25
What is the effect of warm ocean currents on coastal climate?
∴ Warm ocean currents raise the temperature of coastal regions — they bring warm water from tropics, warming the air above and giving mild winters to nearby coasts (e.g. Gulf Stream warms Western Europe).
