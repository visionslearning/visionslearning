Q.1 (i)
Electric field between the plates of a charged capacitor is:
(A) zero
(B) σ/ε₀
(C) σ/2ε₀
(D) 2σ/ε₀
✔ Answer: (B) σ/ε₀
Explanation Each plate contributes σ/2ε₀. Between plates, both contributions add in same direction. E = σ/2ε₀ + σ/2ε₀ = σ/ε₀ (between plates)
Q.1 (ii)
The SI unit of resistivity is:
(A) Ω/m
(B) Ω·m
(C) Ω·m⁻¹
(D) Ω·m²
✔ Answer: (B) Ω·m
Explanation R = ρL/A → ρ = RA/L → unit = Ω × m²/m = Ω·m
Q.1 (iii)
The magnetic force on a stationary charge is:
(A) zero
(B) maximum
(C) qvB
(D) qvB sinθ
✔ Answer: (A) zero
Explanation F = qv⃗ × B⃗ = qvB sinθ. If v = 0: F = 0 Magnetic force requires moving charge. A stationary charge experiences zero magnetic force.

(A) 3×10⁸ m/s
(B) 4×10⁸ m/s
(C) 2×10⁸ m/s
(D) 1×10⁸ m/s
✔ Answer: (C) 2×10⁸ m/s
Solution v = c/n = (3×10⁸)/1.5 = 2×10⁸ m/s
Q.1 (v)
In β-decay, if the mass number increases by 1, then the atomic number:
(A) decreases by 1
(B) increases by 1
(C) remains same
(D) increases by 2
✔ Answer: (A) decreases by 1
Explanation β⁺ decay: p → n + e⁺ + ν → A unchanged, Z decreases by 1 β⁻ decay: n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ → A unchanged, Z increases by 1 Question refers to β⁺ (positron emission) — proton converts to neutron.
Q.1 (vi)
In the photoelectric effect, the saturation current depends on:
(A) intensity of incident light
(B) frequency of incident light
(C) nature of metal
(D) stopping potential
✔ Answer: (A) intensity of incident light
Explanation More intensity = more photons per second = more electrons ejected = higher saturation current. Stopping potential and KE_max depend on frequency, not intensity.
Q.1 (vii)
The resolving power of a telescope is proportional to:
(A) diameter of objective lens
(B) focal length of eyepiece
(C) wavelength of light
(D) magnification
✔ Answer: (A) diameter of objective lens
Explanation RP = D/(1.22λ) — directly proportional to aperture D Larger objective → better resolution → can distinguish finer angular separations.
Q.1 (viii)
The output of a NOR gate is 1 when:
(A) both inputs are 0
(B) both inputs are 1
(C) one input is 1
(D) any input is 1
✔ Answer: (A) both inputs are 0
Explanation NOR = NOT(A OR B): Y = ‾(A+B) Only when A=0 AND B=0: Y = NOT(0+0) = NOT(0) = 1
Q.1 (ix)
In a pure inductor connected to AC, the voltage leads current by:
(A) 0°
(B) 45°
(C) 90°
(D) 180°
Pure inductor: V = V₀sin(ωt + π/2), I = I₀sinωt → V leads I by 90°
✔ Answer: (C) 90°
Explanation If I = I₀sinωt, then v = L(dI/dt) = LωI₀cosωt = V₀sin(ωt + π/2) Voltage is π/2 (90°) ahead of current in a pure inductor.
Q.1 (x)
Ferromagnetic materials above the Curie temperature become:
(A) diamagnetic
(B) paramagnetic
(C) ferrimagnetic
(D) antiferromagnetic
✔ Answer: (B) paramagnetic
Explanation Below Curie temp: magnetic domains aligned → ferromagnetic (strong). Above Curie temp (e.g., Fe: 1043 K): thermal energy destroys domain alignment → paramagnetic (weak, random).

Q.3 (i) [2 Marks]
Derive the expression for electric field E on the equatorial line of an electric dipole.
E on equatorial line: perpendicular components cancel, axial components add → opposite to p⃗
Derivation Distance from each charge to P: r' = √(r² + a²) E₊ = E₋ = kq/(r²+a²) Vertical components cancel. Horizontal components add opposite to p⃗: E_eq = 2kq cosθ/(r²+a²) = kp/(r²+a²)^(3/2)
For r >> a
E_eq ≈ kp/r³ (opposite to p⃗)
Q.3 (iii) [2 Marks]
State and explain the laws of electromagnetic induction. Explain Lenz's law.
Faraday's 1st LawWhenever magnetic flux through a circuit changes, an EMF is induced in it.
Faraday's 2nd Lawe = −N(dΦ/dt)   Magnitude of EMF = rate of change of flux linkage.
Lenz's LawThe induced current always opposes the change in flux that caused it. (Negative sign in Faraday's law.) This is a consequence of energy conservation.
Q.3 (v) [2 Marks]
Explain the construction and working of a compound microscope with a ray diagram.
Compound microscope: objective forms magnified real image; eyepiece further magnifies it
ConstructionObjective lens (short f_o, larger aperture) + eyepiece (short f_e). Separated by tube length L.
WorkingObject just beyond F_o → objective forms magnified real image A'B' (within tube). A'B' within F_e → eyepiece forms highly magnified virtual final image.
Magnification
M = (L/f_o)(1 + D/f_e)   where D = 25 cm
Q.3 (vii) [2 Marks]
Derive the de Broglie wavelength λ = h/mv from first principles.
Derivation For photon: E = hν and E = pc → p = h/λ → λ = h/p (photon) de Broglie extended this to matter: every moving particle of momentum p has associated wavelength: λ = h/p = h/mv Verified by Davisson-Germer (1927): electrons showed diffraction confirming matter waves.

Q.4 (i) [3 Marks]
Derive the expression for electric field E on the axial line of an electric dipole.
E on axial line: E₊ > E₋ (P closer to +q), net E along p⃗ direction
Derivation E₊ = kq/(r−a)² (away from +q), E₋ = kq/(r+a)² (towards −q) Both along the axis. E_axial = E₊ − E₋ = kq[1/(r−a)² − 1/(r+a)²] = kq × 4ar/(r²−a²)² = k(2p)r/(r²−a²)²
For r >> a
E_axial ≈ 2kp/r³ (along direction of p⃗)

Q.4 (viii) [3 Marks]
A convex lens of focal length 15 cm has an object at 45 cm. Find the image position using the lens formula. Is it real or virtual?
Convex lens f=15cm, object at u=45cm → image at v=22.5cm (real, inverted, diminished)
Givenf = +15 cm, u = −45 cm
Solution 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/15 + 1/(−45) = 3/45 − 1/45 = 2/45 v = 45/2 = 22.5 cm (positive → other side) m = v/u = 22.5/(−45) = −0.5 (inverted, diminished)
Answer
v = +22.5 cm | Real, inverted, diminished (m = −0.5)
