Section A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
Electric field between the plates of a charged capacitor is:
✔ Answer: (B) σ/ε₀
Explanation For parallel plate capacitor with surface charge density σ: E = σ/ε₀ (between plates, directed from + to − plate) This is derived using Gauss's law with a pillbox Gaussian surface straddling one plate.
Q.1 (ii)
The SI unit of resistivity is:
✔ Answer: (B) Ω·m
Explanation R = ρL/A → ρ = RA/L Units: Ω × m² / m = Ω·m
Q.1 (iii)
The magnetic force on a stationary charge is:
✔ Answer: (A) zero
Explanation F = qv⃗ × B⃗ = qvB sinθ If v = 0 (stationary), then F = q × 0 × B sinθ = zero Magnetic force requires moving charge.
Q.1 (iv)
Speed of light in medium of n = 1.5:
✔ Answer: (C) 2×10⁸ m/s
Solution n = c/v → v = c/n = (3×10⁸)/1.5 = 2×10⁸ m/s
Q.1 (v)
In β-decay, if mass number increases by 1, atomic number:
✔ Answer: (A) decreases by 1
Explanation β⁻ decay: ¹n → ¹p + e⁻ + ν̄   (neutron → proton, A unchanged, Z increases by 1) β⁺ decay: ¹p → ¹n + e⁺ + ν   (proton → neutron, A unchanged, Z decreases by 1) The question refers to β⁺ decay: mass number stays same (A unchanged), Z decreases by 1.
Q.1 (vi)
Saturation current in photoelectric effect depends on:
✔ Answer: (A) intensity of incident light
Explanation Saturation current = maximum photocurrent when all emitted electrons are collected. More intensity = more photons = more electrons emitted per second = higher saturation current Stopping potential and KE_max depend on frequency, not intensity.
Q.1 (vii)
Resolving power of a telescope is proportional to:
✔ Answer: (A) diameter of objective lens
Explanation Resolving power = D/(1.22λ) (Rayleigh criterion) where D = diameter of objective, λ = wavelength of light Larger aperture D → better resolution → can distinguish finer details
Q.1 (viii)
Output of NOR gate is 1 when:
✔ Answer: (A) both inputs are 0
Explanation NOR = NOT(A OR B): Y = ‾(A+B) Truth table: 0+0=0, NOT(0)=1  |  0+1=1, NOT(1)=0  |  1+0=1, NOT(1)=0  |  1+1=1, NOT(1)=0 Only output 1 when BOTH inputs are 0.
Q.1 (ix)
In pure inductor on AC, voltage leads current by:
✔ Answer: (C) 90°
Explanation For a pure inductor: v = L(dI/dt). If I = I₀sinωt, then v = LωI₀cosωt = V₀sin(ωt + π/2) Voltage leads current by 90° (π/2 radians) in a pure inductor.
Q.1 (x)
Ferromagnetic materials above Curie temperature become:
✔ Answer: (B) paramagnetic
Explanation Below Curie temperature T_C: ferromagnetic (strong domains, spontaneous magnetisation) Above T_C: thermal energy destroys magnetic domain alignment → becomes paramagnetic (weak, random alignment) Example: Iron T_C = 1043 K, Nickel T_C = 631 K
Section B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
Q.2 (i)
State the superposition principle of electric charges.
StatementThe total electric force on a charge due to a system of charges is the vector sum of the individual forces exerted on that charge by each charge separately, with all other charges remaining in their positions.
FormulaF⃗_total = F⃗₁₂ + F⃗₁₃ + F⃗₁₄ + ...
Q.2 (ii)
Define the term 'EMF' of a cell.
DefinitionEMF (Electromotive Force) of a cell is the work done by non-electrostatic forces (chemical forces) per unit positive charge in moving the charge from the negative terminal to positive terminal inside the cell.
FormulaEMF = W/q   Unit: Volt (V) = J/C
NoteEMF is a property of the cell, not an actual force. It equals terminal voltage only when no current flows (open circuit).
Q.2 (iii)
What is Curie temperature?
DefinitionCurie temperature (T_C) is the temperature above which a ferromagnetic material loses its ferromagnetism and becomes paramagnetic.
ExamplesIron: T_C = 1043 K (770°C)  |  Nickel: T_C = 631 K  |  Cobalt: T_C = 1388 K
Q.2 (iv)
State Fleming's right-hand rule.
StatementStretch the thumb, index finger and middle finger of the right hand mutually perpendicular to each other:
Thumb → direction of motion of conductor
Index finger → direction of magnetic field (B)
Middle finger → direction of induced current
UseTo find the direction of induced EMF/current in a generator (electromagnetic induction)
Q.2 (v)
Define persistence of vision.
DefinitionPersistence of vision is the property of the human eye by which it retains the impression of an image for about 1/16th of a second (≈ 0.1 s) even after the object is removed from view.
ApplicationUsed in movies/cinema: 24 frames per second create the illusion of smooth motion. Also basis of TV and video.
Q.2 (vi)
What is stimulated emission? How does it differ from spontaneous emission?
Stimulated EmissionWhen an atom in an excited state is triggered by an incident photon of exactly the right frequency, it emits a second photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction as the incident photon.
Differences Spontaneous: No trigger needed, random phase, random direction, incoherent
Stimulated: Triggered by photon, same phase, same direction, coherent → basis of LASER
Q.2 (vii)
Define nuclear cross-section.
DefinitionNuclear cross-section (σ) is a measure of the probability of a nuclear reaction occurring. It represents the effective area of the nucleus for a particular interaction.
UnitBarn = 10⁻²⁸ m²   (1 barn = 10⁻²⁴ cm²)
Q.2 (viii)
What is meant by 'rectification' in electronics?
DefinitionRectification is the process of converting alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC) using a p-n junction diode, which allows current to flow in only one direction.
TypesHalf-wave rectifier (one diode) and Full-wave rectifier (two or four diodes)
Q.2 (ix)
What is space wave propagation?
DefinitionSpace wave propagation (line-of-sight propagation) is the transmission of electromagnetic waves in a straight line directly from the transmitting antenna to the receiving antenna through the space above the Earth's surface.
Frequency RangeFrequencies above 40 MHz (VHF, UHF, microwaves)  |  Used for: TV, satellite, microwave links, RADAR
Q.2 (x)
Define the Q-factor (quality factor) of an LCR circuit.
DefinitionQ-factor (Quality factor) is the ratio of the resonant frequency to the bandwidth of an LCR circuit. It measures the sharpness of resonance.
FormulaQ = ω_r/Δω = ω_r L/R = 1/(ω_r CR)
SignificanceHigher Q → sharper resonance, more selective → better for tuning circuits (radio receivers)
Q.2 (xi)
What is ground wave propagation?
DefinitionGround wave propagation is the transmission of radio waves along the surface of the Earth. The waves follow the curvature of the Earth.
FrequencyFrequencies below 2 MHz (medium wave, AM radio)
LimitationThe signal gets progressively absorbed by the Earth's surface with increasing distance. Effective only up to a few hundred km.
Q.2 (xii)
State Bohr's third postulate.
StatementAn electron can jump from a higher energy orbit (E₂) to a lower energy orbit (E₁) and in doing so, it emits a photon of energy equal to the difference of the two energy levels. Similarly, a photon of that energy must be absorbed for the reverse transition.
Formulahν = E₂ − E₁   (Bohr's frequency condition)

Q.3 (i)
Derive E on the equatorial line of an electric dipole.
SetupDipole with charges +q and −q separated by 2a. Point P on equatorial line at distance r from centre.
Derivation Distance from each charge to P: r' = √(r² + a²) E₊ = kq/(r² + a²) (away from +q)    E₋ = kq/(r² + a²) (towards −q) Vertical components cancel. Only horizontal components (along −p⃗) add: E = 2 × [kq/(r² + a²)] × a/√(r² + a²) E = kqa/(r² + a²)^(3/2) = kp/(r² + a²)^(3/2)
For r >> a
E ≈ kp/r³   (directed opposite to dipole moment p⃗)
Q.3 (ii)
Explain Seebeck effect and the working of a thermocouple.
Seebeck EffectWhen two different metals are joined to form a closed circuit and the two junctions are maintained at different temperatures, an EMF is produced in the circuit. This is called the Seebeck effect (thermoelectric effect).
Thermocouple Two dissimilar metals (e.g., copper-iron) joined at two junctions. One junction = hot junction (at temperature T₁), other = cold junction (at T₂). Seebeck EMF = α(T₁ − T₂) where α = thermoelectric power (depends on metals)
UsesTemperature measurement, thermoelectric generators, temperature controllers
Q.3 (iii)
State and explain the laws of electromagnetic induction. Explain Lenz's law.
Faraday's 1st LawWhenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an EMF is induced in it.
Faraday's 2nd LawThe magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.
e = −N(dΦ/dt)
Lenz's Law The direction of induced current is always such that it opposes the change in flux that caused it. Example: When a bar magnet (N-pole) is pushed into a solenoid, the near end of the solenoid becomes N-pole (repels the magnet) to oppose the increasing flux. Lenz's law is a consequence of conservation of energy.
Q.3 (iv)
Derive the condition for a balanced Wheatstone bridge.
SetupFour resistors P, Q, R, S in diamond configuration. Galvanometer between B and D. Battery between A and C.
At Balance No current through galvanometer → V_B = V_D Current through P and Q: I₁ = V_AC/(P+Q) Current through R and S: I₂ = V_AC/(R+S) V_B = V_A − I₁P = V_A − VP/(P+Q) V_D = V_A − I₂R = V_A − VR/(R+S) Setting V_B = V_D: P/(P+Q) = R/(R+S)
Result
P/Q = R/S   (Balanced Wheatstone Bridge condition)

Types of radioactive decay (α, β, γ) with equations.
α decay Heavy nucleus emits α-particle (⁴₂He) ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ⁻⁴_(Z−2) Y + ⁴_₂He Example: ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He

Q.3 (vii)
Derive de Broglie wavelength λ = h/mv.
Derivation For a photon: E = hν = hc/λ   and   E = pc (from relativity) So: p = E/c = hν/c = h/λ → λ = h/p (for photon) de Broglie's hypothesis: the same relation holds for matter particles λ = h/p = h/mv This was verified experimentally by Davisson and Germer (1927) using electron diffraction.
Result
λ = h/mv = h/p   (de Broglie wavelength)
Q.3 (viii)
Explain the construction and working of a Coolidge X-ray tube.
Construction Cathode: Tungsten filament — produces electrons by thermionic emission Anode (target): Tungsten/Molybdenum — receives fast electrons; high melting point needed Focusing cup: Focuses electron beam onto small spot on anode High voltage: 10 kV to 100 kV applied between cathode and anode
Working Filament heated → thermionic emission of electrons Electrons accelerated toward anode by high voltage → kinetic energy = eV On hitting the anode: 99% energy → heat, 1% → X-rays (bremsstrahlung + characteristic X-rays)
Q.3 (ix)
Explain beats — formation, conditions, and two uses.
FormationWhen two sound waves of slightly different frequencies f₁ and f₂ superpose, the resultant amplitude varies periodically. The periodic rise and fall in loudness are called beats.
Beat Frequencyf_beat = |f₁ − f₂|
Conditions(1) Frequencies must be close to each other  (2) Amplitudes must be equal or nearly equal  (3) Waves must travel in the same direction
Uses(1) Tuning musical instruments (adjust until beat frequency = 0)  (2) Detecting minor frequency differences in sound
Q.3 (x)
Explain working of a photodiode and solar cell.
Photodiode Operated in reverse bias. A window allows light to fall on the junction. Photons create electron-hole pairs in depletion region → electric field separates them Photo-generated minority carriers cross junction → reverse current (photocurrent) ∝ light intensity
Solar Cell No external bias needed. Light incident on large-area p-n junction Photons create e-h pairs near junction → built-in field drives electrons to n-side, holes to p-side This creates a potential difference (open-circuit voltage V_oc) — photovoltaic effect Connected to load → current flows → electrical energy generated from sunlight

Q.3 (xii)
Explain the transverse nature of electromagnetic waves.
Nature In an EM wave, the electric field E⃗ and magnetic field B⃗ oscillate perpendicular to each other AND perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. This makes EM waves transverse waves (not longitudinal like sound). If wave travels in x-direction: E⃗ along y, B⃗ along z (E⃗ ⊥ B⃗ ⊥ direction of propagation)
EvidenceEM waves can be polarised (only transverse waves show polarisation) — e.g., Polaroid sunglasses reduce glare.

Q.4 (ii)
Derive the expression for self-inductance of a solenoid.
SetupSolenoid of length l, n turns per metre, cross-sectional area A, carrying current I.
Derivation Magnetic field inside: B = μ₀nI Magnetic flux through each turn: Φ_per_turn = BA = μ₀nIA Total flux linkage: NΦ = (nl) × μ₀nIA = μ₀n²lAI By definition: NΦ = LI → L = μ₀n²lA
Result
L = μ₀n²lA = μ₀N²A/l   (Self-inductance of a solenoid)
Q.4 (iii)

Derivation Using small angle approximation (paraxial rays): At point of incidence M: angle of incidence α, angle of refraction β Using Snell's law (small angles): n₁α = n₂β Using geometry of the triangle OMP and IMP: α ≈ h/u + h/R, β ≈ h/R − h/v (h = height of incidence point above axis) Substituting and simplifying:
Result
n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R   (Refraction at spherical surface)

Q.4 (v)
Describe a nuclear reactor — all components and functions.
Components 1. Fuel rods: Enriched ²³⁵U or ²³⁹Pu — fissile material; rods inserted in moderator 2. Moderator: Heavy water (D₂O) or graphite — slows fast neutrons to thermal speeds needed for fission 3. Control rods: Cadmium or boron steel — absorb neutrons; inserted/withdrawn to control power 4. Coolant: Heavy water or CO₂ gas — absorbs heat; transferred to steam generator → turbine → electricity 5. Pressure vessel: Steel container — holds the reactor core under pressure 6. Concrete shield: Thick concrete + lead — absorbs neutrons and gamma radiation; protects workers
PrincipleControlled nuclear chain reaction: each fission releases 2–3 neutrons; control rods absorb excess to maintain exactly 1 neutron per fission (critical condition).
