1.1–1.2

🔄 Circular Motion — Basics

🔑 Key Points
  • Revolution: object moves in circular path about an external point
  • Rotation: object spins about an axis passing through itself
  • Circular motion is accelerated (direction of velocity changes at every instant)
  • Circular motion is periodic (particle repeats same trajectory)
  • Angular displacement (θ): angle swept; unit: radian
  • Angular velocity (ω): ω = dθ/dt; unit: rad/s; ω = 2πn = 2π/T
  • Angular acceleration (α): α = dω/dt; unit: rad/s²
  • Relation: v = ωr (tangential velocity); a_r = ω²r (centripetal acceleration)
  • Direction of ω: along axis of rotation, by right-hand thumb rule
  • UCM: uniform circular motion — speed constant, direction changes
  • Non-UCM: speed changes; has both centripetal (radial) and tangential acceleration

1.2.2

⚡ Centripetal & Centrifugal Force

🔑 Key Points
  • Centripetal force (CPF): resultant of all real forces acting on body in circular motion; directed towards centre
  • CPF = mω²r = mv²/r — it is NOT a new force; it is the name given to the resultant of real forces
  • Centrifugal force (CFF): pseudo force experienced in rotating frame of reference; directed away from centre; magnitude = mω²r
  • Centrifugal force is a non-real but NOT imaginary force; it can be measured by instruments
  • In rotating frame: CFF exactly balances CPF (resultant = 0)
  • Two ways to write force equation: (a) Resultant force = −mω²r (inertial frame) OR (b) mω²r + Σreal forces = 0 (rotating frame)
1.3

🚗 Applications of UCM

📌 1. Vehicle on Horizontal Circular Road
  • Static friction f_s provides centripetal force: f_s = mv²/r
  • Maximum speed: v_max = √(μ_s·r·g)
  • Beyond v_max, vehicle skids outward
📌 2. Well (Wall) of Death — मौत का कुआं
  • Vertical cylindrical wall; vehicle moves in horizontal circles inside
  • Normal reaction N (horizontal) = centripetal force = mv²/r
  • Static friction f_s (vertical) = mg (upward, prevents sliding down)
  • Minimum speed: v_min = √(rg/μ_s)
📌 3. Vehicle on Banked Road
  • Road tilted at angle θ; reduces dependence on friction
  • N cosθ = mg (balances weight); N sinθ = mv²/r (centripetal)
  • Most safe speed: v_s = √(rg tanθ) [no friction needed]
  • Banking angle: θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)
  • Maximum speed: v_max = √[rg·(tanθ + μ_s)/(1 − μ_s tanθ)]
  • Minimum speed: v_min = √[rg·(tanθ − μ_s)/(1 + μ_s tanθ)]
  • If μ_s ≥ tanθ → v_min = 0 (most rough roads at small angles)
📌 4. Conical Pendulum
  • Bob of mass m, string of length L at angle θ with vertical
  • T₀ cosθ = mg (vertical balance); T₀ sinθ = mω²r (centripetal)
  • Radius r = L sinθ
  • Period: T = 2π√(L cosθ/g)
  • Frequency: n = (1/2π)√(g/L cosθ)
  • String can NEVER become horizontal (would need infinite speed)

🎡 Vertical Circular Motion

🔑 Case I — Mass Tied to String
  • Forces on bob: weight mg (down) + tension T (along string toward centre)
  • Uppermost point (A): T_A + mg = mv_A²/r → at minimum speed T_A = 0
  • Minimum speed at top: v_A(min) = √(rg)
  • Lowermost point (B): T_B − mg = mv_B²/r
  • Minimum speed at bottom: v_B(min) = √(5rg)
  • Key relation: v_B² − v_A² = 4rg (from energy conservation, height = 2r)
  • T_B − T_A = 6mg (this is INDEPENDENT of speed and radius — very important!)
  • At horizontal position (C or D): v_C(min) = √(3rg); T_C − T_A = 3mg
🔑 Case II — Mass Tied to Rod
  • Rod is rigid — can push or pull; no minimum speed needed at top
  • v_top can be zero (rod provides upward force)
  • v_bottom(min) = √(4rg) = 2√(rg) [less than string case √(5rg)]
🔑 Vehicle at Top of Convex Over-bridge
  • mg − N = mv²/r; at maximum speed, N = 0
  • Maximum safe speed: v_max = √(rg)
  • Beyond this speed, vehicle loses contact with road

⚖️ Moment of Inertia & Theorems

🔑 Key Points
  • Moment of Inertia (I): rotational analogue of mass; I = Σmᵢrᵢ² = ∫r²dm
  • I depends on: (i) mass of object and (ii) distribution of mass about axis
  • Rotational KE = ½Iω² (analogous to ½mv²)
  • Radius of gyration (K): I = MK²; distance from axis where total mass is effectively concentrated
  • Larger K → mass farther from axis → more resistance to rotation

📌 Theorem of Parallel Axes
  • Applicable to any object
  • Needs two parallel axes — one through CM, one through any point at distance h
  • I_O = I_C + Mh²
  • Example: Rod about one end = ML²/12 + M(L/2)² = ML²/3
📌 Theorem of Perpendicular Axes
  • Applicable only to laminar (2D) objects: ring, disc, plate
  • Three mutually perpendicular concurrent axes; x and y in plane, z perpendicular
  • I_z = I_x + I_y
  • Example: For disc — I_z = ½MR², I_x = I_y (by symmetry) = ¼MR²
1.8–1.10

🌀 Angular Momentum, Torque & Conservation

🔑 Key Points
  • Angular momentum: L = r × p; magnitude L = Iω = mvr sinθ; unit: kg·m²/s
  • L = Iω is analogous to p = mv (linear momentum)
  • Torque: τ = r × F; τ = Iα (analogous to F = ma)
  • τ = dL/dt (rate of change of angular momentum = torque)
  • Conservation of Angular Momentum: If net external torque = 0, then L = Iω = constant
  • If I decreases → ω increases; if I increases → ω decreases
⭐ Examples of Conservation
  • Ballet dancer / ice skater: pulls arms in → I decreases → ω increases (spins faster)
  • Diver: folds body after leaving board → I decreases → more rotations in air
  • Both: once angular momentum is set, Iω = constant throughout

🎯 Rolling Motion

🔑 Key Points
  • Pure rolling = rotational motion about own axis + translational motion of CM
  • Condition: v = ωR (linear speed = angular speed × radius)
  • Static friction is essential for pure rolling (prevents sliding)
  • Total KE = ½Mv² + ½Iω² = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)
  • Where I = MK²; K = radius of gyration
  • Acceleration down incline: a = g sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)
  • Speed at bottom: v = √[2gh / (1 + K²/R²)]
  • K²/R² values: Ring=1, Disc=½, Solid sphere=2/5, Hollow sphere=2/3
  • Largest K²/R² → slowest rolling; smallest K²/R² → fastest rolling
  • Solid sphere rolls fastest down incline; ring rolls slowest

⚖️ Moment of Inertia & Theorems
Q1
State and prove the theorem of parallel axes for moment of inertia.
🔴 Every Year3–4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The moment of inertia of an object about any axis is equal to the sum of its moment of inertia about a parallel axis passing through the centre of mass, and the product of the mass of the object and the square of the perpendicular distance between the two parallel axes.

I_O = I_C + Mh²

where I_C = MOI about CM axis, M = total mass, h = perpendicular distance between axes.

Proof:

Let O be any axis and C be a parallel axis through CM, distance h apart. For a mass element dm at point D:

I_O = ∫(DO)² dm = ∫([DC]² + 2·NC·h + h²) dm I_O = ∫(DC)² dm + 2h∫NC·dm + h²∫dm ∫(DC)² dm = I_C and ∫dm = M ∫NC·dm = 0 (by definition of centre of mass)
∴ I_O = I_C + Mh² ✓

Example: MOI of uniform rod about one end = ML²/12 + M(L/2)² = ML²/12 + ML²/4 = ML²/3

Q2
State and prove the theorem of perpendicular axes. State its limitations.
🔴 High Freq3–4 Marks2024·2022·2021
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The moment of inertia of a laminar object about an axis perpendicular to its plane is equal to the sum of its moments of inertia about two mutually perpendicular axes in the plane of the object, all three axes being concurrent.

I_z = I_x + I_y

Proof: For mass element dm at point P (x, y), distances from x, y, z axes are y, x, √(x²+y²).

I_x = ∫y² dm, I_y = ∫x² dm I_z = ∫(x²+y²) dm = ∫x² dm + ∫y² dm = I_y + I_x
∴ I_z = I_x + I_y ✓

Limitation: Applicable only to laminar (2D/flat) objects — ring, disc, plate. NOT applicable to 3D objects like sphere, cylinder.

Application: For uniform disc: I_z = ½MR², by symmetry I_x = I_y. ∴ I_x = I_y = ¼MR² (MOI of disc about diameter)

🌀 Angular Momentum & Conservation
Q3
State and explain the law of conservation of angular momentum. Give two examples from daily life.
🔴 Every Year3–4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Statement: The angular momentum of a system remains constant (conserved) if no external unbalanced torque acts on it.

Proof: τ = dL/dt. If τ = 0, then dL/dt = 0, so L = Iω = constant.

Therefore, if moment of inertia I changes, angular velocity ω changes inversely to keep Iω constant:

I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂ (or I₁n₁ = I₂n₂)

Example 1 — Ballet dancer / ice skater: When dancer pulls arms and legs close to body, moment of inertia I decreases. Since Iω = constant, angular velocity ω increases — spins faster. When arms are outstretched, I increases → ω decreases to prevent slipping.

Example 2 — Diver: On diving board, stretches body → large I. After jumping, folds body into tuck → I decreases sharply → ω increases → completes more rotations. Stretches again before entering water → smooth entry.

Q4
Derive an expression for angular momentum of a rigid body in terms of moment of inertia. (L = Iω)
🔴 High Freq3 Marks2023·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

Consider a rigid body of N particles rotating with angular velocity ω about a fixed axis.

Masses m₁, m₂, ... m_N at distances r₁, r₂, ... r_N from axis.

Linear speeds: v₁ = r₁ω, v₂ = r₂ω, ... v_N = r_Nω

Angular momentum of first particle: L₁ = p₁·r₁ = m₁v₁·r₁ = m₁r₁²ω

Similarly: L₂ = m₂r₂²ω, L₃ = m₃r₃²ω, ... L_N = m_Nr_N²ω

All angular momenta are directed along the axis (same direction), so they add algebraically:

L = L₁+L₂+...+L_N = (m₁r₁²+m₂r₂²+...+m_Nr_N²)ω
L = Iω where I = Σmᵢrᵢ² = moment of inertia ✓
🎡 Vertical Circular Motion
Q5
Derive expressions for minimum speeds at the uppermost and lowermost points of a vertical circle (mass on string). Prove that T_B − T_A = 6mg.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

At uppermost point A: Both T_A and mg act downward (toward centre).

T_A + mg = mv_A²/r ... (1)

For minimum speed, T_A = 0 (string just taut):

v_A(min) = √(rg)

At lowermost point B: T_B acts upward, mg downward.

T_B − mg = mv_B²/r ... (2)

Energy conservation from B to A (height = 2r):

½mv_B² = ½mv_A² + mg(2r) v_B² = v_A² + 4rg ... (3)

Using v_A(min) = √(rg) in (3):

v_B(min) = √(5rg)

Prove T_B − T_A = 6mg: Subtract eq(1) from eq(2):

T_B − T_A − 2mg = m(v_B² − v_A²)/r Using eq(3): v_B² − v_A² = 4rg T_B − T_A − 2mg = m(4rg)/r = 4mg
∴ T_B − T_A = 6mg ✓ (independent of v and r)
🚗 Banked Road & Applications
Q6
What is banking of roads? Derive an expression for the angle of banking: tan θ = v²/rg. What is the most safe speed?
🔴 Every Year4 Marks2024·2023·2022·2020
✅ Model Answer

Banking of roads: Tilting the outer edge of a curved road with the horizontal by angle θ so that vehicles can safely negotiate the curve without depending entirely on friction.

Forces on vehicle (ignoring friction): (i) Weight mg downward, (ii) Normal reaction N perpendicular to road surface.

Resolving N:

N cosθ = mg (vertical balance) ... (1) N sinθ = mv²/r (centripetal force) ... (2)

Dividing (2) by (1):

tan θ = v²/rg ✓

Most safe speed (no friction needed): v_s = √(rg tanθ)

Banking angle: θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

Significance: At speed v_s, normal reaction alone provides the centripetal force — no friction required. This is safe even on wet/slippery roads.

Q7
Derive an expression for the period of revolution of a conical pendulum.
🔴 High Freq3–4 Marks2023·2022·2021
✅ Model Answer

Bob of mass m, string of length L, makes angle θ with vertical. Radius r = L sinθ.

Forces: T₀ (tension along string) and mg (weight downward).

T₀ cosθ = mg (vertical balance) ... (1) T₀ sinθ = mω²r (centripetal) ... (2)

Dividing: tanθ = ω²r/g = ω²L sinθ/g

ω² = g/(L cosθ) → ω = √(g/L cosθ)
Period T = 2π/ω = 2π√(L cosθ / g)
Frequency n = 1/T = (1/2π)√(g / L cosθ)

Note: String can NEVER become horizontal (θ = 90°) because that would require infinite frequency and infinite tension.

🎯 Rolling Motion
Q8
Derive an expression for the kinetic energy of a body rolling without slipping on a surface.
🔴 High Freq3 Marks2024·2022·2021·2020
✅ Model Answer

For a body rolling without slipping, two simultaneous motions occur:

(i) Translational motion of centre of mass with speed v

(ii) Rotational motion about its own axis with angular speed ω

Since rolling condition: v = ωR → ω = v/R

Total KE = Translational KE + Rotational KE E = ½Mv² + ½Iω² I = MK² (K = radius of gyration) and ω = v/R E = ½Mv² + ½(MK²)(v/R)² = ½Mv² + ½Mv²(K²/R²)
E = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)

For a solid sphere: K²/R² = 2/5 → E = ½Mv²(1 + 2/5) = 7/10 Mv²

Rotational KE fraction = (K²/R²)/(1 + K²/R²); Translational KE fraction = 1/(1 + K²/R²)

Q9
Derive an expression for the acceleration of a body rolling down an inclined plane without slipping.
🔴 High Freq3 Marks2024·2022·2020
✅ Model Answer

Body of mass M, radius R, MOI = I = MK² rolls down incline of angle θ without slipping.

Starting from rest, after travelling distance s along plane (height h = s sinθ):

By energy conservation: Loss in PE = Gain in rolling KE

Mgh = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²) v² = 2gh / (1 + K²/R²) = 2g·s sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)

Using v² = u² + 2as (u = 0):

v² = 2as → a = v²/2s
a = g sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)

For pure sliding (no rotation): a = g sinθ. Rolling factor (1 + K²/R²) > 1, so rolling acceleration is always less than sliding.

Solid sphere (K²/R² = 2/5): a = 5g sinθ/7 — fastest

Ring (K²/R² = 1): a = g sinθ/2 — slowest

N4. A tiny stone of mass 20 g is whirled in vertical circles. Speed = 8 m/s when string is horizontal. String length = 1.8 m. Find minimum and maximum KE. (g = 10 m/s²) [Textbook Ex 1.6]
m = 0.02 kg | v = 8 m/s (when horizontal) | r = 1.8 m | g = 10 m/s²

KE_min (at top) = ½mv² − mgr = ½×0.02×64 − 0.02×10×1.8 = 0.64 − 0.36 = 0.28 J

N5. A ceiling fan (I = 2 kg·m²) attains 60 rpm in 2π seconds. Find power rating. [Textbook Ex 1.9]
I = 2 kg·m² | n = 60 rpm = 1 rps | ω = 2π rad/s | t = 2π s | ω₀ = 0

N6. A spherical water balloon rotates at 60 rpm. 48.8% water leaks out. Find new frequency of rotation. [Textbook Ex 1.8]
n₁ = 60 rpm = 1 rps | m₂ = 51.2% of m₁ = m₁/0.512 → m₁/m₂ = 100/51.2 = 1/0.512
