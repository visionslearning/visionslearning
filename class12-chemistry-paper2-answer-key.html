SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10
(i) Rate of reaction — definition
Answer: decrease in concentration of reactants per unit time
Rate = −d[A]/dt = +d[P]/dt   Unit: mol·L⁻¹·s⁻¹
(ii) EMF of cell = E_cathode − E_anode
Answer: E_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode
E°_cell = E°_reduction(cathode) − E°_reduction(anode)
(iii) Chelate complex — definition
Answer: complex formed by polydentate ligand forming a ring
Example: [Cu(en)₂]²⁺ (en = ethylenediamine, bidentate → 5-membered chelate ring)
(iv) Example of addition polymer
Answer: polyethylene (polythene)
nCH₂=CH₂ → −(CH₂−CH₂)_n−   Other examples: PVC, polystyrene, Teflon
(v) Amino acid with two amino groups
Answer: lysine
Lysine has −NH₂ at α-carbon AND at side chain (ε-amino group) → dibasic amino acid
(vi) Conductance increases on dilution because
Answer: more ions are available due to greater dissociation
On dilution: degree of dissociation increases → more ions → higher conductance (molar conductivity increases)
(vii) Colligative property
Answer: depends on number of solute particles, not their nature
Four colligative properties: (1) ΔP (vapour pressure lowering), (2) ΔTb (boiling point elevation), (3) ΔTf (freezing point depression), (4) π (osmotic pressure)
(viii) Nucleoside = ?
Answer: base + sugar (without phosphate)
Nucleoside = nitrogenous base + pentose sugar  |  Nucleotide = nucleoside + phosphate group
(ix) IUPAC name of CH₃CH₂NH₂
Answer: ethanamine
CH₃CH₂NH₂ = primary amine, 2-carbon chain → ethanamine
(x) Analgesic medicine example
Answer: aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)
Aspirin = pain reliever, anti-inflammatory, antipyretic.   Also: paracetamol, morphine (narcotic analgesic)
SECTION B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8
(i) Define molarity
Molarity (M) = number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution. M = n/V(L) = (w × 1000)/(M_r × V_mL)   Unit: mol/L
(ii) Electrolytic cell vs galvanic cell
Galvanic cell: Chemical energy → electrical energy (spontaneous, ΔG < 0) Electrolytic cell: Electrical energy → chemical energy (non-spontaneous, external EMF required)
(iii) What is peptide bond?
Peptide bond = amide linkage formed between −COOH of one amino acid and −NH₂ of another with elimination of water. −CO−NH− (peptide bond)   Connects amino acids in proteins
(iv) Define coagulation
Coagulation (flocculation) = aggregation and settling of colloidal particles on addition of electrolyte. Hardy-Schulze rule: Higher valence of the ion, greater its coagulating power.
(v) What is Nernst equation?
E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q = E° − (0.0592/n) log Q   (at 25°C) Relates electrode potential to concentration; when Q = 1, E = E° (standard conditions)
(vi) Biodegradable polymers — two examples
Polymers that decompose naturally by microorganisms: (1) PHBV — poly-β-hydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate (surgical sutures) (2) Poly lactic acid (PLA) — packaging, surgical implants
(vii) What are tranquilisers?
Tranquilisers are drugs that act on CNS to relieve anxiety, stress, tension and mental disorders. Examples: Equanil (meprobamate), Valium (diazepam), Serotonin (natural)
(viii) State Arrhenius equation
k = Ae^(−Ea/RT) log k = log A − Ea/(2.303RT)   Plot of log k vs 1/T gives slope = −Ea/2.303R
SECTION C — Short Answers (selected) 8 × 2 = 16
(i) SN2 mechanism
SN2 = Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular Rate = k[R-X][Nu⁻] → 2nd order reaction, one-step mechanism Nucleophile attacks from back of C bearing leaving group → Walden inversion (configuration inverts) Example: CH₃Br + OH⁻ → CH₃OH + Br⁻ (concerted mechanism)
(iii) Aldol condensation
When acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO) is treated with dilute NaOH → forms aldol (3-hydroxybutanal) 2CH₃CHO → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO (aldol) On heating: aldol → CH₃CH=CHCHO + H₂O (crotonaldehyde — aldol condensation product)
(v) Preparation of aspirin
Aspirin is prepared by acetylation of salicylic acid with acetic anhydride: C₆H₄(OH)COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)COOH + CH₃COOH Uses: Analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory, antiplatelet (prevents blood clotting)
SECTION D — Medium Answers (selected) 6 × 3 = 18
(iv) Electrophilic aromatic substitution — nitration
Nitration of benzene: Benzene + conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ (55°C) → nitrobenzene Mechanism: Step 1: H₂SO₄ protonates HNO₃ → forms NO₂⁺ (nitronium ion, electrophile) Step 2: NO₂⁺ attacks π electron cloud of benzene → arenium ion (σ complex) Step 3: Loss of H⁺ (deprotonation) → nitrobenzene (aromaticity restored)
(vi) ΔTf depression of freezing point — molecular mass
ΔTf = Kf × m = Kf × (w_B × 1000)/(M_B × w_A) M_B = (Kf × w_B × 1000)/(ΔTf × w_A) Kf for water = 1.86 K·kg/mol  |  Kf for benzene = 5.12 K·kg/mol Advantage: Freezing point depression is larger → more accurate for molecular mass determination
SECTION E — Long Answers (selected) 4 × 4 = 16
(i) Colloids — preparation, properties
Chemical method: double decomposition, oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis e.g., FeCl₃ + 3H₂O → Fe(OH)₃ sol (hydrolysis) Electrical disintegration (Bredig's arc): Metal electrodes in dispersion medium, electric arc vaporises metal → condenses into colloidal particles Brownian motion: Zig-zag movement of colloidal particles due to unequal bombardment by solvent molecules → stabilises colloid Tyndall effect: Scattering of visible light beam by colloidal particles (1–100 nm size) Electrophoresis: Migration of charged colloidal particles towards oppositely charged electrode under applied electric field
(iv) Free radical polymerisation of ethylene + Nylon-6,6 and Dacron
Free radical polymerisation (ethylene → polyethylene): Initiation: R• + CH₂=CH₂ → R-CH₂-CH₂• Propagation: R-CH₂-CH₂• + nCH₂=CH₂ → growing chain Termination: two radicals combine → dead chain Nylon-6,6: Adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine → condensation polymer [−NH(CH₂)₆NH−CO(CH₂)₄CO−]_n Used: stockings, ropes, carpets Dacron (Terylene): Ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid → polyester [−OCH₂CH₂OOC−C₆H₄−CO−]_n Used: fabrics, bottles, films
