Section A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
The half-life t₁/₂ for a first-order reaction is:
✔ Answer: (B) 0.693/k
Derivation At t = t₁/₂, [A] = [A]₀/2: k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log([A]₀/([A]₀/2)) = (2.303 × 0.3010)/t₁/₂ t₁/₂ = 0.693/k Independent of initial concentration — unique feature of 1st order reactions
Q.1 (ii)
Coagulation of a colloid by electrolytes follows:
✔ Answer: (C) Hardy-Schulze rule
Hardy-Schulze Rule The coagulating power of an ion is directly proportional to the valency (charge) of the ion with opposite sign to the colloidal particle. For +ve colloid (Fe(OH)₃): coagulating power: PO₄³⁻ > SO₄²⁻ > Cl⁻ For −ve colloid (As₂S₃): coagulating power: Al³⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Na⁺
Q.1 (iii)
During electrolysis of dilute H₂SO₄, gas at anode is:
✔ Answer: (A) O₂
Explanation Anode (oxidation): 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (water is oxidised preferentially over SO₄²⁻) Cathode (reduction): 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ (hydrogen liberated at cathode)
Q.1 (iv)
The hybridisation of carbon in ethyne (C₂H₂) is:
✔ Answer: (D) sp
Explanation Ethyne: H–C≡C–H, each C forms: 1 C–H bond + 1 triple bond (σ + 2π) 2 regions of electron density → linear geometry → sp hybridisation Bond angle: 180°. Both σ bonds use sp orbitals, 2 π bonds use unhybridised p orbitals.
Q.1 (v)
The monomer of PVC is:
✔ Answer: (B) vinyl chloride
Explanation PVC = Polyvinyl chloride = poly(chloroethylene) nCH₂=CHCl → −(CH₂–CHCl)_n−   (addition polymerisation) Monomer = vinyl chloride = chloroethene = CH₂=CHCl
Q.1 (vi)
Glucose is classified as:
✔ Answer: (B) aldohexose
Explanation Hexose = 6 carbon atoms  |  Aldo = has aldehyde (–CHO) group at C1 Glucose: CH₂OH–(CHOH)₄–CHO → aldehyde hexose = aldohexose Fructose = ketohexose (keto at C2). Ribose = aldopentose (5 carbons).
Q.1 (vii)
Reagent to distinguish aldehyde from ketone:
✔ Answer: (A) Fehling's solution   OR   (C) Tollens' reagent (both work)
Explanation Fehling's solution (Cu²⁺ in alkaline solution): Aldehyde → red precipitate (Cu₂O) ✓  |  Ketone → no reaction Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺: Aldehyde → silver mirror ✓  |  Ketone → no reaction Both Fehling's and Tollens' distinguish aldehydes from ketones (either A or C is correct)
Q.1 (viii)
Trans isomers of 2-butene generally have a:
✔ Answer: (B) lower boiling point than cis
Explanation trans-2-butene is more symmetrical (non-polar) → lower intermolecular forces → lower boiling point cis-2-butene is polar (dipole moments don't cancel) → higher intermolecular forces → higher b.p. But trans has higher melting point (more symmetrical → packs better in crystal)
Q.1 (ix)
Type of isomerism in [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺:
✔ Answer: (C) optical isomerism
Explanation en = ethylenediamine (bidentate). cis-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ has a non-superimposable mirror image → chiral molecule → optical isomers (d and l forms) trans-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ does NOT show optical isomerism (has a plane of symmetry)
Q.1 (x)
Fehling's solution contains:
✔ Answer: (A) Cu²⁺ ions in alkaline solution
Composition Fehling's A: CuSO₄ solution (blue, Cu²⁺ ions) Fehling's B: Sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt) + NaOH (alkaline) Mixed just before use. Tartrate keeps Cu²⁺ in solution (forms complex, prevents Cu(OH)₂ precipitation) Aldehydes reduce Cu²⁺ → Cu⁺ (Cu₂O, brick-red precipitate)
Section B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
Q.2 (i)
Define half-life of a reaction.
DefinitionHalf-life (t₁/₂) of a reaction is the time required for the concentration of a reactant to decrease to half its initial value.
Formulae1st order: t₁/₂ = 0.693/k (independent of concentration)
0th order: t₁/₂ = [A]₀/2k (depends on initial concentration)
Q.2 (ii)
What is dialysis? In what process is it used?
DefinitionDialysis is the process of removing dissolved electrolytes and small molecules from a colloidal solution by diffusion through a semipermeable membrane (parchment, cellophane).
UsePurification of colloids from electrolyte impurities. Also used in kidney dialysis (haemodialysis) to clean blood of patients with kidney failure.
Q.2 (iii)
Define molarity. Write its formula and SI unit.
DefinitionMolarity (M) is the number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution.
FormulaM = n/V(L) = (w × 1000)/(M_r × V_mL)
SI Unitmol/L = mol·L⁻¹ (also written as M). Changes with temperature (∵ volume changes).
Q.2 (iv)
What is hydrolysis of an ester? Write the equation.
DefinitionHydrolysis of an ester is the reverse of esterification — reaction of an ester with water (acid or base catalyst) to give an alcohol and a carboxylic acid (or its salt).
Equations Acid hydrolysis: RCOOR' + H₂O ⇌ RCOOH + R'OH (reversible)
Alkaline hydrolysis (saponification): RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH (irreversible)
Q.2 (v)
What is a peptide bond? Where is it formed?
DefinitionA peptide bond (amide bond) is the covalent bond formed between the –COOH group of one amino acid and the –NH₂ group of another amino acid with the elimination of water.
Formula–CO–NH– (peptide linkage)
LocationForms between amino acids in the primary structure of proteins (in the ribosome during translation)
Q.2 (vi)
What is degree of polymerisation?
DefinitionDegree of polymerisation (n) is the number of monomer units present in one polymer chain (one macromolecule).
Formulan = M_polymer / M_monomer
ExamplePolythene from ethylene (M=28): if M_polythene = 14000, n = 14000/28 = 500
Q.2 (vii)
Define lanthanoid contraction. What causes it?
DefinitionLanthanoid contraction is the steady decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of lanthanoid elements (La to Lu) with increasing atomic number across the series.
CauseAddition of electrons to the inner 4f orbitals, which have poor shielding ability. As Z increases, nuclear attraction increases but shielding by 4f electrons is ineffective → net ionic radius decreases.
Consequence5th period transition metals (Zr, Nb...) have almost same size as 4th period (Hf, Ta...) — used in alloys; difficult to separate by chemical methods.
Q.2 (viii)
What is disproportionation? Give one example.
DefinitionDisproportionation is a redox reaction in which a single species simultaneously undergoes both oxidation and reduction (one part is oxidised, another part is reduced).
Example 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂
O in H₂O₂ is −1. In H₂O it is −2 (reduced); in O₂ it is 0 (oxidised). Both changes happen to the same compound.
Q.2 (ix)
Define osmotic pressure. State its SI unit.
DefinitionOsmotic pressure (π) is the minimum excess pressure that must be applied on the solution side of a semipermeable membrane to prevent the flow of solvent (osmosis) into it from the pure solvent side.
Formulaπ = CRT = (n/V)RT
SI UnitPascal (Pa) or atm. Very sensitive — used to determine high molar masses (polymers, proteins).
Q.2 (x)
What is inductive effect? Distinguish +I and −I effects.
DefinitionInductive effect is the permanent displacement of electrons along the chain of atoms in a molecule due to the electronegativity difference between bonded atoms.
+I EffectElectron-donating: alkyl groups (CH₃, C₂H₅) push electrons toward the reaction site. Stabilise carbocations. Example: (CH₃)₃C⁺ more stable than CH₃⁺
−I EffectElectron-withdrawing: halogens, NO₂, CN pull electrons away from reaction site. Stabilise carbanions. Example: –Cl, –NO₂, –COOH groups
Q.2 (xi)
Define specific conductance.
DefinitionSpecific conductance (κ, kappa) is the conductance of 1 cm³ (1 mL) of a solution placed between two electrodes of 1 cm² area and 1 cm apart.
Formulaκ = G × (l/A) = conductance × cell constant
SI UnitS·cm⁻¹ or S·m⁻¹. Increases with dilution for weak electrolytes.
Q.2 (xii)
What is a biodegradable polymer? Give one example.
DefinitionA biodegradable polymer is a polymer that can be decomposed by microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) in the environment under natural conditions, reducing environmental pollution.
ExamplePHBV — poly(3-hydroxybutyrate-co-3-hydroxyvalerate). Used in packaging, surgical sutures. Other example: PLA (polylactic acid).

Q.3 (i)
Derive the integrated rate law for a second-order reaction.
Rate Law−d[A]/dt = k[A]²

Result
1/[A] = kt + 1/[A]₀   Graph: 1/[A] vs t → straight line, slope = k
t₁/₂ = 1/(k[A]₀) — depends on initial concentration
Q.3 (ii)
State the Freundlich adsorption isotherm. Write equation and draw graph.
StatementThe amount of gas (x) adsorbed per unit mass (m) of adsorbent is proportional to the pressure (P) of gas to a fractional power (1/n, where n > 1).
Equationx/m = k × P^(1/n)
Log Formlog(x/m) = log k + (1/n) log P → straight line when log(x/m) plotted vs log P, slope = 1/n, intercept = log k
LimitationOnly an empirical relation — not valid at high pressures; at saturation x/m reaches a constant maximum (Langmuir isotherm is better).

Q.3 (iv)
Explain the mechanism of electrophilic addition of HBr to ethylene.
Step 1 — Protonation H–Br is polarised (H⁺ end). π electrons of ethylene attack H⁺ (electrophile) CH₂=CH₂ + H⁺ → CH₃–CH₂⁺ (carbocation, slow step)
Step 2 — Attack CH₃–CH₂⁺ + Br⁻ → CH₃–CH₂Br (bromoethane, fast step)
Markovnikov's RuleFor unsymmetrical alkenes: H⁺ adds to carbon with more H atoms → more stable (more substituted) carbocation forms → overall Markovnikov product.
Q.3 (v)
Write the preparation and important properties of ethanoic acid.
Preparation (1) Oxidation of ethanol: CH₃CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃COOH (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄) (2) From acetaldehyde: CH₃CHO + [O] → CH₃COOH (3) Industrial: CH₃OH + CO → CH₃COOH (Monsanto process, Rh catalyst)
Properties Weak acid: CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ (Ka = 1.8×10⁻⁵) Reacts with Na, NaOH, Na₂CO₃ to give sodium acetate + CO₂ (with carbonate) Forms esters with alcohols: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
Q.3 (vi)
What are enantiomers? State conditions for optical isomerism. Give one example.
EnantiomersEnantiomers are stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other. They have the same physical properties except that they rotate the plane of polarised light in opposite directions (+/−).
ConditionsOptical isomerism requires a chiral centre (asymmetric carbon): a carbon atom bonded to four different groups (ABCD).
Example Lactic acid: CH₃–CH(OH)–COOH — C2 is chiral (bonded to CH₃, OH, COOH, H) d-(+)-lactic acid (muscle) and l-(−)-lactic acid (fermentation) — enantiomers
Q.3 (vii)
Write the Arrhenius equation. Explain effect of temperature on rate.
Arrhenius Equation k = A × e^(−Ea/RT) or log k = log A − Ea/(2.303RT) A = frequency factor (pre-exponential factor), Ea = activation energy, R = 8.314 J/mol·K
Effect of Temperature As T increases, e^(−Ea/RT) increases → k increases → rate increases Rule of thumb: Rate doubles for every 10°C rise in temperature (approximately) At higher T, more molecules have energy ≥ Ea (Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution shifts)
Q.3 (viii)
Explain peptide linkage formation with a diagram showing dipeptide.
Formation Two amino acids condense: –COOH of amino acid 1 + –NH₂ of amino acid 2 → peptide bond + H₂O H₂N–CHR₁–COOH + H₂N–CHR₂–COOH → H₂N–CHR₁–CO–NH–CHR₂–COOH + H₂O The –CO–NH– linkage is the peptide bond (amide bond)
DipeptideProduct of two amino acids = dipeptide (one peptide bond)
Tripeptide = 3 amino acids, 2 peptide bonds; n amino acids, (n−1) peptide bonds

Q.5 (i)
(a) Faraday's laws of electrolysis. (b) Time for 2A to deposit 1.27 g Cu from CuSO₄.
Faraday's 1st LawMass (m) of substance deposited at electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (Q) passed: m = ZQ = ZIt   Z = electrochemical equivalent
Faraday's 2nd LawSame quantity of electricity deposits masses of different substances proportional to their equivalent masses: m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂
Numerical Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, I = 2 A, m = 1.27 g, Cu = 63.5 g/mol Equivalent mass of Cu = 63.5/2 = 31.75 g/eq m = (E/F) × Q = (E/F) × It → 1.27 = (31.75/96500) × 2 × t t = (1.27 × 96500)/(31.75 × 2) = 122555/63.5 = 1930 s
Answer
t = 1930 s ≈ 32.2 minutes
Q.5 (iii)
(a) Free radical polymerisation of ethylene. (b) Nylon-6,6 and PVC.
Free Radical Mechanism Initiation: R–O–O–R → 2RO•  |  RO• + CH₂=CH₂ → RO–CH₂–CH₂• Propagation: RO–CH₂–CH₂• + n(CH₂=CH₂) → growing chain Termination: Two radicals combine → dead polymer
Nylon-6,6 H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ + HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH → [–NH(CH₂)₆NHCO(CH₂)₄CO–]_n + H₂O Uses: Ropes, stockings, toothbrush bristles, surgical sutures

Q.5 (iv)
(a) Four levels of protein structure. (b) DNA vs RNA differences.
Primary StructureThe sequence of amino acids linked by peptide bonds in the polypeptide chain. Determined by genes. Example: Insulin (A chain: 21 aa, B chain: 30 aa)
Secondary StructureLocal folding: α-helix (right-handed coil, stabilised by H-bonds between C=O and N–H of same chain, every 4th residue) or β-pleated sheet (parallel chains linked by H-bonds). Example: α-keratin (hair, nails)
Tertiary StructureOverall 3D folding of the entire polypeptide chain, stabilised by S–S disulfide bonds, H-bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions. Determines function. Example: myoglobin
Quaternary StructureAssembly of two or more polypeptide subunits. Example: haemoglobin (2α + 2β chains)
DNA vs RNA Double-stranded / Single-stranded  |  Deoxyribose / Ribose  |  Thymine / Uracil  |  Genetic info storage / Protein synthesis

OsmosisSpontaneous flow of solvent from dilute to concentrated solution through a semipermeable membrane. Stops when pressure difference equals osmotic pressure π.
