Section A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
The electric potential energy stored in a capacitor is:
✔ Answer: (B) ½CV²
Explanation Work done in charging capacitor: dW = V dq = (q/C)dq U = ∫₀^Q (q/C)dq = Q²/2C = ½CV² = ½QV Note: CV² is wrong (off by factor 2). Q²/C is also wrong (off by factor 2).
Q.1 (ii)
For cells in parallel, the terminal voltage equals:
✔ Answer: (A) EMF − I·r/n
Explanation For n identical cells (each EMF = E, internal resistance r) in parallel: Effective EMF = E (same as one cell) Effective internal resistance = r/n V = E − I(r/n)
Q.1 (iii)
The cyclotron frequency does NOT depend on:
✔ Answer: (C) speed of particle
Explanation f_c = qB/(2πm) Cyclotron frequency depends on q (charge), B (magnetic field) and m (mass) — but NOT on speed or radius. This is why the cyclotron works — as the particle speeds up, the radius increases but the time for each semicircle stays the same.
Q.1 (iv)
For total internal reflection, critical angle C satisfies:
✔ Answer: (A) sin C = 1/n
Explanation At critical angle: angle of refraction = 90° in rarer medium (air). n₁ sin C = n₂ sin 90° → n sin C = 1 → sin C = 1/n
Q.1 (v)
Balmer series in hydrogen: transitions to n=2 give:
✔ Answer: (B) visible light
Explanation Balmer series: transitions from n = 3, 4, 5... down to n = 2 First line (Hα): 656 nm (red), Hβ: 486 nm (blue-green), Hγ: 434 nm (violet) All Balmer lines fall in the visible region (400–700 nm)
Q.1 (vi)
E = mc² was proposed by:
✔ Answer: (A) Einstein
Explanation Albert Einstein (1905) — Special Theory of Relativity — derived mass-energy equivalence: E = mc² This shows mass is a concentrated form of energy. 1 amu = 931.5 MeV (from E = mc² conversion)
Q.1 (vii)
A p-n junction diode acts as:
✔ Answer: (B) one-way valve for current
Explanation In forward bias: diode conducts (low resistance ~few ohms) — current flows easily. In reverse bias: diode blocks (resistance ~MΩ) — almost no current. This unidirectional conduction makes it a "one-way valve" — basis of rectification.
Q.1 (viii)
In AM modulation, the modulation index m is:
✔ Answer: (A) m = Am/Ac
Explanation m = Am/Ac = (A_max − A_min)/(A_max + A_min) Am = amplitude of message signal, Ac = amplitude of carrier wave For good AM: 0 ≤ m ≤ 1. If m > 1: over-modulation (distortion).
Q.1 (ix)
For TIR, the angle of incidence must be:
✔ Answer: (C) greater than critical angle
Explanation Two conditions for TIR: (1) denser to rarer medium, (2) i > C (critical angle) At i = C: refracted ray grazes the surface (90°) At i > C: all light is internally reflected — no refracted ray
Q.1 (x)
Electric field inside a hollow charged conductor is:
✔ Answer: (A) zero
Explanation By Gauss's law: for a Gaussian surface just inside the hollow conductor, enclosed charge = 0 ∮E·dA = q_enc/ε₀ = 0 → E = 0 inside This is the principle of electrostatic shielding. All charge resides on the outer surface.

Q.2 (i)
Define potential gradient. State its SI unit.
DefinitionPotential gradient is the rate of change of electric potential with respect to distance along the direction of maximum decrease of potential.
FormulaPotential gradient = −E = −dV/dx (negative because potential decreases in direction of E)
SI UnitV/m (Volt per metre)
Q.2 (ii)
What is meant by electromagnetic 'induction'?
DefinitionElectromagnetic induction is the phenomenon of generation of an EMF (and hence current) in a conductor whenever the magnetic flux linked with it changes. Discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831.
Formulae = −N(dΦ/dt)
Q.2 (iii)
Define angular momentum of electron in Bohr orbit.
DefinitionAngular momentum of an electron in the nth Bohr orbit is an integral multiple of h/2π (Bohr's quantisation condition).
FormulaL_n = mvr = nh/2π = nħ where ħ = h/2π = reduced Planck constant  (n = 1, 2, 3...)
Q.2 (iv)
What is nuclear force? State two properties.
DefinitionNuclear force is the strongest fundamental force that holds nucleons (protons and neutrons) together inside the nucleus, overcoming electrostatic repulsion between protons.
Properties (1) Short range: Effective only within ~10⁻¹⁵ m (1 fm)
(2) Charge independent: Equal between p-p, p-n, and n-n pairs (same magnitude)
Q.2 (v)
Define threshold frequency in photoelectric effect.
DefinitionThreshold frequency (ν₀) is the minimum frequency of incident light below which no photoelectric emission occurs from a metal surface, regardless of the intensity of the light.
Formulaφ = hν₀ (work function = energy of threshold photon)
Q.2 (vi)
State Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.
StatementIt is impossible to simultaneously measure the exact position and exact momentum of a particle. The more precisely one is known, the less precisely the other can be known.
ExpressionΔx · Δp ≥ h/4π = ħ/2
Also: ΔE · Δt ≥ h/4π
Q.2 (vii)
What is the principle of an electric motor?
PrincipleAn electric motor works on the principle that a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a mechanical force (F = BIL sinθ), which causes it to rotate.
FormulaF = BIL  |  Torque τ = NBIA sinθ
Q.2 (viii)
What is the depletion region in a p-n junction?
DefinitionThe depletion region is a thin layer at the p-n junction where free electrons from n-side and holes from p-side recombine, leaving behind fixed positive ions (n-side) and negative ions (p-side), creating an electric field (potential barrier).
Barrier Potential~0.3 V for Ge, ~0.7 V for Si. Prevents further flow of majority carriers without external bias.
Q.2 (ix)
Define modulation. Why is it necessary?
DefinitionModulation is the process of superimposing a low-frequency information signal (audio/video) onto a high-frequency carrier wave for efficient long-distance transmission.
Necessity (1) Audio frequencies (20 Hz–20 kHz) have very short range — cannot travel far.
(2) High-frequency carrier waves can travel long distances with less energy loss.
(3) Allows multiple signals simultaneously without interference (different carrier frequencies).
Q.2 (x)
What is sky wave propagation?
DefinitionSky wave propagation is the transmission of radio waves that are reflected back to Earth by the ionosphere (layer of ionised air at height 60–1000 km).
Frequency Range3 MHz – 30 MHz (short waves/HF band)
ApplicationLong-distance international shortwave radio communication; can cover entire globe by multiple reflections.
Q.2 (xi)
State Kepler's second law of planetary motion.
StatementThe line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time (law of equal areas).
ImplicationA planet moves fastest when closest to the Sun (perihelion) and slowest when farthest (aphelion). This is because angular momentum L = mvr = constant.
Q.2 (xii)
Define refractive index of a medium.
DefinitionThe refractive index (n) of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (c) to the speed of light in that medium (v).
Formulan = c/v = sin θᵢ / sin θᵣ (Snell's law ratio form)
Noten ≥ 1 always. For vacuum n = 1, water n = 1.33, glass n = 1.5, diamond n = 2.42.
Section C — Short Answer Questions 8 × 2 = 16 Marks
Q.3 (i)
Derive torque on electric dipole in uniform electric field.
SetupDipole with +q and −q separated by 2a, placed at angle θ to uniform field E⃗.
Forces Force on +q: F = qE (along E⃗) Force on −q: F = qE (opposite to E⃗) Net translating force = 0 (equal, opposite). But they form a couple (torque).
Torque τ = Force × perpendicular distance = qE × 2a sinθ = (q·2a)E sinθ = pE sinθ
Result
τ = pE sinθ = p⃗ × E⃗   (in vector form)
τ is maximum when θ = 90° (p⃗ ⊥ E⃗) and zero when θ = 0°.
Q.3 (ii)
Derive Kirchhoff's voltage law from conservation of energy.
Derivation Consider a closed loop ABCDA in a circuit. As a positive test charge q moves around the loop: Work done by E sources (EMFs): W_E = q × ΣE Work done against resistors (IR drops): W_R = −q × Σ(IR) In a closed loop, work done = 0 (potential is a state function; returns to start) W_E + W_R = 0 → ΣE + Σ(IR) = 0
Result
ΣE = Σ(IR)   — Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (from conservation of energy)
Q.3 (iii)
Explain helical motion of charged particle in magnetic field.
ConditionWhen a charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field at an angle θ (not 0° or 90°) to the field direction.
Analysis Resolve velocity: v‖ = v cosθ (parallel to B) and v⊥ = v sinθ (perpendicular to B) v⊥ causes circular motion (radius r = mv⊥/qB) v‖ causes straight motion (no force, since F = qv‖B sin0° = 0) Combined: particle moves in a helix (coiled spring shape)
Pitch
Pitch = v‖ × T = v cosθ × (2πm/qB)
Q.3 (iv)
Define eddy currents. State causes, disadvantages, and uses.
DefinitionEddy currents are induced currents that circulate in closed loops within a conductor (like swirling eddies in water) when the conductor is placed in a changing magnetic field.
CauseChanging magnetic flux through the conductor induces EMF (Faraday's law) → currents circulate in the plane ⊥ to B.
Disadvantages(1) Cause unwanted heating in transformer cores (energy loss)
(2) Slow down the motion in certain devices (back torque)
Uses(1) Electromagnetic braking in trains (eddy current brakes — no friction)
(2) Induction furnaces (melting metals by eddy current heating)
Q.3 (v)
Derive the lens formula 1/f = 1/v − 1/u.
Derivation For a thin convex lens with object at O, image at I, focal length f. Using similar triangles with the geometry of the ray diagram: △OAB and △IA'B': AB/A'B' = OB/IB' → AB/A'B' = u/v ...(1) △FOC and △FIA': OC/A'I = OF/IF → AB/A'I = f/(v−f) ...(2) From (1) and (2): u/v = f/(v−f) Cross-multiplying: u(v−f) = vf → uv − uf = vf Dividing by uvf: 1/f − 1/v = 1/u, rearranging:
Result
1/v − 1/u = 1/f   (Lens formula)
Q.3 (vi)
Explain nuclear fusion — conditions and two examples.
DefinitionNuclear fusion is the process of combining two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing enormous energy (more per nucleon than fission).
Conditions (1) Very high temperature (~10⁷ to 10⁸ K) to overcome electrostatic repulsion between nuclei
(2) Very high pressure/density so nuclei come within range of nuclear force (~10⁻¹⁵ m)
Examples (1) ¹H + ¹H → ²H + e⁺ + ν + 0.42 MeV (2) ²H + ³H → ⁴He + ¹n + 17.6 MeV
ApplicationH-bomb (uncontrolled), stars/Sun (thermonuclear reactions), proposed ITER fusion reactor
Q.3 (vii)
Explain LED — construction, working and two advantages.
ConstructionA heavily doped p-n junction diode made from direct bandgap semiconductors (GaAs, GaP, InGaN etc.) with a transparent cover.
Working LED is forward biased. Electrons from n-side and holes from p-side recombine at junction. Energy released = band gap energy Eg → emitted as photon of light (electroluminescence). hν = Eg   (wavelength/colour depends on Eg of material)
Advantages(1) Very low power consumption (energy efficient)
(2) Long lifetime (~100,000 hours), compact, rugged, fast switching
Q.3 (viii)
Derive the expression for frequency range in AM signal.
AM Signal Carrier: c(t) = Ac sin(2πfct)  |  Message: m(t) = Am sin(2πfmt) AM signal: u(t) = Ac sin(2πfct) + (AmAc/2)[cos 2π(fc−fm)t − cos 2π(fc+fm)t] This contains three frequencies: fc (carrier), fc+fm (upper sideband), fc−fm (lower sideband)

Q.3 (x)
Explain interference in thin films.
FormationWhen light falls on a thin film, it is reflected from both the upper and lower surfaces. These two reflected rays interfere with each other.
Path Difference Δ = 2μt cosθ   (μ = refractive index, t = thickness) Extra half-wavelength shift occurs at the denser medium reflection.
Conditions Constructive: 2μt cosθ = (2n−1)λ/2 → bright (for normal incidence: 2μt = (2n−1)λ/2) Destructive: 2μt cosθ = nλ → dark
ExampleColours in soap bubbles, oil films on water, anti-reflection coatings on lenses
Q.3 (xi)
Derive the expression for energy stored in an inductor: E = ½LI².
Derivation When current I flows in an inductor L, back EMF = L(dI/dt) Power supplied by source against back EMF: P = e × I = L(dI/dt) × I Energy stored for current increasing from 0 to I: U = ∫P dt = ∫₀^I LI dI = L × I²/2
Result
U = ½LI²   (energy stored in magnetic field of inductor)
Q.3 (xii)
Explain the photovoltaic effect and working of a solar cell.
Photovoltaic EffectThe generation of an EMF directly from light in a p-n junction is called the photovoltaic effect (discovered by Becquerel, 1839).
Working Light photons fall on a thin p-type layer → electron-hole pairs created near the junction Built-in electric field at junction sweeps electrons to n-side, holes to p-side This creates a potential difference (V_oc ~ 0.5 V for Si solar cell) External circuit: current flows from n-side through external load to p-side
ApplicationSolar panels (series/parallel combinations) for rooftop power, satellites, calculators

Q.4 (i)
Derive E due to a charged infinite thin spherical shell.
Outside (r > R) Gaussian surface: sphere of radius r (concentric with shell). Q_enc = Q E × 4πr² = Q/ε₀ → E = Q/(4πε₀r²) = kQ/r² Same as if all charge were at the centre (shell acts as point charge for outside points).
On Surface (r = R) E = Q/(4πε₀R²) = σ/ε₀
Inside (r < R) Gaussian sphere inside: Q_enc = 0 (no charge inside shell) E × 4πr² = 0 → E = 0
Summary
E = 0 (inside)  |  E = σ/ε₀ (on surface)  |  E = kQ/r² (outside)

Section E — Long Answer Questions 4 × 4 = 16 Marks
Q.5 (i)
(a) Van de Graaff generator. (b) Electrostatic shielding.
VdG PrincipleCharge given to a hollow conductor completely moves to outer surface. Continuous supply of charge increases potential indefinitely.
Construction Large hollow metallic sphere (dome) on insulating column. Endless belt of insulating material (silk/rubber) runs between two pulleys. Lower pulley: spray comb (connected to + HV) sprays +ve charge onto belt. Upper pulley: collecting comb inside the dome — removes charge from belt → charge flows to dome surface.
Working Charge continuously delivered to dome → potential builds up to millions of volts (3–10 MV) Used to accelerate ions in nuclear physics research.
Electrostatic Shielding Principle: E = 0 inside a hollow conductor (by Gauss's law, Q_enc = 0 inside hollow conductor). Application: Sensitive electronic equipment is enclosed in metal box (Faraday cage) → external electric fields cannot penetrate → protection from lightning, electrostatic interference. Examples: Coaxial cables (inner conductor shielded by outer metal braid), car body protects occupants during lightning.
