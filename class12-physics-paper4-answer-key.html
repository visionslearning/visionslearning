Q.1 (i)
The electric potential energy stored in a capacitor is:
(A) CV²
(B) ½CV²
(C) 2CV²
(D) Q²/C
✔ Answer: (B) ½CV²
ExplanationU = ∫₀^Q (q/C)dq = Q²/2C = ½CV²
Q.1 (ii)
For cells connected in parallel, the terminal voltage equals:
(A) EMF − I·r/n
(B) EMF − I·r
(C) n·EMF − I·r
(D) EMF + I·r
✔ Answer: (A) EMF − I·r/n
Explanation n cells in parallel: effective EMF = E, effective r = r/n V = E − I(r/n) = E − Ir/n
Q.1 (iii)
The cyclotron frequency does NOT depend on:
(A) charge of particle
(B) mass of particle
(C) speed of particle
(D) applied magnetic field
✔ Answer: (C) speed of particle
Explanation f_c = qB/(2πm) — depends on q, B, m but NOT on speed v This independence is why cyclotron works — as speed increases, radius increases but period stays constant.
Q.1 (iv)
For total internal reflection, the critical angle C satisfies:
(A) sin C = 1/n
(B) sin C = n
(C) cos C = 1/n
(D) tan C = n
✔ Answer: (A) sin C = 1/n
Explanation At critical angle, refracted angle = 90°. Snell's law: n sinC = 1 × sin90° sin C = 1/n
Q.1 (v)
In the Balmer series of hydrogen, transitions to n = 2 give:
(A) UV radiation

Q.1 (vii)
A p-n junction diode acts as:
(A) amplifier
(B) one-way valve for current
(C) oscillator
(D) resistor
✔ Answer: (B) one-way valve for current
ExplanationForward bias: conducts (low resistance). Reverse bias: blocks (very high resistance). Acts as one-way valve → basis of rectification.
Q.1 (viii)
In AM modulation, the modulation index m is:
(A) m = Am/Ac
(B) m = Ac/Am
(C) m = Am + Ac
(D) m = Am × Ac
✔ Answer: (A) m = Am/Ac
Explanation Modulation index m = Am/Ac (ratio of message amplitude to carrier amplitude) For good AM: 0 ≤ m ≤ 1. Overmodulation (m > 1) causes distortion.
Q.1 (ix)
For total internal reflection to occur, the angle of incidence must be:
(A) equal to critical angle
(B) less than critical angle
(C) greater than critical angle
(D) 90°
✔ Answer: (C) greater than critical angle
ExplanationTIR occurs when i > C (critical angle). At i = C, refracted ray just grazes surface (θ_r = 90°). For i > C, all light is reflected back.
Q.1 (x)
The electric field inside a hollow charged conductor is:
(A) zero
(B) uniform and non-zero
(C) maximum at centre
(D) equal to field outside
✔ Answer: (A) zero
Explanation By Gauss's law: for any Gaussian surface inside a hollow conductor, Q_enclosed = 0 → E = 0 Principle of electrostatic shielding. All charge resides on the outer surface.

Q.3 (i) [2 Marks]
Derive the expression for torque on an electric dipole placed in a uniform electric field.
Dipole in uniform E⃗: equal and opposite forces → couple (torque) = pE sinθ
Electric dipole in uniform field: equal and opposite forces form a couple (torque)
Derivation Force on +q: qE (along E⃗); Force on −q: qE (opposite to E⃗) Net translational force = 0. But they form a couple. Perpendicular distance = 2a sinθ τ = qE × 2a sinθ = (q·2a)·E·sinθ = pE sinθ
Result
τ = pE sinθ = p⃗ × E⃗   (max when θ = 90°)
Q.3 (iv) [2 Marks]
Define eddy currents. State their causes, two disadvantages and two uses.
DefinitionEddy currents are induced currents that circulate in closed loops within a conductor placed in a changing magnetic field (like swirling eddies in water).
CauseChanging magnetic flux through the conductor bulk → induced EMF → circulating currents by Faraday's law.
Disadvantages(1) Cause energy loss (heat) in transformer cores
(2) Slow down motion in rotating machines (back torque)
Uses(1) Electromagnetic braking in trains (no friction)
(2) Induction furnaces (melting metals by heating)
Q.3 (v) [2 Marks]
Derive the lens formula 1/f = 1/v − 1/u using sign convention.
Convex lens ray diagram: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f (lens formula with sign convention)
Derivation Using similar triangles for thin convex lens with real image: △OAB ~ △OA'B': A'B'/AB = v/u ...(1) △OCF ~ △A'B'F: A'B'/OC = A'F/OF = (v−f)/f Since OC = AB: A'B'/AB = (v−f)/f ...(2) From (1) and (2): v/u = (v−f)/f → vf = uv − uf → dividing by uvf: 1/u = 1/f − 1/v → 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
Result
1/v − 1/u = 1/f (lens formula with sign convention)
Q.3 (xi) [2 Marks]
Derive the expression for energy stored in an inductor: E = ½LI².
Energy stored in inductor: U = ∫₀ᴵ LI dI = ½LI²
Derivation When current I flows in inductor L, back EMF = L(dI/dt) Power supplied against back EMF: P = eI = L(dI/dt)·I U = ∫P dt = ∫₀^I LI dI = L[I²/2]₀^I = ½LI²
Result
U = ½LI² (energy stored in magnetic field of inductor)

Q.4 (vii) [3 Marks]
A capacitor of 20 μF is connected to a 100 V supply. Find the charge and energy stored. If it is then connected to another uncharged 20 μF capacitor, find the new voltage.
Initial Q = CV = 20×10⁻⁶ × 100 = 2×10⁻³ C = 2 mC U = ½CV² = ½ × 20×10⁻⁶ × 10⁴ = 0.1 J
After Connection Charge Q = 2 mC is shared between C_total = 20+20 = 40 μF V' = Q/C_total = 2×10⁻³/(40×10⁻⁶) = 50 V
Answer
Initial: Q = 2 mC, U = 0.1 J  |  After connection: V' = 50 V
Section E — Long Answers 4 × 4 = 16 Marks
Q.5 (i) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain the construction and working of a Van de Graaff generator. (b) Explain electrostatic shielding — principle and practical uses.
Van de Graaff generator: belt continuously delivers +charges to the dome
PrincipleCharge given to a hollow conductor moves entirely to its outer surface (Gauss's law). Fresh charge can always be added to the inside, increasing the dome potential indefinitely.
Construction & Working Lower spray comb (connected to +HV) sprays + charges onto moving belt. Belt carries charges up into the dome. Upper collecting comb removes charges from belt → deposits on dome outer surface. Potential builds up to millions of volts (3–10 MV). Used to accelerate ions in nuclear physics.
Electrostatic Shielding Principle: E = 0 inside a hollow conductor. Enclosing a region in a metal cage blocks external electric fields. Uses: Faraday cage (electronics shielding), coaxial cables (inner conductor shielded from external noise), car body protection from lightning.
