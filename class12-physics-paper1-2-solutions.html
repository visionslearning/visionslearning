Section B — Very Short Answer Questions (1 Mark Each) 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
B(i) Define electric flux. 2024 1 Mark
Definition:Electric flux (Φ) is defined as the total number of electric field lines passing normally through a given surface area.
Formula:Φ = E · A · cosθ   where θ is the angle between E and the normal to the surface.
SI Unit:N·m²/C   (or   V·m)
∴ Answer:Φ = E A cosθ  ;   Unit = N·m²/C
B(ii) State Ohm's law. 2023 1 Mark
Statement:At constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends.
Formula:V = I × R   where R = resistance of the conductor (constant at fixed temperature).
Note:Ohm's law is valid for metallic conductors at constant temperature (not for semiconductors, diodes etc.).
∴ Answer:V ∝ I  ⟹  V = IR
B(iii) Define magnetic flux and state its SI unit. 2022 1 Mark
Definition:Magnetic flux (ΦB) through a surface is the total number of magnetic field lines passing normally through that surface.
Formula:ΦB = B · A · cosθ   where θ = angle between B and area vector A.
SI Unit:Weber (Wb)   [1 Wb = 1 T·m²]
B(iv) Define power factor of an AC circuit. 2024 1 Mark
Definition:Power factor is the ratio of true (active) power to apparent power in an AC circuit.
Formula:Power factor = cosφ = R/Z   where φ = phase angle between V and I, Z = impedance.
Range:0 ≤ cosφ ≤ 1  |  For pure resistor: cosφ = 1  |  For pure inductor/capacitor: cosφ = 0
∴ Answer:Power factor = cosφ = R/Z
B(v) What is total internal reflection (TIR)? 2023 1 Mark
Definition:Total Internal Reflection (TIR) is the phenomenon where light traveling from a denser medium to a rarer medium is completely reflected back into the denser medium when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle.
Conditions:(1) Light must travel from denser → rarer medium.   (2) Angle of incidence must be ≥ critical angle (i ≥ ic).
Examples:Optical fibre, diamond sparkle, mirages, periscope prisms.
∴ Answer:Complete reflection of light back into denser medium when i ≥ ic.
B(vi) Define work function of a metal. 2022 1 Mark
Definition:Work function (φ) of a metal is the minimum amount of energy required to completely remove one electron from the surface of the metal.
Formula:φ = h × ν0   where ν0 = threshold frequency, h = Planck's constant.
Unit:Joule (J) or electron-volt (eV)
Note:Different metals have different work functions (e.g., Cs ≈ 2 eV, W ≈ 4.5 eV).
∴ Answer:Minimum energy to remove electron from metal surface. φ = hν0.
B(vii) What is a rectifier? 2024 1 Mark
Definition:A rectifier is an electronic device (using a p-n junction diode) that converts alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC) by allowing current flow in only one direction.
Types:(1) Half-wave rectifier — uses one diode, passes only positive half cycle.
     (2) Full-wave rectifier — uses two or four diodes, passes both half cycles.
∴ Answer:Device that converts AC → DC using a p-n junction diode.
B(viii) State De Morgan's second theorem. 2023 1 Mark
Statement:The complement of the OR of two variables equals the AND of their individual complements.
Formula:(A + B)' = A' · B'
Meaning:NOT(A OR B) = (NOT A) AND (NOT B)
Note:First theorem: (A · B)' = A' + B'  |  Second theorem: (A + B)' = A' · B'
∴ Answer:(A + B)' = A' · B'
B(ix) Define nuclear binding energy. 2022 1 Mark
Definition:Nuclear binding energy is the minimum energy required to completely separate all nucleons (protons and neutrons) in a nucleus to an infinite distance apart.
Formula:BE = Δm × c²   where Δm = mass defect = (sum of masses of free nucleons) − (actual nuclear mass).
Note:Higher binding energy per nucleon = more stable nucleus. Fe-56 has highest BE/A ≈ 8.8 MeV.
∴ Answer:Energy to separate all nucleons. BE = Δm·c².
B(x) What is critical angle? On what factors does it depend? 2024 1 Mark
Definition:Critical angle (ic) is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is exactly 90°.
Formula:sin ic = n₂/n₁ = 1/n   (where n = refractive index of denser w.r.t. rarer medium)
Depends on:(1) Refractive indices of the two media.   (2) Wavelength (colour) of light — violet has smaller ic than red.
∴ Answer:sin ic = 1/n  ; depends on refractive indices and wavelength of light.
B(xi) What is a photon? State its properties. 2023 1 Mark
Definition:A photon is a quantum (packet) of electromagnetic energy. It is the basic particle of light proposed by Einstein.
Properties: (1) Energy: E = hν = hc/λ
(2) Rest mass = 0  (but has relativistic mass)
(3) Speed = c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum
(4) Momentum: p = h/λ = E/c
(5) No charge; travels in straight line.
∴ Answer:Quantum of EM energy. E = hν; p = h/λ; m₀ = 0; speed = c.
B(xii) State Coulomb's law of electrostatics. 2022 1 Mark
Statement:The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
Formula:F = k·q₁·q₂/r²   where k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
Direction:Along the line joining the two charges. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract.
∴ Answer:F = kq₁q₂/r²  ; acts along the line joining the charges.
Section C — Short Answer Questions (2 Marks Each) 8 × 2 = 16 Marks
C(i) Derive the expression for energy stored in a capacitor. 2024 2 Marks
Setup:Let a capacitor of capacitance C be charged gradually from 0 to charge Q (voltage 0 to V).
At instant:When charge on capacitor = q, voltage = q/C.
Small work:dW = V · dq = (q/C) · dq
Total work:W = ∫₀Q (q/C) dq = [q²/2C]₀Q = Q²/2C
Substitute:Q = CV  ⟹  W = (CV)²/2C = ½CV²
∴ Energy:U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/2C
Units:Joules (J). This energy is stored in the electric field between the plates.
C(ii) Explain Kirchhoff's current law (KCL) with a diagram. 2023 2 Marks
KCL Statement:The algebraic sum of all currents meeting at a junction (node) in a circuit is zero. (Equivalently: sum of currents entering = sum of currents leaving.)
Formula:ΣI = 0   at a node  ⟹  I₁ + I₂ = I₃ + I₄ (for a 4-wire node)
Diagram:Junction with I₁, I₂ entering and I₃, I₄ leaving → KCL: I₁ + I₂ = I₃ + I₄
     I₁ →     ← I₂
        ● (Node)
    I₃ ↓     ↓ I₄
Currents IN: I₁ + I₂  |  Currents OUT: I₃ + I₄  |  KCL: I₁ + I₂ = I₃ + I₄
Basis:Based on conservation of electric charge — charge cannot accumulate at a junction.
∴ Answer:ΣIin = ΣIout at every node. Based on charge conservation.
C(iii) Coil: 50 turns, A = 0.02 m², B = 0.4 T, f = 60 Hz. Find max EMF. 2022 2 Marks
Given:N = 50 turns, A = 0.02 m², B = 0.4 T, f = 60 Hz
Formula:Emax = NBAω = NBA(2πf)
ω=2π × 60 = 120π rad/s
Substitute:Emax = 50 × 0.4 × 0.02 × 120π
Calculate:= 50 × 0.4 × 0.02 × 376.99 = 50 × 0.4 × 7.54
Simplify:= 50 × 3.016 = 150.8
∴ Emax=≈ 150.8 V   (or 48π V ≈ 150.8 V)
C(iv) Draw the circuit diagram of a step-up transformer and label its parts. 2024 2 Marks
Principle:Based on mutual electromagnetic induction. AC input in primary coil induces varying EMF in secondary coil.
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ AC Input (VP) Iron Core Output (VS > VP) │
│ ~~~⊂⊃⊂⊃⊂⊃⊂⊃──[Iron Core]──⊂⊃⊂⊃⊂⊃⊂⊃⊂⊃⊂⊃ ~~~│
│ Primary Coil (NP turns) Secondary Coil (NS turns, NS > NP) │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
Step-up: NS > NP → VS > VP but IS < IP
Parts:Primary coil (NP), Secondary coil (NS > NP), Laminated iron core, Input terminals, Output terminals.
Ratio:VS/VP = NS/NP > 1  (voltage increases, current decreases)
∴ Answer:Step-up: NS > NP  ; VS/VP = NS/NP
C(v) Explain Huygens' principle of wave propagation. 2023 2 Marks
Principle:Every point on a wavefront acts as a new source of secondary wavelets that spread out in all directions with the same speed as the wave.
New wavefront:The new wavefront at a later time is the common tangent (envelope) to all the secondary wavelets.
Wavefront W₁ → points A, B, C, D each emit secondary wavelets →
Envelope of wavelets = new wavefront W₂
[Plane wavefront remains plane; spherical wavefront remains spherical]
Application:Used to explain reflection, refraction, interference, and diffraction of light.
∴ Answer:Each point on wavefront = new source of secondary wavelets. New wavefront = envelope of these wavelets.
C(vi) State the laws of photoelectric effect. 2022 2 Marks
Law 1:Photoelectric emission occurs only if the frequency of incident light is ≥ threshold frequency (ν ≥ ν₀). Below ν₀, no emission however intense the light.
Law 2:The maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons depends only on the frequency of incident light, NOT on its intensity.
Law 3:The number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the intensity of incident light.
Law 4:Photoelectric emission is instantaneous (no time delay) — emission occurs within 10⁻⁹ s of light falling on the metal.
Einstein eq.:KEmax = hν − φ  (where φ = work function)
∴ Summary:Need ν ≥ ν₀; KE depends on ν only; number ∝ intensity; instantaneous.
C(vii) Derive the radius of the nth Bohr orbit in hydrogen atom. 2024 2 Marks
Bohr's Cond.:The centripetal force on the electron = electrostatic attraction.
Force balance:mv²/r = ke²/r²  ⟹  mv² = ke²/r  …(1)
Quantisation:mvr = nh/2π  ⟹  v = nh/(2πmr)  …(2)
Substitute (2)→(1):m[nh/(2πmr)]² = ke²/r
Simplify:n²h²/(4π²mr) = ke²  ⟹  r = n²h²/(4π²mke²)
∴ rn=rn = n² × a₀   where a₀ = 0.529 Å = 0.529 × 10⁻¹⁰ m (Bohr radius)
Values:r₁ = 0.529 Å, r₂ = 4 × 0.529 = 2.116 Å, r₃ = 9 × 0.529 = 4.76 Å
C(viii) Draw circuit of half-wave rectifier and explain working. 2023 2 Marks
AC Input ~~~~ → [Transformer] → Diode (D) → RL → Output DC
During +ve half cycle: diode forward biased → conducts → current through RL
During −ve half cycle: diode reverse biased → does not conduct → no current
+ve half cycle:Diode is forward biased (p-side at +ve, n-side at −ve) → conducts → output voltage appears across RL.
−ve half cycle:Diode is reverse biased → does not conduct → no output.
Output:Pulsating DC (only positive half cycles). Ripple frequency = f (input frequency).
∴ Answer:One diode. Conducts only during +ve half cycle. Output = pulsating DC at frequency f.
C(ix) YDSE: d = 0.5 mm, D = 1 m, λ = 500 nm. Find fringe width. 2022 2 Marks
Given:d = 0.5 mm = 0.5 × 10⁻³ m  , D = 1 m  , λ = 500 nm = 500 × 10⁻⁹ m
Formula:β = λD/d   (fringe width)
Substitute:β = (500 × 10⁻⁹ × 1) / (0.5 × 10⁻³)
Calculate:= (500 × 10⁻⁹) / (5 × 10⁻⁴)
Simplify:= 100 × 10⁻⁵ = 1 × 10⁻³ m
∴ β=1 mm = 1 × 10⁻³ m
C(x) State the significance of Compton effect. 2024 2 Marks
Compton Effect:When X-rays (or high-energy photons) collide with free/loosely bound electrons, the scattered X-rays have a longer wavelength than the incident X-rays.
Shift:Δλ = λ' − λ = (h/m₀c)(1 − cosθ)   where θ = scattering angle
Significance 1:Confirms the particle nature of light — photons carry momentum p = h/λ.
Significance 2:Proves wave-particle duality of radiation experimentally.
Significance 3:Cannot be explained by wave theory; validates quantum theory of radiation.
∴ Answer:Confirms photon momentum (p = h/λ) and particle nature of EM radiation. Validates quantum theory.
C(xi) Write four differences between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion. 2023 2 Marks
Fission:Heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei + energy (e.g., U-235 + n → Ba + Kr + 3n + ~200 MeV)
Fusion:Two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus + energy (e.g., ₁H² + ₁H³ → ₂He⁴ + n + ~17.6 MeV)
Diff. 1:Fission: heavy → light  |  Fusion: light → heavy
Diff. 2:Fission: needs slow neutron trigger  |  Fusion: needs very high temperature (~10⁷ K)
Diff. 3:Fission: produces radioactive waste  |  Fusion: cleaner, less radioactive waste
Diff. 4:Fission: less energy per nucleon  |  Fusion: more energy per nucleon (more efficient)
∴ Summary:Fission = split heavy nucleus; Fusion = join light nuclei. Fusion gives more energy/nucleon but needs higher temperature.

Br ─●─   │     ← Retentivity (Br)

Hysteresis loop: O→A(saturation)→B(retentivity)→C(coercivity)→D(reverse sat)→loop back

Section B — Very Short Answer Questions (1 Mark Each) 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
B(i) Define dielectric constant of a medium. 2024 1 Mark
Definition:Dielectric constant (K or εr) is the ratio of the permittivity of the medium to the permittivity of free space (vacuum).
Formula:K = ε/ε₀   (dimensionless quantity, always ≥ 1)
Physical meaning:K indicates how much the electric force between charges is reduced when a medium is placed between them.
∴ Answer:K = ε/ε₀  ; ratio of permittivity of medium to that of free space. K ≥ 1.
B(ii) State Faraday's first law of electromagnetic induction. 2023 1 Mark
Statement:Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed circuit changes, an EMF (electromotive force) is induced in the circuit. The induced EMF lasts as long as the change in flux continues.
Formula:e = −dΦ/dt   (Faraday-Lenz law; negative sign = Lenz's law)
Note:Second law gives the magnitude: e = N·dΦ/dt for N turns. This is the basis of generators and transformers.
∴ Answer:EMF is induced whenever magnetic flux changes. e = −dΦ/dt.
B(iii) What is resonant frequency of an LCR circuit? Write its formula. 2022 1 Mark
Definition:Resonant frequency (f₀) is the frequency at which inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance (XL = XC), making impedance minimum (Z = R) and current maximum.
Formula:f₀ = 1 / (2π√LC)   where L = inductance (H), C = capacitance (F)
At resonance:XL = XC  ,  Z = R (minimum)  ,  I = V/R (maximum)  ,  cosφ = 1
∴ Answer:f₀ = 1/(2π√LC)
B(iv) Define magnifying power of a microscope. 2024 1 Mark
Definition:Magnifying power (M) of a microscope is the ratio of the angle subtended by the image at the eye to the angle subtended by the object when placed at the least distance of distinct vision (D = 25 cm).
Formula:M = mo × Me = (L/fo) × (1 + D/fe)   where L = tube length, fo = focal length of objective, fe = focal length of eyepiece.
∴ Answer:M = angle(image)/angle(object at D). M = (L/fo)(1 + D/fe)
B(v) What is spontaneous emission? 2023 1 Mark
Definition:Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom in an excited energy state returns to a lower (ground) state by emitting a photon without any external triggering.
Energy of photon:E = hν = E₂ − E₁   (difference between excited and ground state energies)
Compare:Stimulated emission = external photon triggers emission (basis of LASER). Spontaneous emission = random, no external trigger.
∴ Answer:Atom in excited state spontaneously emits photon of energy hν = E₂ − E₁ without external trigger.
B(vi) State the law of radioactive decay. Write the decay equation. 2022 1 Mark
Law:The rate of radioactive decay at any instant is directly proportional to the number of undecayed radioactive atoms present at that instant.
Equation:dN/dt = −λN  ⟹  N = N₀e−λt
Where:N₀ = initial atoms, N = atoms at time t, λ = decay constant (s⁻¹)
Half-life:T½ = 0.693/λ
∴ Answer:dN/dt ∝ −N  ; N = N₀e−λt  ; T½ = 0.693/λ
B(vii) Define mobility of electrons. State its SI unit. 2024 1 Mark
Definition:Mobility (μ) of an electron is defined as the drift velocity acquired per unit electric field applied.
Formula:μ = vd/E   where vd = drift velocity, E = electric field
Also:μ = eτ/m   where τ = relaxation time, m = electron mass
SI Unit:m²/(V·s)
B(viii) What is an intrinsic semiconductor? Give one example. 2023 1 Mark
Definition:An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure semiconductor with no intentional impurities added, whose electrical properties depend only on the material itself.
Properties:At 0 K: acts as insulator. At room temp: thermal energy breaks bonds → equal electrons and holes (n = p = ni).
Examples:Silicon (Si) and Germanium (Ge) in pure form.
B(ix) State Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL). 2022 1 Mark
Statement:In any closed loop of a circuit, the algebraic sum of all the EMFs is equal to the algebraic sum of all the products of current and resistance (voltage drops).
Formula:ΣE = ΣIR   (around any closed loop)
Basis:Based on conservation of energy — no net energy gain or loss in a closed loop.
∴ Answer:ΣE = ΣIR  ; based on energy conservation.
B(x) What is the principle of a transformer? 2024 1 Mark
Principle:A transformer works on the principle of mutual electromagnetic induction. When AC flows in the primary coil, it creates a changing magnetic flux which induces an EMF in the secondary coil.
Turns ratio:VS/VP = NS/NP = IP/IS
Note:Works only with AC. DC cannot change flux, so no EMF is induced with DC input.
∴ Answer:Mutual electromagnetic induction. VS/VP = NS/NP.
B(xi) Define nuclear fission. Give one example. 2023 1 Mark
Definition:Nuclear fission is the process in which a heavy nucleus (like U-235) is split into two lighter nuclei of comparable masses, with the release of a large amount of energy and 2–3 neutrons.
Example:₉₂U²³⁵ + ₀n¹ → ₅₆Ba¹⁴¹ + ₃₆Kr⁹² + 3₀n¹ + ~200 MeV
Chain reaction:The released neutrons cause further fissions → chain reaction → basis of nuclear reactor and atom bomb.
∴ Answer:Heavy nucleus splits into lighter nuclei + neutrons + large energy. Example: U-235 + n → Ba + Kr + 3n + 200 MeV.
B(xii) What is a wavefront? State types of wavefronts. 2022 1 Mark
Definition:A wavefront is the locus of all points in a medium that are in the same phase of vibration at a given instant of time.
Types: (1) Spherical wavefront — from a point source in 3D (like a candle flame).
(2) Cylindrical wavefront — from a line source (like a fluorescent tube).
(3) Plane wavefront — from a distant (effectively infinite) source.
∴ Answer:Locus of points in same phase. Types: spherical, cylindrical, plane.

C(i) Derive the expression for electric potential due to a point charge. 2024 2 Marks
Definition:Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to that point.
Setup:Point charge +Q at origin. Find V at point P at distance r.
Small work:dW = −F·dx = −(kQ/x²)·dx   (against the field, for +ve test charge)
Total work:V = W/q₀ = ∫∞r (−kQ/x²)dx = [kQ/x]∞r = kQ/r − 0
∴ V=kQ/r = Q/(4πε₀r)
Note:Positive for +ve charge (potential increases near it); negative for −ve charge.
C(ii) Three resistors in parallel — derive expression for equivalent resistance. 2023 2 Marks
Setup:R₁, R₂, R₃ connected in parallel across voltage V.
Same voltage:Voltage across each = V. Currents: I₁ = V/R₁, I₂ = V/R₂, I₃ = V/R₃
Total current:I = I₁ + I₂ + I₃   (KCL)
Substitute:V/Req = V/R₁ + V/R₂ + V/R₃
Divide by V:1/Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
∴ Formula:1/Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃   (Req < smallest R)

C(vi) Explain pair production and annihilation of matter. 2022 2 Marks
Pair Production:A high-energy photon (γ-ray, E ≥ 1.02 MeV) spontaneously converts into an electron-positron pair in the presence of a heavy nucleus.
Equation:γ → e⁻ + e⁺   (energy → mass, E = 2m₀c² = 1.02 MeV minimum)
Annihilation:When an electron meets a positron, they annihilate each other and produce two γ-ray photons of equal energy moving in opposite directions.
Equation:e⁻ + e⁺ → γ + γ   (each photon has energy 0.511 MeV)
∴ Summary:Pair production: energy→mass (γ→e⁻+e⁺). Annihilation: mass→energy (e⁻+e⁺→2γ). Both obey E = mc².

C(x) State and explain Ampere's circuital law. 2024 2 Marks
Statement:The line integral of magnetic field B around any closed loop (Amperian loop) is equal to μ₀ times the total current enclosed by the loop.
Formula:∮ B·dl = μ₀Ienclosed
Application 1:Magnetic field inside a long solenoid: B = μ₀nI (n = turns per meter)
Application 2:Magnetic field due to long straight wire: B = μ₀I/(2πr)
Note:Maxwell later modified this to include displacement current: ∮B·dl = μ₀(I + ε₀dΦE/dt)
∴ Answer:∮B·dl = μ₀Ienc  ; used to find B for symmetric current configurations.
C(xi) Derive the expression for magnetic field inside a long solenoid. 2023 2 Marks
Setup:Long solenoid: n turns per unit length, current I. Take a rectangular Amperian loop abcd of length L.
[Solenoid]
a────────b (inside, B uniform along ab)
│        │
d────────c (outside, B = 0)
Amperian loop abcd. Length ab = cd = L
Apply Ampere's law:∮B·dl = μ₀Ienc
Evaluate:B·L (along ab) + 0 (along bc, da — B ⊥ dl) + 0 (along cd — B = 0 outside) = μ₀(nL)I
Simplify:BL = μ₀nLI
∴ B=B = μ₀nI   (uniform inside, zero outside for ideal solenoid)
C(xii) What is the photoelectric effect? State the experimental observations. 2022 2 Marks
Definition:Photoelectric effect is the phenomenon of emission of electrons from the surface of a metal when light of suitable frequency falls on it. Discovered by Hertz (1887); explained by Einstein (1905).
Observation 1:Emission occurs only if incident frequency ν ≥ threshold frequency ν₀ (characteristic of metal).
Observation 2:KEmax of emitted electrons increases linearly with frequency, NOT with intensity.
Observation 3:Number of electrons emitted per second ∝ intensity of light.
Observation 4:Emission is instantaneous (no time lag), even at low intensities.
Einstein eq.:KEmax = hν − φ   (h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)
∴ Summary:Needs ν ≥ ν₀; KE ∝ ν; count ∝ intensity; instantaneous. Explained by Einstein's photon theory.
