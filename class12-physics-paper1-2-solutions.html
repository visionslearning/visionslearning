B·iDefine electric flux. 20241 Mark
Definition:Electric flux (ΦE) through a surface is defined as the total number of electric field lines passing normally (perpendicularly) through that surface.
Formula:ΦE = E · A · cosθ   where E = electric field (N/C), A = area of surface (m²), θ = angle between the electric field vector and the outward normal to the surface.
When θ = 0°:Φ = EA (maximum) — field lines pass straight through, fully perpendicular to surface.
When θ = 90°:Φ = 0 (minimum) — field lines are parallel to surface, none pass through it.
SI Unit:N·m²/C or equivalently V·m (since 1 V = 1 N·m/C)
Sign:Flux is positive when field lines leave the surface (outward); negative when field lines enter the surface (inward).
💡 Gauss's Law connection: Total electric flux through a closed surface = Qenc/ε₀. This makes electric flux extremely important in solving electrostatics problems.
Memory tip:Think of flux as "how much field goes through" — like counting the number of arrows piercing a window.
∴ Answer:ΦE = EA cosθ  ; Unit = N·m²/C  ; maximum when θ=0°, zero when θ=90°.
B·iiState Ohm's law. 20231 Mark
Statement:At constant temperature, the electric current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) applied across its ends.
Formula:V = I × R   where R = resistance of the conductor (constant at fixed temperature). Also written as I = V/R.
Graph:V vs I graph is a straight line through the origin (slope = R). A material obeying this is called an ohmic conductor.
Unit of R:Ohm (Ω). 1 Ω = 1 V/A (when 1 V produces 1 A of current).
Limitations:Ohm's law is NOT obeyed by: semiconductors (diodes, transistors), electrolytes, discharge tubes, thyristors — these are non-ohmic devices.
💡 Microscopic form: J = σE, where J = current density (A/m²), σ = conductivity, E = electric field. This is the more fundamental form of Ohm's law.
Exam tip:Always write "at constant temperature" — without this condition Ohm's law may not hold even for metals.
∴ Answer:V ∝ I at constant temperature. V = IR. Valid for ohmic conductors only.
B·iiiDefine magnetic flux and state its SI unit. 20221 Mark
Definition:Magnetic flux (ΦB) through a surface is the total number of magnetic field lines passing normally through that surface. It is a measure of the total magnetic field passing through a given area.
Formula:ΦB = B · A · cosθ   where B = magnetic field (T), A = area (m²), θ = angle between B and the area vector (outward normal).
Vector form:ΦB = B · A = BA cosθ (dot product of B and area vector)
SI Unit:Weber (Wb).   1 Wb = 1 T·m² = 1 V·s
CGS Unit:Maxwell (Mx). 1 Wb = 10⁸ Mx
💡 Faraday's law connection: Induced EMF = −dΦB/dt. The rate of change of magnetic flux determines the induced EMF. This is the foundation of all electrical generators.
Important:For a closed surface, total magnetic flux is always zero (∮B·dA = 0) because magnetic monopoles do not exist — field lines always form closed loops.
∴ Answer:ΦB = BAcosθ  ; SI unit = Weber (Wb) = T·m²
B·ivDefine power factor of an AC circuit. 20241 Mark
Definition:Power factor is the ratio of true (active/useful) power consumed to the apparent (total) power supplied in an AC circuit. It tells us how efficiently an AC circuit uses the supplied power.
Formula:Power factor = cosφ = R/Z   where φ = phase angle between voltage V and current I, R = resistance, Z = impedance.
Also written:Ptrue = Vrms · Irms · cosφ   and   Papparent = Vrms · Irms
Range:0 ≤ cosφ ≤ 1 (dimensionless)
Pure resistor:cosφ = 1 (φ = 0°) → all power consumed as heat. Best case.
Pure L or C:cosφ = 0 (φ = 90°) → no power consumed (power just stored and returned). Wattless current.
At resonance:cosφ = 1 (Z = R, XL = XC) → maximum power transfer.
💡 Practical importance: Power companies penalise industries with low power factor (e.g., 0.6) because they draw more current than needed. Capacitor banks are added to correct power factor to ≈1.
∴ Answer:Power factor = cosφ = R/Z = True power / Apparent power. Range 0 to 1.
B·vWhat is total internal reflection (TIR)? 20231 Mark
Definition:Total Internal Reflection (TIR) is the optical phenomenon where light traveling from a denser medium to a rarer medium is completely reflected back into the denser medium when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle. No refracted ray exists.
Condition 1:Light must travel from denser → rarer medium (e.g., glass → air, water → air).
Condition 2:Angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle: i > ic
Denser medium (glass)
─────────────────────────────
i<ic → refracted ray exits  |  i=ic → refracted at 90°  |  i>ic → TIR (no refracted ray)
Rarer medium (air)
At i = ic: refracted ray grazes the surface (angle of refraction = 90°)
Critical angle:sin ic = nrarer/ndenser = 1/n (for glass-air interface, sin ic = 1/1.5 → ic ≈ 42°)
Applications:1. Optical fibres (communication, endoscopy)   2. Diamond brilliance   3. Mirage formation   4. Periscope prisms   5. Totally reflecting prisms (binoculars)
💡 Optical fibre: Light is trapped inside the glass core by TIR and travels thousands of km without loss. The core has higher refractive index than the cladding.
∴ Answer:Complete reflection of light at denser-rarer boundary when i > ic. Conditions: denser→rarer medium AND i > ic.
B·viDefine work function of a metal. 20221 Mark
Definition:Work function (φ) of a metal is the minimum amount of energy required to completely remove one electron from the surface of the metal against the attractive forces of the positive ions in the lattice.
Why minimum?:Electrons at the surface need the least energy to escape (electrons deeper inside need more energy). Work function is for surface electrons.
Formula:φ = h · ν0   where h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, ν0 = threshold frequency
Unit:Joule (J) in SI  ; in practice measured in electron-volt (eV). 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
Values:Caesium (Cs): ~2.0 eV (lowest) — used in photoelectric cells  |  Tungsten (W): ~4.5 eV  |  Platinum (Pt): ~5.7 eV (highest common metal)
Einstein eq.:KEmax = hν − φ → "energy of photon = work function + kinetic energy of electron"
💡 Physical picture: Imagine electrons trapped in a potential well. Work function = depth of the well. Only photons with E ≥ φ can kick the electron out.
∴ Answer:Minimum energy to eject an electron from metal surface. φ = hν0. Unit: eV or J. Depends on the metal.
B·viiWhat is a rectifier? 20241 Mark
Definition:A rectifier is an electronic circuit (using one or more p-n junction diodes) that converts alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC) by allowing current to flow in only one direction.
Principle:A p-n junction diode conducts in forward bias (low resistance, current flows) and blocks in reverse bias (high resistance, no current). This one-way property is used for rectification.
Type 1:Half-wave rectifier — uses one diode. Only positive half cycles pass. Ripple frequency = f (input). Efficiency = 40.6%.
Type 2:Full-wave rectifier — uses two diodes (centre-tap) or four diodes (bridge). Both half cycles pass. Ripple frequency = 2f. Efficiency = 81.2%.
AC Input ~~~~ → [Transformer] → [Diode D] → [Load RL] → DC Output (pulsating)
Half-wave: only ⊕ half cycles appear at output
Full-wave bridge: both ⊕ and ⊖ half cycles converted to ⊕
DC output is smoothed using a capacitor filter to reduce ripple
Filter:A capacitor (C filter) is connected in parallel with RL to smooth the pulsating DC into steady DC.
∴ Answer:Device that converts AC → DC using p-n junction diode. Types: half-wave (1 diode) and full-wave (2 or 4 diodes).
B·viiiState De Morgan's second theorem. 20231 Mark
Statement:The complement of the OR of two Boolean variables equals the AND of their individual complements.
Formula:(A + B)' = A' · B'   ← De Morgan's Second Theorem
In words:NOT (A OR B) = (NOT A) AND (NOT B)
Truth table proof: A=0,B=0: (0+0)'=1  ; 0'·0'=1·1=1 ✓
A=0,B=1: (0+1)'=0  ; 0'·1'=1·0=0 ✓
A=1,B=0: (1+0)'=0  ; 1'·0'=0·1=0 ✓
A=1,B=1: (1+1)'=0  ; 1'·1'=0·0=0 ✓
First theorem:(A · B)' = A' + B'   ← For comparison (NOT of AND = OR of NOTs)
💡 Practical use: De Morgan's theorems allow us to build any logic gate using only NAND gates or only NOR gates (universal gates). NOR gate is equivalent to (A+B)' = A'·B'.
Memory trick:"Break the bar, change the operator" → (A+B)' break the long bar over A+B → A'·B' (+ changed to ·)
∴ Answer:(A + B)' = A' · B'   [Second Theorem]  |  (A · B)' = A' + B'   [First Theorem]
B·ixDefine nuclear binding energy. 20221 Mark
Definition:Nuclear binding energy (BE) is the minimum energy required to completely separate all the nucleons (protons and neutrons) of a nucleus from one another to an infinite distance apart.
Equivalently:It is the energy released when free protons and neutrons combine to form a nucleus. Higher binding energy = more stable nucleus.
Mass defect:Δm = (Z·mp + N·mn) − Mnucleus   where Z = proton number, N = neutron number, M = actual nuclear mass
Formula:BE = Δm × c²   (Einstein's mass-energy relation, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
In MeV:BE = Δm × 931.5 MeV/u   (1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 931.5 MeV/c²)
BE per nucleon:BE/A = binding energy per nucleon. Maximum ≈ 8.8 MeV at Fe-56 (most stable nucleus in the universe).
💡 Why does nucleus stay together? The strong nuclear force between nucleons is much stronger than the electrostatic repulsion between protons. Binding energy is the "cement" that holds the nucleus together.
∴ Answer:Energy to separate all nucleons. BE = Δm·c². More BE = more stable. Max BE/A at Fe-56.
B·xWhat is critical angle? On what factors does it depend? 20241 Mark
Definition:Critical angle (ic) is the specific angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is exactly 90° (refracted ray grazes the interface).
Physical meaning:At i = ic: refracted ray just grazes the surface. For i > ic: Total Internal Reflection occurs (no refracted ray).
Formula:sin ic = nrarer/ndenser = 1/n   (derived from Snell's law with r = 90°)
Example:Glass (n=1.5) to Air (n=1): sin ic = 1/1.5 = 2/3 → ic ≈ 41.8° ≈ 42°
Depends on 1:Refractive indices of both media — higher the refractive index of denser medium, smaller the critical angle.
Depends on 2:Wavelength (colour) of light — since n varies with λ (dispersion), ic also varies. Violet light (smaller λ, larger n) has smaller ic than red light.
Depends on 3:Temperature — refractive index changes slightly with temperature, so ic also changes slightly.
∴ Answer:sin ic = 1/n. Depends on: refractive indices of media, wavelength of light, temperature.
B·xiWhat is a photon? State its properties. 20231 Mark
Definition:A photon is the fundamental quantum (smallest discrete packet) of electromagnetic energy. It is a massless, chargeless particle that carries electromagnetic radiation. Proposed by Einstein (1905) to explain photoelectric effect.
Property 1:Energy: E = hν = hc/λ   (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s, ν = frequency, λ = wavelength)
Property 2:Rest mass: m₀ = 0 (but has relativistic energy and momentum)
Property 3:Speed: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum (always — never slows down or speeds up in vacuum)
Property 4:Momentum: p = h/λ = E/c = hν/c
Property 5:Charge: Zero — photons are electrically neutral.
Property 6:Wave-particle duality: Acts as a wave (interference, diffraction) and as a particle (photoelectric effect, Compton effect).
💡 Visible light photon energy: For λ = 500 nm (green), E = hc/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸)/(500×10⁻⁹) ≈ 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 2.5 eV. Very tiny energy!
∴ Answer:Quantum of EM energy. E = hν; p = h/λ; m₀ = 0; charge = 0; speed = c; shows wave-particle duality.
B·xiiState Coulomb's law of electrostatics. 20221 Mark
Statement:The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The force acts along the line joining the two charges.
Formula:F = k·q₁·q₂/r²   where k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² (Coulomb's constant)
In vector form:F = (kq₁q₂/r²) r̂   (r̂ = unit vector from q₁ to q₂; positive = repulsive, negative = attractive)
Direction:Like charges (++ or −−): repulsive (force pushes apart). Unlike charges (+−): attractive (force pulls together).
In medium:F = kq₁q₂/(Kr²) where K = dielectric constant. Medium reduces the force by factor K.
Inverse sq. law:Double the distance → force becomes 1/4th. Triple → 1/9th. Very similar to Newton's gravitational law.
💡 Comparison with gravity: Both are inverse square laws. But Coulomb force can be attractive OR repulsive; gravity is always attractive. Coulomb force is ~10³⁶ times stronger than gravity between proton and electron!
∴ Answer:F = kq₁q₂/r². Inverse square law. Acts along line joining charges. Like charges repel, unlike attract.

C·iDerive the expression for energy stored in a capacitor. 20242 Marks
Concept:When a capacitor is charged, work is done to push charge against the existing electric field. This work is stored as electrostatic potential energy in the capacitor (between the plates).
Setup:Capacitor C being charged from 0 to Q. At any instant, charge = q → voltage = q/C.
Small work dW:To add a small charge dq against voltage q/C:   dW = V · dq = (q/C) · dq
Total work W:W = ∫₀Q (q/C) dq = (1/C) · [q²/2]₀Q = Q²/(2C)
Substitute Q=CV:W = (CV)²/(2C) = ½CV²   Also: W = ½QV
∴ Energy U:U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/(2C)   (all three forms are equivalent)
Energy density:u = U/(volume) = ½ε₀E² J/m³ — energy per unit volume stored in electric field between plates.
💡 Numerical: C = 10 μF, V = 100 V → U = ½ × 10×10⁻⁶ × 100² = ½ × 10⁻⁵ × 10⁴ = 0.05 J = 50 mJ. This is stored in the electric field, not in the plates.
Exam note:Write all three forms: U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/2C. Also state that energy is stored in the electric field between the plates.
C·iiExplain Kirchhoff's current law (KCL) with a diagram. 20232 Marks
KCL Statement:At any node (junction) in an electrical circuit, the algebraic sum of all currents is zero. Equivalently: the total current entering a node equals the total current leaving it at every instant.
Formula:ΣIin = ΣIout   or   ΣI = 0 (taking inward as +ve, outward as −ve)
     I₁=3A → ●            ← I₂=2A
              (Node J)
          I₃=?↓
KCL: I₁ + I₂ = I₃ → 3 + 2 = 5A (leaving node)
Total entering (I₁+I₂=5A) = Total leaving (I₃=5A)
Physical basis:Based on conservation of electric charge — charge cannot accumulate at a node. Whatever charge enters must leave immediately.
Sign convention:Currents flowing into the node: taken as positive (+). Currents flowing out of the node: taken as negative (−).
Application:Used with KVL to solve complex circuit networks (mesh analysis, nodal analysis). Essential for circuit analysis.
💡 Water analogy: At a pipe junction, the total flow of water in = total flow of water out. Similarly, current (charge flow) cannot disappear at a node.
∴ Answer:ΣIin = ΣIout at every node. Based on charge conservation. Draw diagram with currents labeled.

C·vExplain Huygens' principle of wave propagation. 20232 Marks
Huygens' Principle:Every point on a given wavefront acts as a new independent source of secondary spherical wavelets, which spread out in all directions with the same speed as the original wave. The new wavefront at a later time is the common tangent (envelope) to all these secondary wavelets.
Plane wavefront W₁ at time t=0:
A •───────────────────── • B
    ↓secondary wavelets↓
A'•───────────────────── •B'
New wavefront W₂ at time t=Δt (envelope of wavelets)
Each point A, B emits a spherical wavelet of radius cΔt. Tangent to all wavelets = W₂.
Forward envelope:Only the forward envelope is taken (backward wave is not observed due to superposition — it cancels out).
Wavefront types:Point source → spherical wavefront. Line source → cylindrical. Very distant source → plane wavefront.
Applications:1. Explains reflection of light   2. Explains refraction (Snell's law derivation)   3. Explains diffraction   4. Explains interference
💡 Why does light travel in straight lines? For plane waves, all secondary wavelets have the same radius, and their envelope is another plane — so wavefront moves straight forward. This explains rectilinear propagation of light.
∴ Answer:Every wavefront point = secondary source. New wavefront = envelope of secondary wavelets. Explains reflection, refraction, diffraction.
C·viState the laws of photoelectric effect. 20222 Marks
Definition:Photoelectric effect: Emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of suitable frequency falls on it. Discovered by Hertz (1887), explained by Einstein (1905) — Nobel Prize 1921.
Law 1 — Threshold:Photoelectric emission occurs only if the frequency of incident light ν ≥ threshold frequency ν₀ (characteristic of metal). Below ν₀ → no emission, however intense the light.
Law 2 — KE vs ν:The maximum kinetic energy (KEmax) of the emitted electrons depends only on the frequency of incident light, NOT on intensity. KEmax increases linearly with ν.
Law 3 — Intensity:The number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the intensity of incident light (for ν ≥ ν₀).
Law 4 — Instantaneous:Photoelectric emission is instantaneous — it occurs within 10⁻⁹ seconds of light falling on metal, with no time lag, even at very low intensities.
Einstein's equation:KEmax = hν − φ = hν − hν₀ = h(ν − ν₀)
Stopping potential:eV₀ = KEmax → V₀ = KEmax/e   (minimum retarding voltage to stop all photoelectrons)
💡 Why wave theory fails: Wave theory predicts (1) any frequency should work at sufficient intensity — WRONG; (2) time delay for energy accumulation — WRONG. Only quantum (photon) theory explains all 4 laws.
∴ Answer:4 laws: (1) ν≥ν₀ needed (2) KE depends only on ν (3) count ∝ intensity (4) instantaneous. Einstein: KEmax = hν − φ.
C·viiDerive the radius of the nth Bohr orbit in hydrogen atom. 20242 Marks
Bohr's model:Electrons revolve in fixed circular orbits without radiating energy. Centripetal force = Electrostatic attraction between electron and proton.
Force balance:mv²/r = ke²/r²  ⟹  mv²r = ke²  …(1)  [k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C², e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C]
Quantisation:Angular momentum: mvr = nh/(2π) = nℏ  …(2)  [n = 1, 2, 3, ... (principal quantum number)]
From (2):v = nh/(2πmr)  …(3)
Substitute (3) in (1):m × [nh/(2πmr)]² / r = ke²/r²  ⟹  m × n²h²/(4π²m²r²) = ke²/r
Simplify:n²h²/(4π²mr) = ke²  ⟹  r = n²h²/(4π²mke²)
∴ rn=rn = n² × a₀   where a₀ = h²/(4π²mke²) = 0.529 Å = 0.529 × 10⁻¹⁰ m (Bohr radius)
Values:r₁ = 0.529 Å  |  r₂ = 4 × 0.529 = 2.116 Å  |  r₃ = 9 × 0.529 = 4.76 Å  |  rn ∝ n²
💡 Radius increases as n² — orbit spacing gets larger as we go further from nucleus. This is why atoms mostly empty space!
Write clearly:State both Bohr's postulates (equations 1 and 2), eliminate v, solve for r. Final answer: rn = n²a₀.

C·ixYDSE: d=0.5mm, D=1m, λ=500nm. Find fringe width. 20222 Marks
Given:d = 0.5 mm = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m  |  D = 1 m  |  λ = 500 nm = 5 × 10⁻⁷ m

C·xState the significance of Compton effect. 20242 Marks
Compton Effect:When X-rays (or high energy photons) collide with free electrons, the scattered X-rays have a longer wavelength (lower energy/frequency) than the incident X-rays. Discovered by Arthur Compton (1923) — Nobel Prize 1927.
Compton shift:Δλ = λ' − λ = (h/m₀c)(1 − cosθ) where θ = angle of scattering, h/m₀c = 0.0243 Å = Compton wavelength
When θ=0°:Δλ = 0 (no scattering, photon continues forward — no change in wavelength)
When θ=90°:Δλ = h/m₀c = 0.0243 Å
When θ=180°:Δλ = 2h/m₀c = 0.0486 Å (maximum, photon bounces back)
Significance 1:Confirms particle nature of light — photons carry momentum p = h/λ. This is a direct experimental proof.
Significance 2:Wave theory fails to explain it — scattered wave should have same frequency as incident wave (by classical theory). Change in λ ONLY possible if photon acts as particle.
Significance 3:Validates de Broglie's hypothesis (wave-particle duality) and supports quantum mechanics.
Significance 4:Showed that photon-electron collision obeys conservation of energy AND momentum — same as particle collision.
∴ Answer:Confirms photon momentum p=h/λ, particle nature of light, wave-particle duality. Wave theory cannot explain it. Δλ = (h/m₀c)(1−cosθ).
C·xiWrite four differences between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion. 20232 Marks
Fission:A heavy nucleus (like U-235) splits into two lighter nuclei of comparable mass, releasing 2-3 neutrons + energy ~200 MeV. Triggered by a slow (thermal) neutron.
Fusion:Two light nuclei (like H-2, H-3) combine to form a heavier nucleus + energy ~17.6 MeV per reaction. Requires extremely high temperature (~10⁷ K) to overcome Coulomb barrier.
Difference 1:Type: Fission = splitting heavy → light  |  Fusion = joining light → heavy
Difference 2:Trigger: Fission needs slow neutron (easily achievable)  |  Fusion needs ~10⁷–10⁸ K temperature (very difficult — sun-like conditions)
Difference 3:Waste: Fission produces radioactive products (dangerous, long half-life)  |  Fusion produces mostly helium + neutrons (much cleaner)
Difference 4:Energy per nucleon: Fission ≈ 0.85 MeV/nucleon  |  Fusion ≈ 6 MeV/nucleon (fusion gives ~7× more energy per unit mass)
Difference 5:Control: Fission: chain reaction can be controlled (nuclear reactor)  |  Fusion: extremely difficult to control (ITER project still ongoing)
💡 The Sun runs on fusion: 4H → He + 2e⁺ + 2ν + 26.7 MeV. The sun converts ~4 million tonnes of mass to energy per second via fusion!
∴ Answer:Any 4 of the above 5 differences. State fission reaction (U-235) and fusion reaction (H-2 + H-3) for full marks.

B·iDefine dielectric constant of a medium. 20241 Mark
Definition:Dielectric constant (K), also called relative permittivity (εr), is the ratio of the permittivity of the medium (ε) to the permittivity of free space (ε₀). It tells us how much a medium reduces the electric force between charges compared to vacuum.
Formula:K = ε / ε₀   (dimensionless quantity)
Physical meaning:In a medium with K, the Coulomb force becomes: F = kq₁q₂/(Kr²) → force reduced by factor K compared to vacuum.
Range:K ≥ 1 always. K = 1 for vacuum. K > 1 for all real materials.
Values:Air: K ≈ 1.0006 ≈ 1  |  Water: K ≈ 80  |  Glass: K ≈ 5–10  |  Mica: K ≈ 6  |  Barium titanate: K ≈ 10,000
Capacitor effect:Inserting a dielectric (K > 1) between capacitor plates: C increases to KC → stores more charge at same voltage.
💡 Why K > 1? When placed in an electric field, dielectric molecules polarise (dipoles align). Their internal field partially cancels the applied field, reducing effective E — so more charge/voltage can be sustained.
∴ Answer:K = ε/ε₀. Dimensionless. Always ≥ 1. Higher K = better dielectric for capacitors.
B·iiState Faraday's first law of electromagnetic induction. 20231 Mark
Statement:Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed conducting circuit changes, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced in the circuit. The induced EMF exists only as long as the change in flux continues. When the flux stops changing, the induced EMF becomes zero.
Mathematical form:e = −dΦB/dt   (negative sign from Lenz's law — opposes the cause)
For N turns:e = −N · dΦB/dt   (flux linkage = NΦ)
Ways to change flux:(1) Change B (move a magnet)   (2) Change area A (expand/contract coil)   (3) Change angle θ (rotate coil)   (4) Any combination of above.
Second law:Magnitude of induced EMF = rate of change of magnetic flux: |e| = dΦ/dt (2nd law gives magnitude; 1st law says EMF exists).
💡 Foundation of generators: Faraday's law is why rotating a coil in a magnetic field generates electricity (AC generator). Changing B with time creates EMF in a loop (transformer). All of modern power generation is based on this law.
∴ Answer:EMF induced whenever flux changes. e = −dΦ/dt. EMF lasts only while flux is changing.
B·iiiWhat is the resonant frequency of an LCR circuit? Write its formula. 20221 Mark
Definition:Resonant frequency (f₀) is the specific frequency at which the inductive reactance equals the capacitive reactance (XL = XC). At resonance: impedance is minimum (Z = R), current is maximum, and phase difference = 0°.
Formula:f₀ = 1 / (2π√LC)   or   ω₀ = 1/√LC   (L in Henry, C in Farad)
Derivation idea:At resonance: XL = XC  ⟹  ω₀L = 1/(ω₀C)  ⟹  ω₀² = 1/LC  ⟹  ω₀ = 1/√LC  ⟹  f₀ = ω₀/2π = 1/(2π√LC)
At resonance:Z = R (min), I = V/R (max), cosφ = 1, power factor = 1 (maximum power transfer)
Quality factor Q:Q = ω₀L/R = 1/(ω₀CR) = (1/R)√(L/C). Higher Q = sharper resonance = more selective circuit.
💡 Radio tuning: When you tune a radio, you adjust C (variable capacitor) to make f₀ = f(broadcast station). At resonance, that station's signal causes maximum current → loudest reception.
∴ Answer:f₀ = 1/(2π√LC). At resonance: XL = XC, Z = R (min), I = max, cosφ = 1.

B·vWhat is spontaneous emission? 20231 Mark
Definition:Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom in a higher energy state (excited state E₂) returns to a lower energy state (E₁) by emitting a photon spontaneously, without any external trigger, after an average lifetime of ~10⁻⁸ s.
Energy of photon:E = hν = E₂ − E₁
Characteristics:(1) Occurs randomly — cannot predict exactly when an atom will emit   (2) Emitted photons have random direction and random phase   (3) Results in incoherent light (e.g., ordinary bulb, LED).

B·viState the law of radioactive decay. Write the decay equation. 20221 Mark
Law:The rate of radioactive decay (disintegration) of a radioactive substance at any instant is directly proportional to the number of undecayed atoms (N) present at that instant. This is a purely random, statistical process.
Differential form:dN/dt = −λN   (negative sign = N is decreasing with time)
Integrated form:N = N₀ e−λt   (exponential decay law)
Where:N₀ = initial number of atoms at t=0, N = atoms remaining at time t, λ = decay constant (s⁻¹), unique to each isotope
Half-life T½:T½ = 0.693/λ = ln2/λ   (time for half the atoms to decay)
Activity A:A = |dN/dt| = λN = λN₀e−λt. Unit: Becquerel (Bq) = 1 disintegration/second. Old unit: Curie (Ci) = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq.
💡 Example: C-14 (T½ = 5730 years) is used for radiocarbon dating of ancient organic materials. U-238 (T½ = 4.5×10⁹ years) is used to date rocks and Earth's age.
∴ Answer:dN/dt = −λN  ⟹  N = N₀e−λt. Half-life T½ = 0.693/λ.
B·viiDefine mobility of electrons. State its SI unit. 20241 Mark

Formula 1:μ = vd / E   where vd = drift velocity (m/s), E = electric field (V/m)
Formula 2:μ = eτ / m   where e = electron charge, τ = relaxation time (mean free time between collisions), m = electron mass

∴ Answer:μ = vd/E = eτ/m. Unit: m²/(V·s). Electrons more mobile than holes in semiconductors.
B·viiiWhat is an intrinsic semiconductor? Give one example. 20231 Mark

B·ixState Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL). 20221 Mark
Statement:In any closed loop of a circuit, the algebraic sum of all EMFs is equal to the algebraic sum of all the voltage drops (products of current and resistance). Equivalently: the algebraic sum of all potential differences around any closed loop is zero.
Formula:ΣE = ΣIR   or   ΣV = 0   (around any closed loop)
Sign convention:Travel in direction of current through R: voltage drop = −IR (loss). Travel against current: voltage gain = +IR. EMF source traversed from − to +: gain = +E. From + to −: loss = −E.
Example:Loop with E = 12V, R₁ = 2Ω, R₂ = 4Ω, I = ? KVL: 12 = I(2+4) = 6I → I = 2A.
Physical basis:Based on conservation of energy. A charge going around a loop returns to the same potential — no net energy gain or loss.
💡 KCL + KVL together allow us to solve ANY circuit, no matter how complex. KCL uses charge conservation; KVL uses energy conservation. These are Kirchhoff's two fundamental circuit laws.
∴ Answer:ΣE = ΣIR in any closed loop. Based on conservation of energy. Used with KCL to solve circuit networks.
B·xWhat is the principle of a transformer? 20241 Mark
Principle:A transformer works on the principle of mutual electromagnetic induction. When an alternating current flows through the primary coil, it produces a changing magnetic flux in the laminated iron core, which induces an alternating EMF in the secondary coil.
Governing equation:VS/VP = NS/NP   (turns ratio determines voltage transformation)
Why only AC?:DC produces constant flux → dΦ/dt = 0 → no induced EMF in secondary. AC essential — changing current → changing flux → induced EMF.
Energy conservation:For ideal transformer: VPIP = VSIS  (input power = output power). If voltage steps up, current steps down proportionally.
Real transformer:Efficiency = (VSIS)/(VPIP) × 100%. Modern large transformers achieve 98–99% efficiency.
💡 Power transmission: Step-up transformer (11 kV → 220 kV) before transmission → very high V, very low I → small I²R losses in long cables. Step-down transformer at consumer end restores low voltage.
∴ Answer:Mutual electromagnetic induction. AC only. VS/VP = NS/NP. Step-up: NS>NP. Step-down: NS<NP.

B·xiiWhat is a wavefront? State types of wavefronts. 20221 Mark
Definition:A wavefront is the locus of all points in a medium that are in the same phase of vibration at a given instant. All points on a wavefront have the same phase, and the wave travels perpendicular to the wavefront.
Type 1 — Spherical:From a point source in 3D → spherical wavefronts expanding outward. E.g., a candle flame, a glowing bulb filament. As r→∞, becomes approximately plane.
Type 2 — Cylindrical:From a line source (long narrow source) → cylindrical wavefronts. E.g., a long fluorescent tube lamp, a slit illuminated by parallel light.

Point source → ○ ○ ○ (spherical wavefronts, concentric circles)
Line source → ⊂ ⊂ ⊂ (cylindrical wavefronts)
Distant src → | | | (plane wavefronts, parallel lines)
Arrow = direction of wave propagation (always perpendicular to wavefront)
💡 Key property: The wave energy travels in the direction perpendicular to the wavefront (= direction of rays). This is why plane wavefronts give parallel rays — used in searchlights and lasers.
∴ Answer:Locus of points in same phase. Types: (1) Spherical — point source (2) Cylindrical — line source (3) Plane — very distant source.

Modified form:Maxwell added displacement current: ∮B·dl = μ₀(I + ε₀ dΦE/dt) — accounts for E-field changes in capacitor gaps.
💡 Comparison with Gauss's law: Gauss's law: ∮E·dA = Q/ε₀ (uses closed surface). Ampere's law: ∮B·dl = μ₀I (uses closed loop). Both are among Maxwell's four equations that describe all of electromagnetism.
∴ Answer:∮B·dl = μ₀Ienc. Applied to wire: B = μ₀I/2πr. Applied to solenoid: B = μ₀nI.

Solenoid:A long solenoid is a closely wound helix of wire with n turns per metre. Inside: B is uniform and parallel to axis. Outside: B ≈ 0 (for ideal infinite solenoid).

Rectangular Amperian loop abcda. ab = cd = L (length). bc, da are perpendicular to B.
Apply Ampere's law:∮B·dl = μ₀Ienclosed
Along ab (inside):B·L   (B parallel to dl, length = L)
Along bc and da:0   (B ⊥ dl, so B·dl = 0)
Along cd (outside):0   (B = 0 outside ideal solenoid)
Total ∮B·dl:= BL + 0 + 0 + 0 = BL
Enclosed current:Number of turns in length L = nL. Each turn carries current I. Ienc = nLI
Equate:BL = μ₀(nLI)  ⟹  B = μ₀nI

n = N/L:If total turns = N and length = L, then n = N/L → B = μ₀NI/L.
💡 Core material: With iron core (μr ≈ 1000): B = μ₀μrnI ≈ 1000 times stronger. Used in electromagnets.
C·xiiWhat is photoelectric effect? State the experimental observations. 20222 Marks
Definition:Photoelectric effect is the phenomenon of emission of electrons from the surface of a metal when electromagnetic radiation (light, UV, X-rays) of sufficiently high frequency falls on it. Discovered by Heinrich Hertz (1887). Explained by Albert Einstein (1905).
Observation 1:Threshold frequency ν₀: Emission occurs only if ν ≥ ν₀. If ν < ν₀, no emission regardless of how intense or long-lasting the light is.
Observation 2:KE depends on frequency: Maximum kinetic energy (KEmax = eV₀) of emitted electrons increases linearly with frequency, completely independent of intensity.
Observation 3:Current ∝ intensity: For ν ≥ ν₀, the number of photoelectrons per second (hence the photoelectric current) is directly proportional to the intensity of incident light.
Observation 4:Instantaneous: Emission is immediate (within ~10⁻⁹ s) even at extremely low light intensities — no time delay for energy accumulation.
Why wave theory fails:Wave theory predicts: (1) any frequency works if intensity is high enough — WRONG; (2) time delay for energy accumulation — WRONG; (3) KE should increase with intensity — WRONG.
Einstein's explanation:Light consists of photons, each with energy E = hν. One photon knocks out one electron: KEmax = hν − φ = h(ν − ν₀)
Light hν → [Metal surface] → Photoelectron (KE = hν − φ)
Stopping potential V₀: eV₀ = KEmax → V₀ = (hν − φ)/e
KEmax vs ν graph: straight line with slope h and x-intercept at ν₀
💡 Applications: Solar cells (photovoltaic), photodiodes, CCD cameras, night vision devices, burglar alarms, automatic door openers — all use the photoelectric effect.
∴ Answer:4 observations: (1) ν≥ν₀ needed (2) KE ∝ ν, not intensity (3) current ∝ intensity (4) instantaneous. Einstein: KEmax = hν − φ.
