Class 11 Geography · Chapter 2

⛰️ Weathering and Mass Wasting

Notes · Key Points · Important Q&A · Maharashtra Board

⭐ Key Points at a Glance

  • Weathering = breaking/disintegration of rocks in situ (no transportation) by climate, water, heat, organisms
  • Mass wasting = downslope movement of weathered material under gravity
  • Types of weathering: Physical (Mechanical), Chemical, Biological
  • Physical weathering: Freeze-thaw, Block disintegration, Granular disintegration, Exfoliation (onion weathering), Shattering
  • Chemical weathering: Solution, Carbonation, Oxidation, Hydration, Salt weathering
  • Biological weathering: Plant roots, burrowing animals, lichens, humans
  • Factors affecting weathering: Climate (temperature + rainfall), Rock type, Slope, Time, Vegetation
  • Mass wasting types: Fall, Creep, Slide (Landslide), Flow, Solifluction, Debris avalanche
  • Factors affecting mass wasting: Gravity, Slope, Water/rainfall, Climate, Rock type, Vegetation
  • Carbonation: CO₂ + water → carbonic acid → dissolves limestone → karst topography
  • Oxidation: Rocks react with oxygen → rust (limonite/hematite) → colour change + disintegration
  • Solifluction: Flow of waterlogged soil down gentle slopes in cold regions (periglacial)
  • Deccan Trap (Maharashtra): Basalt rock → physical weathering → laterite soil formation
  • Konkan region: High rainfall → chemical weathering + mass wasting (landslides) common
1

Rock Types & How Rocks Break — Introduction

Three types of rocks:

Rock TypeHow FormedExample
IgneousFrom lava/magma cooling and solidifying; FIRST rocks formed on EarthBasalt, Granite
SedimentarySediments deposited in layers under immense pressure + cementing materialSandstone, Limestone, Shale
MetamorphicIgneous or sedimentary rocks subjected to intense heat and pressureMarble (from limestone), Quartzite (from sandstone)
Weathering: The process of disintegration and decomposition of rocks in situ (at the same place, without transportation) due to climate, water, pressure, heat, and organisms. Rocks with joints or layers break more easily.
2

Physical (Mechanical) Weathering

Disintegration of rocks into smaller pieces without changing their chemical composition. Caused by temperature, pressure, water (as ice).

TypeProcessWhere Common
Freeze-Thaw (Frost action)Water enters rock cracks → freezes → expands (9% more volume) → cracks widen → rocks split. Repeated freezing and thawing breaks rocksHigh altitude, polar and sub-polar regions; mountains
Block DisintegrationRock with joints/cracks breaks into large rectangular blocks due to temperature changes. Expansion (day) and contraction (night) stresses the rock along jointsDeserts, dry regions with extreme temperature range
Granular DisintegrationIndividual mineral grains of rock separate due to different rates of expansion and contraction of different minerals with temperature. Rock crumbles into grainsDeserts; rocks with coarse mineral grains (granite)
Exfoliation (Onion weathering)Outer layers of rock peel off like onion skins due to repeated heating and cooling. Surface expands more than interior → concentric shells break offHot deserts, tropical regions; dome-shaped rocks
ShatteringRocks shatter into angular fragments due to sudden change in temperature or pressure (e.g., forest fires). Also by lightning strike.Areas with sudden temperature changes
3

Chemical Weathering

Decomposition of rocks by chemical reactions that change their mineral composition. Aided by water, oxygen, CO₂ and acids. Most effective in warm, humid regions.

TypeProcessExample/Result
SolutionSoluble minerals in rocks dissolve in water. Rock disintegrates.Rock salt, gypsum dissolve in water
CarbonationCO₂ + water → carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) → reacts with calcium carbonate (limestone) → calcium bicarbonate (soluble) → rock dissolvesLimestone caves, stalactites, stalagmites; Karst topography
OxidationIron-bearing minerals react with oxygen → iron oxide (rust) forms. Changes colour of rock (yellow, red, brown). Weakens rock structure.Formation of limonite and hematite; red laterite soils
HydrationMinerals absorb water → swell in volume → mechanical stress → rock breaks. Feldspars → clay mineralsFeldspars → Kaolinite (clay)
Salt WeatheringSalt crystals in rock pores grow as water evaporates → exert pressure → rock crumbles. Common in coastal areas.Coastal rocks, arid/semi-arid areas
4

Biological Weathering

Weathering caused by living organisms — plants, animals, microorganisms, and humans.

  • Plant roots: Roots grow into rock cracks → widen with growth → split rocks apart (root wedging)
  • Lichens and mosses: Secrete acids that dissolve rock minerals; first colonisers on bare rock
  • Burrowing animals: Earthworms, moles, rodents loosen soil and expose fresh rock to weathering
  • Microorganisms: Bacteria decompose rock minerals
  • Humans: Mining, quarrying, construction, deforestation expose fresh rock faces to rapid weathering — humans are agents of weathering
Homogeneity of rocks: Uniform rocks (same minerals throughout) weather uniformly. Non-homogeneous rocks (different minerals) weather unevenly — some parts weather faster → uneven weathering → unique landforms like tors, inselbergs.
5

Factors Affecting Weathering

FactorHow it affects weathering
TemperatureHigh temperature increases chemical weathering rates; extreme diurnal temperature range → physical weathering (granular disintegration, exfoliation)
Rainfall / WaterWater is essential for chemical weathering; high rainfall → chemical weathering dominant; freeze-thaw in cold regions
Rock typeSoft/porous/jointed rocks weather faster; granite weathers slowly; limestone weathers by carbonation
SlopeSteep slopes — rapid removal of weathered material exposes fresh rock; gentle slopes retain weathered material
VegetationDense vegetation protects rock from direct weathering; roots cause biological weathering
TimeLonger exposure → deeper and greater weathering
6

Mass Wasting (Mass Movement)

Mass wasting: The downslope movement of weathered rock and soil material under the influence of gravity. Does not require a transporting agent like water or wind. Also called mass movement.

Types of Mass Wasting:

TypeDescriptionExample
FallFree fall of rocks/debris from a cliff or steep slope. Very sudden and fast. No water needed.Rockfall from mountain cliffs
CreepVery slow, almost imperceptible downslope movement of soil and rock. Movement in mm/year. Trees tilt, fence posts lean.Soil creep on gentle slopes
Slide (Landslide)Rapid movement of large masses of rock and soil down a slope along a definite slip plane. Triggered by heavy rain, earthquakes.Himalayan landslides, Konkan landslides during monsoon
FlowWater-saturated soil/rock flows down slope like a viscous fluid. Includes mudflow, debris flow. Faster than creep.Mudflows in heavy rainfall areas; Lahar (volcanic mudflow)
SolifluctionSlow flow of waterlogged soil over frozen ground (permafrost) down gentle slopes in periglacial regions. Surface layer thaws in summer → saturated → flows.Arctic tundra, high-altitude areas
Debris AvalancheVery fast movement of large volume of loose rock, snow and debris. Most destructive. Triggered by earthquakes or heavy snow.Himalayas — Kedarnath (2013), Uttarakhand

Factors affecting Mass Wasting:

FactorRole
GravityPrimary driving force — pulls all material downslope. Steeper slope = greater gravitational pull component along slope
Slope (gradient)Steeper slope = faster and more frequent mass wasting. Critical threshold angle — above this, material becomes unstable
Rainfall / WaterWater adds weight and reduces friction (cohesion) between particles → instability → slides and flows; common in high rainfall areas like Konkan, Himalayas
ClimateHigh rainfall → slides common; cold climates → solifluction; dry areas → rockfall
Rock typeWeak, porous, jointed rocks → more susceptible; clay → absorbs water → flows
VegetationTree roots bind soil and stabilise slopes; deforestation → increased landslides
EarthquakesTrigger sudden mass movements on unstable slopes

🔁 Quick Revision — One Liners

  • Weathering = in situ breakdown; Mass wasting = downslope movement under gravity
  • Physical weathering: Freeze-thaw, Block disintegration, Granular disintegration, Exfoliation, Shattering
  • Chemical weathering: Solution, Carbonation, Oxidation, Hydration, Salt weathering
  • Carbonation: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → dissolves limestone → karst topography
  • Oxidation → iron oxide (rust) → limonite/hematite → colour change (yellow/red/brown)
  • Exfoliation = onion peeling of rock due to repeated heating/cooling
  • Granular disintegration = caused by temperature; different minerals expand/contract differently
  • Freeze-thaw = common in mountains and polar regions (NOT deserts)
  • Solifluction = waterlogged soil flows over permafrost; periglacial regions
  • Creep = slowest mass wasting; Fall = free-fall; Landslide = rapid along slip plane
  • Gravity + Slope + Water = three main factors of mass wasting
  • Konkan = high rainfall → chemical weathering + landslides dominant
  • Himalayas = young mountains + earthquakes + steep slopes + heavy rain → frequent landslides
  • Western slope of Sahyadris = receives heavy monsoon rain → more mass wasting than eastern (rain shadow)
  • Humans = agent of weathering through mining, quarrying, deforestation

❓ Important Questions & Answers

Q1
What is weathering? How is it different from erosion?★★★
Show ▾
Weathering: The process of disintegration and decomposition of rocks in situ (at the same place) by the action of climate (temperature, rainfall), water, heat, organisms etc. The broken material stays at the same location — it is NOT transported.
Erosion: The picking up and removal (transportation) of weathered material by agents like water, wind, glaciers.
Key difference: Weathering = in situ breakdown; Erosion = transportation of broken material. Weathering must happen before erosion can occur.
Q2
Explain freeze-thaw weathering (frost action) with a neat description.★★★
Show ▾
Freeze-Thaw Weathering (Frost action):
  • Water seeps into cracks and joints of rocks
  • When temperature drops below 0°C, water freezes into ice
  • Ice occupies about 9% more volume than water → exerts tremendous pressure on rock walls
  • The crack widens with each freeze-thaw cycle
  • When temperature rises, ice melts → water seeps deeper → freezes again
  • Repeated freeze-thaw eventually breaks the rock apart into angular fragments (scree/talus)
Where common: High altitude mountain areas, polar and sub-polar regions, any area with temperatures oscillating around 0°C.
Note: This is NOT common in deserts — deserts have little water. It IS common in cold, moist mountainous regions.
Q3
Write a short note on Exfoliation.★★★
Show ▾
Exfoliation (Onion weathering / Spheroidal weathering):
  • The outer layers of a rock peel off in curved sheets, similar to the layers of an onion
  • Cause: Repeated heating during the day and cooling at night — surface expands more than interior → differential stress → outer shells detach
  • Also helped by chemical weathering — feldspar minerals convert to clay minerals which occupy more volume
  • The rock surface becomes rounded and dome-shaped over time (inselberg shape)
  • Produces dome-shaped rock outcrops
Where common: Hot deserts and tropical regions with large diurnal temperature range (e.g., Sahara, granite regions of Africa, Australia).
Q4
Write a short note on Carbonation.★★★
Show ▾
Carbonation: A type of chemical weathering where carbon dioxide (CO₂) dissolved in rainwater forms carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) which attacks and dissolves rocks containing calcium carbonate (limestone).
Reaction:
CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid)
H₂CO₃ + CaCO₃ (limestone) → Ca(HCO₃)₂ (calcium bicarbonate — soluble, washed away)
Results:
  • Limestone rocks dissolve → Karst topography (caves, sinkholes, dolines)
  • Stalactites hang from cave ceilings; stalagmites grow upward from floor
  • Underground caves, disappearing rivers
Where common: Limestone regions — Meghalaya (Cherrapunji area), parts of Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan, Croatia (Dinaric Alps).
Q5
Write a short note on Gravity and Solifluction.★★★
Show ▾
Gravity in mass wasting: Gravity is the primary driving force behind all mass wasting. It pulls all loose material downslope. The steeper the slope, the greater the component of gravitational force acting along the slope, making mass movements faster and more frequent. Even on gentle slopes, gravity causes slow creep over long periods.

Solifluction:
  • A type of slow mass wasting in periglacial (cold) regions
  • In cold areas, subsoil remains permanently frozen (permafrost)
  • During summer, the surface layer (active layer) thaws and becomes saturated with water (cannot drain through frozen layer below)
  • This waterlogged surface soil slowly flows downslope over the frozen layer
  • Movement is slow (few cm per year) but affects large areas
  • Creates distinct lobes and terraces on hillslopes
Where common: Arctic tundra, Antarctic, high mountains (Himalayas, Alps) — anywhere with permafrost.
Q6
Write a short note on the role of water in mass wasting.★★★
Show ▾
Role of water in mass wasting: Water plays a crucial role in triggering and accelerating mass wasting:
  • Adds weight: Water saturates soil and rock pores → increases overall weight of material → increases downslope force
  • Reduces friction: Water acts as a lubricant between particles → reduces cohesion and friction → material slides more easily
  • Increases pore pressure: Water in pores creates upward pressure → reduces effective stress holding material in place
  • Triggers flows: Heavily saturated material loses strength and flows like a fluid → mudflows, debris flows
  • Undercutting: Running water erodes base of slopes → removes support → landslides
  • High rainfall areas: Konkan coast (heavy monsoon), Himalayas → frequent landslides and flows
Q7
Geographical reason: Temperature is the main factor behind granular weathering.★★★
Show ▾
  • Rocks are made of different minerals, each with a different coefficient of thermal expansion
  • During the day (especially in deserts), intense solar heating causes rocks to expand. At night, rapid cooling causes them to contract
  • Since different minerals expand and contract at different rates, stress builds up between adjacent minerals
  • This differential expansion and contraction weakens the bonds between mineral grains
  • Over many cycles, individual mineral grains break apart and separate from each other
  • The rock crumbles into granular fragments (grains) — this is granular disintegration
  • Temperature variation (not water or chemical action) is the primary cause, hence temperature is the main factor behind granular weathering
Q8
Geographical reason: Human is an agent of weathering.★★
Show ▾
  • Mining and quarrying: Humans blast and drill into rocks, breaking them up and exposing fresh surfaces to rapid weathering
  • Construction activity: Cutting hillsides for roads, buildings, dams exposes fresh rock faces
  • Deforestation: Removal of vegetation removes the protective cover → rock exposed directly to sun, rain, frost
  • Agriculture: Ploughing breaks up soil and exposes subsurface material
  • Industrial pollution: Acid rain (SO₂, NO₂ emissions) accelerates chemical weathering of buildings and rocks
  • Water management: Irrigation changes moisture levels → affects chemical weathering rates
Therefore, humans actively contribute to weathering processes and are considered agents of weathering.
Q9
Geographical reason: Slope is a major factor in mass wasting.★★★
Show ▾
  • Mass wasting depends on gravity pulling material downslope. The steeper the slope, the greater the gravitational component acting along the slope surface
  • On steep slopes, friction between particles may not be enough to counteract gravity → material moves
  • Steep slopes also promote rapid runoff of rainwater which destabilises the slope further
  • Steep slopes have less stable root systems → trees fall → material exposed
  • Critical angle: Above a certain slope angle, material will not remain stable — any disturbance triggers mass movement
  • Example: Sahyadris (Western Ghats) — steep western slopes facing the Arabian Sea receive heavy monsoon rainfall → mass wasting very common here compared to the gentler eastern slopes
Therefore slope gradient directly controls the frequency and intensity of mass wasting.
Q10
Geographical reason: Oxidation changes the size and colour of rocks.★★
Show ▾
  • Colour change: When iron-bearing minerals (like ferrous oxide, FeO) in rocks react with atmospheric oxygen, they are oxidised to form iron oxides:
    4FeO + O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ (hematite — red/brown colour); hydrated form → limonite (yellow/brown colour)
    This gives rocks a reddish, yellowish or brownish colour (like rust)
  • Size change: The iron oxide minerals (hematite, limonite) occupy greater volume than the original iron silicate minerals
    This volume expansion creates internal stress in the rock → weakens bonds → causes disintegration → rock crumbles and breaks into smaller pieces
Hence oxidation causes both colour change (yellow/red/brown) and size increase/disintegration of rocks.
Q11
Geographical reason: Effect of mass movement will be greater along the western slope of Sahyadris than the eastern slope.★★★
Show ▾
  • Western slope: Faces the Arabian Sea → receives the full force of the southwest monsoon → very heavy rainfall (Konkan and Goa receive 2500–5000 mm annual rainfall)
  • Heavy, concentrated rainfall saturates the soil rapidly → increases weight, reduces friction → triggers landslides, mudflows, debris flows
  • Western slopes are also generally steeper than eastern slopes
  • The steep gradient + heavy rainfall combination → frequent and severe mass wasting
  • Eastern slope: Lies in the rain shadow zone → receives much less rainfall (600–1000 mm) → soil less saturated → less mass movement
  • Eastern slopes are also more gradual in descent → reduced gravitational component
Therefore, the western slopes of the Sahyadris experience significantly greater mass wasting than the eastern slopes.
Q12
Identify correct correlation: A: In areas of high rainfall, slides are very common. R: Types of mass wasting movements are dependent on a region's climate.★★
Show ▾
Answer: Option 3 — Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • A is correct: High rainfall saturates soil, adds weight, reduces friction → slides and landslides become very frequent. Konkan and Himalayan regions confirm this.
  • R is correct: Climate (especially rainfall and temperature) determines which type of mass wasting dominates — high rainfall areas → slides/flows; cold climate → solifluction; dry areas → rockfall/creep
  • R correctly explains A: The reason slides are common in high-rainfall areas is that the climate (heavy rainfall) directly controls the type and frequency of mass wasting movements
Q13
Identify correct correlation: A: Gravity is a major factor in mass wasting. R: Gravity pulls all things down to the earth's surface.★★
Show ▾
Answer: Option 4 — Both A and R are correct but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
  • A is correct: Gravity is indeed the primary driving force of all mass wasting — it pulls weathered material downslope
  • R is correct: Gravity does pull all objects toward Earth's surface (downward)
  • But R does NOT correctly explain A: The specific reason gravity is important in mass wasting is not just that it pulls things "down to Earth's surface" but that it creates a downslope component of force along inclined surfaces. On flat ground, gravity cannot cause mass wasting. It is the slope gradient combined with gravity that drives mass wasting.
Q14
Identify correct correlation: A: Freeze-thaw weathering is common in desert areas. R: Water gets into cracks and breaks the rocks.★★
Show ▾
Answer: Option 2 — Only R is correct.
  • A is incorrect: Freeze-thaw weathering is NOT common in desert areas. Deserts are dry with very little water — water is essential for freeze-thaw action. Freeze-thaw is common in high altitude/polar regions where water is available and temperatures frequently cross 0°C. In deserts, the dominant physical weathering is granular disintegration and exfoliation (caused by temperature extremes, not freeze-thaw).
  • R is correct: The mechanism of freeze-thaw is indeed that water gets into cracks, freezes, expands, and breaks the rocks apart.
Q15
Explain weathering processes happening in Konkan region.★★★
Show ▾
The Konkan region receives very heavy monsoon rainfall (2500–5000 mm annually). This promotes both chemical weathering and mass wasting:
Chemical weathering dominant processes:
  • Solution and hydration: Constant moisture promotes dissolution of minerals
  • Oxidation: Basalt rocks (Deccan Trap) rich in iron silicates → oxidise to form laterite soils — Konkan is famous for red laterite soils
  • Carbonation: Slight carbonation where limestone pockets exist
Physical weathering:
  • Wet-dry cycles during and after monsoon → block disintegration in exposed areas
Mass wasting:
  • Heavy monsoon rain → rapid saturation of soil on steep Sahyadri western slopes → frequent landslides and mudflows during monsoon
  • Deforestation in Konkan has increased mass wasting in recent years
Q16
Explain the correlation between Himalayas and mass movements.★★★
Show ▾
The Himalayas are highly susceptible to mass movements due to multiple factors:
  • Young and fragile rocks: Himalayas are young fold mountains — rocks are not fully consolidated, jointed and fractured → easily dislodged
  • Steep slopes: Extremely steep gradients → high gravitational force component → any disturbance triggers mass movement
  • Heavy rainfall: Southern Himalayas receive heavy monsoon rain → rapid saturation → landslides and debris flows. E.g., Kedarnath disaster (2013) — cloudburst + glacial lake outburst → devastating debris flow.
  • Seismic activity: Himalayas are in Zone IV-V (high seismic risk) → frequent earthquakes → destabilise slopes → trigger landslides
  • Glaciers and snowmelt: Glacial meltwater saturates slopes; glacial retreat destabilises moraine deposits → landslides
  • Deforestation: Cutting of Himalayan forests for agriculture, roads, hydropower → removal of slope stabilisation → increased mass movements
Famous examples: Kedarnath (2013), Bhagirathi valley slides, Joshimath subsidence (2023), Malpa landslide (Uttarakhand).
