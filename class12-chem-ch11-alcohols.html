PART 1 — THEORY SECTION

Classification · Structure · Nomenclature · Properties · Acidity — All definition & explanation questions

T 1Classify alcohols, phenols and ethers. Give examples. ⭐ Most IMP3-4 Marks
ALCOHOLS — Classification based on OH group position: Primary (1°) –OH attached to C bonded to ONE other C. E.g., CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol), CH₃OH (methanol) Secondary (2°) –OH attached to C bonded to TWO other C's. E.g., CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ (isopropanol) Tertiary (3°) –OH attached to C bonded to THREE other C's. E.g., (CH₃)₃COH (tert-butanol) Based on number of –OH groups: • Monohydric: 1 –OH (ethanol) • Dihydric: 2 –OH (ethylene glycol — HOCH₂CH₂OH) • Trihydric: 3 –OH (glycerol — HOCH₂CH(OH)CH₂OH) PHENOLS: –OH directly attached to benzene ring. E.g., C₆H₅OH (phenol) ETHERS: Two organic groups linked by –O–. R–O–R'. E.g., C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ (diethyl ether) • Symmetrical (simple): R–O–R (both groups same). E.g., CH₃–O–CH₃ • Asymmetrical (mixed): R–O–R' (different groups). E.g., CH₃–O–C₂H₅

T 3Methods of Preparation of Alcohols. ⭐ Most IMP4 Marks
1. Hydration of Alkenes (Industrial method): Alkene + H₂O → Alcohol (H₂SO₄ catalyst, follows Markovnikov's rule) 2. Hydroboration-Oxidation (anti-Markovnikov): Alkene + B₂H₆ → Trialkylborane → H₂O₂/NaOH → Primary alcohol (syn addition) 3. Reduction of Aldehydes/Ketones: Aldehyde + H₂ → (LiAlH₄ or NaBH₄) → Primary alcohol Ketone + H₂ → (LiAlH₄ or NaBH₄) → Secondary alcohol 4. Reduction of Carboxylic Acids: RCOOH + LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH (Primary alcohol) 5. Grignard Reagent: HCHO + RMgX → (i) dry ether (ii) H₃O⁺ → Primary alcohol RCHO + R'MgX → (i) dry ether (ii) H₃O⁺ → Secondary alcohol R₂CO + R'MgX → (i) dry ether (ii) H₃O⁺ → Tertiary alcohol 6. Fermentation (ethanol only): C₆H₁₂O₆ → (yeast/zymase) → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
T 4Physical properties and acidity of Alcohols. Why do alcohols have higher boiling points? ⭐ Most IMP3 Marks
Physical Properties of Alcohols: Boiling Point Alcohols have higher boiling points than alkanes/ethers of similar MW due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding (O–H···O). More energy needed to break H-bonds. BP: CH₃OH (65°C) > CH₃OCH₃ (−24°C) for same MW Solubility Lower alcohols (C1–C4) are miscible with water due to H-bonding with water. Solubility decreases with increase in carbon chain (hydrophobic part increases). Acidity of Alcohols: R–O–H ⇌ R–O⁻ + H⁺  (pKa ≈ 16–18) Order of acidity: CH₃OH > 1° > 2° > 3° Reason: Alkyl groups (+I effect) increase electron density on O → less tendency to release H⁺ → less acidic. Alcohols less acidic than water and phenols. Acidity order: H₂O > ROH;   Phenol (pKa 10) >> Alcohol (pKa 16) Reason phenol more acidic Phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻) is stabilised by resonance with benzene ring → more stable → equilibrium shifts toward dissociation → more acidic.
T 5Why is phenol more acidic than alcohol but less acidic than carboxylic acid? ⭐ Most IMP3 Marks
Acidity comparison: Carboxylic acid > Phenol > Water > Alcohol Phenol > Alcohol • Phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻) is stabilised by resonance — lone pair on O delocalised into benzene ring → 5 resonance structures • Alkoxide ion (RO⁻) has no such resonance stabilisation • More stable conjugate base → stronger acid → phenol more acidic than alcohol Carboxylic acid > Phenol • Carboxylate ion (RCOO⁻) has two equivalent resonance structures — charge delocalised equally over two O atoms → more stable than phenoxide • Phenoxide: resonance with ring — not as effective (charge goes on ring carbons, not on electronegative O) Effect of substituents on acidity of phenol: • Electron-withdrawing groups (–NO₂, –Cl) at ortho/para increase acidity (stabilise phenoxide) • Electron-donating groups (–CH₃, –OCH₃) decrease acidity
📐 Draw resonance structures of phenoxide ion showing negative charge on O, ortho and para carbons. 5 structures total.
T 6Structure and properties of Ethers. Why are ethers good solvents? ⭐ IMP3 Marks
Structure of Ethers (R–O–R'): • C–O–C bond angle ≈ 111.7° (slightly more than tetrahedral due to lone pairs on O) • Oxygen has 2 lone pairs; C–O bond is polar but molecule may be non-polar (symmetrical ether) Physical Properties: • Lower boiling points than alcohols (no O–H bond → no H-bonding between ether molecules) • Diethyl ether BP = 34.6°C (highly volatile and flammable) • Slightly soluble in water (lone pairs on O can H-bond with water) Why ethers are good solvents: 1. Low boiling point → easy to remove by evaporation after reaction 2. Chemically inert (do not react with most reagents) 3. Dissolve both polar and non-polar organic compounds 4. Used as solvent for Grignard reactions, extraction Limitations: Highly flammable (flash point −45°C); peroxides form on long storage (explosive).

PART 2 — CHEMICAL REACTIONS & FORMULAE

All reactions with equations — Alcohols · Phenols · Ethers · Name Reactions

R 1Chemical Reactions of Alcohols ⭐ Most IMPBoard
1. Reactions with Active Metals (Acidic nature)
Reaction with Na metal
2R–OH + 2Na → 2R–O⁻Na⁺ + H₂↑ 2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑
Sodium ethoxide formed; H₂ evolved; confirms acidic –OH group
2. Dehydration Reactions
Intramolecular dehydration (high temp → alkene)
CH₃CH₂OH → (conc. H₂SO₄, 443K) → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
Intermolecular dehydration (low temp → ether)
2C₂H₅OH → (conc. H₂SO₄, 413K) → C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + H₂O
413K → ether; 443K → alkene
3. Reactions with Phosphorus Halides (OH → X)
With PCl₅
R–OH + PCl₅ → R–Cl + POCl₃ + HCl
With PCl₃
3R–OH + PCl₃ → 3R–Cl + H₃PO₃
With SOCl₂ (thionyl chloride) — best method
R–OH + SOCl₂ → R–Cl + SO₂ + HCl
SOCl₂ is best because byproducts SO₂ and HCl are gases → pure product obtained
4. Esterification (with carboxylic acids)
Fischer Esterification
RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ (H⁺ catalyst, Δ) ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
Reversible reaction; equilibrium reaction; H₂SO₄ catalyst; fragrant ester ethyl acetate formed
5. Oxidation of Alcohols
Primary alcohol → Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid
RCH₂OH → [O] (K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺) → RCHO → [O] → RCOOH C₂H₅OH → [O] → CH₃CHO → [O] → CH₃COOH
Secondary alcohol → Ketone
R₂CHOH → [O] (K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺) → R₂C=O CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → [O] → CH₃COCH₃ (acetone)
Tertiary alcohol — resistant to oxidation
3° Alcohol → No reaction with mild oxidising agents (no α-H)
Victor Meyer test distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols
6. Lucas Test (distinguish 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols)
Lucas Reagent = anhydrous ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl
3° alcohol + Lucas reagent → Immediate turbidity (cloudiness at room temp) 2° alcohol + Lucas reagent → Turbidity after 5 min 1° alcohol + Lucas reagent → No turbidity at room temp (reacts on heating)
Turbidity = alkyl chloride (RCl) forms, insoluble in reagent
R 2Chemical Reactions of Phenols ⭐ Most IMPBoard
1. Acidic nature of Phenol
With NaOH (phenol is acidic — reacts with base)
C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O (Sodium phenoxide formed)
With Na₂CO₃ (stronger acid displaces weaker)
2C₆H₅OH + Na₂CO₃ → 2C₆H₅ONa + H₂O + CO₂↑
Phenol is acidic enough to react with Na₂CO₃ but NOT with NaHCO₃ (phenol pKa ≈ 10; carbonic acid pKa₁ = 6.3)
2. Electrophilic Substitution Reactions (EAS) — OH is ortho/para directing
Nitration — with dilute HNO₃
C₆H₅OH + dilute HNO₃ → o-nitrophenol + p-nitrophenol
Nitration — with conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄
C₆H₅OH + 3HNO₃ → 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) + 3H₂O
Bromination (Br₂/water — no catalyst needed!)
C₆H₅OH + 3Br₂(aq) → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt) + 3HBr
Phenol reacts with Br₂ water WITHOUT catalyst (unlike benzene needs FeBr₃) — used as test for phenol
Sulphonation — with conc. H₂SO₄
C₆H₅OH + H₂SO₄ → o-hydroxybenzenesulphonic acid + p-hydroxybenzenesulphonic acid
Friedel-Crafts Alkylation
C₆H₅OH + CH₃Cl → (AlCl₃) → o-cresol + p-cresol
3. Kolbe-Schmitt Reaction ⭐ Name Reaction
Phenol → Sodium phenoxide → Salicylic acid (aspirin precursor)
C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa (sodium phenoxide) C₆H₅ONa + CO₂ → (300K, 4-7 atm) → sodium salicylate Sodium salicylate + HCl → 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid) + NaCl
Used in manufacture of aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)
4. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction ⭐ Name Reaction
Phenol → Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde)
C₆H₅OH + CHCl₃ + 3KOH → (aq, 340K) → 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde) + 3KCl + 3H₂O
CHCl₃ acts as electrophile (via :CCl₂ dichlorocarbene intermediate); –CHO introduced at ortho position
5. FeCl₃ Test for Phenol
C₆H₅OH + FeCl₃(aq) → Violet/purple colour complex
Characteristic test for phenols — purple/violet colour confirms phenolic –OH group
R 3Chemical Reactions of Ethers ⭐ IMPBoard
1. Cleavage by HI (or HBr) — most important reaction of ethers
With excess HI (for dialkyl ether)
R–O–R' + HI → R–OH + R'–I (less HI) C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + HI → C₂H₅OH + C₂H₅I
With excess HI (both groups become iodides)
C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + 2HI → 2C₂H₅I + H₂O
Reactivity: HI > HBr > HCl for ether cleavage. SN2 mechanism for 1° ether; SN1 for 3° ether.
2. Preparation of Ethers — Williamson Synthesis ⭐ Name Reaction
Alkyl halide + Sodium alkoxide → Ether (SN2)
R–O⁻Na⁺ + R'–X → R–O–R' + NaX C₂H₅ONa + CH₃I → C₂H₅–O–CH₃ + NaI (Methoxyethane formed)
Use primary alkyl halide (SN2); tertiary halide → elimination instead. Best for asymmetric ethers.

MCQ SECTION — Most Repeated Board Questions

1 mark each — all from previous board papers

MCQMost Repeated MCQs from Board Papers ⭐ All IMP1M each
1. Which reagent is used to distinguish 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols?
Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl). 3° → immediate turbidity; 2° → 5 min; 1° → no turbidity at room temp.
2. What product is formed when ethanol reacts with conc. H₂SO₄ at 443K?
Ethylene (CH₂=CH₂) — intramolecular dehydration at 443K (170°C). At 413K → diethyl ether.
3. Phenol reacts with Br₂ water to give ________.
2,4,6-tribromophenol (white precipitate) — phenol undergoes electrophilic substitution at ortho and para positions without any catalyst.
4. The reagent used in Williamson synthesis is ________.
Sodium alkoxide (R–ONa) + Alkyl halide (R'–X) → Ether. SN2 mechanism. Best method for preparing asymmetric ethers.
5. Which of the following gives a positive FeCl₃ test? Alcohol / Phenol / Ether
Phenol — gives violet/purple colour with neutral FeCl₃ solution. Alcohol and ether do NOT give this test.
6. Kolbe-Schmitt reaction is used to prepare ________.
Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) — from sodium phenoxide + CO₂ at 300K and 4-7 atm. Used to make aspirin.
7. Arrange in increasing order of acidity: Ethanol, Phenol, Water
Ethanol < Water < Phenol
pKa: Ethanol (16) > Water (15.7) > Phenol (10). Phenol most acidic due to resonance stabilisation of phenoxide ion.
8. Which alcohol cannot be oxidised to aldehyde/ketone?
Tertiary (3°) alcohol — has no α-hydrogen atom on the carbon bearing –OH, so cannot be oxidised by mild oxidising agents like K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺.
