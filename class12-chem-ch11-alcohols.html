T 3Methods of Preparation of Alcohols ⭐ Most IMP4 M
6 Important Methods
1. Hydration of Alkenes (Industrial — Markovnikov's rule)
CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O conc. H₂SO₄→300–500°C CH₃CH₂OH
2. Hydroboration-Oxidation (anti-Markovnikov → primary alcohol)
CH₃CH=CH₂ (i) B₂H₆→(ii) H₂O₂/NaOH CH₃CH₂CH₂OH
Anti-Markovnikov addition · OH goes to less substituted C
3. Grignard Reagent (most versatile method)
HCHO + RMgX (i) dry ether→(ii) H₃O⁺ RCH₂OH
HCHO → 1° alcohol  |  RCHO → 2° alcohol  |  R₂CO → 3° alcohol
4. Reduction of Carbonyl Compounds
RCHO LiAlH₄→or NaBH₄ RCH₂OH (1° alcohol)
R–CO–R' LiAlH₄→ R–CH(OH)–R' (2° alcohol)
RCOOH LiAlH₄→ RCH₂OH (1° alcohol)
5. Fermentation (ethanol only)
C₆H₁₂O₆ Yeast / Zymase→310–315 K 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂↑
Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide
6. Preparation of Phenol
From Chlorobenzene (Dow's process)
C₆H₅Cl + NaOH(aq) 600 K→300 atm C₆H₅OH + NaCl
From Benzene sulphonic acid
C₆H₅SO₃H NaOH (fuse)→ C₆H₅ONa H⁺→ C₆H₅OH
T 4Physical Properties and Acidity of Alcohols ⭐ Most IMP3 M
Why do alcohols have higher boiling points than alkanes?
Reason: Alcohols form intermolecular hydrogen bonds (O–H···O) between molecules. Extra energy is needed to break these H-bonds during boiling → higher boiling point.
H-bonding in Alcohols
R–O–H···O–R | H···O–R | H
Intermolecular H-bond
→ high BP
BP Comparison
CH₃OH = 65°C CH₃CH₃ = −89°C (same MW, no H-bond in alkane)
Alcohol BP >> Alkane BP
Solubility
C1–C4: Miscible C5+: Decreasing R–OH + H₂O → H-bonding → soluble
Hydrophilic –OH
vs hydrophobic R–
Acidity of Alcohols:
R–O–H ⇌ R–O⁻ + H⁺ pKa ≈ 16–18
Order: CH₃OH > 1° > 2° > 3°  (alkyl groups donate electrons → decrease acidity)

PART 2 — CHEMICAL REACTIONS

All equations in clean format — Reactants → Conditions → Products

R 1Reactions of Alcohols ⭐ Most IMPBoard
1. With Active Metals (Acidic nature)
Reaction with Sodium
2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑
Sodium ethoxide formed · H₂ gas evolved · Confirms acidic –OH
2. Dehydration (elimination)
Intramolecular — forms ALKENE (high temperature)
CH₃CH₂OH conc. H₂SO₄→443 K (170°C) CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
Intermolecular — forms ETHER (low temperature)
2C₂H₅OH conc. H₂SO₄→413 K (140°C) C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + H₂O
⭐ Remember: 413 K → Ether  |  443 K → Alkene
3. With Phosphorus Halides (OH replaced by X)
With PCl₅
R–OH + PCl₅ → R–Cl + POCl₃ + HCl↑
With SOCl₂ — Best Method ⭐
R–OH + SOCl₂ → R–Cl + SO₂↑ + HCl↑
SOCl₂ is BEST — byproducts SO₂ and HCl are gases → escape → pure R–Cl obtained
With PCl₃
3R–OH + PCl₃ → 3R–Cl + H₃PO₃
4. Esterification (with carboxylic acids)
Fischer Esterification — reversible reaction
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH H⁺ / Δ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
Acetic acid + Ethanol ⇌ Ethyl acetate (fragrant) + Water · Reversible · H₂SO₄ catalyst
5. Oxidation of Alcohols
Primary Alcohol → Aldehyde → Carboxylic Acid
RCH₂OH [O]→ RCHO [O]→ RCOOH
C₂H₅OH K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺→ CH₃CHO [O]→ CH₃COOH
Secondary Alcohol → Ketone
CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺→ CH₃COCH₃ (Acetone)
Tertiary Alcohol → No reaction
(CH₃)₃COH K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺→ No reaction (No α-H)
6. Lucas Test — Distinguish 1°, 2°, 3° Alcohols ⭐
Lucas Reagent = anhydrous ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl
3° Alcohol + Lucas reagent Room temp→ Immediate turbidity
2° Alcohol + Lucas reagent Room temp→ Turbidity after 5 min
1° Alcohol + Lucas reagent Room temp→ No turbidity
Turbidity = formation of insoluble alkyl chloride (R–Cl)
R 2Reactions of Phenols ⭐ Most IMPBoard
1. Acidic nature of Phenol
With NaOH
C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O
With Na₂CO₃ (phenol is strong enough)
2C₆H₅OH + Na₂CO₃ → 2C₆H₅ONa + H₂O + CO₂↑
⭐ Phenol reacts with Na₂CO₃ but NOT with NaHCO₃
2. Electrophilic Substitution (EAS) — –OH is ortho/para director
Nitration — dilute HNO₃
C₆H₅OH + dil. HNO₃ Room temp→ o-nitrophenol + p-nitrophenol
Nitration — conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ (picric acid)
C₆H₅OH + 3HNO₃ conc. H₂SO₄→ 2,4,6-(NO₂)₃C₆H₂OH + 3H₂O
Picric acid — yellow explosive compound
Bromination — Br₂/H₂O (no catalyst needed!) ⭐
C₆H₅OH + 3Br₂(aq) Room temp→ 2,4,6-Br₃C₆H₂OH↓ + 3HBr
White precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol · No catalyst needed (unlike benzene) · Test for phenol
3. Kolbe-Schmitt Reaction ⭐ Name Reaction
Step 1 — Form sodium phenoxide
C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O
Step 2 — React with CO₂
C₆H₅ONa + CO₂ 300 K→4–7 atm Sodium salicylate H⁺→ Salicylic acid
Salicylic acid = 2-hydroxybenzoic acid · Used to make Aspirin
4. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction ⭐ Name Reaction
C₆H₅OH + CHCl₃ + 3KOH aq. KOH→340 K 2-HOC₆H₄CHO + 3KCl + 3H₂O
Product = Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) · CHCl₃ generates :CCl₂ (carbene) · –CHO at ortho
5. FeCl₃ Test — Identification of Phenol ⭐
C₆H₅OH + FeCl₃(aq) → Violet / Purple complex
Characteristic test · Purple/violet colour = phenol present · Alcohol → no colour
R 3Reactions of Ethers ⭐ IMPBoard
1. Cleavage by HI (most important reaction of ethers)
With limited HI — one group becomes alcohol
C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + HI limited→ C₂H₅OH + C₂H₅I
With excess HI — both groups become iodides
C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + 2HI excess HI→ 2C₂H₅I + H₂O
Reactivity: HI > HBr > HCl  |  SN2 for 1° ether; SN1 for 3°
2. Williamson Synthesis ⭐ Name Reaction — Preparation of Ethers
Sodium alkoxide + Primary alkyl halide → Ether (SN2)
C₂H₅O⁻Na⁺ + CH₃I SN2→ C₂H₅–O–CH₃ + NaI
Use PRIMARY alkyl halide only (3° → elimination by E2) · Best method for asymmetric ethers

MCQMost Repeated MCQs ⭐ All IMP1M each
1. Which reagent is used to distinguish 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols?
Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl). 3° → immediate turbidity · 2° → 5 min · 1° → no turbidity
2. What product forms when ethanol reacts with conc. H₂SO₄ at 443 K?
Ethylene (CH₂=CH₂) — intramolecular dehydration at 443 K. At 413 K → diethyl ether
3. Phenol + Br₂ water gives ________.
2,4,6-tribromophenol (white precipitate) — no catalyst needed (unlike benzene)
4. The reagent used in Williamson synthesis is ________.
Sodium alkoxide (R–ONa) + primary alkyl halide (R'–X) → ether by SN2
5. Which gives positive FeCl₃ test?
Phenol → violet/purple colour. Alcohol and ether do NOT give this test
6. Kolbe-Schmitt reaction prepares ________.
Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) — from sodium phenoxide + CO₂ at 300 K, 4–7 atm
7. Arrange in increasing order of acidity: Ethanol, Phenol, Water
Ethanol (pKa 16) < Water (pKa 15.7) < Phenol (pKa 10) — Phenol most acidic due to resonance
8. Which alcohol cannot be oxidised by mild oxidising agents?
Tertiary (3°) alcohol — has no α-H on C bearing –OH → cannot be oxidised by K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺
