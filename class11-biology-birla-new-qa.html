🌱 Biodiversity, Classification & Basics
Q.1
Define Biodiversity.
Definition
Biodiversity refers to the variety and variability of life on Earth — including all species of plants, animals, microorganisms, the genes they contain, and the ecosystems they form.
∴ Biodiversity = variety of life at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels.
Q.2
Define Taxonomy.
Definition
Taxonomy is the branch of biology that deals with the identification, nomenclature, and classification of living organisms into groups based on their similarities and differences.
∴ Taxonomy = Identification + Nomenclature + Classification of organisms.
Q.3
What is Binomial Nomenclature? Give the common name of Mangifera indica.
Definition
A system of giving two-part scientific names to organisms — Genus name (capital) + Species name (small). Introduced by Carolus Linnaeus.
Rules
Written in italics (printed) or underlined (handwritten). e.g. Mangifera indica
Common name
Mangifera indica = Mango
∴ Binomial = Genus + species. Mangifera indica = Mango.
Q.4
What is Chemotaxonomy? Explain with example.
Definition
Chemotaxonomy is classification of organisms based on their biochemical composition — chemical compounds like proteins, DNA sequences, alkaloids, pigments present in organisms.
Example
Plants containing similar alkaloids are grouped together. DNA barcoding — using specific gene sequences to identify organisms. Chlorophyll types used to classify algae.
∴ Chemotaxonomy = classification based on chemical compounds (DNA, proteins, pigments).
Q.5
Who proposed the 5 Kingdom system of classification?
Answer
R.H. Whittaker (1969) proposed the Five Kingdom classification: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.
∴ Five Kingdom system = R.H. Whittaker (1969).
Q.6
Give the salient feature of the kingdom in which Paramoecium belongs.
Kingdom
Protista — Paramoecium belongs to Kingdom Protista.
Features
  • Unicellular eukaryotes
  • Aquatic, mostly free-living
  • Nutrition: autotrophic or heterotrophic
  • Locomotion by cilia, flagella, or pseudopodia
  • Sexual and asexual reproduction
  • e.g. Amoeba, Euglena, Diatoms, Paramecium
∴ Paramecium → Kingdom Protista (unicellular eukaryotes).
Q.7
Define metabolism.
Definition
Metabolism is the sum total of all chemical reactions occurring inside a living organism — includes anabolism (building up) and catabolism (breaking down) of molecules.
∴ Metabolism = Anabolism (synthesis) + Catabolism (breakdown). It is the most reliable criterion of life.
Q.8
Name the sub-kingdoms of Plantae.
Sub-kingdoms
  • Cryptogamae (non-seed plants) — Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta
  • Phanerogamae (seed plants) — Gymnosperms, Angiosperms
∴ Plantae has 2 sub-kingdoms: Cryptogamae (no seeds) and Phanerogamae (seed-bearing).
Q.9
Give an example of endocrine gland and exocrine gland.
Endocrine gland
Ductless glands that secrete hormones directly into the blood. e.g. Pituitary gland, Thyroid gland, Adrenal gland.
Exocrine gland
Glands with ducts that secrete substances to specific sites. e.g. Salivary gland, Sweat gland, Liver (bile), Pancreas (digestive enzymes).
∴ Endocrine (ductless, hormones): Pituitary. Exocrine (with duct): Salivary gland.
🦠 Bacteria, Virus, Fungi & Algae
Q.10
What is the shape of Bacillus and Coccus type of bacteria?
Bacillus
Rod-shaped bacteria. e.g. Bacillus subtilis, Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
Coccus
Spherical/round-shaped bacteria. e.g. Staphylococcus (cluster), Streptococcus (chain), Diplococcus (pairs).
∴ Bacillus = rod-shaped; Coccus = spherical/round.
Q.11
Write a note on beneficial bacteria.
In agriculture
Rhizobium (nitrogen fixation in root nodules of legumes), Azotobacter (free-living N-fixer), Nitrosomonas (nitrification).
In industry
Lactobacillus (curd/yoghurt formation), Acetobacter (vinegar), Clostridium (acetone production), Streptomyces (antibiotics like Streptomycin).
In medicine
Antibiotics produced — Streptomycin, Bacitracin. Insulin production using E. coli (genetic engineering).
Decomposers
Break down dead organic matter → recycle nutrients into soil.
∴ Beneficial bacteria: Rhizobium (N-fixation), Lactobacillus (curd), Streptomyces (antibiotics).
Q.12
Write harmful and useful effects of Bacteria.
Harmful effects
  • Cause diseases: TB (Mycobacterium), Cholera (Vibrio), Typhoid (Salmonella), Pneumonia (Pneumococcus)
  • Food spoilage and putrefaction
  • Plant diseases — citrus canker
  • Dental caries — Streptococcus mutans
Useful effects
  • Nitrogen fixation — Rhizobium, Azotobacter
  • Curd, cheese, vinegar production
  • Antibiotic production — Streptomycin
  • Decompose dead matter — maintain soil fertility
  • Sewage treatment
Q.13
Give the general characters of virus.
Characters
  • Non-cellular — not made of cells
  • Ultramicroscopic — visible only under electron microscope
  • Obligate intracellular parasites — can replicate only inside a living host cell
  • Contain either DNA or RNA (never both)
  • Protein coat called capsid
  • No metabolism — show life only inside host
  • Can be crystallized (like non-living matter)
  • Cause diseases: Influenza, AIDS, Polio, Rabies, TMV (tobacco)
∴ Virus: non-cellular, obligate parasite, DNA or RNA, protein capsid, no metabolism.
Q.14
Write a note on useful Fungi.
Food
Mushrooms (Agaricus) — edible. Yeast (Saccharomyces) — bread making, alcohol fermentation.
Medicine
Penicillin antibiotic from Penicillium notatum. Cyclosporin (immunosuppressant) from Trichoderma.
Industry
Aspergillus niger — citric acid. Yeast — ethanol production, baking.
Ecology
Decomposers — break dead matter, recycle nutrients. Mycorrhizae — mutualistic association with plant roots (improves nutrient absorption).
∴ Useful fungi: Yeast (fermentation), Penicillium (antibiotic), Agaricus (food), Aspergillus (industry).
Q.15
Name the chlorophyll pigment present in Brown algae.
Pigment
Brown algae contain fucoxanthin (brown carotenoid pigment) along with chlorophyll a and chlorophyll c. The brown colour is due to fucoxanthin masking the green chlorophyll. e.g. Laminaria, Fucus, Sargassum.
∴ Brown algae contain Fucoxanthin (brown pigment) + Chlorophyll a and c.
Q.16
Write a note on parasitic adaptation of Liverfluke.
Liverfluke
Fasciola hepatica — endoparasite in liver of sheep/cattle.
Adaptations
  • Flat, leaf-shaped body — less resistance inside host
  • Suckers (oral + ventral) — for attachment to host tissues
  • Loss of digestive system complexity — absorbs pre-digested food
  • Thick tegument (body covering) — resists host enzymes and immune response
  • High reproductive rate — produces thousands of eggs (compensation for larval mortality)
  • Complex life cycle with intermediate host (snail)
  • No sense organs — not needed in stable internal environment
∴ Liverfluke adaptations: suckers, flat body, tegument, high egg production, intermediate host.
🌿 Kingdom Plantae
Q.17
Give an example of Angiosperm.
Angiosperm
Flowering plants with enclosed seeds inside fruits. e.g. Mango (Mangifera indica), Rose, Wheat, Maize, Sunflower.
∴ Angiosperm example: Mango (Mangifera indica) — seeds enclosed in fruit.
Q.18
Why are Bryophytes called the amphibians of plants?
Reason
Bryophytes are called amphibians of plant kingdom because: (1) They live on land but require water for fertilization — the male gametes (antherozoids) swim through water to reach the egg. (2) They can survive on land but cannot complete their life cycle without water. (3) They represent transition between aquatic algae and fully terrestrial plants.
∴ Bryophytes = amphibians of plants: live on land but need water for fertilization (like frogs need water).

Q.21
Name a plant based on the following habitat: xeric, aquatic, saline soil, rocks.
Xeric (dry)
Cactus (Opuntia), Euphorbia — xerophytes adapted to dry, arid conditions.
Aquatic
Lotus (Nelumbo), Water hyacinth (Eichhornia), Hydrilla — hydrophytes.
Saline soil
Mangrove (Rhizophora), Salicornia — halophytes, adapted to salty soil.
Rocks (Lithophytes)
Lichens, mosses (Bryophytes) — grow on rocks by secreting acids to dissolve minerals.
∴ Xeric=Cactus; Aquatic=Lotus; Saline=Mangrove; Rocks=Lichens.
Q.22
Differentiate between tap root and adventitious root.
Tap root
Develops from radicle of seed. One main primary root with lateral branches. Found in dicots. e.g. Carrot, Radish, Mustard.
Adventitious root
Develops from any part other than radicle (stem, leaves). Found in monocots (fibrous roots are adventitious). e.g. Banyan tree prop roots, grass roots, Monstera.
∴ Tap root = from radicle (dicots). Adventitious = from stem/leaf (monocots, banyan).

Q.24
What are permanent plant tissues? Mention their types.
Definition
Permanent tissues are those that have lost the ability to divide — cells are fully differentiated and specialized.
Types
  • Simple permanent: Made of one type of cell — Parenchyma, Collenchyma, Sclerenchyma
  • Complex permanent: Made of more than one type of cell — Xylem, Phloem
∴ Permanent tissues: Simple (Parenchyma, Collenchyma, Sclerenchyma) + Complex (Xylem, Phloem).
🐾 Kingdom Animalia
Q.25
Give an example of Arthropoda.
Example
Cockroach (Periplaneta americana). Others: Prawn (Palaemon), Butterfly, Housefly (Musca), Scorpion, Crab, Spider.
∴ Arthropoda example: Cockroach — jointed appendages, chitinous exoskeleton, largest phylum.
Q.26
Write characteristic features of phylum in which Octopus is included.
Phylum
Mollusca — Octopus belongs here.
Features
  • Soft-bodied, unsegmented animals
  • Mantle — fold of skin that may secrete shell
  • Radula — file-like rasping organ for feeding
  • Open circulatory system (except cephalopods like Octopus which have closed)
  • Organ system level of organization
  • e.g. Pila (snail), Chiton, Squid, Octopus, Oyster
∴ Octopus → Phylum Mollusca: soft body, mantle, radula, open circulation (Octopus has closed).

Q.28
Give adaptive radiation in birds.
Definition
Adaptive radiation is the evolution of different species from a common ancestor to fill different ecological niches — adapting to different environments and food sources.
Examples in Birds
  • Beak adaptations: Seed-eating (finch — thick beak), nectar-feeding (hummingbird — long thin beak), fish-eating (pelican — large pouch beak), insect-eating (woodpecker — chisel beak)
  • Darwin's finches in Galapagos Islands — classic example of adaptive radiation
  • Feet adaptations: Swimming (duck — webbed), perching (sparrow), hunting (eagle — talons)
∴ Adaptive radiation in birds: Darwin's finches — beak shapes adapted for different food sources.
Q.29
Name the protective covering present on the body of endoparasite.
Answer
Tegument — a syncytial, non-cellular, protective covering present on the body of endoparasites like tapeworm and liver fluke. It resists host digestive enzymes and immune reactions. It also absorbs nutrients.
∴ Endoparasite protective covering = Tegument (e.g. Tapeworm, Liverfluke).
Q.30
Describe sexual dimorphism in Ascaris.
Ascaris
Ascaris lumbricoides — roundworm, endoparasite in human intestine.
Sexual dimorphism
  • Female: Larger (20–40 cm), straight posterior end, two uteri, genital opening on ventral side at anterior 1/3
  • Male: Smaller (15–30 cm), curved/coiled posterior end, single testis, two chitinous spicules at posterior end for copulation
  • Both sexes are clearly distinguishable — hence sexual dimorphism
∴ Ascaris: Female = larger, straight. Male = smaller, curved posterior end with spicules.
Q.31
Give the importance of Zoological parks.
Importance
  • Ex-situ conservation of endangered species
  • Captive breeding programs — e.g. Indian one-horned rhinoceros, White tiger
  • Scientific research and study of animal behaviour
  • Education and public awareness about wildlife conservation
  • Rehabilitation of injured/orphaned wild animals
  • Gene banks — preserving genetic diversity
∴ Zoological parks = conservation + captive breeding + research + education.
Q.32
What is ecdysis or moulting?
Definition
Ecdysis (moulting) is the periodic shedding of the exoskeleton (cuticle) in arthropods to allow body growth. Since the exoskeleton cannot grow, it must be shed and a new larger one secreted.
Process
Old cuticle is secreted by underlying epidermis → old cuticle splits and is shed → new soft cuticle hardens → animal grows during soft stage.
Hormone
Controlled by Ecdysone hormone (moulting hormone).
∴ Ecdysis = shedding of exoskeleton for growth in arthropods, controlled by ecdysone hormone.
Q.33
Phylum Ctenophora considered as minor phylum. Explain.
Ctenophora
Called "comb jellies" — marine, radially symmetrical, diploblastic, bioluminescent organisms.
Why minor
  • Very small number of species (~100–150)
  • Exclusively marine, no freshwater or terrestrial members
  • No economic importance to humans
  • Highly specialized — limited ecological role
  • No cnidoblasts (unlike Coelenterata)
  • Move by comb plates (cilia rows = 8 comb rows)
∴ Ctenophora = minor phylum: few species (~150), marine only, no economic value, limited distribution.
🔬 Tissues
Q.34
Define tissue.
Definition
A tissue is a group of cells that are similar in structure, origin, and function, working together to perform a specific function in the body.
∴ Tissue = group of similar cells performing a specific function together.
Q.35
Write a note on Ciliated Columnar Epithelium.
Description
Tall, column-like cells with cilia on free surface. Single layer (simple) or multiple layers (compound).
Location
Respiratory tract (trachea, bronchi), fallopian tubes, uterus, ventricles of brain.
Function
Cilia beat rhythmically to move mucus, dust, and foreign particles out. Move ovum from ovary to uterus (fallopian tubes). Circulate cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).
∴ Ciliated columnar epithelium: tall cells + cilia; in trachea (move mucus), fallopian tubes (move ovum).

Q.37
What is adipose tissue? Give its function.
Definition
Adipose tissue is a type of loose connective tissue composed of fat cells (adipocytes) that store fat (triglycerides) as energy reserve.
Location
Under skin (subcutaneous), around organs (kidney, heart), in yellow bone marrow.
Functions
  • Energy storage (fat reserves)
  • Thermal insulation (reduces heat loss)
  • Cushioning and protection of organs
  • Buoyancy in aquatic mammals
∴ Adipose tissue = fat-storing cells. Functions: energy reserve, insulation, organ protection.
Q.38
Distinguish between Ammonotelism and Ureotelism.
Ammonotelism
Excretion of nitrogenous waste as ammonia. Needs large amount of water to dilute toxic ammonia. Found in aquatic animals — fish, aquatic amphibians, aquatic insects. e.g. Bony fish (teleosts).
Ureotelism
Excretion of nitrogenous waste as urea. Less toxic, needs moderate water. Found in terrestrial animals, mammals, adult amphibians. e.g. Humans, frogs (adults), dogs.
∴ Ammonotelisnm = ammonia (aquatic, fish). Ureotelism = urea (terrestrial, mammals including humans).
Q.39
Define excretion.
Definition
Excretion is the biological process of elimination of metabolic waste products (especially nitrogenous wastes) from the body of an organism to maintain homeostasis.
∴ Excretion = removal of metabolic waste (urea, CO₂, excess water/salts) from the body.
Q.40
Distinguish between Simple epithelial tissue and Compound Epithelial tissue.
Simple epithelium
Single layer of cells resting on basement membrane. Each cell touches the membrane. Types: squamous, cuboidal, columnar, ciliated. Function: absorption, secretion, filtration.
Compound epithelium
Multiple layers of cells. Only the basal layer rests on basement membrane. Provides mechanical protection. e.g. Skin epidermis (stratified squamous), oral cavity, oesophagus.
∴ Simple = one layer (absorption). Compound = many layers (protection). e.g. skin epidermis.
Q.41
Describe structure, location, and function of squamous epithelial tissue.
Structure
Flat, scale-like (squamous) cells arranged in a single layer. Cells tightly packed, very thin, irregular in shape. Nuclei centrally placed.
Location
Lining of blood vessels (endothelium), air sacs of lungs (alveoli), Bowman's capsule of kidney, skin surface (stratified when multi-layered).
Function
Diffusion of gases (lungs), filtration (kidneys), protection (skin), reduces friction (blood vessel lining).
∴ Squamous epithelium = flat cells, single layer; in lungs (gas diffusion), kidneys (filtration).
