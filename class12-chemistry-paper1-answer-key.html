Rate Law Zero order: k units = mol·L⁻¹·s⁻¹  |  First order: k units = s⁻¹  |  Second order: k units = L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹ General: k units = (mol·L⁻¹)^(1−n) · s⁻¹ for nth order
Colloids Lyophilic (solvent-loving): starch, gelatin, gum. Self-stabilising. Lyophobic (solvent-hating): Fe(OH)₃, Au sol, As₂S₃. Need stabilisers. Hardy-Schulze rule: coagulating power ∝ valency of counter-ion. Fe(OH)₃ (positive) coagulated by PO₄³⁻ > SO₄²⁻ > Cl⁻
Electrochemistry SHE potential = 0.00 V (reference). E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode. Nernst equation: E = E° − (0.0592/n) log Q   at 25°C 1 Faraday = 96500 C = charge of 1 mole of electrons.
Organic Chemistry IUPAC nomenclature: longest chain, lowest locants. (CH₃)₂CHCHO = 2-methylpropanal Hybridisation: sp³(single bonds), sp²(double), sp(triple). Ethyne C₂H₂ = sp (linear, 180°) SN1: tertiary substrate → carbocation intermediate → racemic product SN2: primary substrate → backside attack → Walden inversion → single stereoisomer
Polymers Addition polymers: polythene (ethylene), PVC (vinyl chloride), polypropylene (propylene) Condensation polymers: Nylon-6,6 (adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine), Dacron (ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid) Bakelite (thermosetting), PVC/nylon (thermoplastic)
Biomolecules Glucose = aldohexose (aldehyde group, 6 carbons). Reducing sugar (free anomeric C). Sucrose = non-reducing disaccharide (both anomeric C involved in glycosidic bond). Fehling's test: aldehydes → Cu₂O (red precipitate). Tollens': Ag mirror.
Coordination Ionisation isomers: [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Cl — differ in ions inside/outside coordination sphere Geometrical isomers: cis/trans in square planar and octahedral complexes EAN rule: Z − oxidation state + (2 × CN) = noble gas configuration (stable)

Define mole fraction. Write its formula.
DefinitionMole fraction = moles of component / total moles of all components.
Formulax_A = n_A/(n_A + n_B)   Dimensionless, range 0 to 1, x_A + x_B = 1
Q.2 (ii) [1 Mark]
What is activation energy? How does a catalyst affect it?
Catalyst lowers activation energy (Ea) — more molecules can react at same temperature
Activation EnergyMinimum extra energy that reactant molecules must possess (above their average energy) to form products.
Catalyst EffectCatalyst provides an alternate pathway with lower Ea. More molecules can react → faster rate. ΔH of reaction unchanged.
Q.2 (iii) [1 Mark]
State Henry's law. Write its mathematical expression.
StatementAt constant temperature, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid.
Formula
p = K_H × x   (K_H = Henry's law constant)
Q.2 (iv) [1 Mark]
What is the Tyndall effect? In which type of colloid is it observed?
DefinitionScattering of a beam of visible light by colloidal particles (1–100 nm), making the path of light visible as a cone (Tyndall cone).
Observed inBoth lyophilic and lyophobic colloids. NOT in true solutions (particles too small to scatter light).

Q.3 (i) [2 Marks]
Derive the integrated rate law for a first-order reaction.
First-order reaction: plot of ln[A] vs t is a straight line (slope = −k)
Rate Law−d[A]/dt = k[A]
Integration d[A]/[A] = −k dt → ∫d[A]/[A] = −k∫dt ln[A] = −kt + ln[A]₀ → [A] = [A]₀ e^(−kt) Or: k = (2.303/t) log([A]₀/[A])
Half-life
t₁/₂ = 0.693/k (independent of initial concentration)
Q.3 (iii) [2 Marks]
State and explain Raoult's law for an ideal solution.
Raoult's law: partial pressure ∝ mole fraction (ideal solution)
StatementThe partial vapour pressure of each component in an ideal solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction.
Formulap_A = p°_A × x_A  |  P_total = p°_A x_A + p°_B x_B
Ideal SolutionΔH_mix = 0, ΔV_mix = 0. Examples: benzene + toluene, hexane + heptane.

Q.3 (ix) [2 Marks]
Write the open chain structure of glucose. Show its reactions with HCN and Tollens' reagent.
Open chain (Fischer projection) of glucose: CHO at top, CH₂OH at bottom
With HCN C₆H₁₂O₆ + HCN → C₆H₁₂O₆·(CN)(OH) (cyanohydrin — nucleophilic addition, chain elongation)
With Tollens' C₆H₁₂O₆ + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 2OH⁻ → C₆H₁₂O₇ + 2Ag↓ + 4NH₃ Silver mirror formed → confirms aldehyde group → reducing sugar.
Q.3 (xii) [2 Marks]
Derive the expression for elevation of boiling point: ΔTb = Kb × m.
BasisNon-volatile solute lowers vapour pressure → solution must be heated to higher temperature to reach atmospheric pressure → boiling point rises.
Result ΔTb = Kb × m = Kb × (w_B × 1000)/(M_B × w_A) Kb = ebullioscopic constant (water: 0.52 K·kg/mol)
Molecular mass
M_B = (Kb × w_B × 1000)/(ΔTb × w_A)
Section D — Medium Answers 6 × 3 = 18 Marks
Q.4 (i) [3 Marks]
Write the first-order rate constant formula. Show t₁/₂ = 0.693/k. Calculate t₁/₂ when k = 2.31 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
Formulak = (2.303/t) × log([A]₀/[A])
Proof At t₁/₂: [A] = [A]₀/2 → k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log 2 = (2.303 × 0.3010)/t₁/₂ = 0.693/t₁/₂
t₁/₂ = 0.693/k (independent of [A]₀)
Numerical k = 2.31 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ t₁/₂ = 0.693/(2.31×10⁻³) = 300 s = 5 min
Q.4 (viii) [3 Marks]
Explain Werner's theory of coordination compounds. Calculate EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻.
Werner's Theory (1) Metal has primary valency (= oxidation state, ionisable) and secondary valency (= coordination number, non-ionisable) (2) Secondary valency satisfied by ligands directly bonded to metal — directional, determines geometry.
EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ Fe²⁺ (oxidation state +2). Atomic number of Fe = 26 EAN = (26 − 2) + (6 × 2) = 24 + 12 = 36 = Kr (noble gas configuration)
EAN = 36 ✓ (confirms stability — noble gas configuration)
Q.4 (vi) [3 Marks]
Derive ΔTf = Kf × m. Explain how molecular mass is determined from depression of freezing point.
Colligative properties: solute lowers VP → raises boiling point (ΔTf) and lowers freezing point (ΔTf)
Derivation Non-volatile solute lowers vapour pressure → solid-liquid equilibrium at lower temperature → ΔTf ΔTf = Kf × m = Kf × (w_B × 1000)/(M_B × w_A)
Molecular Mass
M_B = (Kf × w_B × 1000)/(ΔTf × w_A)
Kf for water = 1.86 K·kg/mol. Measure ΔTf experimentally → calculate M_B.

Q.5 (i) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain preparation of colloids by chemical method and electrical disintegration. (b) Describe the properties: Brownian motion, Tyndall effect and electrophoresis.
Left: Brownian motion (random collisions) | Right: Tyndall effect (light scattering)
Chemical Methods Reduction: AuCl₃ + formaldehyde → Au sol Hydrolysis: FeCl₃ + 3H₂O (boiling) → Fe(OH)₃ sol Double decomposition: As₂O₃ + H₂S → As₂S₃ sol + H₂O
Bredig's ArcMetal electrodes in dispersion medium. Electric arc vaporises metal → condenses into colloidal particles.
Brownian MotionContinuous zig-zag random motion of colloidal particles due to unequal bombardment by solvent molecules. Prevents sedimentation → stabilises colloid.
Tyndall EffectScattering of visible light by colloidal particles (1–100 nm) making the path visible. Not seen in true solutions.
ElectrophoresisMigration of charged colloidal particles towards opposite electrode under electric field. Used in Cottrell precipitator (smoke removal), gel electrophoresis (DNA separation).
Q.5 (ii) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain the electrolytic cell and Faraday's laws of electrolysis. (b) Calculate the mass of copper deposited when 0.5 F of charge is passed through CuSO₄ solution. (Cu = 63.5)
Electrolytic cell: Cu²⁺ ions migrate to cathode (reduction), SO₄²⁻ to anode
Faraday's 1st Lawm = ZQ = ZIt (mass ∝ quantity of electricity)
Faraday's 2nd LawSame charge deposits masses ∝ equivalent masses
Numerical Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. Q = 0.5 F. Cu = 63.5 g/mol Equivalent mass of Cu = 63.5/2 = 31.75 g m = (E/F) × Q = 31.75 × 0.5 = 15.875 g
Mass of Cu deposited = 15.875 g ≈ 15.9 g
