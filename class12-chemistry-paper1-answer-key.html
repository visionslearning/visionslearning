Q.3 (i) [2 Marks]
Derive the integrated rate law for a first-order reaction.
First-order reaction: plot of ln[A] vs t is a straight line (slope = −k)
Definition A first-order reaction is one in which the rate depends on the first power of the concentration of one reactant. Rate = k[A]¹  →  −d[A]/dt = k[A]
Derivation From rate law: d[A]/[A] = −k dt Integrating both sides: ∫[A₀ to A] d[A]/[A] = −k ∫[0 to t] dt ln[A] − ln[A]₀ = −kt ln[A]₀/[A] = kt  ⟹  k = (2.303/t) log([A]₀/[A]) Exponential form: [A] = [A]₀ e^(−kt)
Half-Life (t₁/₂) At t = t₁/₂: [A] = [A]₀/2 Substituting: k·t₁/₂ = ln([A]₀/[A]₀/2) = ln 2 = 0.693 t₁/₂ = 0.693/k — constant; independent of initial concentration [A]₀
Graph Plot of ln[A] vs t → straight line with slope = −k, y-intercept = ln[A]₀ Plot of [A] vs t → exponential decay curve
Units of k For first-order reaction: units of k = s⁻¹ (or min⁻¹, hr⁻¹)
Examples
First-order reactions: Decomposition of N₂O₅, radioactive decay, hydrolysis of sucrose (pseudo first-order). t₁/₂ = 0.693/k (constant at all [A]₀)
Q.3 (iii) [2 Marks]
State and explain Raoult's law for an ideal solution.
Raoult's law: partial pressure ∝ mole fraction (ideal solution)
Statement Raoult's Law: At constant temperature, the partial vapour pressure of each volatile component of an ideal solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the solution. p_A = p°_A × x_A    p_B = p°_B × x_B where p°_A = vapour pressure of pure A, x_A = mole fraction of A in solution.
Total Vapour Pressure By Dalton's law of partial pressures: P_total = p_A + p_B = p°_A x_A + p°_B x_B Since x_A + x_B = 1:   P_total = p°_B + (p°_A − p°_B) x_A This is a linear equation — P_total varies linearly with x_A between p°_B and p°_A.
Ideal Solution An ideal solution obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations. Conditions: ΔH_mix = 0 (no heat evolved/absorbed), ΔV_mix = 0 (no volume change) Intermolecular forces A-A ≈ B-B ≈ A-B (similar molecules) Examples: benzene + toluene, hexane + heptane, chlorobenzene + bromobenzene
Non-Ideal Solutions Positive deviation: A-B attraction < A-A or B-B → P_total > ideal (e.g., ethanol + water) Negative deviation: A-B attraction > A-A or B-B → P_total < ideal (e.g., chloroform + acetone)
Significance
Raoult's law governs all colligative properties: vapour pressure lowering (ΔP = p°_A × x_B), boiling point elevation (ΔTb = Kb × m), freezing point depression (ΔTf = Kf × m), osmotic pressure (π = CRT).

Q.4 (viii) [3 Marks]
Explain Werner's theory of coordination compounds. Calculate EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻.
Werner's Theory (1) Metal has primary valency (= oxidation state, ionisable) and secondary valency (= coordination number, non-ionisable) (2) Secondary valency satisfied by ligands directly bonded to metal — directional, determines geometry.
EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ Fe²⁺ (oxidation state +2). Atomic number of Fe = 26 EAN = (26 − 2) + (6 × 2) = 24 + 12 = 36 = Kr (noble gas configuration)

Q.5 (ii) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain the electrolytic cell and Faraday's laws of electrolysis. (b) Calculate the mass of copper deposited when 0.5 F of charge is passed through CuSO₄ solution. (Cu = 63.5)
Fig: Electrolytic cell — external battery forces non-spontaneous reaction. Cathode (−): Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (reduction). Anode (+): H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (oxidation).
Electrolytic Cell An electrolytic cell converts electrical energy into chemical energy. Components: electrolyte solution, cathode (connected to −ve terminal), anode (connected to +ve terminal), external battery/power source. Cathode (reduction): Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu  (cations migrate to cathode, get reduced) Anode (oxidation): Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻  (if Cu anode) or 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻  (if Pt anode)
Faraday's First Law Statement: The mass of substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode during electrolysis is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (charge) passed. m = Z × Q = Z × I × t where m = mass deposited, Z = electrochemical equivalent, Q = charge (coulombs), I = current (A), t = time (s) Z = M/(n × F) where M = molar mass, n = number of electrons, F = Faraday constant (96500 C/mol)
Faraday's Second Law Statement: When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes, the masses of substances deposited are proportional to their equivalent masses (gram equivalent weights). m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂    where E = M/n (equivalent mass)
Numerical Solution Given: Q = 0.5 F, Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, Molar mass of Cu = 63.5 g/mol 1 Faraday deposits: M/n = 63.5/2 = 31.75 g of Cu Mass of Cu = 31.75 × 0.5 = 15.875 g
Mass of copper deposited = 15.875 g ≈ 15.9 g
Q.5 (v) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain the DNA double helix structure — base pairing rules. (b) Write four differences between DNA and RNA.
Fig: DNA double helix (Watson-Crick, 1953) — two antiparallel strands. Complementary base pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds). 3.4 nm per turn = 10 bp.
Discovery DNA double helix model proposed by James Watson and Francis Crick (1953), based on X-ray diffraction data by Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins. Nobel Prize in Physiology/Medicine 1962 — Watson, Crick and Wilkins.
Structure DNA is a double-stranded polynucleotide — two antiparallel chains wound helically around a common axis. Each nucleotide = phosphate group + deoxyribose sugar + nitrogenous base. Backbone: Sugar-phosphate alternating along the outside of the helix. Bases (inside): Purines (Adenine, Guanine) and Pyrimidines (Thymine, Cytosine)
Base Pairing Rules Chargaff's rules: A always pairs with T; G always pairs with C. A = T (connected by 2 hydrogen bonds)    G ≡ C (connected by 3 hydrogen bonds) G-C pair is stronger (3 H-bonds) → organisms with higher G-C content are more heat-resistant. Two chains are antiparallel — one runs 5'→3', the other 3'→5'.
Dimensions Pitch (one complete turn): 3.4 nm (34 Å) Base pairs per turn: 10 (each base pair = 0.34 nm apart) Diameter of helix: 2 nm (20 Å)
DNA vs RNA DNA: double-stranded | Sugar = deoxyribose | Bases: A, T, G, C | Location: mainly nucleus RNA: single-stranded | Sugar = ribose | Bases: A, U, G, C | Location: nucleus + cytoplasm DNA stores genetic info; RNA carries instructions for protein synthesis (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA).
Significance
The double helix explains: (1) DNA replication (complementary base pairing), (2) transcription (unwinding), (3) mutation (base pair changes). Central to molecular biology.
