The unit of rate constant for a zero-order reaction is:

Q.1 (ii)
An example of a lyophilic colloid is:
✔ Answer: (C) starch
Explanation Lyophilic = solvent-loving colloids. Disperse readily in solvent; self-stabilising. Examples: starch, gelatin, gum, protein, egg white in water Lyophobic = solvent-hating: gold sol, Fe(OH)₃, As₂S₃ — need stabilisers

Q.2 (i)
Define mole fraction. Write its formula.
DefinitionMole fraction of a component is the ratio of the number of moles of that component to the total number of moles of all components in the mixture.
Formulax_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B)   x_A + x_B = 1 (for two-component system)
NoteMole fraction has no units. It is dimensionless. Range: 0 to 1.
Q.2 (ii)
What is activation energy? How does a catalyst affect it?
DefinitionActivation energy (Ea) is the minimum extra energy that the reactant molecules must possess over the average energy for them to react and form products.
Catalyst EffectA catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. The catalyst is not consumed. More molecules can now reach the lower Ea → faster reaction. (ΔH of reaction unchanged)
Q.2 (iii)
State Henry's law. Write its mathematical expression.
StatementAt constant temperature, the solubility (mole fraction x) of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure (p) of the gas above the liquid.
Expressionp = K_H × x   where K_H = Henry's law constant (depends on gas, solvent, temperature)
ApplicationWhy soda fizzes when opened (CO₂ comes out of solution as pressure decreases). Decompression sickness in divers.
Q.2 (iv)
What is the Tyndall effect? In which type of colloid is it observed?
DefinitionThe scattering of a beam of light by colloidal particles (size 1–100 nm), making the path of light visible, is called the Tyndall effect. The illuminated path is called the Tyndall cone.
Observed inBoth lyophilic and lyophobic colloids (but NOT in true solutions — particles too small)
ExamplesSunlight through clouds, headlights in fog, beam of light through colloidal gold sol
Q.2 (v)
Define standard electrode potential.
DefinitionStandard electrode potential (E°) is the EMF of a half-cell under standard conditions (ion concentration = 1M, temperature = 25°C, pressure = 1 atm) measured against the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE, E° = 0.00V).
SignificanceUsed to calculate: cell EMF (E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode), ΔG° = −nFE°, equilibrium constant K
Q.2 (vi)
Define order of reaction. Can it be fractional?
DefinitionOrder of reaction is the sum of the powers (exponents) of the concentration terms in the experimentally determined rate law.
FormulaRate = k[A]^m[B]^n → Order = m + n
Can be fractionalYes. Example: H₂ + Br₂ → 2HBr has order = 3/2 (1.5) — experimentally determined. Order can be 0, 1, 2, 3, or even fractional; it is NOT necessarily related to stoichiometric coefficients.

Q.2 (xii)
State Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions.
StatementAt infinite dilution, the molar conductance of an electrolyte (Λ°_m) is equal to the sum of the molar ionic conductances of its constituent cations and anions, each ion contributing independently.
FormulaΛ°_m = ν₊λ°₊ + ν₋λ°₋   where ν = number of ions, λ° = molar ionic conductance
ApplicationFinding Λ°_m of weak electrolytes (like acetic acid) which cannot be measured directly by extrapolation.

Q.3 (i)
Derive the integrated rate law for a first-order reaction.
Rate Law−d[A]/dt = k[A]
Integration Separating variables: d[A]/[A] = −k dt Integrating: ∫_[A]₀^[A] d[A]/[A] = −k∫₀^t dt ln[A] − ln[A]₀ = −kt
Result
ln([A]₀/[A]) = kt   or   [A] = [A]₀ × e^(−kt)
Graph of ln[A] vs t: straight line with slope = −k and intercept = ln[A]₀
Q.3 (ii)
Explain preparation of colloids by peptisation.
DefinitionPeptisation is the process of converting a freshly prepared precipitate (suspension) into a colloid by addition of a small amount of a suitable electrolyte (peptising agent).
Mechanism The peptising agent (electrolyte) is adsorbed by the precipitate particles. This gives the particles an electric charge → they repel each other → remain dispersed as colloidal particles.
Example Fe(OH)₃ precipitate + FeCl₃ → Fe(OH)₃ sol (reddish brown) Fe³⁺ ions are adsorbed → positive charge → stable colloid
Q.3 (iii)
State and explain Raoult's law for an ideal solution.
StatementThe partial vapour pressure of each component of an ideal solution at constant temperature is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the solution.
Formulap_A = p°_A × x_A   and   p_B = p°_B × x_B
Total pressure: P_total = p_A + p_B = p°_A x_A + p°_B x_B
Ideal SolutionΔH_mix = 0, ΔV_mix = 0, follows Raoult's law exactly. Example: benzene + toluene, hexane + heptane.

AdvantagesNo pollutants (only water produced), high efficiency (~70%), continuous supply of H₂ and O₂. Used in space shuttles, fuel cell vehicles.
Q.3 (v)
Explain the SN1 reaction mechanism with a suitable example.
DefinitionSN1 = Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular. Two-step mechanism; rate depends only on substrate concentration.
Mechanism Step 1 (slow, rate-determining): R–X → R⁺ (carbocation) + X⁻ (ionisation) Step 2 (fast): R⁺ + Nu⁻ → R–Nu (nucleophile attacks planar carbocation) Rate = k[R–X] (first order, unimolecular)
Example (CH₃)₃C–Br + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃C–OH + Br⁻ Favoured by tertiary substrates and polar protic solvents Produces racemic mixture (attack from both sides of flat carbocation)

Q.4 (i)
First-order rate constant formula. Show t₁/₂ = 0.693/k. Calculate t₁/₂ when k = 2.31×10⁻³ s⁻¹.
Rate Constantk = (2.303/t) × log([A]₀/[A])
Half-life Derivation At t = t₁/₂: [A] = [A]₀/2 k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log([A]₀/([A]₀/2)) = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log 2 k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × 0.3010 = 0.693/t₁/₂

t₁/₂ = 300 s = 5 minutes

Q.4 (vi)
Derive ΔTf = Kf × m. Explain how molecular mass is determined.
Derivation When non-volatile solute is added, vapour pressure decreases → solid-liquid equilibrium shifts → solution freezes at lower temperature. ΔTf = Tf° − Tf = Kf × m Kf = RT²_f M_A/1000 × ΔH_fus   (cryoscopic constant)
Result
ΔTf = Kf × m = Kf × (w_B × 1000)/(M_B × w_A)
Molecular Mass M_B = (Kf × w_B × 1000)/(ΔTf × w_A) By measuring ΔTf experimentally, unknown molar mass M_B can be calculated.

Q.4 (viii)
Explain Werner's theory of coordination compounds. Calculate EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻.
Werner's Theory 1. Primary valency: Electrovalent/ionic valency = oxidation state of metal. Satisfied by negative ions outside coordination sphere. 2. Secondary valency: Covalent/coordination valency = CN. Directed in space → determines geometry. Satisfied by ligands inside coordination sphere.
EAN Calculation [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻: Fe = Fe²⁺ (oxidation state +2), 6 CN⁻ ligands (each donates 2 electrons) Atomic number of Fe = 26 EAN = (26 − 2) + (6 × 2) = 24 + 12 = 36
Answer
EAN = 36 = Krypton (noble gas configuration) — confirms stability

Q.5 (i)
(a) Preparation of colloids by chemical method and electrical disintegration. (b) Brownian motion, Tyndall effect and electrophoresis.
Chemical Method Reduction: AuCl₃ + formaldehyde → Au sol (gold colloid) Oxidation: H₂S + H₂O → S sol (with SO₂ as oxidant) Double decomposition: As₂O₃ + H₂S → As₂S₃ sol + H₂O Hydrolysis: FeCl₃ + 3H₂O → Fe(OH)₃ sol + 3HCl (Bredig's method)
Bredig's Arc (Electrical) Metal electrodes (Au, Ag, Pt) in dispersion medium. Electric arc struck between tips. Metal vaporises → condenses into colloidal particles in the cold medium.
Brownian MotionContinuous zig-zag random motion of colloidal particles due to unequal bombardment by solvent molecules. Prevents settling — stabilises the colloid.
Tyndall EffectScattering of a beam of light by colloidal particles (1–100 nm), making the path of light visible. Not seen in true solutions.
ElectrophoresisMigration of charged colloidal particles towards the electrode of opposite charge when an electric field is applied. Used in determination of charge on colloid, purification (electric precipitation of smoke).
