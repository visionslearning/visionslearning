SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10
(i) Unit of rate constant for zero-order reaction
Answer: (A) mol·L⁻¹·s⁻¹
Zero-order: rate = k[A]⁰ = k → unit of k = unit of rate = mol·L⁻¹·s⁻¹
(ii) Example of lyophilic colloid
Answer: (C) starch
Lyophilic = solvent-loving. Starch, gelatin, gum arabic, protein sols are lyophilic. Lyophobic (solvent-hating): gold sol, As₂S₃, Fe(OH)₃
(iii) Standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) potential
Answer: (C) 0 V
SHE is the reference electrode with E° = 0.00 V by convention (H₂/H⁺, 1 atm, 1M)
(iv) IUPAC name of (CH₃)₂CHCHO
Answer: (A) 2-methylpropanal
Structure: CH₃-CH(CH₃)-CHO → longest chain = 3C (propanal), methyl at C2 → 2-methylpropanal
(v) Bulletproof glass polymer
Answer: (B) Lexan
Lexan = polycarbonate polymer → used in bulletproof glass (very tough, transparent) Kevlar = body armour  |  Teflon = non-stick coating  |  Bakelite = thermosetting
(vi) p-aminobenzenesulphonic acid common name
Answer: (A) sulphanilic acid
p-aminobenzenesulphonic acid = sulphanilic acid (used in dye industry, synthesis of azo dyes)
(vii) One Faraday equivalent
Answer: (C) 1 mole of electrons
1 Faraday = 96500 C = charge of 1 mole of electrons (N_A × e = 6.023×10²³ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 96500 C)
(viii) Non-reducing sugar
Answer: (C) sucrose
Sucrose: no free aldehyde/ketone group (both anomeric carbons are linked) → non-reducing Glucose, fructose, maltose: reducing sugars (free aldehyde/ketone present)
(ix) Ionisation isomers
Answer: (B) [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Cl
Ionisation isomers differ in ions inside and outside coordination sphere. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ → Cl inside, SO₄ outside  |  [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Cl → SO₄ inside, Cl outside
(x) Sodium reacts with ethanol
Answer: (B) H₂
2Na + 2C₂H₅OH → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑ (sodium ethoxide + hydrogen gas)
SECTION B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8
(i) Define mole fraction
Mole fraction of a component = moles of that component / total moles of all components in solution. x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B)   x_A + x_B = 1   No unit
(ii) Activation energy and catalyst
Activation energy (Ea): Minimum energy that reactant molecules must possess to undergo chemical reaction. Catalyst: Lowers activation energy by providing an alternate reaction pathway → increases rate of reaction.
(iii) Henry's law
At constant temperature, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid. p = K_H × x   where K_H = Henry's law constant, x = mole fraction of dissolved gas
(iv) Tyndall effect
Tyndall effect: Scattering of light by colloidal particles (size 1–100 nm) making the path of light visible. Observed in: lyophobic colloids, colloidal sols. NOT in true solutions (particles too small).
(v) Standard electrode potential
The EMF of a half-cell under standard conditions (1M concentration, 1 atm pressure, 25°C) measured against SHE (reference, E° = 0).
(vi) Order of reaction
Order of reaction = sum of powers of concentration terms in the experimentally determined rate law. Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n → Order = m + n   Yes, order can be fractional (e.g., H₂ + Br₂ reaction is 3/2 order)
(vii) IUPAC name of CH₃COCH₂CH₃
CH₃-CO-CH₂-CH₃ → ketone group at C2 in a 4-carbon chain Butan-2-one (or methyl ethyl ketone)
(viii) Saponification
Saponification = alkaline hydrolysis of an ester (especially fats/oils) to give soap (fatty acid salt) and glycerol. Fat + NaOH → Soap (sodium salt of fatty acid) + Glycerol
SECTION C — Short Answers (selected) 8 × 2 = 16
(i) Integrated rate law — first order reaction
Rate: dx/dt = k(a−x) → dx/(a−x) = k dt Integrating: −ln(a−x) = kt + C  |  At t=0, x=0 → C = −ln(a) k = (1/t) ln[a/(a−x)]   OR   k = (2.303/t) log[a/(a−x)] Graph of log(a−x) vs t is a straight line → confirms first order
(iii) Raoult's law for ideal solution
Statement: The vapour pressure of a component in an ideal solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction. p_A = p°_A × x_A   |   p_B = p°_B × x_B Total pressure: p_total = p_A + p_B = p°_A x_A + p°_B x_B Ideal solution: ΔH_mix = 0, ΔV_mix = 0, follows Raoult's law
(xii) ΔTb = Kb × m derivation
When non-volatile solute is added, vapour pressure decreases → boiling point rises. ΔTb = Tb − Tb° = Kb × m where Kb = ebullioscopic constant = RT²M_A/1000L_v (L_v = latent heat) m = molality = (w_B × 1000)/(M_B × w_A)   ΔTb ∝ molality
SECTION D — Medium Answers (selected) 6 × 3 = 18
(i) First-order half-life t₁/₂ = 0.693/k
From first-order: k = (2.303/t) × log[a/(a−x)] At t₁/₂, x = a/2 → a−x = a/2 k = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log[a/(a/2)] = (2.303/t₁/₂) × log 2 = (2.303 × 0.3010)/t₁/₂ t₁/₂ = 0.693/k Given: k = 2.31×10⁻³ s⁻¹ t₁/₂ = 0.693/(2.31×10⁻³) = 300 s
(viii) Werner's theory + EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻
Werner's theory: (1) Metal has primary valency (ionic, = oxidation state) and secondary valency (coordination number, directional). (2) Secondary valency is satisfied by ligands. (3) Geometry of complex depends on coordination number. EAN for [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻: Fe is Fe²⁺ (oxidation state = +2) Atomic number of Fe = 26 EAN = (26 − 2) + 2×6 = 24 + 12 = 36 (= Kr, noble gas configuration ✓)
SECTION E — Long Answers (selected) 4 × 4 = 16
(ii) Faraday's laws + copper deposition
1st Law: Mass of substance deposited ∝ quantity of electricity passed: m = ZQ = ZIt 2nd Law: Same quantity of electricity deposits equivalent masses of different substances: m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂ Calculation: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu   Equivalent mass of Cu = 63.5/2 = 31.75 g Q = 0.5 F = 0.5 × 96500 C m = (E/F) × Q = (31.75/96500) × (0.5×96500) = 31.75 × 0.5 m = 15.875 g of copper deposited
(vi) Osmosis + Van't Hoff + numerical
Osmosis: Flow of solvent from dilute to concentrated solution through semipermeable membrane. Van't Hoff law: π = CRT (similar to ideal gas) → π = (n/V)RT Given: 6 g urea in 100 g water, M_urea = 60 Moles of urea = 6/60 = 0.1 mol  |  Moles of water = 100/18 = 5.56 mol Mole fraction of urea: x = 0.1/(0.1 + 5.56) = 0.1/5.66 = 0.0177 Molality: m = (0.1 × 1000)/100 = 1 mol/kg
