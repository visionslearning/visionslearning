Extract from: 1.2 The Swiss Family Robinson (Adapted)

When the storm finally passed, the ship lay wrecked on the rocks. There was no other ship in sight, no shore visible — only the wild, open sea in all directions. The father looked at his wife and four sons and made a silent decision: they would not give up.

Using ropes, barrels, and broken timber from the ship, they built a raft, large enough to carry the family and their most important belongings. The boys worked with surprising energy, forgetting their fear in the excitement of doing something. By evening, the raft was ready.

Q.3 — Unseen Passage10 Marks
Read the following passage carefully and do the activities.[10 Marks]
Unseen Passage

Every year, millions of sea turtles are born on sandy beaches around the world. As soon as the eggs hatch, the tiny turtles begin their dangerous journey to the sea. They must cross the beach quickly because birds, crabs, and other animals are always waiting to catch them. Only a few out of every hundred will survive to reach the water.

Once in the ocean, sea turtles spend their lives travelling across thousands of kilometres of open water. They eat jellyfish, seagrass, and small fish. What is most remarkable is that female sea turtles, when they are ready to lay their eggs, always return to the very same beach where they were born. Scientists believe they use the earth's magnetic field to find their way.

Today, sea turtles face many dangers — fishing nets, plastic waste in the ocean, and the destruction of their nesting beaches by human activity. Many countries have passed laws to protect them. Volunteers guard nesting beaches at night and help baby turtles reach the sea safely. Every effort counts when a species is fighting for survival.

Q.4 — Seen Prose (Unit 2) + Vocabulary16 Marks
(A) Read the following extract and do the activities.[10 Marks]
Extract from: 2.3 Somebody's Mother (Adapted)

The woman was old and ragged and grey, and bent with the chill of a winter's day. The street was wet with a recent snow, and the woman's feet were aged and slow. She stood at the crossing, trembling with cold, trying to cross the busy road in the sleet and cold. Nobody seemed to notice her. Cars rushed past and people hurried on.

Then a young boy stopped. He was probably on his way to school. He did not hurry past like the others. Instead, he walked up to the old woman, offered her his arm, and helped her carefully across the road. The old woman thanked him with a tired smile.

"She's somebody's mother," the boy thought, "and if my mother were old and alone on a street, I would want somebody to help her too."

Acts of kindness cost nothing, but they mean everything to those who receive them. The boy who helped that old woman that day did not know her name, her story, or where she lived — but he knew what it meant to be human.
