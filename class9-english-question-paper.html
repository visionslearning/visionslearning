When the storm finally died down, the ship lay wrecked on the sharp rocks. There was no land visible — only the open, wild sea in every direction. The father looked at his wife and four sons and made a silent decision: they would not give up.

Using ropes, barrels, and broken timber from the ship, they built a raft large enough to carry the family and their most important belongings. The boys worked with surprising energy, forgetting their fear in the excitement of building something useful. By evening, the raft was ready.

Life on the island was hard but rewarding. The family learned to hunt, to farm, and to live in harmony with nature. The mother, who had been the quietest on the ship, became the strongest on the island.

The woman was old and ragged and grey, bent with the chill of a winter's day. The street was wet with fresh snow, and the woman's feet were slow and uncertain. She stood at the crossing, trembling with cold, trying to cross the road while cars rushed past and people hurried on without a glance.

Passage — Unseen

Every year, millions of sea turtles hatch on sandy beaches around the world. As soon as the eggs crack open, the tiny turtles begin a dangerous race to the sea. They must cross the beach quickly, because seabirds, crabs, and other creatures are waiting to catch them. Only a few out of every hundred will survive to reach the water.

Once in the ocean, sea turtles travel thousands of kilometres across open water. They feed on jellyfish, seagrass, and small fish. What is most astonishing is that female sea turtles, when they are ready to lay eggs, always return to the very beach where they were born. Scientists believe they use the earth's magnetic field to navigate back.

Today, sea turtles face serious threats — fishing nets, plastic waste in the ocean, and the destruction of their nesting beaches by human activity and construction. Many countries have passed laws to protect them. Volunteers patrol nesting beaches at night, guiding baby turtles safely to the sea. Every small effort, every piece of plastic picked up, every nest protected, counts when a species is fighting for survival.

(B) Read the following passage and do the activities.[8 Marks]
Passage — Unseen (2)

Trees are more than just wood and leaves. They are the lungs of our planet. A single large tree can absorb up to 22 kilograms of carbon dioxide per year and release enough oxygen for two people to breathe. In cities, trees lower temperatures by providing shade and releasing water vapour. Streets with trees are cooler by up to 5 degrees Celsius compared to treeless ones.

Trees also hold the soil together, preventing erosion during heavy rains. Their roots act like sponges, absorbing rainwater and slowly releasing it into the ground, which prevents floods and keeps wells and rivers full. A forest is like a giant water tank hidden underground.

Despite all this, forests around the world are disappearing at an alarming rate. An area of forest the size of a football field is cut down every single second. The reasons are many — farming, construction, and fuel. But the solutions are also available. Planting trees, reducing paper waste, and supporting forest protection laws are all within our reach. Every tree planted is a step towards a healthier planet.
