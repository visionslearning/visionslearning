🔬 5.1–5.4 Cell Basics, Cell Theory, Totipotency, Scientists
Definition of Cell Cell is the structural and functional unit of life capable of independent existence and can perform all functions of life.

Simple Microscope

Magnifies 50–100×; for larger cells

Compound Microscope

1000× or more; uses beam of light

Electron Microscope ★

Magnifies up to 500,000×; to see interior of cell

Smallest cell

Mycoplasma (0.3 µm)

Largest cell

Ostrich egg (~15 cm)

Longest cell

Nerve cell (Neuron)

Cell Theory ★ Schleiden (1838) — German botanist; plant tissues composed of cells
Schwann (1839) — British zoologist; animals & plants made of cells
Virchow (1855) — new cells arise from pre-existing cells: Omnis cellula-e-cellulla ★
Postulates of Modern Cell Theory 1. All living organisms are made up of cells.
2. Living cells arise from pre-existing cells.
3. Cell is the structural and functional unit of life.
4. Total activities of cells = activity of organism.
5. Cells show transformation of energy.
6. Cells contain DNA & RNA in nucleus and cytoplasm.
Totipotency & Stem Cells ★ Totipotency: Capacity of a living nucleated cell to differentiate and divide to form any other type of cell and thereby a complete new organism.
• Cell is totipotent because it has the entire genetic information in its nucleus
• Embryonic animal cells are totipotent → called Stem Cells
• Stem cells have great medical applications including cure for diseases ★

• Middle → Cell Wall

🧱 5.7–5.8 Cell Wall & Plasma Membrane (Fluid Mosaic Model)
Cell Wall (Eukaryotic) ★ Rigid, supportive, protective outer covering; present in plant cells, fungi and some protists. Animal cells do NOT have cell wall.
• Microfibrils — cellulose → responsible for rigidity ★
• Plasmodesmata — cytoplasmic bridges between neighbouring cells ★
3 Layers: Middle Lamella (thinnest; pectin; softening of ripe fruit ★) → Primary Wall (capable of growth) → Secondary Wall (inner to primary; forms pits)
Fluid Mosaic Model ★★ MOST IMPORTANT — Singer & Nicholson (1972) • Thickness → 75 Å | Under electron microscope → trilamellate (3 layers) ★
• Contains lipids (mostly phospholipids) arranged in bilayer
• Phospholipids are amphipathic — one hydrophilic polar head + two hydrophobic non-polar tails ★
• Proteins like 'icebergs in sea of lipids'
• Intrinsic / Transmembrane proteins — span entire membrane; form channels
• Extrinsic / Peripheral proteins — found on two surfaces
• Lateral movement of proteins → measured as fluidity
Transport across Plasma Membrane Passive Transport — no energy; along concentration gradient (higher → lower); neutral solutes by simple diffusion; water by osmosis
Active Transport — against concentration gradient (lower → higher); requires ATP; e.g. Na⁺/K⁺ pump ★
⭐ Passive = no Power needed | Active = ATP needed. Fluid Mosaic Model = Singer & Nicholson 1972 ★★ (most asked)
🌐 5.9–5.11 Endomembrane System, ER & Golgi Complex
Endomembrane System ★ Nuclear membrane + ER + Golgi complex + Lysosomes + vesicles and vacuoles
NOT included: Mitochondria and Chloroplasts (carry out specific independent energy conversions)
Flow: Nuclear envelope → ER → Transport Vesicles → Golgi (cis → trans) → Lysosomes / Vacuoles / Secretory Vesicles → Plasma Membrane
FeatureRER (Rough ER)SER (Smooth ER)
RibosomesPresent (studded) ★Absent
FunctionProtein synthesis; secretory proteins (e.g. insulin from pancreas)Lipid/steroid synthesis; detoxification (liver); Ca²⁺ storage (muscle) ★
Golgi Complex ★ = assembly + manufacturing + packaging + transport unit of cell = Post Office of the cell
• Consists of stacks of membranous sacs = cisternae (diameter 0.5–1 µm)
• Each cisterna: cis face (forming/receiving from ER) and trans face (maturing/secreting) ★
• Cisternae in Golgi are NOT physically connected to each other (unlike ER)
• Cisternal Maturation Model — cisternae themselves mature moving from cis to trans face ★
• Markers get imprinted on vesicle membrane to identify specific target cell/organelle ★
⭐ Trick: RER = Rough = Ribosomes (protein) | SER = Smooth = Steroids & Storage | CIS = Come IN | TRANS = Transfer OUT
💊 5.12–5.13 Lysosomes & Vacuoles
Lysosomes ★★ = Suicide Bag / Digestive bag of the cell Membrane-bound vesicles containing hydrolytic enzymes. Optimal activity at acidic pH ★. Arise from Golgi-associated ER.
TypeDescription
Primary LysosomeMembrane-bound vesicles; enzymes in inactive state
Secondary / HeterophagicFormed by fusion with endocytic vesicle; larger than primary ★
Residual BodyVesicle with undigested remains after digestion
Autophagic Vesicle (Suicide Bags)Digest cell's own damaged organelles ★★
  • Acrosome in human sperm = modified lysosome; contains hyaluronidase; dissolves protective layers of ovum ★
  • Tay-Sach's disease — lipase deficiency → fat accumulation → brain damage ★
  • Human liver cell recycles half of its macromolecules every week
  • Help in destruction of malignant cells e.g. T-lymphocytes
Vacuoles & Microbodies • In large plant cells → single central vacuole; up to 90% of cell volume ★
• Bounded by tonoplast membrane (semipermeable)
• Attractive colours of petals → pigments stored in vacuoles ★
• Sphaerosomes — fat storage; half unit membrane | Peroxisomes — produce H₂O₂; detoxify alcohol | Glyoxysomes — convert fatty acids to sugar; germinating seeds ★
⚠️ Tay-Sach's disease: Lipase deficiency → fat accumulation → brain damage ★ (very frequently asked)
⚡ 5.14 Mitochondria — Powerhouse of the Cell
Mitochondria ★★ Powerhouse of the cell — involved in aerobic respiration.
Absent in: Prokaryotic cells and Red Blood Corpuscles (RBCs) ★
  • Double membrane bound organelle; oval/spherical/spiral strip-like shape
  • Outer membrane — permeable to various metabolites; has protein Porin/Parson's particles
  • Inner membrane — selectively permeable; shows finger-like folds → Cristae ★
  • Inner membrane bears: Oxysomes (F₁-F₀ particles) and cytochromes/electron carriers
  • Matrix contains: circular DNA + RNA + 70S ribosomes (like prokaryotes!) + Krebs cycle enzymes ★
Oxysomes (F₁-F₀ particles) ★ Each oxysome: Head (F₁) = lollipop head → faces matrix → enzyme ATP synthase ★
Foot (F₀) = embedded in inner membrane → proton channel
Involved in proton pumping and ATP synthesis
✅ Mitochondria has own DNA + 70S ribosomes → semi-autonomous! (Endosymbiont Theory) — primitive eukaryote engulfed aerobic prokaryote → symbiotic relationship → mitochondrion
🌱 5.15 Plastids & Chloroplast
Plastids ★ Like mitochondria → double membraned; contain DNA, RNA and 70S ribosomes ★
Larger than mitochondria; visible under light microscope
Found in: plant cells, algae and some protists (e.g. Euglena)
TypePigmentFunctionExamples
LeucoplastsNo pigment (white/colourless)Storage of nutrientsAmyloplasts (starch★), Elaioplasts (oil), Aleuroplasts (protein)
ChromoplastsCarotene and xanthophyllImpart red/yellow/orange colour to flowers & fruits ★Tomato, carrot
ChloroplastsChlorophyll (green)PhotosynthesisMesophyll cells (20–40 per cell)
  • Inside cavity of inner membrane → membranous sacs = Thylakoids
  • Thylakoids stacked as Grana (singular: granum) ★
  • Grana connected by membranous tubules = Stroma lamellae
  • Space outside thylakoids = Stroma (contains enzymes + DNA + ribosomes)
⭐ Trick: Leuco = White (no pigment) | Chromo = Colour (carotene) | Chloro = Green (chlorophyll)
Endosymbiont Theory: one cell engulfed photosynthetic prokaryote → evolved into chloroplast
🧬 5.16 Ribosomes — Protein Factories of the Cell
Ribosomes ★ = Protein factories of the cell. First observed by Pallade (1953) as dense particles in EM. Made of rRNA + Proteins; no membranous covering ★.
FeatureProkaryotic RibosomeEukaryotic Ribosome (cytoplasmic)
Type70S80S
Large subunit50S (23S rRNA + 5S rRNA + 34 proteins)60S (28S + 5.8S + 5S rRNA + 50 proteins)
Small subunit30S (16S rRNA + 21 proteins)40S (18S rRNA + 33 proteins)
  • Bound ribosomes → produce proteins transported outside the cell (e.g. acinar cells of pancreas → digestive enzymes) ★
  • Free ribosomes → produce enzymatic proteins used in cytoplasm
  • Free ribosomes form chains = Polyribosomes for protein synthesis
  • Size measured in Svedberg units (S)
📌 Nobel Prize: Venkatraman Ramakrishnan — Nobel in Chemistry (2009) for structure & working of ribosomes. Shared with Yonath (Israel) & Thomas Steitz (USA). ★
🧠 5.17 Nucleus — Master Cell Organelle
Nucleus ★★ = Master cell organelle Interphase nucleus = nuclear envelope + nucleoplasm + nucleolus + chromatin network
  • Nuclear Envelope — double-layered; two membranes separated by perinuclear space (10–50 nm) ★
  • Outer membrane connected with ER; inner membrane lined by nuclear lamina
  • Nucleopores — small openings guarded by pore complexes; regulate flow of substances ★
  • Nucleolus — made of rRNA + ribosomal proteins; site of ribosome biogenesis ★; NOT bound by membrane
  • Chromatin = DNA + histone proteins + non-histone proteins + RNA
  • Euchromatin — more DNA; genetically active ★
  • Heterochromatin — more proteins, less DNA; genetically inert
  • Normal human cell → 46 chromosomes
⭐ Nucleolus = Site of ribosome BIRTH (biogenesis). Euchromatin = active, lightly stained. Heterochromatin = inert, deeply stained.
🌀 5.18–5.20 Cytoskeleton, Cilia & Flagella, Centrioles
Cytoskeleton Network of fibrils throughout the cytoplasm. 3 components:
• Microtubules — Tubulin protein; shape maintenance, cell division, spindle formation
• Microfilaments — Actin protein; cell contraction, movement
• Intermediate Filaments — fibrous proteins; mechanical support, cell shape
Cilia & Flagella ★ Fine hair-like membrane-bound protoplasmic outgrowths on free surface of cell.
Cilia — small, many in number; act as oars | Flagella — longer, few in number
Structure: Basal body + Basal plate + Shaft
• Axoneme — core; shows 9 peripheral doublets + 2 single central fibrils = 9+2 arrangement ★★ MOST ASKED
• Central sheath connected to peripheral doublets by radial spokes
• Peripheral doublets connected by linkers / interdoublet bridges
• Basal body — derived from centriole; has 9 peripheral triplets of fibrils ★
Centrioles & Centrosomes ★ Centrosome — found near nucleus of animal cell; contains a pair of cylindrical structures → centrioles
• Each cylinder = nine sets of triplet microtubules made of tubulin ★
• Proximal end looks like a cartwheel
• Functions: assembly of spindle apparatus during cell division ★ | form basal body of cilia and flagella
⚠️ AXONEME = 9+2 arrangement — MOST asked in exams! Draw a cross-section diagram: 9 peripheral doublets surrounding 2 central fibrils.

★★ 2 Mark Questions
  • Write the postulates of Modern Cell Theory. 2M
    1. All living organisms are made up of cells. 2. Living cells arise from pre-existing cells. 3. Cell = structural and functional unit of life. 4. Total activities of cells = activity of organism. 5. Cells show transformation of energy. 6. Cells contain DNA & RNA in nucleus and cytoplasm.
  • What is totipotency? Give example. 2M
    Totipotency = capacity of a living nucleated cell to differentiate and divide to form any other type of cell and thereby a complete new organism. Cell is totipotent because it has entire genetic information in its nucleus. Embryonic animal cells = totipotent → called Stem Cells. Example: embryonic stem cells can give rise to any tissue.
  • Difference between cilia and flagella. 2M
    Cilia: small/short; many in number; act as oars; e.g. Paramecium, tracheal cells.
    Flagella: longer; few in number; whip-like movement; e.g. Chlamydomonas, sperm. Both have same 9+2 axoneme arrangement ★.
  • What are plasmids? Give types with functions. 2M
    Plasmids = extrachromosomal, self-replicating, circular DNA in bacterial cells. F-plasmid → reproduction/conjugation. R-plasmid → antibiotic resistance to bacteria.
  • Difference between RER and SER with functions. 2M
    RER: ribosomes present; rough surface; protein synthesis; secretory proteins (e.g. insulin).
    SER: no ribosomes; smooth surface; lipid/steroid synthesis; detoxification (liver); Ca²⁺ storage (muscle) ★.
  • What are mesosomes? Give their functions. 2M
    Mesosomes = infoldings of cell membrane in prokaryotic (bacterial) cells. Functions: cell wall formation; cellular respiration (substitute for mitochondria); DNA replication ★.
  • What are Oxysomes? Role in ATP synthesis. 2M
    Oxysomes (F₁-F₀ particles) located on inner mitochondrial membrane. Head (F₁) = lollipop head → faces matrix → enzyme ATP synthase ★. Foot (F₀) = embedded in inner membrane → proton channel. Involved in proton pumping and ATP synthesis.
  • Difference between euchromatin and heterochromatin. 2M
    Euchromatin: more DNA; less proteins; genetically active; lightly stained ★.
    Heterochromatin: less DNA; more proteins; genetically inert; deeply stained ★.
  • What is the endomembrane system? Name components. 2M
    Endomembrane system = group of membrane-bound organelles that coordinate to complete specific tasks. Components: Nuclear membrane + ER + Golgi complex + Lysosomes + vesicles and vacuoles. NOT included: Mitochondria and Chloroplasts.
★★★ 3 Mark Questions
  • Explain Fluid Mosaic Model of plasma membrane with diagram. 3M
    Proposed by Singer and Nicholson (1972). Plasma membrane = phospholipid bilayer + proteins. Proteins like icebergs in sea of lipids. Intrinsic/Transmembrane proteins span entire membrane; form channels for water passage. Extrinsic/Peripheral proteins found on two surfaces. Lateral movement of proteins = fluidity. Phospholipids are amphipathic (one hydrophilic polar head + two hydrophobic tails). Thickness = 75 Å; trilamellate under EM.
  • Describe structure of Golgi complex. 3M
    Golgi Complex = assembly + manufacturing + packaging + transport unit of cell (Post Office of cell). Consists of stacks of membranous sacs = cisternae (0.5–1 µm). cis face (receiving from ER) + trans face (secreting products). Cisternal Maturation Model — cisternae mature moving cis → trans ★. Markers get imprinted on vesicle membrane to identify target. Functions: modification of ER secretions (glycolipids, glycoproteins), production of own secretions (pectin in plants), packaging and transport.
  • Explain Lysosomes — formation, types and functions. 3M
    Lysosomes = dismantling and restructuring units / Suicide Bags. Arise from Golgi-associated ER. 4 Types: Primary (inactive enzymes), Secondary/Heterophagic (formed with endocytic vesicle), Residual Body (undigested remains), Autophagic Vesicle (digest own damaged organelles) ★★. Functions: intracellular digestion; autophagy; acrosome in sperm (hyaluronidase) ★; Tay-Sach's disease (lipase deficiency → fat accumulation → brain damage) ★; destruction of malignant cells.
  • Explain Endosymbiont Theory. 3M
    Both mitochondria and chloroplast = double-walled, own DNA and ribosomes, duplicate within cell. Origin of Mitochondria: primitive eukaryote engulfed aerobic non-photosynthetic prokaryote → symbiotic relationship → became mitochondrion. Origin of Chloroplast: one cell engulfed photosynthetic prokaryote → evolved into chloroplast. Evidence: both have 70S ribosomes (like prokaryotes), circular DNA, and can divide independently.
★★★★ 4 Mark Questions
  • Compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells with tabular form. 4M
    (See comparison table at top of this page — 8 features: nuclear membrane, true nucleus, organelles, cell organisation, ribosome type, DNA type, cell wall, examples) + PRO = Primitive, EU = True nucleus. In prokaryotes, DNA is circular and NOT associated with histone proteins. In eukaryotes, DNA is linear + histone proteins → chromatin.
  • Describe structure and functions of Mitochondria with labelled diagram. 4M
    Structure: Double membrane bound; oval/spherical. Outer membrane = permeable (Porin). Inner membrane = selective; Cristae (finger-like folds). Both membranes separated by outer chamber. Inner membrane bears Oxysomes (F₁-F₀) + cytochromes. Matrix = circular DNA + RNA + 70S ribosomes + Krebs cycle enzymes + lipids. Oxysomes F₁ = ATP synthase, head faces matrix; F₀ = proton channel in inner membrane. Functions: aerobic respiration, ATP production, semi-autonomous (own DNA + 70S ribosomes), Endosymbiont Theory. Absent in prokaryotes and RBCs. Draw: outer membrane, inner membrane + cristae, matrix, oxysomes ★.
  • Describe structure and functions of Nucleus with labelled diagram. 4M
    Nucleus = Master cell organelle. Interphase nucleus = nuclear envelope + nucleoplasm + nucleolus + chromatin. Nuclear Envelope = double-layered; perinuclear space (10–50 nm); outer membrane connected to ER; inner membrane = nuclear lamina. Nucleopores — pore complexes regulate flow of substances. Nucleolus = rRNA + ribosomal proteins; site of ribosome biogenesis; NOT bound by membrane. Chromatin = DNA + histone + non-histone + RNA. Euchromatin = active; Heterochromatin = inert. 46 chromosomes in normal human cell. Functions: heredity, variation, site of DNA/RNA/ribosome synthesis, protein synthesis, phylogenetic studies.
  • What is axoneme? Describe 9+2 arrangement in cilia/flagella with diagram. 4M
    Axoneme = core of cilia/flagella running parallel to long axis. 9+2 arrangement: 9 peripheral doublets + 2 single central fibrils ★★ (MOST ASKED). Central tubules enclosed by central sheath. Central sheath connected to peripheral doublets by radial spokes. Peripheral doublets connected by linkers/interdoublet bridges. Structure of Cilium/Flagellum: Basal body (from centriole; 9 peripheral triplets) + Basal plate + Shaft (sheath + axoneme). Draw: cross-section showing 9 peripheral doublets surrounding 2 central fibrils.
