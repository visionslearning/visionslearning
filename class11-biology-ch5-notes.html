5.1 The Cell – Basics & Microscopy ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Definition: Cell = structural and functional unit of life capable of independent existence
  • Simple Microscope → 50–100× | Compound Microscope → 1000×+ | Electron Microscope → 500,000× ★
  • Smallest cell → Mycoplasma (0.3 µm) | Bacterial cell → 3–5 µm
  • Largest cell → Ostrich egg (~15 cm) | Longest cell → Nerve cell (Neuron)
5.2 Cell Theory & Modern Cell Theory ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Schleiden (1838) – Plants | Schwann (1839) – Animals | Virchow (1855) – New cells from old
  • Omnis cellula-e-cellulla – Rudolf Virchow ★
  • Modern Cell Theory has 6 postulates (see Q1 below)
  • Trick: Schleiden=Plants | Schwann=Animals | Virchow=New cells from old
5.3 Totipotency & Stem Cells ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Totipotency: Capacity of a living nucleated cell to differentiate and divide to form any other type of cell → complete new organism
  • Cell is totipotent because it has entire genetic information in its nucleus
  • Embryonic animal cells = totipotent → called Stem Cells ★
  • Stem cells have great medical applications including cure for diseases

5.6 Prokaryotic Cell – Detailed Structure ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Cell Envelope (3-layered)

  • Outer → Glycocalyx (slime layer = loose / capsule = tough)
  • Middle → Cell Wall
  • Inner → Plasma Membrane (phospholipid bilayer)

📌 Key Facts

  • Gram Staining – developed by Danish bacteriologist Hans Christian Gram ★
  • Gram +ve → cell wall: Peptidoglycan | Gram –ve → cell wall: Murein
  • Mesosomes – infoldings of cell membrane; help in: cell wall formation, cellular respiration, DNA replication ★
  • Plasmids – extrachromosomal self-replicating DNA ★ | F-plasmid → reproduction | R-plasmid → antibiotic resistance
  • DNA replication pattern in bacteria: Theta model ★
  • Basal body of bacterial flagella = smallest motor in the world!
5.7 Cell Wall (Eukaryotic) ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Rigid, supportive, protective outer covering; present in plant cells, fungi and some protists
  • Animal cells do NOT have cell wall
  • Microfibrils – cellulose → responsible for rigidity ★
  • Plasmodesmata – cytoplasmic bridges between neighbouring cells ★
  • Middle Lamella – thinnest; mainly pectin; first formed from cell plate; softening of ripe fruit = solubilisation of pectin ★
  • Primary Wall – capable of growth in young cells
  • Secondary Wall – inner to primary; formed after primary growth stops
5.8 Plasma Membrane & Fluid Mosaic Model ★★ MOST IMPORTANT

📌 Key Points

  • Thickness → 75 Å | Under electron microscope → trilamellate (3 layers) ★
  • Phospholipids are amphipathic – one hydrophilic polar head + two hydrophobic non-polar tails ★
  • Fluid Mosaic Model – Proposed by Singer and Nicholson (1972)
  • Proteins like 'icebergs in sea of lipids' | Intrinsic proteins span entire membrane | Extrinsic proteins on surface
  • Passive Transport – no energy; along concentration gradient | Active Transport – against gradient; requires ATP; e.g. Na⁺/K⁺ pump ★
5.9 Cytoplasm & Endomembrane System ★

📌 Key Points

  • Ground substance = cytoplasmic matrix / cytosol – shows streaming movements called cyclosis
  • Endomembrane System = Nuclear membrane + ER + Golgi complex + Lysosomes + vesicles and vacuoles
  • NOT included: Mitochondria and Chloroplasts (carry out specific independent energy conversions)
  • Flow: Nuclear envelope → ER → Transport Vesicles → Golgi (cis → trans) → Lysosomes / Vacuoles / Secretory Vesicles → Plasma Membrane
5.10 Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Present in all eukaryotic cells except ova and mature RBCs ★
  • Forms more than 50% of total membrane of a eukaryotic cell
  • RER – studded with ribosomes; protein synthesis (e.g. pancreatic cells secreting insulin) ★
  • SER – no ribosomes; synthesis of lipids/steroids; detoxification (liver cells); storage of Ca²⁺ (muscle cells) ★
  • Trick: RER = Rough = Ribosomes (protein) | SER = Smooth = Steroids & Storage
5.11 Golgi Complex ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Golgi Complex = assembly + manufacturing + packaging + transport unit of cell
  • Consists of stacks of membranous sacs = cisternae; diameter 0.5–1 µm
  • Each cisterna has: cis face (forming/receiving) and trans face (maturing/secreting) ★
  • Cisternal Maturation Model – cisternae themselves mature moving from cis to trans face ★
  • Golgi = Post Office of the cell – receives, modifies, packages and sends!
  • Trick: CIS = Come IN (receiving) | TRANS = Transfer OUT (shipping)
5.12 Lysosomes ★★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Lysosomes = dismantling and restructuring units of cell = Suicide Bag / Digestive bag
  • Membrane-bound vesicles containing hydrolytic enzymes; optimal activity at acidic pH ★
  • Arise from Golgi-associated endoplasmic reticulum
  • 4 Types: Primary, Secondary/Heterophagic, Residual Body, Autophagic Vesicle (Suicide Bags) ★★
  • Acrosome in human sperm = modified lysosome; contains hyaluronidase ★
  • Tay-Sach's disease – lipase deficiency → fat accumulation → brain damage ★
5.13 Vacuoles & Microbodies ★

📌 Key Points

  • In large plant cells → single central vacuole; can occupy up to 90% of cell volume ★
  • Bounded by tonoplast membrane (semipermeable)
  • Attractive colours of petals → pigments stored in vacuoles ★
  • Contractile vacuoles – in Paramoecium → excretion and osmoregulation
  • Sphaerosomes – fat storage; half unit membrane | Peroxisomes – produce H₂O₂; detoxify alcohol | Glyoxysomes – convert fatty acids to sugar; germinating seeds ★
5.14 Mitochondria ★★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Mitochondria = Powerhouse of the cell – involved in aerobic respiration
  • Absent in: Prokaryotic cells and Red Blood Corpuscles (RBCs) ★
  • Double membrane bound | Inner membrane shows finger-like folds → Cristae ★
  • Inner membrane bears: Oxysomes (F₁-F₀ particles) and cytochromes/electron carriers
  • Matrix contains: circular DNA + RNA + 70S ribosomes (like prokaryotes!) + Krebs cycle enzymes ★
  • Oxysomes F₁ = lollipop head faces matrix = enzyme ATP synthase ★ | F₀ = proton channel in inner membrane
  • Mitochondria has own DNA + 70S ribosomes → semi-autonomous! (Endosymbiont Theory)
5.15 Plastids & Chloroplast ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Like mitochondria → double membraned; contain DNA, RNA and 70S ribosomes ★
  • Leucoplasts → no pigment; storage (Amyloplasts=starch ★, Elaioplasts=oil, Aleuroplasts=protein)
  • Chromoplasts → carotene and xanthophyll; red/yellow/orange colour to flowers & fruits ★
  • Chloroplasts → chlorophyll; photosynthesis
  • Thylakoids stacked as Grana | Grana connected by Stroma lamellae | Space outside = Stroma ★
  • Trick: Leuco=White | Chromo=Colour | Chloro=Green

📌 Endosymbiont Theory ★★

  • Both mitochondria and chloroplast → double-walled, own DNA and ribosomes, duplicate within cell
  • Primitive eukaryote engulfed aerobic non-photosynthetic prokaryote → symbiotic relationship → mitochondrion
  • One cell engulfed photosynthetic prokaryote → evolved into chloroplast
5.16 Ribosomes ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Ribosomes = Protein factories of the cell
  • First observed by Pallade (1953) as dense particles in electron micrograph
  • Made up of rRNA + Proteins; no membranous covering ★
  • Prokaryotic → 70S (50S + 30S) | Eukaryotic cytoplasmic → 80S (60S + 40S)
  • Bound ribosomes → proteins transported outside | Free ribosomes → enzymatic proteins in cytoplasm
  • Free ribosomes form chains = Polyribosomes
  • Nobel Prize: Venkatraman Ramakrishnan – Nobel in Chemistry (2009) for structure & working of ribosomes ★
5.17 Nucleus – Master Cell Organelle ★★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Nucleus = Master cell organelle; contains entire genetic information
  • Nuclear Envelope – double-layered; two membranes separated by perinuclear space (10–50 nm) ★
  • Nucleopores – small openings guarded by pore complexes that regulate flow of substances ★
  • Nucleolus – made of rRNA + ribosomal proteins; site of ribosome biogenesis ★; NOT bound by membrane
  • Chromatin = DNA + histone proteins + non-histone proteins + RNA
  • Euchromatin – more DNA; genetically active ★ | Heterochromatin – more proteins, less DNA; genetically inert
  • Normal human cell → 46 chromosomes

5.19 Cilia & Flagella ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Fine hair-like membrane-bound protoplasmic outgrowths on free surface of cell
  • Cilia – small, many in number; act as oars | Flagella – longer, few in number

5.20 Centrioles & Centrosomes ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Centrosome – found near nucleus of animal cell; contains a pair of cylindrical structures → centrioles
  • Each cylinder = nine sets of triplet microtubules made of tubulin ★
  • Proximal end of centriole looks like a cartwheel
  • Functions: Help in assembly of spindle apparatus during cell division ★ | Form basal body of cilia and flagella

★★ 2 Mark Exam Questions – Chapter 5
Q1. Write the postulates of Modern Cell Theory. 2 marks ▶

Postulates of Modern Cell Theory:

  1. All living organisms are made up of cells.
  2. Living cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  3. Cell is the structural and functional unit of life.
  4. Total activities of cells = activity of organism.
  5. Cells show transformation of energy.
  6. Cells contain DNA & RNA in nucleus and cytoplasm.
✍️ List 6 points clearly = full 2 marks
Q2. What is totipotency? Give example. 2 marks ▶

Totipotency is the capacity of a living nucleated cell to differentiate and divide to form any other type of cell and thereby a complete new organism.

  • Cell is totipotent because it has the entire genetic information in its nucleus.
  • Embryonic animal cells are totipotent → called Stem Cells.
  • Stem cells have great medical applications including cure for diseases.

Example: Embryonic stem cells of animals can give rise to any type of tissue.

Q4. What are plasmids? Give types with functions. 2 marks ▶

Plasmids are extrachromosomal, self-replicating, circular DNA molecules present in bacterial cells. They carry genes apart from the main chromosome.

Types:

  • F-plasmid (Fertility plasmid) → involved in reproduction/conjugation
  • R-plasmid (Resistance plasmid) → provides antibiotic resistance to bacteria
✍️ F = Fertility | R = Resistance — easy memory trick!
Q5. What is Gram staining? Who developed it? 2 marks ▶

Gram Staining is a technique to classify bacteria based on the composition of their cell wall using crystal violet dye and iodine.

  • Developed by Hans Christian Gram (Danish bacteriologist)
  • Gram +ve bacteria → cell wall made of Peptidoglycan → retain violet stain
  • Gram –ve bacteria → cell wall made of Murein → lose violet stain

Q7. What are mesosomes? Give their functions. 2 marks ▶

Mesosomes are infoldings of the cell membrane present in prokaryotic (bacterial) cells.

Functions:

  • Help in cell wall formation
  • Help in cellular respiration (substitute for mitochondria)
  • Help in DNA replication

Q8. What are Oxysomes? Explain their role in ATP synthesis. 2 marks ▶

Oxysomes (also called F₁-F₀ particles / Elementary particles / Fernandez-Moran particles) are located on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

  • Each oxysome has: Head (F₁) = lollipop head → faces matrix → enzyme ATP synthase
  • Foot (F₀) = embedded in inner membrane → proton channel
  • They carry out proton pumping and ATP synthesis

Q10. What is the endomembrane system? Name its components. 2 marks ▶

The endomembrane system is a group of membrane-bound organelles that coordinate with each other and complete specific tasks for the cell.

Components: Nuclear membrane + ER + Golgi complex + Lysosomes + vesicles and vacuoles

NOT included: Mitochondria and Chloroplasts (they carry out specific independent energy conversions)

★★★ 3 Mark Exam Questions – Chapter 5
Q11. Explain the Fluid Mosaic Model of plasma membrane. 3 marks ▶

Fluid Mosaic Model was proposed by Singer and Nicholson (1972).

  • The plasma membrane is made of a phospholipid bilayer with proteins embedded in it.
  • Proteins float like 'icebergs in a sea of lipids'.
  • Intrinsic / Transmembrane proteins – span entire membrane; form channels for water passage.
  • Extrinsic / Peripheral proteins – found on two surfaces of membrane.
  • Lateral movement of proteins is measured as fluidity.
  • Phospholipids are amphipathic – one hydrophilic polar head + two hydrophobic non-polar tails.
  • Thickness of plasma membrane → 75 Å | trilamellate under electron microscope.
✍️ Mention: proposed by, components, intrinsic/extrinsic proteins, fluidity = full 3 marks
Q12. Describe the structure of prokaryotic cell with labelled diagram. 3 marks ▶

Prokaryotic Cell Structure:

  • Cell Envelope (3-layered): Glycocalyx (outer) + Cell Wall (middle) + Plasma Membrane (inner)
  • Glycocalyx – loose = slime layer; tough = capsule
  • Mesosomes – infoldings of cell membrane; help in respiration, DNA replication, cell wall formation
  • Ribosomes – 70S type (50S + 30S); help in protein synthesis
  • Nucleoid – region with circular DNA (not true nucleus)
  • Plasmids – extrachromosomal DNA
  • Pili – for inter-cellular communication | Fimbriae – clinging to support
  • Motile bacteria have cilia or flagella driven by basal body + filament + hook

Q13. Describe the structure of Golgi complex. 3 marks ▶

Golgi Complex = assembly + manufacturing + packaging + transport unit of cell

  • Consists of stacks of membranous sacs called cisternae (diameter 0.5–1 µm)
  • Each cisterna has two faces:
    • cis face (forming/receiving face) – receives transport vesicles from ER
    • trans face (maturing/secreting face) – products leave from here
  • Cisternae in Golgi are NOT physically connected to each other (unlike ER)
  • Cisternal Maturation Model – cisternae themselves mature moving from cis to trans face
  • Vesicles with markers identify specific target cell/organelle ★
  • Functions: Modification of ER secretions, production of own secretions (e.g. pectin), packaging and transport
✍️ Golgi = Post Office of the cell. Mention cis/trans, cisternal maturation model = full marks
Q14. Explain Lysosomes – formation, types and functions. 3 marks ▶

Lysosomes = dismantling and restructuring units of cell (Suicide Bag / Digestive bag)

Formation: Arise from Golgi-associated endoplasmic reticulum

Types:

  1. Primary Lysosome – membrane-bound vesicles; enzymes in inactive state
  2. Secondary / Heterophagic Lysosome – formed by fusion with endocytic vesicle
  3. Residual Body – vesicle with undigested remains after digestion
  4. Autophagic Vesicle (Suicide Bags) – digest cell's own damaged organelles ★★

Functions:

  • Intracellular digestion of food particles
  • Destruction of damaged organelles (autophagy)
  • Acrosome in sperm = modified lysosome (contains hyaluronidase) ★
  • Tay-Sach's disease → lipase deficiency → fat accumulation → brain damage ★
  • Help in destruction of malignant cells
Q15. Explain the Endosymbiont Theory. 3 marks ▶

The Endosymbiont Theory explains the origin of mitochondria and chloroplast in eukaryotic cells.

  • Both mitochondria and chloroplast are double-walled, have own DNA and ribosomes, and duplicate within cell.
  • Origin of Mitochondria: A primitive eukaryote engulfed an aerobic non-photosynthetic prokaryote → symbiotic relationship evolved → became mitochondrion.
  • Origin of Chloroplast: One cell engulfed a photosynthetic prokaryote → evolved into chloroplast.
  • Called: Endosymbiont Theory = coexistence of cell within cell.
  • Evidence: Both organelles have 70S ribosomes (like prokaryotes), circular DNA, and can divide independently.
✍️ Mention both mitochondria and chloroplast origins + evidence = full 3 marks

Mitochondria = Powerhouse of the cell

Structure:

  • Double membrane bound organelle; oval/spherical/spiral strip-like shape
  • Outer membrane – permeable to various metabolites; has protein Porin/Parson's particles

Q18. Describe the structure and functions of Nucleus with labelled diagram. 4 marks ▶

Nucleus = Master cell organelle

Structure:

  • Interphase nucleus = nuclear envelope + nucleoplasm + nucleolus + chromatin network
  • Nuclear Envelope – double-layered; two membranes separated by perinuclear space (10–50 nm)
  • Outer membrane connected with ER; inner membrane lined by nuclear lamina
  • Nucleopores – small openings guarded by pore complexes; regulate flow of substances
  • Nucleoplasm (Karyolymph) – contains nucleic acids, proteins, minerals, chromatin, nucleolus
  • Nucleolus – made of rRNA + ribosomal proteins; site of ribosome biogenesis; NOT bound by membrane
  • Chromatin = DNA + histone + non-histone proteins + RNA | Euchromatin (active) + Heterochromatin (inert)

Functions:

  • Contains entire genetic information → important in heredity and variation
  • Site for synthesis of DNA, RNA and ribosomes
  • Important role in protein synthesis
  • Chromosome number constant for a species → important in phylogenetic studies
Q19. Describe structure and functions of Chloroplast with labelled diagram. 4 marks ▶

Chloroplast – site of photosynthesis

Structure:

  • Lens-shaped in plants; also oval, spherical, discoid or ribbon-like
  • Double membraned organelle; contain DNA, RNA and 70S ribosomes (semi-autonomous)
  • Inside cavity of inner membrane → membranous sacs = Thylakoids
  • Thylakoids stacked as Grana (singular: granum) ★
  • Grana connected by membranous tubules = Stroma lamellae
  • Space outside thylakoids = Stroma
  • Stroma also contains DNA and ribosomes

Functions:

  • Site of photosynthesis (light reactions in thylakoids; dark reactions in stroma)
  • Has own DNA + 70S ribosomes → semi-autonomous
  • Contains enzymes for photosynthesis
✍️ Draw: outer membrane, inner membrane, thylakoids, grana, stroma, stroma lamellae = full marks
Q20. What is axoneme? Describe 9+2 arrangement in cilia/flagella. 4 marks ▶

Axoneme is the core of cilia/flagella that runs parallel to the long axis.

9+2 arrangement:

  • The axoneme shows 9 peripheral doublets + 2 single central fibrils (9+2 arrangement) ★★
  • Central tubules are enclosed by central sheath
  • Central sheath connected to peripheral doublets by radial spokes
  • Peripheral doublets connected by linkers / interdoublet bridges

Structure of Cilium/Flagellum:

  • Basal body – in outer cytoplasm; derived from centriole; has 9 peripheral triplets of fibrils ★
  • Basal plate – between basal body and shaft
  • Shaft – exposed part; consists of sheath (covering membrane) + axoneme
✍️ MOST ASKED: 9+2 arrangement — draw the cross-section showing 9 peripheral doublets + 2 central fibrils
