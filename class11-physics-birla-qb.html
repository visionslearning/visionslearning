Units, Dimensions & Measurements (Q.1–7)
Q.1
What are the Fundamental quantities and Derived quantities?
Fundamental
Quantities that are independent and cannot be expressed in terms of other quantities. There are 7 fundamental quantities:
  • Length (m), Mass (kg), Time (s), Temperature (K)
  • Electric current (A), Luminous intensity (cd), Amount of substance (mol)
Derived
Quantities that are expressed in terms of fundamental quantities. Examples: velocity (m/s), force (kg·m/s²), energy (kg·m²/s²), pressure (kg/m·s²)
∴
7 fundamental + many derived quantities make up the SI system.
Q.2
What are the supplementary units?
Definition
Supplementary units are SI units that are neither fundamental nor derived — they are used for geometric quantities.
Two Units
  • Radian (rad): Unit of plane angle. 1 rad = angle subtended at centre of circle by arc equal in length to radius.
  • Steradian (sr): Unit of solid angle. 1 sr = solid angle subtended at centre of sphere by surface area equal to r².
∴
Radian (rad) and Steradian (sr) are the two supplementary units.
Q.3
Define Dimensions.
Definition
Dimensions of a physical quantity are the powers to which the fundamental units (M, L, T, A, K...) must be raised to represent that quantity.
Examples
  • Force = [M¹L¹T⁻²]
  • Velocity = [M⁰L¹T⁻¹]
  • Energy = [M¹L²T⁻²]
  • Pressure = [M¹L⁻¹T⁻²]
Q.4
State the uses of Dimensional analysis.
Use 1
Checking correctness: A physical equation is correct only if dimensions on both sides are equal (Principle of Homogeneity).
Use 2
Deriving relations: To derive a formula relating different physical quantities using their dimensional equations.
Use 3
Unit conversion: To convert a physical quantity from one system of units to another (CGS ↔ SI).
Use 4
Finding dimensions: To find the dimensions of a physical constant from a given formula.
Q.5
Show that 1 newton = 10⁵ dyne.
Dimension of Force
[F] = M¹L¹T⁻²
SI units
1 N = 1 kg × 1 m × 1 s⁻²
CGS units
1 dyne = 1 g × 1 cm × 1 s⁻²
Conversion
1 N = (1 kg)·(1 m)·(1 s⁻²) = (1000 g)·(100 cm)·(1 s⁻²) = 1000 × 100 dyne = 10⁵ dyne
∴ 1 N
= 10⁵ dyne ✓
Q.6
Show that 1 joule = 10⁷ erg.
Dimension of Energy
[E] = M¹L²T⁻²
SI units
1 J = 1 kg × 1 m² × 1 s⁻²
CGS units
1 erg = 1 g × 1 cm² × 1 s⁻²
Conversion
1 J = (1000 g) × (100 cm)² × (1 s⁻²) = 1000 × 10000 erg = 10⁷ erg
∴ 1 J
= 10⁷ erg ✓
Q.7
Find the dimension of any physical quantity (e.g., Force, Pressure, Energy).
Force
F = ma → [M][LT⁻²] = [M¹L¹T⁻²]
Pressure
P = F/A → [M¹L¹T⁻²]/[L²] = [M¹L⁻¹T⁻²]
Energy/Work
W = F×d → [M¹L¹T⁻²][L] = [M¹L²T⁻²]
Velocity
v = d/t → [L]/[T] = [M⁰L¹T⁻¹]
Gravitational constant G
F = Gm₁m₂/r² → G = Fr²/m² → [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]

Scalars, Vectors & Vector Algebra (Q.8–15)
Q.8
Define scalars and vectors.
Scalar
A physical quantity that has only magnitude and no direction. Examples: mass, speed, temperature, time, energy, distance.
Vector
A physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction and follows the laws of vector addition. Examples: displacement, velocity, force, acceleration, momentum.
Q.9
What is the Triangle law of vector addition?
Statement
If two vectors are represented in magnitude and direction by the two sides of a triangle taken in the same order, then their resultant is represented by the third side taken in the opposite order.
Magnitude
R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ)
Direction
tan α = B sinθ / (A + B cosθ)   where α is angle R makes with A.
Q.10
State parallelogram of vector addition and determine magnitude and direction of resultant vector.
Statement
If two vectors are represented by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from a common point, then the diagonal of the parallelogram drawn from that point represents their resultant in magnitude and direction.
Magnitude
|R| = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ)
Direction
tan α = (B sinθ) / (A + B cosθ)
Special cases
  • θ=0°: R = A+B (maximum)
  • θ=90°: R = √(A²+B²)
  • θ=180°: R = |A−B| (minimum)
Q.11
What is meant by resolution of vector?
Definition
Splitting a vector into two or more component vectors is called resolution of a vector. The components are chosen such that their resultant is the original vector.
In 2D
A vector A at angle θ to x-axis is resolved as:
  • Horizontal component: Aₓ = A cosθ
  • Vertical component: Aᵧ = A sinθ
  • Magnitude: A = √(Aₓ² + Aᵧ²)
Q.12
Define scalar product and state its characteristics.
Definition
The scalar (dot) product of two vectors A and B is the product of their magnitudes and cosine of the angle between them. Result is a scalar.
Formula
A·B = |A||B|cosθ
Characteristics
  • Commutative: A·B = B·A
  • Distributive: A·(B+C) = A·B + A·C
  • If A⊥B: A·B = 0 (cos90°=0)
  • If A∥B: A·B = AB (maximum)
  • Self product: A·A = A²
  • î·î = ĵ·ĵ = k̂·k̂ = 1; î·ĵ = ĵ·k̂ = k̂·î = 0
∴ A·B
= ABcosθ (scalar quantity)
Q.13
Define vector product and state its characteristics.
Definition
The vector (cross) product of two vectors A and B is a vector whose magnitude is |A||B|sinθ and direction is perpendicular to both A and B (given by right-hand rule).
Formula
A×B = |A||B|sinθ · n̂
Characteristics
  • Anti-commutative: A×B = −(B×A)
  • If A∥B (θ=0°): A×B = 0
  • If A⊥B (θ=90°): |A×B| = AB (max)
  • Self product: A×A = 0
  • î×ĵ = k̂; ĵ×k̂ = î; k̂×î = ĵ
  • Applications: Torque τ = r×F, Angular momentum L = r×p
∴ A×B
= ABsinθ n̂ (vector, ⊥ to plane of A and B)
Q.14
Express cross product in determinant form.
Determinant Form
If A = Aₓî + Aᵧĵ + A_zk̂ and B = Bₓî + Bᵧĵ + B_zk̂, then:
Formula
A×B = |î   ĵ   k̂|
       |Aₓ Aᵧ A_z|
       |Bₓ Bᵧ B_z|
Expanding
= î(AᵧB_z − A_zBᵧ) − ĵ(AₓB_z − A_zBₓ) + k̂(AₓBᵧ − AᵧBₓ)
Q.15
Show that magnitude of vector product of two vectors is numerically equal to the area of a parallelogram.
Proof
Consider parallelogram OACB with sides A and B, and angle θ between them.
Step 1
Area of parallelogram = base × height = A × (B sinθ)
Step 2
|A×B| = |A||B|sinθ = AB sinθ
Step 3
Area = AB sinθ = |A×B|
∴ |A×B|
= Area of parallelogram formed by vectors A and B. ✓

Gravitation (Q.16–23)
Q.16
State Newton's law of gravitation and express it in vector form.
Statement
Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
Scalar form
F = Gm₁m₂/r²   G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
Vector form
F⃗₁₂ = −G m₁m₂/r² · r̂₁₂   (negative sign shows attractive force)
Note
Gravitational force is always attractive, central, conservative, and acts along the line joining the two bodies.
Q.17
Draw a graph showing variation of gravitational acceleration due to depth and altitude from the Earth's surface.
With Altitude (h)
g_h = g(1 − 2h/R) for h << R. g decreases as altitude increases. At h=∞, g=0.
With Depth (d)
g_d = g(1 − d/R). g decreases linearly with depth. At d=R (centre), g=0.
Graph Shape
  • Above surface (altitude): g decreases with r² (inverse square law)
  • Below surface (depth): g decreases linearly
  • g is maximum at Earth's surface (r=R)
  • g = 0 at Earth's centre and at r = ∞
Q.18
State Kepler's three laws of planetary motion.
1st Law (Orbit)
Law of Ellipses: Every planet revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one focus.
2nd Law (Area)
Law of Equal Areas: The line joining planet to Sun sweeps equal areas in equal time intervals. (Planet moves faster near Sun.)
3rd Law (Period)
Law of Periods: T² ∝ r³. The square of period is proportional to the cube of the mean orbital radius. T²/r³ = constant
Q.19
What is critical velocity? Obtain an expression for critical velocity of an orbiting satellite.
Definition
Critical velocity (orbital velocity) is the minimum horizontal velocity required for a satellite to orbit the Earth at a given height without falling back.
Derivation
  • For circular orbit: Gravitational force = Centripetal force
  • GMm/(R+h)² = mv²/(R+h)
  • v² = GM/(R+h)
  • v_c = √[GM/(R+h)]
At surface (h=0)
v_c = √(gR) ≈ √(9.8 × 6.4×10⁶) ≈ 7.9 km/s
∴ v_c
= √[GM/(R+h)] ≈ 7.9 km/s near Earth's surface
Q.20
State the formula for acceleration due to gravity in terms of gravitational constant.
Derivation
Gravitational force on mass m at Earth's surface: F = GMm/R²
Also F = mg
mg = GMm/R² → g = GM/R²
Formula
g = GM/R² where G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², M = mass of Earth, R = radius of Earth
∴ g = GM/R²
≈ 9.8 m/s²
Q.21
Define: (i) Escape velocity (ii) Binding energy
(i) Escape velocity
The minimum velocity needed to escape permanently from Earth's gravitational field without any further propulsion.
v_e = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR) ≈ 11.2 km/s
(ii) Binding energy
The minimum energy required to remove a satellite from its orbit to infinity (i.e., to free it from Earth's gravitational pull).
BE = GMm / 2(R+h) = ½mv²   for a satellite in circular orbit.
Note
v_e = √2 × v_c (escape velocity is √2 times orbital velocity)
Q.22
As we go from one planet to another, how will the mass and weight of a body change?
Mass
Mass is the amount of matter in a body. It is a scalar and constant property. Mass does NOT change from planet to planet — it remains the same everywhere.
Weight
Weight = mg. Since g varies from planet to planet (depends on planet's mass and radius), weight changes from planet to planet.
  • On Moon: g_moon = g_earth/6 → Weight = 1/6 of Earth weight
  • On Jupiter: g is larger → Weight increases
∴ Mass = constant;
Weight = mg (changes with planet's g)
Q.23
Discuss the variation of acceleration due to gravity with altitude.
At altitude h
g_h = GM/(R+h)² = g × R²/(R+h)²
For h << R
g_h ≈ g(1 − 2h/R)
Conclusion
  • g decreases as altitude increases (inversely as square of distance)
  • At h = R: g_h = g/4
  • At h = ∞: g_h = 0
∴ g decreases
with altitude: g_h = gR²/(R+h)²

Electrostatics (Q.24–28)
Q.24
Define electric flux. State its unit.
Definition
Electric flux (φ) is the total number of electric field lines passing normally through a given surface. It is the dot product of electric field E and area vector A.
Formula
φ = E · A = EA cosθ   where θ is angle between E and normal to surface.
SI Unit
N·m²/C (Newton-metre² per Coulomb) or V·m (Volt-metre)
Gauss's Law
Total flux through closed surface = q_enc/ε₀
Q.25
Define linear charge density, surface charge density and volume charge density.
Linear (λ)
Charge per unit length. λ = Q/L   Unit: C/m. Used for wires, rods.
Surface (σ)
Charge per unit area. σ = Q/A   Unit: C/m². Used for charged sheets, conductors.
Volume (ρ)
Charge per unit volume. ρ = Q/V   Unit: C/m³. Used for charged solid spheres, clouds.
Q.26
Define an electric dipole.
Definition
An electric dipole is a system of two equal and opposite point charges (+q and −q) separated by a small distance (2l).
Dipole Moment
p = q × 2l   Direction: from −q to +q. Unit: C·m
Examples
Water molecule (H₂O), HCl molecule — natural electric dipoles.
Q.27
State characteristics of electric lines of force.
1
Start from positive charge and end on negative charge.
2
Never intersect each other (field is unique at every point).
3
Always perpendicular to the surface of a conductor.
4
Tangent to field line gives direction of E at that point.
5
Denser lines → stronger field. Parallel equidistant lines → uniform field.
6
Do not pass through a conductor (E = 0 inside conductor).
7
For a positive isolated charge: lines go outward radially. For negative: inward.
Q.28
Derive an expression for electric field intensity due to a point charge in a material medium.
Setup
Point charge +Q in a material medium of permittivity ε = ε₀εᵣ. Test charge +q₀ placed at distance r.
Step 1
By Coulomb's law in medium: F = Qq₀/(4πεr²) = Qq₀/(4πε₀εᵣr²)
Step 2
E = F/q₀ = Q/(4πε₀εᵣr²)
Formula
E = Q/(4πε₀εᵣr²) = kQ/(εᵣr²)
Note
In vacuum/air: εᵣ=1, so E = kQ/r². In medium: E is reduced by factor εᵣ (relative permittivity).
∴ E
= Q/(4πε₀εᵣr²) in vector form: E⃗ = kQ/(εᵣr²) r̂

Current Electricity (Q.29–34)
Q.29
State and explain Ohm's law.
Statement
At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference applied across its ends.
Mathematically
V ∝ I  ⟹  V = IR   where R = resistance (constant for a given conductor at constant T)
Explanation
  • R = V/I (Resistance = voltage / current)
  • Unit of R: Ohm (Ω) = V/A
  • Graph of V vs I is a straight line through origin (slope = R)
Limitations
Not valid for semiconductors, diodes, electrolytes, at high temperatures or high voltages.
Q.30
Explain series/parallel combination of resistors.
Series Combination
  • Resistors connected end-to-end (same current through all).
  • R_s = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...
  • Effective resistance is greater than any individual resistance.
  • Same current; voltage divides.
Parallel Combination
  • Resistors connected across same two terminals (same voltage).
  • 1/R_p = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...
  • Effective resistance is less than any individual resistance.
  • Same voltage; current divides.
Series: R increases;
Parallel: R decreases

Q.32
Define the conductivity/resistivity of the material and temperature coefficient of resistance.
Resistivity (ρ)
Resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area. R = ρL/A  ⟹  ρ = RA/L   Unit: Ω·m
Conductivity (σ)
Reciprocal of resistivity. σ = 1/ρ   Unit: S/m (Siemens per metre). Measures how well a material conducts electricity.
Temp Coefficient (α)
The fractional change in resistance per degree Celsius change in temperature. α = (R_T − R₀)/(R₀ × T)   Unit: /°C or K⁻¹
Formula
R_T = R₀(1 + αT)   For metals: α is positive (resistance increases with T).
Q.33
Prove that the current density of metallic conductor is directly proportional to the drift speed of electrons.
Setup
Consider a conductor of cross-sectional area A, with n free electrons per unit volume, each having drift velocity v_d.
Step 1
In time dt, electrons traveling distance v_d·dt pass through area A.
Step 2
Volume of conductor = A × v_d × dt
Step 3
Number of electrons = n × A × v_d × dt
Step 4
Charge: dQ = n × A × v_d × dt × e
Step 5
Current: I = dQ/dt = neAv_d
Current density
J = I/A = nev_d
∴ J ∝ v_d
Current density is directly proportional to drift speed. ✓
Q.34
Define EMF and state its formula.
Definition
EMF (Electromotive Force) of a cell is the energy supplied by the cell per unit charge to move the charge through the entire circuit (including internal resistance). It is the open-circuit terminal voltage.
Formula
EMF (ε) = W/Q   Unit: Volt (V) = J/C
Terminal Voltage
V = ε − Ir   where I = current, r = internal resistance
For external circuit
ε = I(R + r)   where R = external resistance
Note
EMF is not a force — it is energy per unit charge (measured in Volts).
