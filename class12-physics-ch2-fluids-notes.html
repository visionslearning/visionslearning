2.1

🌊 Pressure in Fluids — Pascal's Law

🔑 Key Points
  • Pressure: P = F/A; force per unit area; SI unit: N/m² = Pascal (Pa)
  • Pressure in liquid at depth h: P = P₀ + ρgh (ρ = density, g = 9.8 m/s²)
  • Pressure is a scalar — acts equally in all directions in a static fluid
  • Pascal's Law: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished to every part of the fluid and walls of container
  • Hydraulic lift: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂ → small force on small piston creates large force on large piston
  • 1 atm = 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa = 760 mm of Hg = 76 cm of Hg
  • Gauge pressure: P_gauge = P − P₀ = ρgh (pressure above atmospheric)

🏊 Archimedes' Principle & Buoyancy

🔑 Key Points
  • Buoyant force: upward force on submerged object = weight of fluid displaced

🌀 Equation of Continuity

🔑 Key Points
  • Steady flow (streamline flow): velocity at any point is constant in time
  • Equation of Continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ = constant (conservation of mass for incompressible fluid)
  • Narrow pipe → faster flow; Wider pipe → slower flow
  • Reynolds number: Re = ρvd/η; Re < 1000: laminar; Re > 2000: turbulent
  • Volume flow rate: Q = Av = constant throughout pipe

⚡ Bernoulli's Theorem — MOST IMP

🔑 Statement
  • For steady, non-viscous, incompressible flow along a streamline, the sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy and potential energy per unit volume is constant
P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant
P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂
📌 Applications of Bernoulli's Theorem
  • 1. Torricelli's theorem (Speed of efflux): v = √(2gh) — speed of liquid emerging from hole at depth h in a large tank (P₀ same on both sides, v₁≈0)
  • 2. Venturi meter: measures flow speed using pressure difference between wide and narrow sections
  • 3. Aerofoil / Aircraft lift: curved upper surface → air moves faster over top → lower pressure on top → net upward lift force
  • 4. Spray gun / Atomiser: fast air over narrow tube → low pressure → liquid sucked up and sprayed
  • 5. Magnus effect: spinning ball curves due to pressure difference (swing bowling, tennis topspin)
  • 6. Blowing between papers: fast air → low pressure → papers come together
📐 Derivation of Torricelli's Theorem
Apply Bernoulli between surface (1) and hole (2):
P₀ + ½ρ(0)² + ρgh = P₀ + ½ρv² + 0
ρgh = ½ρv²
∴ v = √(2gh) — Torricelli's theorem ✓

🌿 Viscosity & Stokes' Law — MOST IMP

🔑 Key Points
  • Viscosity (η): internal friction between adjacent layers of a moving fluid; resists relative motion
  • SI unit of viscosity: N·s/m² = Pa·s (CGS: Poise)
  • Liquids: viscosity decreases with temperature increase (honey flows easily when hot)
  • Gases: viscosity increases with temperature increase
  • Stokes' Law: viscous drag force on sphere: F = 6πηrv
  • Terminal velocity: constant velocity when net force = 0 (weight = buoyancy + viscous drag)
📐 Derivation of Terminal Velocity
At terminal velocity v_T: W = F_B + F_v
(4/3)πr³ρg = (4/3)πr³σg + 6πηrv_T
6πηrv_T = (4/3)πr³(ρ−σ)g
∴ v_T = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η ✓
  • v_T ∝ r² → larger sphere, higher terminal velocity
  • v_T ∝ 1/η → more viscous fluid, lower terminal velocity
  • v_T ∝ (ρ−σ) → denser object in lighter fluid, higher v_T

🔮 Surface Tension — MOST IMP

🔑 Key Points
  • Surface tension (T): force per unit length acting tangentially along liquid surface; T = F/L
  • SI unit: N/m; arises due to cohesive forces between surface molecules
  • Surface acts like a stretched elastic membrane
  • Surface energy: W = T × ΔA (work done to increase surface area by ΔA)
  • T decreases with increase in temperature; becomes zero at critical temperature
  • Excess pressure inside a drop: ΔP = 2T/r (one surface)
  • Excess pressure inside soap bubble: ΔP = 4T/r (two surfaces — inner and outer)
📐 Derivation — Excess Pressure in Soap Bubble (ΔP = 4T/r)
Consider soap bubble of radius r split into 2 halves
Force due to surface tension on each half = T × 2πr (along rim)
Soap bubble has 2 surfaces → net force = T × 2πr × 2 = 4πrT
Force due to excess pressure = ΔP × πr²
At equilibrium: ΔP × πr² = 4πrT
∴ ΔP = 4T/r (soap bubble) ✓; ΔP = 2T/r (liquid drop)
📐 Capillarity & Capillary Rise: h = 2T cosθ / ρgr
  • Capillarity: rise or fall of liquid in a narrow tube due to surface tension
  • Rise: wetting liquid (θ < 90°) — e.g., water in glass
  • Fall: non-wetting liquid (θ > 90°) — e.g., mercury in glass
Upward force = T × 2πr × cosθ (surface tension along contact line)
Weight of liquid column = ρ × πr²h × g
Equate: T × 2πr cosθ = ρπr²hg
∴ h = 2T cosθ / (ρgr) ✓
  • Thinner tube (smaller r) → greater capillary rise
  • Used in: plants (xylem vessels), pen nib, soil water movement, towels

⚡ Bernoulli's Theorem
Q1
State Bernoulli's theorem. Derive Bernoulli's equation. Give two applications.
🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·20204 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Statement: For steady, non-viscous, incompressible fluid flow along a streamline, the total energy (pressure + kinetic + potential) per unit volume remains constant.

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

Derivation using Work-Energy theorem:

Consider fluid element of volume ΔV flowing from (1) to (2) Work done by pressure: W = (P₁ − P₂)ΔV Change in KE = ½ρΔV(v₂² − v₁²) Change in PE = ρΔVg(h₂ − h₁) By work-energy theorem: W = ΔKE + ΔPE (P₁−P₂)ΔV = ½ρΔV(v₂²−v₁²) + ρΔVg(h₂−h₁)
P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂ ✓

Applications:

1. Aerofoil (aircraft lift): Curved upper surface → air moves faster over top → lower pressure on top → net upward lift force (F = ΔP × A)

2. Spray gun / Atomiser: Fast-moving air over tube opening creates low pressure → liquid sucked up and sprayed as fine droplets.

Q2
Derive an expression for the speed of efflux (Torricelli's theorem): v = √(2gh).
🔴 HSC 2024·2023·20223 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Consider a large tank with a small hole at depth h below free surface.

At free surface (1): P = P₀, v₁ ≈ 0 (large tank), height = h

At hole (2): P = P₀ (open to atmosphere), v₂ = v (efflux speed), height = 0

Applying Bernoulli's equation between (1) and (2):

P₀ + ½ρ(0)² + ρgh = P₀ + ½ρv² + 0 ρgh = ½ρv²
∴ v = √(2gh) — Torricelli's theorem ✓

Note: This is the same speed as a body falling freely through height h — the liquid behaves as if it fell freely from the surface level to the hole.

🔮 Surface Tension
Q3
Derive an expression for excess pressure inside a soap bubble: ΔP = 4T/r. How does it differ for a liquid drop?
🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·20203–4 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Excess pressure in a liquid drop:

Split the drop into two halves. Surface tension acts along the circular rim (circumference 2πr).

Force due to surface tension = T × 2πr (pulling halves together) Force due to excess pressure = ΔP × πr² (pushing outward) At equilibrium: ΔP × πr² = T × 2πr
ΔP = 2T/r (liquid drop — 1 surface)

Excess pressure in a soap bubble:

Soap bubble has TWO surfaces (inner and outer). Each surface contributes pressure 2T/r:

ΔP = 2 × (2T/r) = 4T/r (soap bubble — 2 surfaces) ✓

Conclusion: Soap bubble has greater excess pressure than a liquid drop of the same radius. A smaller bubble has higher internal pressure than a larger bubble (ΔP ∝ 1/r).

Q4
What is capillarity? Derive the expression for capillary rise: h = 2T cosθ / ρgr.
🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·20203–4 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Capillarity: The phenomenon of rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow (capillary) tube due to surface tension is called capillarity.

Rise: Wetting liquid (θ < 90°) — e.g., water in glass capillary

Fall: Non-wetting liquid (θ > 90°) — e.g., mercury in glass capillary

Derivation:

For capillary tube of radius r, surface tension T acts at angle θ (angle of contact) along the glass surface.

Upward force due to surface tension: F_up = T × 2πr × cosθ Weight of liquid column of height h: W = ρ × πr²h × g At equilibrium: F_up = W T × 2πr cosθ = ρπr²hg
∴ h = 2T cosθ / (ρgr) ✓

Conclusion: h ∝ 1/r — thinner tube → greater capillary rise. This explains water transport in plants through narrow xylem vessels.

🌿 Viscosity & Stokes' Law
Q5
State Stokes' Law. Derive the expression for terminal velocity: v_T = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η.
🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·20204 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Stokes' Law: When a sphere of radius r moves with velocity v through a fluid of viscosity η, the viscous drag force is:

F = 6πηrv

Derivation of terminal velocity:

Forces on sphere (density ρ, radius r) falling through fluid (density σ):

(i) Weight W = (4/3)πr³ρg (downward) (ii) Buoyant force F_B = (4/3)πr³σg (upward) (iii) Viscous drag F_v = 6πηrv_T (upward, at terminal velocity)

At terminal velocity v_T: net force = 0

W = F_B + F_v (4/3)πr³ρg = (4/3)πr³σg + 6πηrv_T 6πηrv_T = (4/3)πr³(ρ−σ)g
∴ v_T = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η ✓

Conclusions: v_T ∝ r² (double radius → 4× v_T); v_T ∝ 1/η (more viscous → smaller v_T)

Q6
State Archimedes' Principle. How is it used to find relative density of a solid?
🔴 HSC 2023·2022·20213 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Archimedes' Principle: When a body is partially or fully immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

F_B = ρ_fluid × V_submerged × g

Relative density:

Let W₁ = weight in air, W₂ = apparent weight in water (W₂ = W₁ − F_B)

Loss in weight in water = W₁ − W₂ = weight of water displaced = F_B Relative density = density of solid / density of water = mass of solid / mass of equal volume of water
Relative density = W₁ / (W₁ − W₂)
Q7
What is the difference between viscosity of liquids and gases on heating? Explain with reason.
🔴 HSC 2024·2023·20212 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Liquids: Viscosity decreases with increase in temperature.

Reason: Viscosity in liquids is due to cohesive forces between molecules. When temperature increases, cohesive forces weaken → molecules slide past each other more easily → less internal friction → lower viscosity.

Example: Honey flows easily when heated.

Gases: Viscosity increases with increase in temperature.

Reason: Viscosity in gases is due to momentum transfer between gas molecules. At higher temperatures, molecules move faster → more momentum exchange between layers → greater resistance to flow → higher viscosity.

Q8
State Pascal's Law. Explain the working of a hydraulic lift with formula.
🔴 HSC 2023·2022·20203 Marks
✅ Model Answer

Pascal's Law: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished to every part of the fluid and to the walls of the container, in all directions.

Hydraulic Lift:

Two pistons of areas A₁ (small) and A₂ (large) connected by fluid-filled pipe.

Apply small force F₁ on small piston → pressure P = F₁/A₁ transmitted everywhere.

Pressure on large piston = F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂
F₂ = F₁ × A₂/A₁ (mechanical advantage = A₂/A₁)

Since A₂ >> A₁ → F₂ >> F₁. A small force lifts a heavy car!

Application: Hydraulic press, brakes, lifts in garages

⭐ Extra IMP Q1 — 2 Marks
What is surface energy? Derive the relation between surface energy and surface tension: W = T × ΔA.
Surface energy: The potential energy stored per unit area of the liquid surface. When surface area increases, work is done against cohesive forces.

Derivation: Consider a rectangular frame with a sliding wire. Move wire by dx → surface area increases by ΔA = L × dx (film has 2 surfaces → ΔA = 2L·dx)
Work done: W = F × dx = (T × 2L) × dx
W = T × ΔA ✓
Surface energy = T × surface area. When drops merge → surface area decreases → energy released (exothermic).
⭐ Extra IMP Q2 — 2 Marks
Write 4 factors affecting surface tension of a liquid.
1. Temperature: T decreases as temperature increases; zero at critical temperature
2. Impurities: Soluble impurities (soap, detergent) decrease T; insoluble impurities may increase T
3. Electrification: Surface tension decreases when liquid is electrically charged
4. Nature of medium above liquid: T of water in contact with air ≠ T in contact with other liquids
⭐ Extra IMP Q3 — 3 Marks
Explain why a small bubble has higher internal pressure than a large bubble. What happens when two soap bubbles of different sizes are connected?
From ΔP = 4T/r: excess pressure is inversely proportional to radius.
Smaller bubble (small r) → higher ΔP
Larger bubble (large r) → lower ΔP

When connected: Air flows from small bubble (high pressure) to large bubble (low pressure). Small bubble shrinks and large bubble grows — until small bubble disappears or both are in equilibrium. This is a beautiful demonstration of Bernoulli's principle and surface tension.
⭐ Extra IMP Q4 — 3 Marks
Explain the application of Bernoulli's theorem to explain lift on an aeroplane wing (aerofoil).
An aerofoil is curved on the upper surface and flat (or less curved) on the lower surface.

When the wing moves through air:
• Air over the curved upper surface must travel a longer path → moves FASTER
• Air under the flat lower surface moves SLOWER
By Bernoulli's theorem: P + ½ρv² = constant
• Upper surface: v_upper↑ → P_upper↓
• Lower surface: v_lower↓ → P_lower↑
Lift force F = (P_lower − P_upper) × A
This net upward force (lift) supports the aircraft. Greater speed → greater lift. Angle of attack also increases lift.
⭐ Extra IMP Q5 — 3 Marks
Explain 4 everyday applications of capillarity.
1. Plants: Water absorbed by roots rises through narrow xylem vessels to leaves by capillary action
2. Pen/fountain pen: Ink rises in narrow capillary channel in pen nib to the writing tip
3. Towel/sponge: Absorbs water by capillary action through the spaces between cloth fibres
4. Soil water: Water rises from deep soil layers to the surface through narrow pores in soil (important for plant growth)
5. Candle wick draws molten wax by capillary action
⭐ Extra IMP Q6 — 2 Marks
What is angle of contact? When does liquid rise and when does it fall in a capillary tube?
Angle of contact (θ): The angle between the tangent to the liquid surface at the point of contact and the solid surface, measured inside the liquid.

θ < 90° (acute): Liquid wets the solid → concave meniscus → liquid RISES (e.g., water in glass, h positive)
θ > 90° (obtuse): Liquid does not wet solid → convex meniscus → liquid FALLS (e.g., mercury in glass, h negative)
θ = 0°: Perfect wetting (cosθ = 1 → maximum rise)
θ = 90°: No capillary effect (cosθ = 0 → h = 0)
⭐ Extra IMP Q7 — 3 Marks
Write short note on streamline flow, turbulent flow and Reynolds number.
Streamline (Laminar) flow: Smooth, ordered flow in parallel layers; each fluid particle follows a definite path; no mixing of layers. Occurs at low speeds.

Turbulent flow: Irregular, chaotic flow; eddies, whirls and vortices form; energy wasted as heat. Occurs at high speeds.

Reynolds number (Re): Dimensionless number that predicts type of flow:
Re = ρvd/η
Re < 1000 → Laminar flow
1000 < Re < 2000 → Transitional
Re > 2000 → Turbulent flow
Used by engineers to design pipes, aircraft and ships.

⭐ Extra IMP Q9 — 3 Marks
Explain the phenomenon of Magnus effect with an example.
Magnus effect: When a spinning ball moves through air, the spinning motion drags air around it. The resulting pressure difference creates a force perpendicular to the direction of motion — causing the ball to curve.

Explanation: On one side, air movement due to spin adds to the air velocity → lower pressure (Bernoulli). On the other side, they oppose → higher pressure. Net force pushes ball towards low-pressure side.

Examples:
• Swing bowling in cricket — ball swings in air
• Topspin in tennis — ball dips down
• Banana kick in football — ball curves around wall
• Spin of a golf ball affecting trajectory
⭐ Extra IMP Q10 — 2 Marks
What is Venturi meter? Write the principle on which it works.
Venturi meter: A device used to measure the flow speed (velocity) of a fluid in a pipe. It consists of a wide section (A₁) connected to a narrow constriction (A₂).

Principle: Based on Bernoulli's theorem — when fluid flows through the narrow section, its speed increases and pressure decreases. By measuring the pressure difference (using a manometer), the flow speed can be calculated:
v₁ = A₂√(2ΔP/ρ(A₁²−A₂²))
Use: Measuring water flow in pipes, air speed measurement, carburettors in engines.
