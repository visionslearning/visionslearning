📖 Part 1 — Short Answer Questions
Q1
Define Biodiversity.★★
Show ▾
Biodiversity is the variety of life on Earth — the total number of species of plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms existing in all ecosystems. It includes three levels:
  • Genetic diversity — variety of genes within a species
  • Species diversity — variety of species in an area
  • Ecosystem diversity — variety of ecosystems/habitats
Term coined by Walter G. Rosen (1985); popularised by E.O. Wilson.

Q4
Define excretion.★★
Show ▾
Excretion is the process of removal of metabolic waste products from the body of a living organism. These are nitrogenous wastes produced during metabolism.
  • In humans — urea excreted mainly through kidneys (urine)
  • Other excretory products: CO₂ (lungs), water and salts (skin), bile pigments (liver)
Types based on waste excreted: Ammonotelic (ammonia), Ureotelic (urea — humans), Uricotelic (uric acid — birds, reptiles).
Q5
Write a note on ciliated columnar epithelium.★★
Show ▾
Ciliated Columnar Epithelium:
  • Made of tall, column-shaped cells with cilia on their free surface
  • Cells contain an oval nucleus near the base
  • Cilia beat rhythmically to move substances across the surface
Location:
  • Lining of trachea (windpipe) and bronchi — moves mucus with trapped dust/germs upward
  • Fallopian tubes (oviducts) — moves the ovum toward the uterus
  • Lining of ventricles of brain — moves cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
Function: Movement of materials (mucus, eggs, CSF) across epithelial surface.

Q7
What is chemotaxonomy? Explain with a suitable example.★★
Show ▾
Chemotaxonomy is a method of biological classification based on similarities and differences in the chemical constituents (chemical compounds) present among the organisms being classified.
In short, classification based on chemistry of organisms.
Examples:
  • Archaea — cell wall WITHOUT peptidoglycan
  • Eubacteria — cell wall WITH peptidoglycan (murein)
  • Fungi — cell wall made of chitin
  • Plants — cell wall made of cellulose
  • Serology (blood proteins), DNA analysis, and secondary metabolites are used in chemotaxonomy

Q10
Define tissue.★★
Show ▾
Tissue is a group of cells that are similar in structure, origin and function, and work together to perform a specific function.
Term 'tissue' coined by Bichat (1771–1802). Study of tissues = Histology.
Types in animals: Epithelial, Connective, Muscular, Nervous.
Types in plants: Meristematic (dividing), Permanent (non-dividing).
Q11
What is Binomial Nomenclature system? Give common name of Mangifera indica.★★★
Show ▾
Binomial Nomenclature is the system of giving every organism a scientific name consisting of exactly two words — genus name (Generic epithet) and species name (Specific epithet).
Introduced by Carl Linnaeus in "Species Plantarum" (1753).
Rules:
  • Genus name starts with capital letter; species name with small letter
  • Written in italics when printed; underlined separately when handwritten
  • Author's abbreviated name may follow
Mangifera indica L. — Common name = Mango
Q12
What are permanent plant tissues and mention their types.★★
Show ▾
Permanent tissues are plant tissues whose cells have lost the ability to divide (non-meristematic). They are derived from meristematic tissue and have undergone differentiation.
Types:
  • Simple permanent tissue — made of one type of cells:
    • Parenchyma — thin-walled, loosely packed; storage and photosynthesis
    • Collenchyma — unevenly thick-walled; provides mechanical support
    • Sclerenchyma — very thick-walled, dead cells; provides strength
  • Complex permanent tissue — made of more than one type of cells:
    • Xylem — transports water and minerals upward
    • Phloem — transports food (organic solutes) downward
  • Special tissues: Secretory tissues (latex, resin canals)

Q15
Define metabolism.★★
Show ▾
Metabolism is the sum total of all the chemical reactions occurring in a living organism. It includes:
  • Anabolism (Constructive metabolism): Building up of complex molecules from simpler ones; requires energy. E.g., photosynthesis, protein synthesis.
  • Catabolism (Destructive metabolism): Breaking down of complex molecules into simpler ones; releases energy. E.g., respiration, digestion.
Metabolism is a key characteristic of living organisms. All metabolic reactions are controlled by enzymes.
Q16
Why are Bryophytes called the amphibians of plants?★★
Show ▾
Bryophytes (mosses, liverworts, hornworts) are called amphibians of the plant kingdom because:
  • They can live on land (soil, rocks, bark of trees) — adapted to terrestrial conditions
  • But they require water for fertilization — the male gametes (antherozoids) need a film of water to swim to the female archegonium
  • They lack true vascular tissue (xylem and phloem) so cannot transport water efficiently on land
Just like amphibians (frogs) which live on land but need water for reproduction, Bryophytes are tied to moist environments for reproduction.

Q19
Name the protective covering present on the body of an endoparasite.★★
Show ▾
Endoparasites (parasites living inside host body) have special protective coverings:
  • Tegument (cuticle) — in flatworms like Taenia (tapeworm) and Fasciola (liver fluke). It protects from host's digestive enzymes.

  • Highly developed reproductive system (produce thousands of eggs)
  • Hooks and suckers for attachment
  • Anaerobic respiration (no oxygen inside host gut)

Pedicel (stalk) → Receptacle → Calyx (sepals) → Corolla (petals) → Androecium (stamens: filament + anther) → Gynoecium (pistil: stigma + style + ovary with ovules)

Q22
Give adaptive radiation in birds.★★
Show ▾
Adaptive radiation is the evolution of different species from one ancestral species adapting to different ecological niches.
Adaptive radiation in birds — examples:
  • Beak adaptations: Darwin's finches on Galapagos Islands — evolved different beak shapes:
    • Thick beak → cracking hard seeds (ground finch)
    • Long slender beak → probing bark for insects (woodpecker finch)
    • Hooked beak → tearing meat (hawk)
  • Limb/foot adaptations: Duck — webbed feet (swimming); Eagle — talons (catching prey); Ostrich — powerful legs (running)
  • Darwin's finches are the classic example of adaptive radiation
Q23
Who proposed the 5 Kingdom system of classification?★★
Show ▾
R.H. Whittaker proposed the Five Kingdom Classification in 1969.
Five Kingdoms:
  • Kingdom Monera (prokaryotes)
  • Kingdom Protista (unicellular eukaryotes)
  • Kingdom Fungi
  • Kingdom Plantae
  • Kingdom Animalia
This system shows the phylogenetic (evolutionary) relationship between organisms. It was proposed because Linnaeus' two-kingdom system could not classify organisms like Bacteria, Fungi, and Euglena.

Q25
Give the general characters of virus.★★★
Show ▾
General characteristics of viruses:
  • Non-cellular (acellular) — not made of cells
  • Ultramicroscopic — visible only under electron microscope
  • Have protein coat called capsid made of capsomeres; some have additional envelope
  • Genetic material = either DNA or RNA (never both)
  • Obligate intracellular parasites — can reproduce only inside a living host cell
  • Inert/crystallisable outside host cell (demonstrated by Stanley)
  • Can infect all living organisms — bacteria (bacteriophage), plants, animals
  • Cause diseases: AIDS (HIV), influenza, smallpox, rabies, COVID-19, dengue
  • Named by Louis Pasteur; 'virus' by M.J. Beijernek

Q29
What is ecdysis or moulting?★★
Show ▾
Ecdysis (Moulting) is the periodic shedding (casting off) of the exoskeleton in arthropods to allow growth.
Arthropods have a hard chitinous exoskeleton that does not grow with the body. So they must shed it periodically to grow.
Process:
  • A new soft exoskeleton forms under the old one
  • Old exoskeleton splits and is shed
  • The animal rapidly absorbs water/air to expand before new skeleton hardens
  • New exoskeleton hardens (sclerotization)
Ecdysis is controlled by the hormone ecdysone. It occurs in insects, crustaceans, and also in nematodes.

Q37
Write a note on parasitic adaptation of Liverfluke.★★
Show ▾
Liverfluke (Fasciola hepatica) belongs to Phylum Platyhelminthes. It is an endoparasite in the liver of sheep and cattle.
Parasitic adaptations:
  • Tegument (cuticle) — thick protective layer; resistant to host's digestive enzymes
  • Suckers (oral sucker + ventral sucker/acetabulum) — for attachment to host tissues
  • No locomotory organs — not needed inside host
  • Highly developed reproductive system — hermaphrodite; produces thousands of eggs
  • Anaerobic respiration — survives in low-oxygen environment of host gut/liver
  • Loss of sensory organs — not needed inside host
  • Complex life cycle with intermediate host (snail — Lymnaea) for transmission
Q38
What is adipose tissue? Give its function.★★
Show ▾
Adipose tissue is a type of loose connective tissue made of fat-storing cells called adipocytes (fat cells). The cells are rounded with nucleus pushed to the side by a large fat droplet.
Location: Below skin (subcutaneous layer), around kidneys, heart, around eyeballs, in yellow bone marrow.
Functions:
  • Energy storage — fat stored in adipocytes serves as energy reserve
  • Thermal insulation — subcutaneous fat prevents heat loss
  • Mechanical cushioning — protects organs from mechanical shock
  • Buoyancy — in aquatic mammals
  • Helps in absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)
Q39
Phylum Ctenophora is considered as a minor phylum. Explain.★★
Show ▾
Phylum Ctenophora (comb jellies) is considered a minor phylum because:
  • It has very few species (about 100-150 known species) compared to major phyla
  • Members are exclusively marine (not found in freshwater or land)
  • They are radially symmetrical, diploblastic organisms resembling Cnidaria
  • Have comb plates (ctenes) — rows of cilia used for locomotion — unique feature
  • They have colloblasts (sticky cells) for catching prey — no nematocysts (unlike Cnidaria)
  • Their evolutionary position is uncertain; they form a small, isolated group
  • Economic importance is negligible
E.g., Pleurobrachia (sea gooseberry), Beroe

Q42
Describe structure, location and function of squamous epithelial tissue.★★
Show ▾
Squamous Epithelium (Pavement epithelium):
Structure: Single layer of flat, scale-like (tile-like) cells arranged edge-to-edge. Cells are irregular in shape with centrally placed oval nucleus. Cells fit together like floor tiles.
Location:
  • Lining of air sacs (alveoli) of lungs
  • Bowman's capsule of kidney
  • Blood capillaries and lymph vessels (endothelium)
  • Inner lining of heart (endocardium)
Function:
  • Allows diffusion of gases and fluids (thin cells allow easy passage)
  • Provides smooth surface to reduce friction
  • Filtration in kidney glomerulus
Q43
Draw a suitable diagram and describe the structure of Areolar connective tissue.★★
Show ▾
Areolar Connective Tissue: Most common type of loose connective tissue.
Structure:
  • Matrix (ground substance) — semi-fluid, viscous hyaluronic acid
  • Fibres in matrix:
    • White collagen fibres — inelastic; give strength
    • Yellow elastic fibres — give elasticity
  • Cells in matrix:
    • Fibroblasts — secrete fibres and ground substance
    • Mast cells — secrete heparin (anticoagulant) and histamine (inflammation)
    • Macrophages (Histiocytes) — phagocytose foreign particles
    • Plasma cells — produce antibodies
    • Fat cells (adipocytes) — store fat
Location: Beneath skin, between organs, around blood vessels and nerves.
Function: Packing material, connects and supports organs, wound healing, immune defense.

Q45
Identify the class of plants: a) seed producing plant b) spore producing plant c) plant body undifferentiated into root stem and leaves d) plant needs water for fertilization★★★
Show ▾
  • a) Seed producing plant → Angiosperm (enclosed seeds/fruits) or Gymnosperm (naked seeds). E.g., Mango (Angiosperm), Pinus (Gymnosperm)
  • b) Spore producing plant → Thallophyta (Algae), Bryophyta, or Pteridophyta (Ferns). E.g., Fern produces spores
  • c) Plant body undifferentiated into root, stem and leaves → Thallophyta (Algae and Fungi). Body = thallus. E.g., Spirogyra, Ulva
  • d) Plant needs water for fertilization → Bryophyta. They need a film of water for antherozoids (male gametes) to swim to archegonium for fertilization. This is why Bryophytes are called amphibians of plant kingdom.
Q46
Define Taxonomy.★★
Show ▾
Taxonomy is the science of classification of organisms following certain rules or principles. It involves:
  • Identification of organisms
  • Nomenclature (giving scientific names)
  • Classification (grouping into categories)
The word taxonomy comes from Greek: taxis (arrangement) + nomos (law).
Term first introduced by A.P. de Candolle (Swiss Botanist, 1778–1841).
"Systematics is the study of kinds and diversity of organisms and their evolutionary relationship" — G. Simpson, 1961.

Epidermis (outermost) → Hypodermis → Cortex (parenchyma) → Endodermis → Pericycle → Vascular bundles (xylem + phloem — collateral, open type in dicot) → Pith (large central parenchyma)
Functions of stem:
  • Supports leaves, flowers, fruits
  • Conducts water (xylem) and food (phloem)
  • Stores food (e.g., sugarcane, potato tuber)
  • Modified stems for vegetative propagation: Rhizome (ginger), Tuber (potato), Bulb (onion)
  • Photosynthesis in green stems
