General Instructions:

  1. All questions are compulsory.
  2. Use of a simple calculator is allowed.
  3. Draw neat, labelled diagrams wherever necessary.
  4. Figures to the right indicate full marks.
  5. For numerical problems, show all working steps clearly.
  6. Physical constants: g = 10 m/s², h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3×10⁸ m/s, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C

(A) Zero(B) Infinite(C) Very high(D) Same as at room temperature

(A) Tesla(B) Weber(C) Henry(D) Farad

(A) intensity of light(B) frequency of light(C) area of cathode(D) nature of anode

(A) charge(B) momentum(C) energy(D) temperature

(A) 1/4(B) 1/8(C) 1/16(D) 1/32
Section B — Very Short Answer Questions 2 Marks Each
8.[2] State Gauss's theorem in electrostatics. Write its mathematical form.

10.[2] State Faraday's laws of electromagnetic induction.

Section C — Short Answer Questions 3 Marks Each
14.[3] State and prove Coulomb's law of electrostatics in vector form.
15.[3] Derive an expression for the electric field due to an infinitely long straight charged wire using Gauss's law.
16.[3] Explain with a circuit diagram the working of a Wheatstone bridge. When is it said to be balanced?
17.[3] A coil of 200 turns, area 0.05 m² is placed in a uniform magnetic field of 0.2 T. Calculate the maximum EMF induced when the coil rotates at 50 rev/s.
18.[3] Draw a labelled diagram of a compound microscope and explain its working. Write the formula for its magnifying power.
19.[3] What is Compton effect? Explain it with the help of quantum theory of radiation.
20.[3] Explain the construction and working of a nuclear reactor. Name the components and their functions.
Section D — Long Answer Questions 4 Marks Each
21.[4] (a) Derive an expression for the electric potential at a point due to a point charge.
(b) The electric potential at a distance of 0.3 m from a charge is 600 V. Find the magnitude of the charge. (K = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²)
22.[4] (a) State Kirchhoff's laws of current and voltage. Apply them to derive the condition for a balanced Wheatstone bridge.
(b) Three resistors of 4 Ω, 6 Ω and 12 Ω are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.
23.[4] (a) Explain interference of light. State the conditions for constructive and destructive interference.
(b) In Young's double slit experiment, the slit separation is 0.5 mm and the screen is 1.5 m away. If the fringe width is 1.8 mm, find the wavelength of light used.
24.[4] (a) Derive Einstein's photoelectric equation. Define threshold frequency and stopping potential.
(b) Light of frequency 6×10¹⁴ Hz falls on a metal of work function 2.0 eV. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of emitted photoelectrons. (h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s, 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J)
25.[4] (a) State Bohr's postulates for hydrogen atom. Derive the formula for the radius of nth orbit.
(b) Calculate the energy of an electron in the second orbit of hydrogen atom. (E₁ = −13.6 eV)
