SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10
(i) Fertilisation occurs in humans in
Answer: (B) Fallopian tube
Fertilisation occurs in the ampulla of Fallopian tube (oviduct). Ovum released at ovulation, sperm swim up to meet it here.
(ii) Dihybrid cross F₂ phenotypic ratio
Answer: (C) 9:3:3:1
Dihybrid cross: YYRR × yyrr → F₁: YyRr → F₂: 9 Y_R_ : 3 Y_rr : 3 yyR_ : 1 yyrr = 9:3:3:1
(iii) Taq polymerase is used in
Answer: (B) PCR
Taq polymerase (from Thermus aquaticus) is thermostable → used in PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction). Works at 72°C extension temperature.
(iv) Central dogma of molecular biology
Answer: (C) DNA → RNA → Protein
Francis Crick's central dogma: DNA (replication) → DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → Protein
(v) Darwin's finches — example of
Answer: (D) adaptive radiation
Adaptive radiation = evolution of different species from a common ancestor in different environments. Darwin's finches adapted beaks to different food sources.
(vi) Term 'gene' coined by
Answer: (C) Johanssen
Wilhelm Johanssen (1909) coined the terms "gene," "genotype," and "phenotype." Mendel used "factors."
(vii) NOT a greenhouse gas
Answer: (C) N₂
N₂ (nitrogen) is not a greenhouse gas. Greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O, O₃, CFCs
(viii) Ozone depletion caused by
Answer: (A) CFCs
CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) from refrigerants/aerosols release Cl atoms in stratosphere → destroy O₃: Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂   (catalytic cycle)
(ix) First hormone by rDNA technology
Answer: (B) insulin
Humulin (recombinant human insulin) — first commercial product of rDNA technology (Eli Lilly, 1982). Human insulin gene inserted in E. coli/yeast.
(x) Vaccination provides
Answer: (D) artificial active immunity
Vaccination introduces antigen → body produces antibodies actively → artificial active immunity (long-lasting)
SECTION B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8
(i) Spermatogenesis
Spermatogenesis: Process of formation of spermatozoa (sperm) from spermatogonia. Location: Seminiferous tubules of testes (in the testis)
(ii) Test cross
Test cross: Crossing an organism showing dominant phenotype with homozygous recessive (aa) to determine genotype. Purpose: To check if dominant individual is AA or Aa. If any offspring show recessive trait → individual is Aa.
(iii) What is a codon?
A codon is a sequence of 3 consecutive nucleotides (triplet) on mRNA that codes for a specific amino acid. Example: AUG = start codon (methionine)  |  UAA, UAG, UGA = stop codons
(iv) Replication fork
Replication fork is the Y-shaped structure formed at the point where the double helix is unwound and new DNA strands are being synthesised during replication.
(vii) Vector in rDNA technology
A vector is a DNA molecule (carrier) that is used to carry foreign DNA fragment into a host cell where it can replicate. Example: pBR322 (plasmid vector), λ phage (viral vector), BAC (bacterial artificial chromosome)
(x) Translation
Translation: Process of synthesis of protein from mRNA template using ribosomes. Location: Ribosomes (80S in eukaryotes, 70S in prokaryotes). Occurs in cytoplasm.
(xi) Lac operon structural genes
Lac operon: Set of genes in E. coli that are expressed together when lactose is present. Structural genes: (1) lacZ — β-galactosidase   (2) lacY — permease   (3) lacA — transacetylase
(xii) In situ conservation
In situ conservation: Protection of species in their natural habitat. Examples: National parks (Jim Corbett), Wildlife sanctuaries (Bharatpur), Biosphere reserves (Nilgiri)
SECTION C — Short Answers (selected) 8 × 2 = 16
(ii) Mendel's law of dominance
Law of Dominance: In a monohybrid cross, when two contrasting forms of a character are brought together, only one (dominant) is expressed in F₁; the other (recessive) is hidden. Example: Tall × Dwarf pea plant → F₁: All Tall (Tt) → Dominant = Tall F₂ ratio = 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf → 3:1 phenotypic ratio
(iv) Lac operon — role of inducer
Normally: Repressor protein binds to operator → blocks transcription (off) When lactose (inducer) is present: lactose binds to repressor → changes its shape → repressor cannot bind operator → RNA polymerase transcribes lacZ, lacY, lacA → β-galactosidase and permease produced → lactose metabolised Inducer = allolactose (modified lactose) → negative regulation
(vii) Bt toxin and its mode of action
Bt toxin: Protein toxin produced by Bacillus thuringiensis (soil bacterium). cry genes code for Bt toxin. Mode of action: Cry protein (protoxin) is ingested by insect → activated by alkaline pH of midgut → binds to receptors on epithelial cells → creates pores → cell swells and lyses → insect dies Bt crop: Bt cotton (Bollgard) has cry1Ac gene → resistant to bollworm
(xi) Meselson-Stahl experiment
E. coli grown on ¹⁵N (heavy nitrogen) → shifted to ¹⁴N medium → centrifuged after 1 and 2 generations After 1 generation: 1 band — intermediate density (¹⁴N/¹⁵N hybrid) After 2 generations: 2 bands — one intermediate, one light (¹⁴N/¹⁴N) Conclusion: DNA replication is semi-conservative (each new DNA has one old + one new strand)
SECTION D — Medium Answers (selected) 6 × 3 = 18
(ii) Dihybrid cross: YYRR × yyrr
P: YYRR (yellow round) × yyrr (green wrinkled) F₁: all YyRr (yellow round) F₂: YyRr × YyRr → 16 combinations F₂ phenotype ratio: 9 Yellow Round (Y_R_) : 3 Yellow Wrinkled (Y_rr) : 3 Green Round (yyR_) : 1 Green Wrinkled (yyrr) 9:3:3:1 — Mendel's dihybrid ratio (law of independent assortment)
(vi) Steps of recombinant DNA technology
1. Isolation of DNA from donor (gene of interest) 2. Cutting DNA with restriction endonuclease (creates sticky ends) 3. Isolation of vector DNA, cut with same restriction enzyme 4. Ligation: join insert + vector using DNA ligase → recombinant DNA 5. Transformation: insert recombinant DNA into host cell (E. coli) 6. Selection of transformants (using antibiotic resistance) 7. Cloning → expression → isolation of gene product
(vii) Biomagnification with DDT
Biomagnification: Progressive increase in concentration of a non-biodegradable substance at each trophic level in a food chain. DDT sprayed on water → absorbed by phytoplankton (0.003 ppm) → zooplankton (0.04 ppm) → small fish (0.5 ppm) → large fish (2 ppm) → fish-eating birds (25 ppm) DDT causes eggshell thinning in birds (esp. pelicans, eagles) → reproductive failure
SECTION E — Long Answers (selected) 4 × 4 = 16
(iii) DNA structure and semi-conservative replication
DNA structure (Watson-Crick, 1953): • Double helix, two antiparallel strands (5'→3' and 3'→5') • Sugar-phosphate backbone outside; bases inside • Base pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds) • 3.4 Å per base pair; 34 Å per turn (10 bp per turn) Semi-conservative replication: 1. Helicase unwinds double helix at origin 2. Primase adds RNA primer (3'-OH for DNA pol) 3. DNA pol III adds nucleotides 5'→3' (leading = continuous; lagging = Okazaki fragments) 4. DNA pol I replaces RNA primer with DNA 5. DNA ligase joins fragments Result: Each daughter DNA = 1 original + 1 new strand (proved by Meselson-Stahl)
(v) Recombinant DNA technology — insulin production + PCR
Insulin Production: • Human insulin gene (A chain + B chain) identified and synthesised chemically • Inserted into plasmid vector (pBR322) → recombinant DNA • Transformed into E. coli → expressed → A and B chains produced separately • Chains combined → disulfide bonds → active human insulin (Humulin) PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction — Kary Mullis, 1983): Step 1: Denaturation (94°C): DNA strands separate Step 2: Annealing (55°C): Primers bind to complementary sequences Step 3: Extension (72°C): Taq polymerase synthesises new strand One cycle doubles DNA → n cycles = 2ⁿ copies
