Q.1 (i)
In humans, fertilisation occurs in:
(A) uterus
(B) Fallopian tube
(C) ovary
(D) cervix
✔ Answer: (B) Fallopian tube
Fig: Female reproductive system — fertilisation occurs in the ampulla of Fallopian tube (★)
Explanation After ovulation, secondary oocyte is released from Graafian follicle of ovary. Sperm (deposited in vagina) swim up through uterus → Fallopian tube. Fertilisation occurs in the ampulla (widest part) of the Fallopian tube. The fertilised egg (zygote) then travels to the uterus and implants in the endometrium.

The dihybrid cross F₂ phenotypic ratio is:
(A) 3:1
(B) 1:2:1
(C) 9:3:3:1
(D) 1:1:1:1
✔ Answer: (C) 9:3:3:1

Q.1 (iii)
The enzyme Taq polymerase is used in:
(A) gel electrophoresis
(B) PCR
(C) Southern blotting
(D) ELISA
✔ Answer: (B) PCR
Explanation Taq polymerase is extracted from Thermus aquaticus (a thermophilic bacterium found in hot springs). It is heat-stable → remains active at 94°C denaturation temperature of PCR. Used in PCR to synthesise new DNA strands during the extension step at 72°C.
Q.1 (iv)
The central dogma of molecular biology is:
(A) DNA → Protein → RNA
(B) RNA → DNA → Protein
(C) DNA → RNA → Protein
(D) Protein → RNA → DNA
✔ Answer: (C) DNA → RNA → Protein
Fig: Central Dogma — DNA→RNA (Transcription in nucleus) → Protein (Translation at ribosome). Reverse transcription occurs in retroviruses (HIV).
Explanation Francis Crick (1958) proposed the Central Dogma: information flows from DNA → RNA → Protein. It cannot flow backwards from protein to nucleic acid (normal cells).
Q.1 (v)
Darwin's finches on Galápagos Islands are an example of:
(A) convergent evolution
(B) coevolution
(C) microevolution
(D) adaptive radiation
✔ Answer: (D) adaptive radiation
Explanation Adaptive radiation = evolution of many different species from a single common ancestor to fill different ecological niches. Darwin's finches: originated from one ancestor species on mainland, evolved different beak shapes on different Galápagos islands to exploit different food sources (seeds, insects, cactus, etc.).

Q.3 (xi) [2 Marks]
Describe the Meselson-Stahl experiment. What did it prove?
Fig: Meselson-Stahl experiment — CsCl density gradient shows: Gen1 = one hybrid band; Gen2 = one hybrid + one light band → PROVES semi-conservative replication
Experiment E. coli grown in ¹⁵N (heavy) → shifted to ¹⁴N (light) medium After 1 generation: CsCl centrifugation → ONE band at intermediate density (¹⁵N/¹⁴N hybrid) After 2 generations: TWO bands — one hybrid, one light (¹⁴N/¹⁴N)
Conclusion
DNA replication is SEMI-CONSERVATIVE: each daughter DNA has one original (parental) strand and one newly synthesised strand.

Q.4 (ii) [3 Marks]
Perform a dihybrid cross (YYRR × yyrr). Show F₁ and F₂ with Punnett square.
Parents (P) YYRR (Yellow Round seeds) × yyrr (Green Wrinkled seeds) Y = Yellow (dominant), y = Green (recessive) R = Round (dominant), r = Wrinkled (recessive)
Gametes Parent 1 produces only: YR Parent 2 produces only: yr
F₁ Generation YYRR × yyrr → All YyRr (Yellow Round) All F₁ plants show Yellow Round phenotype — both dominant traits expressed. Conclusion: Yellow is dominant over green; Round is dominant over wrinkled.
F₁ × F₁ Cross YyRr × YyRr → 4 types of gametes each: YR, Yr, yR, yr F₂ Punnett square: 4 × 4 = 16 combinations
F₂ Phenotypes 9 Yellow Round (Y_R_) : 3 Yellow Wrinkled (Y_rr) : 3 Green Round (yyR_) : 1 Green Wrinkled (yyrr) Phenotypic ratio = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 Genotypic ratio = 1 YYRR : 2 YYRr : 2 YyRR : 4 YyRr : 1 YYrr : 2 Yyrr : 1 yyRR : 2 yyRr : 1 yyrr
Law Proved Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment: Alleles of different genes assort independently into gametes. Y/y and R/r genes assort independently → 4 different gamete types in equal frequency.
F₂ Ratio = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1  |  New combinations: Yellow Wrinkled and Green Round appear in F₂
Q.4 (iv) [3 Marks]
Explain the light reactions of photosynthesis — photosystems, ETC and ATP synthesis.
Fig: Z-scheme — electrons flow from H₂O (low energy) through PS II → ETC → PS I → NADP⁺ (high energy). ATP produced by chemiosmosis.
Location Light reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes (grana) of chloroplasts. Two photosystems: PS I (P700) and PS II (P680) — named by order of discovery, not function.
Step 1: PS II (P680) Chlorophyll P680 absorbs light at 680 nm → excited electrons released. Photolysis of water: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂↑ (oxygen released as byproduct) These electrons replace those lost from P680.
Step 2: ETC Electrons from PS II pass through Electron Transport Chain: PS II → Plastoquinone (PQ) → Cytochrome b₆f complex → Plastocyanin (PC) → PS I ATP synthesis: Proton gradient across thylakoid membrane drives ATP synthase → photophosphorylation ADP + Pᵢ → ATP (using proton gradient energy)
Step 3: PS I (P700) Chlorophyll P700 absorbs light at 700 nm → electrons re-energised. Electrons pass to Ferredoxin (Fd) → NADP⁺ reductase NADP⁺ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → NADPH (reducing power for Calvin cycle)
Non-Cyclic vs Cyclic Non-cyclic photophosphorylation: electrons flow from H₂O → PS II → PS I → NADP⁺. Produces ATP + NADPH + O₂. Cyclic photophosphorylation: electrons from PS I cycle back via Fd → Cyt b₆f → PC → PS I. Only ATP produced (no NADPH, no O₂).
Overall Equation
12H₂O + 12NADP⁺ + 18ADP + 18Pᵢ → 6O₂ + 12NADPH + 18ATP (Light reactions)
Q.4 (vii) [3 Marks]
Explain biomagnification with DDT as an example.
Fig: Ecological pyramid showing biomagnification — DDT concentration increases 10,000× from phytoplankton (0.003 ppm) to fish-eating birds (25 ppm)
Definition Biomagnification (Biological Magnification): Progressive increase in concentration of non-biodegradable harmful chemicals at each successive trophic level in a food chain. Also called bioaccumulation — occurs because fat-soluble substances cannot be excreted and accumulate in lipid tissues.
Reason DDT is non-biodegradable — not broken down by biological processes. Fat-soluble → stored in fatty tissues of organisms, not excreted. Each organism eats many of the level below → concentrations multiply at each step.
DDT Example Water (0.000003 ppm) → Phytoplankton (0.003 ppm) → Zooplankton (0.04 ppm) → Small fish (0.5 ppm) → Large fish (2 ppm) → Fish-eating birds (25 ppm) Concentration increases 10 million times from water to birds.
Effects In fish-eating birds (osprey, bald eagle, brown pelican): 25 ppm DDT → eggshell thinning (interferes with calcium deposition) Eggs crushed by incubating parents → reproductive failure → population crash Bald eagle nearly went extinct in USA due to DDT accumulation.
Human Impact Humans at apex → highest DDT accumulation. Found in human breast milk. Can cause cancer, hormonal disruption, neurological damage.
Control
DDT banned in most countries. Use biodegradable pesticides. Integrated Pest Management (IPM). Biological control (natural predators).

Q.5 (v) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain recombinant DNA technology — with the example of insulin production. (b) Explain PCR — denaturation, annealing and extension steps.
Fig: PCR cycle — 3 steps: Denaturation (94°C) → Annealing (~55°C, primers bind) → Extension (72°C, Taq polymerase synthesises). Each cycle doubles DNA.
rDNA — Insulin 1. Human insulin gene synthesised (A + B chain genes) 2. Inserted into E. coli plasmid (pBR322) using restriction enzymes + DNA ligase 3. Recombinant E. coli grown → expresses human insulin protein 4. A + B chains isolated separately, joined by disulfide bonds → functional Humulin insulin First approved in 1982 by Eli Lilly (USA)
PCR Steps 1. Denaturation (94°C): H-bonds break → double helix unwinds into single strands 2. Annealing (~55°C): Specific primers bind to complementary sequences on each strand 3. Extension (72°C): Taq polymerase adds dNTPs 5'→3' → new complementary strand One cycle: 2 copies → n cycles: 2ⁿ copies
