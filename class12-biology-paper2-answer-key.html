SECTION A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10
(i) Site of implantation in female reproductive system
Answer: Uterus (endometrium)
Embryo (blastocyst) implants in the endometrium (inner lining of uterus) about 7 days after fertilisation.
(ii) Haploid phase in plant life cycle
Answer: Gametophyte
In plants: gametophyte (n, haploid) → gametes → fertilisation → sporophyte (2n, diploid) → meiosis → spores → gametophyte
(iii) Which nucleotide is NOT found in DNA?
Answer: Uracil (U)
DNA bases: A, T, G, C  |  RNA bases: A, U, G, C  |  Uracil replaces Thymine in RNA
(iv) Monohybrid cross F₂ phenotypic ratio
Answer: 3:1
Aa × Aa → 1AA:2Aa:1aa → 3 dominant : 1 recessive = 3:1
(v) Which enzyme cuts DNA at specific sequences?
Answer: Restriction endonuclease
Restriction endonuclease recognises palindromic sequences and cuts at specific sites. Example: EcoRI recognises GAATTC and cuts between G and A.
(vi) Ozone layer is in which layer of atmosphere?
Answer: Stratosphere
Ozone layer is in the stratosphere (15–35 km altitude). Absorbs harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation.
(vii) Photoperiodism in plants
Answer: flowering response to duration of light and darkness
Photoperiodism: response of plants to the relative length of day and night for flowering. Short day plants (< critical night length), long day plants, day-neutral plants.
(viii) Which hormone triggers ovulation?
Answer: LH (Luteinising Hormone)
LH surge at day 14 of menstrual cycle triggers ovulation (release of secondary oocyte from Graafian follicle).
(ix) Founder effect is a type of
Answer: genetic drift
Founder effect: when a small population establishes a new colony, gene pool has limited variation → genetic drift → allele frequencies change by chance.
(x) Antibiotic resistance in bacteria is due to
Answer: natural selection (mutation + selection)
Random mutation gives some bacteria antibiotic resistance. Antibiotic kills susceptible bacteria, resistant ones survive and reproduce → resistance spreads.
SECTION B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8
(i) What is oogenesis?
Oogenesis is the process of formation of mature ova (egg cells) from oogonia in the ovary of a female. Stages: Oogonium → Primary oocyte (meiosis I) → Secondary oocyte (meiosis II arrested at metaphase II) → Ovum (on fertilisation)
(ii) What is dominance?
Dominance: When two contrasting alleles are present in a heterozygote, only one (dominant) is expressed in the phenotype; the other (recessive) is masked.
(iii) What is template strand?
Template strand (antisense strand) is the DNA strand that serves as a template for mRNA synthesis during transcription. RNA is complementary and antiparallel to this strand.
(iv) What is biodiversity hotspot?
A region with exceptionally high species richness (>1500 endemic plant species) and under significant threat (>70% habitat lost). India has 4 hotspots: Western Ghats, Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundaland
(v) Define ecology
Ecology is the scientific study of the relationships between organisms and their environment (biotic and abiotic factors).
(vi) What is monoclonal antibody?
Monoclonal antibodies are identical antibodies produced by a single clone of B-lymphocytes (hybridoma cells). Highly specific for one antigen. Uses: Cancer therapy, pregnancy tests, HIV detection, ELISA
(vii) Define niche
Ecological niche is the role of an organism in its ecosystem — its functional position including what it eats, what eats it, how it interacts with its environment. "No two species can occupy the same niche" — Competitive exclusion principle (Gause)
(viii) What is gene therapy?
Gene therapy is the introduction of a normal, functional copy of a defective gene into a patient's cells to correct a genetic disease. Example: ADA deficiency — adenosine deaminase gene inserted into patient's lymphocytes
SECTION C — Short Answers (selected) 8 × 2 = 16
(i) Incomplete dominance — snapdragon
In incomplete dominance, neither allele is completely dominant → heterozygote shows intermediate phenotype. Snapdragon: RR (red) × WW (white) → F₁: RW (pink — intermediate) F₂: RR(red) : RW(pink) : WW(white) = 1:2:1 (phenotypic = genotypic)
(iii) Nitrogen cycle — steps
1. Nitrogen fixation: N₂ → NH₃ (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, lightning) 2. Nitrification: NH₃ → NO₂⁻ (Nitrosomonas) → NO₃⁻ (Nitrobacter) 3. Assimilation: Plants absorb NO₃⁻ → amino acids → proteins 4. Ammonification: Decomposers break protein → NH₃ (released to soil) 5. Denitrification: NO₃⁻ → N₂ (Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus)
(v) Cancer — types and detection
Types: Carcinoma (epithelial), Sarcoma (connective/muscle), Lymphoma (lymphatic), Leukaemia (blood) Causes: Oncogenes, carcinogens (tobacco, radiation, asbestos), viruses (HPV, HBV) Detection: Biopsy, X-ray, CT scan, MRI, tumour markers (PSA, AFP), Pap smear Treatment: Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy
SECTION D — Medium Answers (selected) 6 × 3 = 18
(iv) Light reactions of photosynthesis
Occur in thylakoid membrane. Two photosystems: PS II and PS I PS II: Absorbs 680 nm light → water splitting (photolysis): 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂ Electrons passed through ETC (plastoquinone, cytochrome b6f, plastocyanin) Proton gradient drives ATP synthase → photophosphorylation → ATP PS I: Absorbs 700 nm → electrons passed to ferredoxin → NADP reductase → NADPH Net products of light reactions: ATP, NADPH, O₂ (used in Calvin cycle)
(ix) IVF, GIFT and Surrogacy
IVF (In Vitro Fertilisation): Egg collected from female → fertilised with sperm in lab → embryo transferred to uterus at 8-cell stage ("test-tube baby"). Used for blocked Fallopian tubes. GIFT (Gamete Intrafallopian Transfer): Collected eggs and sperm transferred to Fallopian tube for natural fertilisation. Surrogacy: Embryo from genetic parents implanted in surrogate mother's uterus → carries pregnancy to term. Used when mother cannot carry pregnancy.
SECTION E — Long Answers (selected) 4 × 4 = 16
(ii) Mendel's laws + Monohybrid cross
Monohybrid cross (Tall × Dwarf): P: TT × tt   F₁: all Tt (Tall) → Law of Dominance: T dominant over t F₂: Tt × Tt → 1TT : 2Tt : 1tt → 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf Law of Segregation: The two alleles of a gene separate (segregate) during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one allele. Incomplete dominance (Snapdragon): RR(red) × WW(white) → F₁: RW (pink) F₂: 1RR(red) : 2RW(pink) : 1WW(white) → 1:2:1 ratio (phenotype = genotype ratio)
(vi) Ecosystem — energy flow + conservation
Energy flow (10% law — Lindeman): Only ~10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next; 90% lost as heat. Example: Grass (1000J) → Grasshopper (100J) → Frog (10J) → Snake (1J) → Eagle (0.1J) Energy flows unidirectionally (cannot be recycled); material cycles. In situ conservation: Protecting species in natural habitat. Ex: National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves (Sundarbans, Nilgiri) Ex situ conservation: Protecting species outside natural habitat. Ex: Zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks (gene banks), cryopreservation, tissue culture
