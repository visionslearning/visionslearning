Meselson-Stahl: ¹⁵N→¹⁴N medium: Gen1=hybrid, Gen2=hybrid+light (proves semi-conservative)
Experiment E. coli grown in ¹⁵N (heavy) → transferred to ¹⁴N (light) medium. After 1 generation: CsCl gradient → ONE band at intermediate density (¹⁵N/¹⁴N hybrid) After 2 generations: TWO bands — one hybrid, one light (¹⁴N/¹⁴N)
Conclusion
DNA replication is SEMI-CONSERVATIVE — each new molecule has one original + one new strand

Q.4 (iv) [3 Marks]
Explain the light reactions of photosynthesis — photosystems, ETC and ATP synthesis.
Z-scheme: electrons flow from H₂O through PS II → ETC → PS I → NADP⁺
Location Light reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes (grana) of chloroplasts. Two photosystems involved: PS I (P700) and PS II (P680) — named by discovery order, not sequence in Z-scheme.
Step 1 — PS II (P680) P680 chlorophyll absorbs light at 680 nm → electrons become excited and are expelled. Photolysis of water: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂↑ O₂ is released as a byproduct. Electrons from water replace those lost by P680.
Step 2 — Electron Transport Chain Excited electrons from PS II travel through ETC: Plastoquinone (PQ) → Cytochrome b₆f complex → Plastocyanin (PC) → PS I Protons (H⁺) pumped across thylakoid membrane → proton gradient → ATP synthase → ATP (photophosphorylation)
Step 3 — PS I (P700) P700 absorbs light at 700 nm → electrons re-energised to higher level. Electrons → Ferredoxin (Fd) → NADP⁺ reductase → NADP⁺ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → NADPH
Cyclic vs Non-Cyclic Non-cyclic photophosphorylation: H₂O → PS II → ETC → PS I → NADP⁺  (produces ATP + NADPH + O₂) Cyclic photophosphorylation: electrons from PS I → Fd → Cyt b₆f → PC → back to PS I  (produces ATP only, no NADPH, no O₂ released)
Overall Equation
Light reactions: 12H₂O + 12NADP⁺ + 18ADP → 6O₂ + 12NADPH + 18ATP  (all used in Calvin cycle)
Q.4 (vii) [3 Marks]
Explain biomagnification with DDT as an example.
Biomagnification: DDT increases from 0.003 to 25 ppm up the food chain
Definition Biomagnification (Biological magnification): Progressive increase in the concentration of a non-biodegradable, toxic chemical substance at each successive trophic level in a food chain. Occurs because fat-soluble substances (like DDT) cannot be metabolised or excreted — they accumulate in the fatty tissues of organisms.
Why it happens Non-biodegradable: DDT cannot be broken down by biological processes. Lipid-soluble: Dissolves in fats → stored in fatty tissues, not excreted through urine. Each organism eats large numbers of organisms from the level below → concentration multiplies at each step.
DDT Food Chain Water (0.000003 ppm) → Phytoplankton (0.003 ppm) → Zooplankton (0.04 ppm) → Small fish (0.5 ppm) → Large fish (2 ppm) → Fish-eating birds (25 ppm) Concentration increases ~10 million times from water to fish-eating birds (e.g. osprey, bald eagle).
Effects on Birds 25 ppm DDT in predatory birds (osprey, pelican, bald eagle) → interferes with calcium deposition in eggshells. Eggshell thinning → eggs crushed during incubation → reproductive failure → steep population decline. Bald eagle, brown pelican, osprey nearly went extinct in USA (1960–70s).
In Humans Humans at the top of many food chains — highest DDT accumulation. Found in human breast milk. Linked to cancer, hormonal disruption, neurological damage.
Control Measures
DDT banned in most countries (India: 2008 for agriculture) | Use biodegradable pesticides | Integrated Pest Management (IPM) | Biological control using natural predators

Q.5 (i) [4 Marks]
(a) Draw a neat labelled diagram of the male reproductive system. (b) Explain the hormonal control of male reproduction.
Male reproductive system: testes → epididymis → vas deferens → urethra → penis
Primary Sex Organ Testes — paired organs in scrotal sac, 2–3°C below body temperature (essential for spermatogenesis). Each testis has ~250–300 lobules, each containing 1–3 seminiferous tubules. Sertoli cells (sustentacular cells) — nourish developing sperm; secrete inhibin; form blood-testis barrier. Leydig cells (Interstitial cells) — between seminiferous tubules; produce testosterone.
Accessory Ducts Epididymis: Tightly coiled duct on each testis (~6 m long). Sperm maturation (acquire motility and fertilising ability) and temporary storage (2–3 weeks). Vas deferens (ductus deferens): Muscular tube carrying sperm from epididymis up and around bladder to ejaculatory duct. Site of vasectomy. Ejaculatory duct: Junction of vas deferens + seminal vesicle duct. Passes through prostate → opens into urethra. Urethra: Common passage for semen and urine (not simultaneously). Opens at glans penis.
Accessory Glands Seminal vesicles (2): Secrete fructose (energy for sperm motility), prostaglandins, fibrinogen, citric acid. Contribute ~60% of semen volume. Prostate gland (1): Secretes slightly alkaline fluid (pH 7.2–7.4) that neutralises vaginal acidity and activates sperm. Contributes ~30% of semen volume. Cowper's (bulbourethral) glands (2): Secrete pre-ejaculatory mucus — lubricates urethra, neutralises residual urine acidity before ejaculation.
Hormonal Control Hypothalamus → secretes GnRH (Gonadotropin Releasing Hormone) → Anterior pituitary FSH → Sertoli cells → activates and maintains spermatogenesis; stimulates inhibin secretion LH (ICSH) → Leydig cells → stimulates testosterone secretion Testosterone functions: (i) maintains spermatogenesis (ii) develops secondary sex characters — facial/pubic hair, deep voice, increased muscle mass (iii) promotes libido (iv) anabolic effects on bone and muscle Negative feedback: High testosterone → inhibits GnRH and LH secretion (prevents overproduction) Inhibin (from Sertoli cells) → specifically inhibits FSH secretion (fine-tunes spermatogenesis rate)
Semen Composition
Semen = Spermatozoa + secretions of seminal vesicles (60%) + prostate (30%) + Cowper's glands (5%). Volume: 2–5 mL per ejaculation. Sperm count: 40–600 million/mL. pH: 7.2–7.8.
Q.5 (v) [4 Marks]
(a) Explain recombinant DNA technology with insulin production example. (b) Explain PCR.
Left: rDNA technology steps | Right: PCR cycle (3 steps per cycle)
rDNA Technology — Definition Recombinant DNA (rDNA) Technology — a set of techniques used to manipulate DNA in vitro to produce genetically modified organisms that can synthesise desired proteins. Key tools: Restriction endonucleases (molecular scissors), DNA ligase (molecular glue), vectors (plasmids, bacteriophages), host cells (E. coli, yeast).
Steps — Insulin Production 1. Gene Isolation: Human insulin mRNA isolated from pancreatic β-cells → cDNA synthesised using reverse transcriptase. (Eli Lilly used synthetic gene: A-chain and B-chain synthesised separately) 2. Restriction Digestion: Same restriction enzyme cuts both insulin gene and plasmid vector → generates complementary sticky ends. 3. Ligation: Insulin cDNA inserted into plasmid using DNA ligase → forms Recombinant plasmid (rDNA). 4. Transformation: Recombinant plasmid introduced into E. coli by heat shock or electroporation. 5. Cloning and Expression: Transformed E. coli multiply → millions of copies of insulin gene expressed → pre-proinsulin produced → processed to insulin chains A and B → joined by disulfide bonds → functional Humulin (first rDNA drug, Eli Lilly, 1982). 6. Purification: Insulin extracted, purified and formulated for injection.
PCR — Principle Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) — amplifies a specific DNA sequence exponentially in vitro. Invented by Kary Mullis (1983) — Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1993. Key component: Taq DNA polymerase (from Thermus aquaticus, thermostable, works at 72°C).
PCR — 3 Steps (per cycle) 1. Denaturation (94°C, 30 sec): Double-stranded DNA heated → hydrogen bonds break → two single strands. 2. Annealing (50–65°C, 30 sec): Specific primers (short synthetic oligonucleotides, ~20 bp) bind to complementary sequences flanking the target region on each strand. 3. Extension (72°C, 1 min): Taq polymerase extends primers using dNTPs → copies target DNA. Each cycle DOUBLES the DNA. After 30 cycles: 2³⁰ ≈ 1 billion copies of target DNA from just one molecule.
Applications
rDNA: insulin, HGH, erythropoietin, hepatitis B vaccine, Bt crops | PCR: forensic DNA fingerprinting, disease diagnosis (COVID-19, TB, HIV), paternity testing, archaeological DNA studies
