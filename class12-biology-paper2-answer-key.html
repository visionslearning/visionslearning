Section A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
Corpus luteum secretes:
✔ Answer: (B) Progesterone
Explanation After ovulation, the ruptured Graafian follicle transforms into the corpus luteum (yellow body). Corpus luteum secretes progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone: maintains uterine lining for implantation, prevents uterine contractions during pregnancy.
Q.1 (ii)
Sickle cell anaemia inheritance pattern:
✔ Answer: (C) Autosomal recessive
Explanation Sickle cell anaemia: HbS allele on chromosome 11 (autosome) — NOT on sex chromosomes. Autosomal recessive: both alleles must be HbS/HbS for disease. HbA/HbS = trait carrier (sickle cell trait).
Q.1 (iii)
Template strand in DNA is also called:
✔ Answer: (B) Antisense strand
Explanation Template strand = antisense strand = non-coding strand = 3'→5' direction mRNA is complementary to the template strand and has same sequence as the coding (sense) strand (except U instead of T).
Q.1 (iv)
Restriction site of EcoRI is:
✔ Answer: (C) GAATTC
Explanation EcoRI recognises 5'—G↓AATTC—3' (palindromic sequence) Creates sticky ends: 5'—AATT—3' overhangs. Named from E. coli strain RY13.
Q.1 (v)
Kranz anatomy is characteristic of:
✔ Answer: (B) C₄ plants
Explanation Kranz anatomy (wreath-like): bundle sheath cells surrounding vascular bundle are tightly packed with large chloroplasts (agranal). Found in C₄ plants (sugarcane, maize, sorghum). Initial fixation in mesophyll (OAA), CO₂ concentrated in bundle sheath for Calvin cycle.
Q.1 (vi)
Denitrification converts:
✔ Answer: (A) Nitrates to N₂
Explanation Denitrification: NO₃⁻ → NO₂⁻ → NO → N₂O → N₂ (by Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus) Returns fixed nitrogen back to atmosphere. Reduces soil fertility. Occurs under anaerobic conditions.
Q.1 (vii)
Golden Rice was developed to combat deficiency of:
✔ Answer: (B) β-carotene (Vitamin A)
Explanation Golden Rice: transgenic rice expressing β-carotene (provitamin A) genes from daffodil and a bacterium. Rice grains become yellow/golden colour. Targets Vitamin A deficiency (VAD), causing blindness in developing countries.
Q.1 (viii)
Convention on Biological Diversity signed at:
✔ Answer: (C) Rio de Janeiro
Explanation CBD signed at Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil in 1992. Goals: (1) Conservation of biodiversity, (2) Sustainable use, (3) Fair sharing of benefits from genetic resources.
Q.1 (ix)
Lac operon is present in:
✔ Answer: (A) E. coli
Explanation Lac operon is the classic example of prokaryotic gene regulation — found in E. coli (Escherichia coli). Structural genes: lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), lacA (transacetylase).
Q.1 (x)
HIV primarily infects:
✔ Answer: (B) T-helper (CD4⁺) cells
Explanation HIV has gp120 protein that binds specifically to CD4 receptor on T-helper (T₄) lymphocytes. Also infects macrophages. Destruction of CD4⁺ cells → immune deficiency → AIDS.
Section B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
Q.2 (i)
What is oogenesis?
DefinitionOogenesis is the process of formation of haploid female gametes (ova/eggs) from diploid oogonia in the ovary.
StagesOogonium (2n) → Primary oocyte (meiosis I arrested at prophase I) → Secondary oocyte (meiosis II arrested at metaphase II) → Ovum (on fertilisation)
Q.2 (ii)
Define incomplete dominance.
DefinitionIncomplete dominance is a type of inheritance where neither allele is completely dominant over the other. The heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype between the two homozygotes.
ExampleSnapdragon: RR (red) × WW (white) → F₁: RW (pink). F₂ ratio: 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white
Q.2 (iii)
What is an anticodon?
DefinitionAn anticodon is a sequence of three nucleotides (triplet) located at the anticodon loop of tRNA that is complementary and antiparallel to the codon on mRNA. It enables accurate amino acid delivery during translation.
ExamplemRNA codon AUG (methionine) → tRNA anticodon UAC (complementary)
Q.2 (iv)
Define origin of replication.
DefinitionOrigin of replication (ori) is a specific sequence on DNA where replication is initiated. It is the site where the double helix is unwound and new DNA synthesis begins.
SignificanceProkaryotes: single ori (oriC) | Eukaryotes: multiple origins → faster replication of large chromosomes
Q.2 (vii)
Define recombinant DNA (rDNA).
DefinitionRecombinant DNA is a DNA molecule formed by joining DNA fragments from two different organisms (different species) using restriction enzymes and DNA ligase. It is the basis of genetic engineering.
Q.2 (viii)
What is ecosystem diversity?
DefinitionEcosystem diversity refers to the variety of different ecosystems (habitats and communities) in a given area — forests, grasslands, deserts, wetlands, marine ecosystems etc.
SignificanceEach ecosystem provides unique services and supports different species assemblages.
Q.2 (xi)
What is allosteric regulation of enzymes?
DefinitionAllosteric regulation is the regulation of enzyme activity by binding of a molecule (allosteric effector) at a site OTHER than the active site (allosteric site), causing a conformational change that increases (activator) or decreases (inhibitor) enzyme activity.
ExamplePhosphofructokinase (glycolysis) inhibited by ATP (allosteric inhibitor)
Q.2 (xii)
What is ex situ conservation? Give one example.
DefinitionEx situ conservation is the protection of endangered species OUTSIDE their natural habitat, in artificially maintained environments.
ExamplesZoos, botanical gardens, seed banks (gene banks), tissue culture, cryopreservation. Example: National Gene Bank (NBPGR) in New Delhi stores seeds of crop plants.
Section C — Short Answers 8 × 2 = 16 Marks
Q.3 (ii)
Explain co-dominance in ABO blood group system.
Co-dominance When two alleles are both expressed simultaneously in the heterozygote, neither being dominant or recessive — this is co-dominance. ABO blood group: controlled by gene I with three alleles: Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i Iᴬ and Iᴮ are co-dominant (both expressed when present together): Iᴬ codes for antigen A, Iᴮ codes for antigen B Genotype IᴬIᴮ → Blood group AB (both A and B antigens on RBCs) — co-dominance
Q.3 (iii)
Explain the process of translation — initiation, elongation, termination.
Initiation30S ribosome + mRNA + initiator tRNA (met-tRNA) → 70S initiation complex. AUG = start codon. Met is placed at P-site.
Elongation Each cycle: (1) Aminoacyl-tRNA binds to A-site (codon-anticodon pairing) (2) Peptide bond forms: peptidyl transferase transfers peptide from P-site tRNA to amino acid at A-site (3) Translocation: ribosome moves 3 nucleotides in 5'→3' direction; tRNA moves P→E and A→P
TerminationStop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA) at A-site → release factor binds → polypeptide released → ribosome dissociates
Q.3 (v)
Describe the Calvin cycle — three stages.
1. CarboxylationCO₂ + RuBP (5C) → [6C intermediate] → 2 × 3-PGA (3C). Enzyme: RuBisCO. Occurs in stroma.
2. Reduction3-PGA + ATP + NADPH → G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate). Two G3P molecules can form one glucose via gluconeogenesis.
3. Regeneration5 G3P molecules → 3 RuBP (5C) using ATP. RuBP is regenerated for next round of fixation.
Net Products
3CO₂ + 9ATP + 6NADPH → 1 G3P (net) + 9ADP + 6NADP⁺ + 8Pᵢ
Q.3 (vii)
What is RFLP? State its uses.
RFLPRestriction Fragment Length Polymorphism: differences in the pattern of DNA fragments produced when DNA is cut with restriction enzymes. Different individuals have different-sized fragments due to variations in restriction sites (SNPs, insertions, deletions).
Uses (1) DNA fingerprinting: forensic identification, paternity testing
(2) Genetic mapping: locating disease genes
(3) Phylogenetic studies: determining evolutionary relationships
Q.3 (viii)
What are biodiversity hotspots? Name two in India.
DefinitionBiodiversity hotspots are regions that: (1) contain at least 1500 endemic plant species (not found anywhere else) AND (2) have lost >70% of their original habitat due to human activities. First defined by Norman Myers (1988).
India's Hotspots(1) Western Ghats — high endemism of amphibians, mammals, plants
(2) Himalayas (includes Eastern Himalayan Hotspot)
Q.3 (x)
Explain how vaccination provides immunity. Name two types of vaccines.
Mechanism Vaccine introduces a harmless antigen (killed/attenuated pathogen, toxoid, or subunit) into the body. Immune system responds: B-cells produce antibodies, T-cells activated → immunological memory formed. On actual infection: memory cells respond rapidly → infection neutralised before symptoms develop = artificial active immunity
Types (1) Live attenuated vaccine: weakened pathogen (e.g., MMR, oral polio vaccine)
(2) Killed/inactivated vaccine: dead pathogen (e.g., IPV, hepatitis A)
Q.3 (xi)
Explain the HIV life cycle — infection to AIDS.
HIV Life Cycle 1. Attachment: HIV gp120 binds CD4 receptor on T-helper cells 2. Fusion: HIV enters cell, releases RNA 3. Reverse transcription: HIV RNA → HIV DNA (enzyme: reverse transcriptase) 4. Integration: HIV DNA inserted into host genome (as provirus, by integrase) 5. Transcription & Translation: New HIV proteins made 6. Assembly & Budding: New virions released by budding Gradual destruction of CD4⁺ cells → immune system weakens → AIDS (CD4 count <200/μL)
Section D — Medium Answers 6 × 3 = 18 Marks
Q.4 (i)
Describe the structure and functions of the placenta. Name three hormones it secretes.
Structure Placenta = disc-shaped organ formed from trophoblast (fetal) and endometrium (maternal) tissues. Chorionic villi: finger-like projections from trophoblast → interdigitate with uterine tissue → large surface area for exchange Umbilical cord connects fetus to placenta (2 arteries + 1 vein)
Functions (1) Exchange: O₂, nutrients, antibodies from mother → fetus; CO₂, urea → mother (NO mixing of blood) (2) Endocrine organ: secretes hormones to maintain pregnancy (3) Immunological: transfers maternal IgG antibodies to fetus (passive immunity)
Hormones (1) hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) — maintains corpus luteum in early pregnancy (2) Progesterone — maintains endometrium, prevents uterine contractions (3) Estrogen — prepares uterus, develops mammary glands
Q.4 (ii)
Explain sex determination in humans (XX/XY) and in birds (ZZ/ZW).
Humans (XY) Males: XY (heterogametic — produce X or Y sperm) Females: XX (homogametic — produce only X eggs) Sex determined by the sperm (X or Y): X-sperm + X-egg = XX (girl); Y-sperm + X-egg = XY (boy) SRY gene on Y chromosome triggers male development
Birds (ZW) Males: ZZ (homogametic — produce only Z sperm) Females: ZW (heterogametic — produce Z or W eggs) In birds, sex is determined by the EGG (opposite of humans) Examples: Domestic fowl (chicken), duck, pigeon
Q.4 (vi)
Describe the principle and steps of gel electrophoresis used in DNA fingerprinting.
PrincipleDNA fragments migrate through agarose gel under an electric field. Smaller fragments move faster and further than larger ones (DNA is negatively charged → moves toward anode).
Steps 1. DNA extracted from sample (blood, hair, skin) 2. Restriction enzyme cuts DNA → fragments of different sizes 3. Fragments loaded onto agarose gel, electric current applied 4. DNA transfers to nitrocellulose membrane (Southern blotting) 5. Radioactive probes hybridise to specific sequences 6. X-ray film shows pattern of bands = DNA fingerprint (unique to individual)
UsesForensic identification, paternity testing, evolutionary studies
Q.4 (vii)
Explain the three types of ecological pyramids.
Pyramid of NumbersNumber of organisms at each trophic level. Usually narrows upward (more producers than consumers). Exception: tree → insects → birds (inverted base).
Pyramid of BiomassTotal dry weight (biomass) at each trophic level. Generally narrows upward. Exception: in aquatic ecosystems (phytoplankton has less biomass than zooplankton at any time) — inverted.
Pyramid of EnergyEnergy at each trophic level. Always upright (10% law: only 10% energy transferred to next level). Never inverted — reflects thermodynamic laws.
Q.4 (viii)
State and explain the Hardy-Weinberg principle.
StatementIn a large, randomly mating population, in the absence of evolutionary forces, allele and genotype frequencies remain constant from generation to generation.
Equation
p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (genotype frequencies)  |  p + q = 1 (allele frequencies)
5 Factors Disturbing Equilibrium (1) Mutation   (2) Gene flow (migration)   (3) Genetic drift   (4) Natural selection   (5) Non-random mating

(a) Female reproductive system diagram. (b) Menstrual cycle phases with hormone levels.
Female Reprod. SystemComponents: ovaries (pair), fallopian tubes/oviducts, uterus, cervix, vagina. Ovaries produce eggs and hormones (estrogen, progesterone).
Menstrual Cycle — 28 days Day 1–5 (Menstruation): Endometrium sheds. FSH begins to rise. Q.5 (iii)
(a) DNA replication — all enzymes involved and functions. (b) Gene expression regulation in eukaryotes.
Enzymes in DNA Replication Helicase: Unwinds DNA double helix at replication fork Topoisomerase: Relieves torsional strain ahead of replication fork SSB proteins: Stabilise separated strands Primase: Synthesises RNA primer (provides 3'-OH for DNA pol) DNA Pol III: Main enzyme — adds dNTPs 5'→3' (requires primer) DNA Pol I: Removes RNA primer, replaces with DNA DNA Ligase: Joins Okazaki fragments on lagging strand
Eukaryotic Gene Regulation Histone modification: Acetylation of histones loosens chromatin → promotes transcription; Deacetylation tightens chromatin → silences genes DNA methylation: Addition of methyl group (CH₃) to cytosine → gene silencing (genomic imprinting, X-inactivation) Both are epigenetic mechanisms — heritable changes in gene expression without DNA sequence change
Q.5 (v)
(a) Bioremediation — types and examples. (b) Biogas production.
Bioremediation Definition: Use of microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) to degrade or detoxify pollutants in the environment. In situ: Treatment at the site of contamination. e.g., Biostimulation (adding nutrients to existing microbes), Bioaugmentation (adding specific microbes) Ex situ: Polluted material removed and treated elsewhere. e.g., Biopiles (heaps treated with air and nutrients), Bioreactors Examples: Oil spill cleanup (Pseudomonas), Heavy metal removal, Chromium reduction by bacteria
Biogas Production Substrate: Organic waste — cow dung, sewage, agricultural waste, kitchen waste Microorganisms: Anaerobic bacteria — methanogens (Methanobacterium, Methanococcus) Process: Anaerobic digestion in biogas plant (floating drum or fixed dome). Organic matter → methane (CH₄, ~55–65%) + CO₂ + traces of H₂S Uses: Cooking fuel, electricity generation, lighting. Slurry = excellent biofertiliser.
