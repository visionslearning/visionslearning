SECTION A – MCQ Solutions
(i) Correct Answer: (B) 2 m
Relation: E = V/r  ⟹  r = V/E = 100 / 50 r = 2 m
(ii) Correct Answer: (B) halves
C = ε₀A/d  ⟹  if d doubles, C = ε₀A/(2d) = C₀/2 Capacitance becomes half.
(iii) Correct Answer: (C) Henry
Self-inductance L is defined by e = −L(dI/dt). Unit is Henry (H).
(iv) Correct Answer: (B) away from normal
Glass is denser (μ > 1) than air. On going from denser to rarer medium, the refracted ray bends away from the normal.
(v) Correct Answer: (B) hν
Planck's quantum theory: E = hν, where h = Planck's constant.
(vi) Correct Answer: (C) less than reactants
In fission, mass defect Δm = m(reactants) − m(products) > 0. The lost mass converts to energy: E = Δmc². Mass of products < mass of reactants.
(vii) Correct Answer: (C) V₀ ∝ (ν − ν₀)
Einstein's equation: eV₀ = h(ν − ν₀) ∴ V₀ = (h/e)(ν − ν₀)  ⟹  V₀ ∝ (ν − ν₀)
(viii) Correct Answer: (B) 2 mA
Ic = β × Ib = 100 × 20 × 10⁻⁶ = 2000 × 10⁻⁶ A Ic = 2 mA
(ix) Correct Answer: (B) doubles
Fringe width β = λD/d  ⟹  if d → d/2, then β → 2λD/d = 2β Fringe width doubles.
(x) Correct Answer: (B) energy
Lenz's law states induced EMF opposes the change causing it — this is a statement of conservation of energy (work must be done against induced force).
SECTION B – Short Definitions (1 mark each)
(i) Electric Potential:
Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a test charge from infinity to that point. V = W/q  ;  SI unit: Volt (V) = J/C
(ii) Ohm's Law:
At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it. V = IR, where R is the resistance (constant).
(iii) Magnetic Flux:
Magnetic flux through a surface is the total number of magnetic field lines passing perpendicularly through that surface. Φ = B·A·cosθ  ;  SI unit: Weber (Wb)
(iv) Power Factor:
Power factor = cosφ = R/Z, where φ is the phase angle between voltage and current. It is the ratio of true power to apparent power in an AC circuit.
(v) Total Internal Reflection:
When light travels from a denser to a rarer medium, and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, all the light is reflected back into the denser medium. This is called total internal reflection.
(vi) Work Function:
The minimum energy required to remove one electron from the surface of a metal is called the work function (φ₀). Unit: electronvolt (eV) or Joule (J).
(vii) Rectifier:
A rectifier is an electronic device that converts alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC). It uses one or more p-n junction diodes.
(viii) De Morgan's Second Theorem:
The complement of a product of variables equals the sum of their complements. Mathematically: $\overline{A \cdot B} = \bar{A} + \bar{B}$ (i.e., NAND gate output = complement of AND = OR of complements)
(ix) Nuclear Binding Energy:
The energy required to completely separate all the nucleons (protons and neutrons) of a nucleus from each other is called binding energy. BE = Δm × c² = [Z·mp + N·mn − M]c²
(x) Critical Angle:
Critical angle is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is 90°. sinC = 1/μ, where μ is the refractive index of the denser medium.
(xi) Photon:
A photon is a discrete packet (quantum) of electromagnetic energy. Energy E = hν; it has zero rest mass and travels at speed of light.
(xii) Coulomb's Law:
The force of attraction or repulsion between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. F = k·q₁·q₂/r²  ;  k = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
SECTION C – 2-Mark Solutions
(i) Energy stored in a capacitor:
When charge dq is added at potential V = q/C, work done: dW = V·dq = (q/C)dq Total energy: U = ∫₀^Q (q/C) dq = Q²/(2C) Using Q = CV: U = ½CV² = Q²/2C = QV/2
(ii) Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL):
Statement: The algebraic sum of all currents meeting at a junction is zero. ∑I = 0 at any node. Explanation: Current entering = Current leaving. Based on conservation of charge. Example: If I₁ and I₂ enter a node and I₃ leaves: I₁ + I₂ = I₃
(iii) Maximum EMF of rotating coil:
Given: N = 50, A = 0.02 m², B = 0.4 T, f = 60 Hz
Formula: emax = NBAω = NBA(2πf) = 50 × 0.4 × 0.02 × 2π × 60 = 50 × 0.4 × 0.02 × 376.8 = 0.4 × 376.8 emax ≈ 150.72 V
(iv) Step-up Transformer (diagram description):
Primary coil (NP turns) — Core (laminated iron) — Secondary coil (NS > NP turns) Input AC → Primary → Varying flux in core → Induced EMF in secondary VS/VP = NS/NP > 1  (voltage stepped up) [Draw: rectangular laminated core, primary coil on left, secondary on right, label NP, NS, VP, VS]
(v) Huygens' Principle:
1. Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets. 2. These secondary wavelets spread out in all directions with the speed of the wave. 3. The new wavefront is the envelope (tangent surface) of all the secondary wavelets. [Diagram: plane wavefront → secondary wavelets → new plane wavefront]
(vi) Laws of Photoelectric Effect:
1. Photoelectric emission occurs only if frequency of incident light ≥ threshold frequency (ν ≥ ν₀). 2. Max KE of photoelectrons depends on frequency, NOT on intensity. 3. Number of photoelectrons emitted per second is proportional to the intensity of light. 4. The process is instantaneous (no time lag).
(vii) Radius of nth Bohr orbit:
Centripetal force = Electrostatic force: mv²/r = ke²/r²  ⟹  mv²r = ke²  ...(1) Bohr's quantisation: mvr = nh/2π  ...(2) From (2): v = nh/(2πmr) Substituting in (1): m(n²h²/4π²m²r²)r = ke² ⟹ r = n²h²/(4π²mke²) rn = n² × a₀, where a₀ = 0.529 Å (Bohr radius)
(viii) Half-wave Rectifier:
Circuit: AC input → Diode (D) in series → Load resistance RL. Working: During +ve half cycle → diode forward biased → current flows through RL. During −ve half cycle → diode reverse biased → no current flows. Output: Pulsating DC (only positive half cycles appear across RL). Efficiency ≈ 40.6%.
(ix) Fringe Width (YDSE):
Given: d = 0.5 mm = 5×10⁻⁴ m, D = 1 m, λ = 500 nm = 5×10⁻⁷ m
Formula: β = λD/d = (5×10⁻⁷ × 1) / (5×10⁻⁴) = 10⁻³ m β = 1 mm
(x) Compton Effect:
When X-rays are scattered by free electrons, the scattered radiation has longer wavelength than incident radiation. Δλ = (h/mec)(1 − cosφ)   (Compton shift formula) Significance: Proves photons have momentum; confirms particle nature of radiation.
(xi) Fission vs Fusion (4 points):
1. Fission: Heavy nucleus splits; Fusion: Light nuclei combine. 2. Fission: Triggered by slow neutrons; Fusion: Requires very high temperature (10⁷–10⁸ K). 3. Fission: Used in nuclear reactors; Fusion: Occurs in stars (Sun). 4. Fusion releases more energy per unit mass than fission.
(xii) B–H Curve (Magnetisation curve):
Initially B increases slowly with H (domain alignment begins). Then rapid increase (most domains aligned). Saturation: B becomes constant even as H increases. On reducing H: B remains non-zero at H=0 → Retentivity (Br). Reverse H needed to demagnetise → Coercivity (Hc). Complete cycle forms a hysteresis loop; area = energy loss per cycle.
SECTION D – 3-Mark Solutions
(i) Electric field due to uniformly charged ring at axial point:
Consider a ring of radius R, total charge Q, point P at distance x from centre on axis. Each element dq is at distance r = √(R²+x²) from P. dE = k·dq/(R²+x²); component along axis: dEx = dE·cosθ = dE·x/r By symmetry, transverse components cancel. E = ∫dEx = kQx/(R²+x²)3/2 E = kQx / (R² + x²)^(3/2) Maximum E at x = R/√2.
(ii) Mirror Formula Derivation:
Consider a concave mirror with pole P, focus F, centre C. For object at distance u and image at distance v (using sign convention): Triangles ABF and PQF are similar: AB/PQ = BF/FP  ...(1) Triangles ABC and PQC are similar: AB/PQ = BC/CP  ...(2) From (1) and (2): BF/FP = BC/CP (v−f)/f = v/u  ⟹  v−f = vf/u Dividing through by uvf: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (Mirror Formula)
(iii) Bohr's postulates and energy of electron in nth orbit:
Postulates: (1) Electrons revolve in certain stable circular orbits without radiating energy. (2) Electrons can only move in orbits where angular momentum L = nh/2π (n = 1,2,3...). (3) Radiation is emitted/absorbed when electron jumps between orbits: hν = E₂ − E₁. Energy derivation: KE = ke²/(2rn), PE = −ke²/rn Total En = KE + PE = −ke²/(2rn) Substituting rn = n²a₀: En = −13.6/n² eV
(iv) Moving Coil Galvanometer:
Construction: Rectangular coil (N turns) suspended between poles of permanent magnet; soft iron core inside coil; hair springs provide restoring torque. Working: Current I → Torque τ = NBIA Restoring torque by spring: τ' = kθ At equilibrium: NBIA = kθ ⟹ θ = (NBA/k)I  ⟹  deflection ∝ current Current sensitivity = NBA/k (radians per ampere).
(v) Torque on magnetic dipole in uniform field:
Consider dipole (bar magnet) with poles ±m separated by 2l, placed at angle θ in field B. Force on N-pole: F = mB (along B); on S-pole: F = mB (opposite to B). These forces form a couple. Perpendicular distance = 2l·sinθ Torque τ = F × perpendicular distance = mB × 2l sinθ Magnetic moment M = m × 2l: τ = MB sinθ   (or τ⃗ = M⃗ × B⃗ )
(vi) Superposition principle and intensity in interference:
Principle: The resultant displacement at any point = algebraic sum of displacements due to individual waves. Let y₁ = a sinωt and y₂ = a sin(ωt+φ) y = y₁+y₂ = 2a cos(φ/2) · sin(ωt + φ/2) Amplitude A = 2a cos(φ/2)  ⟹  I ∝ A² = 4a²cos²(φ/2) Constructive: φ = 2nπ  ⟹  Imax = 4I₀ Destructive: φ = (2n−1)π  ⟹  Imin = 0
(vii) Capacitance with dielectric:
Given: A = 100 cm² = 10⁻² m², d = 5 mm = 5×10⁻³ m, K = 4, ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m
Formula: C = Kε₀A/d = 4 × 8.85×10⁻¹² × 10⁻² / 5×10⁻³ = (4 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹⁴) / 5×10⁻³ = (35.4 × 10⁻¹⁴) / 5×10⁻³ = 7.08 × 10⁻¹² F C ≈ 7.08 pF
(viii) Radius of proton's circular path:
Given: v = 2×10⁶ m/s, B = 0.5 T, m = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C
Centripetal force = Magnetic force: mv²/r = evB  ⟹  r = mv/(eB) = (1.67×10⁻²⁷ × 2×10⁶) / (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.5) = (3.34×10⁻²¹) / (8×10⁻²⁰) = 0.04175 m r ≈ 4.18 cm
SECTION E – 4-Mark Solutions
(i)(a) Gauss's Law — Statement and Proof:
Statement: The total electric flux through any closed surface is equal to (1/ε₀) times the total charge enclosed. ∮ E⃗·dA⃗ = Qenc/ε₀ Proof (using Coulomb's law): For a point charge Q, draw a spherical Gaussian surface of radius r. E = kQ/r² (radially outward), and dA = dA r̂ Flux = ∮ E·dA = E·4πr² = (kQ/r²)·4πr² = Q·4πk = Q/ε₀ ∴ ∮ E⃗·dA⃗ = Q/ε₀ (proved)
(i)(b) E due to infinite plane sheet of charge:
Surface charge density σ; Gaussian surface = cylinder of cross-section A, axis ⊥ sheet. Flux through flat ends: 2EA; through curved surface: 0. Charge enclosed: Q = σA By Gauss: 2EA = σA/ε₀ E = σ/(2ε₀), directed away from the sheet.
(ii)(a) Lens Maker's Formula:
Using refraction at two spherical surfaces (radii R₁, R₂) for lens of refractive index μ: Surface 1: μ/v₁ − 1/u = (μ−1)/R₁  ...(1) Surface 2 (image of surface 1 is object): 1/v − μ/v₁ = (1−μ)/R₂  ...(2) Adding (1)+(2): 1/v − 1/u = (μ−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂) 1/f = (μ−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂) — Lens Maker's Formula
(ii)(b) Image position:
u = −30 cm, f = +20 cm
1/v − 1/u = 1/f 1/v − 1/(−30) = 1/20 1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = 3/60 − 2/60 = 1/60 v = +60 cm (real, inverted image on other side)
(iii)(a) Interference of Light — Conditions:
Constructive interference: Path difference = nλ (n=0,1,2,...) → Bright fringe Destructive interference: Path difference = (2n−1)λ/2 → Dark fringe Conditions for sustained interference: (1) Coherent sources (constant phase difference), (2) Same frequency, (3) Same amplitude (for perfect dark fringes), (4) Small path difference.
(iii)(b) Finding λ:
d = 0.3 mm = 3×10⁻⁴ m, D = 1.5 m, β = 3 mm = 3×10⁻³ m
β = λD/d  ⟹  λ = βd/D = (3×10⁻³ × 3×10⁻⁴) / 1.5 = 9×10⁻⁷ / 1.5 λ = 6×10⁻⁷ m = 600 nm
(iv)(a) Einstein's Photoelectric Equation:
hν = φ₀ + KEmax hν = energy of incident photon; φ₀ = work function (min. energy to escape); KEmax = ½mv²max = maximum KE of emitted photoelectron. Also: KEmax = eV₀ where V₀ is stopping potential.
(iv)(b) Max KE calculation:
ν = 8×10¹⁴ Hz, φ₀ = 3 eV = 3×1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J = 4.8×10⁻¹⁹ J
Ephoton = hν = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 8×10¹⁴ = 53.04×10⁻²⁰ = 5.304×10⁻¹⁹ J KEmax = hν − φ₀ = 5.304×10⁻¹⁹ − 4.8×10⁻¹⁹ = 0.504×10⁻¹⁹ J KEmax ≈ 0.315 eV ≈ 5.04×10⁻²⁰ J
(v)(a) Time period of satellite derivation:
For circular orbit of radius r, gravitational force provides centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r  ⟹  v = √(GM/r) Time period T = circumference/speed = 2πr/v = 2πr / √(GM/r) = 2πr × √(r/GM) T = 2π√(r³/GM)
(v)(b) Geostationary Satellite:
Definition: A satellite that revolves in the equatorial plane of Earth with a time period of exactly 24 hours, appearing stationary to an observer on Earth. Height ≈ 36,000 km above Earth's surface. Uses: Weather forecasting, live TV broadcast, GPS navigation, telecommunication. Conditions: Orbit must be equatorial, circular, with T = 24 h (same as Earth's rotation).
