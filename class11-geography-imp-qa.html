Chapter 1 — Population: Part 1 (Pg 1–11)
18 Periods
Q.1
Define population distribution. Explain the factors affecting population distribution.
2023, 2022, 2020
Definition
Population distribution refers to the pattern of where people live across the Earth's surface — how population is spread over an area.
Physical Factors
  • Relief/Topography: Plains attract dense population; mountains have sparse population. e.g. Indo-Gangetic Plain — densely populated.
  • Climate: Moderate climate attracts more people. Extreme cold/hot/dry areas are sparsely populated. e.g. Amazon basin (hot, humid) — sparse.
  • Soil fertility: Fertile soils attract farmers and settlements. e.g. River deltas densely populated.
  • Water availability: Areas near rivers, lakes have dense population.
  • Natural resources: Mining areas attract population.
Human Factors
  • Economic factors: Industrialization attracts people (e.g. Mumbai, Pune).
  • Political factors: Stable and peaceful areas attract more population.
  • Social factors: Religious/cultural places (Varanasi, Mecca).
  • Historical factors: Old cities, trade routes.
∴ Population distribution is uneven due to combined physical and human factors.
Q.2
What is population density? Explain its types.
2024, 2022, 2019
Definition
Population density = Number of persons per unit area. Population density = Total population / Total area
Arithmetic Density
Total population divided by total geographical area. Simple but crude measure. Formula: P/A persons per km²
Physiological Density
Total population divided by arable (cultivable) land. More realistic for agricultural countries. High value = pressure on farmland.
Agricultural Density
Number of farmers per unit of agricultural land. Shows pressure on agricultural sector.
India Example
India's arithmetic density ≈ 382 persons/km². Bihar has highest density in India.
∴ Physiological density is more meaningful than arithmetic density.
Q.3
Explain the concept of population growth. Differentiate between growth rate and natural growth rate.
2023, 2021, 2020
Population Growth
Change in the number of inhabitants of a territory during a specific time period. It can be positive (growth) or negative (decline).
Absolute Growth
Actual numerical increase. e.g. India: 1951 population = 361 million; 2011 = 1210 million. Absolute growth = 849 million.
Growth Rate
Percentage change in population per year. Includes both natural growth AND migration. Growth Rate = (Births − Deaths + Net migration) / Total population × 100
Natural Growth Rate
Difference between birth rate and death rate only (does NOT include migration). Natural growth rate = Birth rate − Death rate
∴ Natural growth rate = Birth rate − Death rate (excludes migration)
Q.4
What is demographic transition? Explain its stages with a diagram.
2024, 2022, 2021
Definition
Demographic transition refers to the historical shift in birth rates and death rates from high to low levels as a country develops economically.
Stage 1: Pre-industrial
Both birth rate AND death rate are HIGH. Population grows slowly. Typical of backward/tribal societies. e.g. Some parts of Sub-Saharan Africa.
Stage 2: Early expanding
Death rate FALLS rapidly (due to medical improvements), but birth rate remains HIGH. Population grows rapidly. e.g. Developing countries, India in 1950s-70s.
Stage 3: Late expanding
Birth rate begins to FALL. Death rate continues to fall but slower. Population still grows but rate slows. e.g. India today.
Stage 4: Low stationary
Both birth rate AND death rate are LOW. Population nearly stable. e.g. USA, UK, France, Germany.
Note
Some demographers add Stage 5: Declining — death rate exceeds birth rate, population declines. e.g. Germany, Japan.
∴ India is currently in Stage 3 of demographic transition.
Q.5
What is age structure? Explain the types of age-sex pyramids.
2023, 2022
Age Structure
Composition of a population by age groups (0–14: young; 15–59: adult/working; 60+: elderly).
Expansive Pyramid
Broad base, narrow top. Large proportion of young population. High birth rate, high death rate. Rapidly growing. e.g. India, African nations.
Constrictive Pyramid
Narrow base, broad middle. Low birth rate. Aging population. Stable or declining. e.g. USA, UK, Sweden.
Stationary Pyramid
Near uniform width (bell-shaped). Birth and death rates approximately equal. Population stable. e.g. Australia.
∴ India has an expansive pyramid showing young population dominance.

Chapter 2 — Population: Part 2 (Pg 12–20)
16 Periods
Q.1
Define migration. Explain the types and causes of migration.
2024, 2023, 2021, 2019
Definition
Migration is the movement of people from one place to another with the intention of settling temporarily or permanently at the new place.
Types
  • International migration: Movement between countries (emigration/immigration)
  • Internal migration: Within a country — rural-urban, urban-rural, rural-rural, urban-urban
  • Seasonal migration: Temporary movement for work/seasonal activities
  • Voluntary vs Forced: By choice vs compelled by war, disaster
Push Factors
Factors that force people to leave origin: Poverty, unemployment, natural disasters, wars, drought, lack of amenities.
Pull Factors
Factors that attract to destination: Better jobs, higher wages, good education, healthcare, safety, better quality of life.
∴ Migration is driven by push factors (origin) and pull factors (destination).
Q.2
What is human development? Explain the Human Development Index (HDI) and its components.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2020
Human Development
Enlarging the range of people's choices. Concept developed by UNDP. Focuses on people's capabilities and freedoms, not just income.
HDI Definition
A composite index measuring average achievement in three basic dimensions of human development. Published annually in Human Development Report.
3 Components
  • Long and healthy life: Measured by life expectancy at birth
  • Knowledge/Education: Measured by mean years of schooling + expected years of schooling
  • Decent standard of living: Measured by Gross National Income (GNI) per capita (PPP US$)
HDI Value
Ranges from 0 to 1. >0.8: Very high; 0.7–0.8: High; 0.55–0.7: Medium; <0.55: Low. Norway has highest HDI; Niger among lowest.
India's HDI
India ranks medium on HDI. Improving but challenges remain — inequality, gender gap, regional disparities.
∴ HDI = f(Life expectancy + Education + GNI per capita)
Q.3
What is literacy? Explain factors affecting literacy rate. Write India's literacy scenario.
2022, 2021, 2019
Literacy
Ability to read and write a simple message in any language. India: A person aged 7 years and above who can read and write in any language.
Factors affecting
  • Availability of educational institutions
  • Economic condition (poverty hinders education)
  • Gender bias (less female literacy)
  • Government policies and schemes
  • Urbanization (urban areas have higher literacy)
India's Literacy
2011: 74.04% overall (Male 82.14%, Female 65.46%). Kerala highest (94%); Bihar lowest (63.82%). Urban literacy > Rural literacy.
Q.4
Distinguish between sex ratio and gender ratio. What does a low sex ratio indicate?
2023, 2020
Sex Ratio
Number of females per 1000 males in a population. India 2011: 943 females per 1000 males.
Low sex ratio
Indicates: Female foeticide, female infanticide, neglect of female health, preference for male child. Social evil — needs addressing.
Gender Ratio
Broader concept — includes gender equality in education, employment, political representation, health.
India situation
Haryana has lowest sex ratio. Kerala has highest. Child sex ratio (0–6 years) is very alarming nationally.

Chapter 3 — Human Settlements and Land Use (Pg 21–31)
14 Periods
Q.1
Distinguish between rural and urban settlements. Explain types of rural settlements.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2021
Rural Settlement
Small population size; people engaged in primary activities (farming, fishing). Low density. Direct contact with nature.
Urban Settlement
Large population; engaged in secondary and tertiary activities. High density. Complex infrastructure. e.g. Cities, towns, metropolis.
Types of Rural
  • Compact/Nucleated: Houses clustered together. Common in plains. e.g. Punjab, UP villages.
  • Dispersed/Scattered: Houses far apart. Common in hilly areas, forests. e.g. Parts of Meghalaya, Himachal Pradesh.
  • Linear: Houses along roads, rivers. e.g. Villages along River Ganga.
  • Semi-dispersed: Between compact and dispersed.
∴ Settlement pattern depends on terrain, climate, culture and economic activities.
Q.2
What is urbanization? Explain the functional classification of towns.
2023, 2022, 2020, 2019
Urbanization
The process by which an increasing proportion of population lives in urban areas. Result of rural-to-urban migration and natural increase in cities.
Functional Classification
  • Administrative towns: State capitals, district headquarters. e.g. Nagpur, Pune.
  • Industrial towns: Dominated by manufacturing. e.g. Bhilai, Jamshedpur.
  • Trade/Commercial towns: Market centres. e.g. Mumbai (financial capital).
  • Transport towns: Major road/rail/port junctions. e.g. Kandla port.
  • Educational towns: University towns. e.g. Pune, Varanasi.
  • Religious/Cultural towns: e.g. Varanasi, Tirupati, Amritsar.
  • Tourist towns: e.g. Shimla, Manali, Goa.
  • Defence towns: e.g. Ambala, Jalandhar.
∴ Most cities perform multiple functions — they are multifunctional settlements.
Q.3
What is land use? Explain the different types of land use.
2024, 2021, 2020
Land Use
The way in which land is used by humans for different purposes — influenced by topography, climate, soil, and socioeconomic factors.
Types
  • Agricultural land use: Farming — arable, pasture, orchards
  • Forests: Reserved, protected, unclassed
  • Industrial land use: Factories, manufacturing zones
  • Residential land use: Housing areas
  • Commercial land use: Markets, shops, business districts
  • Waste/Barren land: Desert, rocky areas
  • Recreational land: Parks, sports grounds
India
Net sown area ≈ 43% of total geographical area. Forest cover ≈ 24%. Fallow land is a concern.

Chapter 4 — Primary Economic Activities (Pg 32–41)
15 Periods
Q.1
What are primary economic activities? Explain gathering, hunting and pastoralism.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2020
Primary Activities
Activities directly related to natural resources — involving extraction and collection of natural resources. e.g. Agriculture, Mining, Fishing, Forestry.
Gathering
Collecting plants, fruits, roots, honey, etc. from natural environment. Oldest economic activity. Practiced by tribals in Amazon, Congo, Central Africa. Subsistence level — no surplus.
Hunting
Killing animals for food, clothing, tools. Practiced in cold regions — Arctic, sub-Arctic. e.g. Inuit (Eskimos) hunt seals, whales. Declining due to environmental laws.
Nomadic Pastoralism
Moving with animals (cattle, sheep, goats, camels) in search of pasture and water. Common in: Central Asia, Sahara, African savanna. e.g. Masai (Africa), Kirghiz (Central Asia), Gujjars (India).
Commercial Pastoralism
Large scale rearing for commercial purpose. Scientific methods. e.g. Sheep ranching in Australia, New Zealand; Cattle ranching in Argentina (Pampas), USA.
∴ Primary activities form the base of economic development for all countries.
Q.2
Distinguish between subsistence agriculture and commercial agriculture.
2024, 2023, 2021, 2019
Subsistence Agriculture
  • Farming for self-consumption; little or no surplus
  • Small land holdings
  • Traditional methods, low input
  • Labour intensive
  • Low productivity per hectare
  • e.g. Shifting cultivation (Jhum), Intensive subsistence farming in South/Southeast Asia
Commercial Agriculture
  • Farming for sale in market — profit oriented
  • Large farms with mechanization
  • Scientific techniques, high inputs
  • Capital intensive
  • High productivity per farmer
  • e.g. Wheat farming in USA, Canada; Plantation agriculture (tea, rubber, coffee)
∴ Subsistence = for self; Commercial = for market. Both differ in scale, method and purpose.
Q.3
What is plantation agriculture? Give examples. Explain its characteristics.
2023, 2022, 2020
Definition
A type of commercial farming where a single crop is grown on a large estate. Colonial legacy. Single crop dominates. Capital and labour intensive.
Characteristics
  • Large estate/farm with one cash crop
  • Scientific methods and management
  • Cheap labour (historically slave/indentured)
  • Processing factory on farm itself
  • Products mainly for export
Examples
Tea (Assam, Sri Lanka), Coffee (Brazil, Colombia), Rubber (Malaysia, Indonesia), Sugarcane (Cuba, Mauritius), Cotton (Sudan), Banana (Central America)
Q.4
Write a note on mining — types and significance.
2022, 2021, 2019
Mining
Extraction of minerals from the earth's crust for human use.
Types
  • Open-cast/Surface mining: When mineral deposit is near surface. e.g. Coal in Jharkhand, Iron ore in Goa
  • Underground/Shaft mining: Deep deposits. Expensive and dangerous. e.g. Gold mines in Kolar (Karnataka)
  • Placer mining: From river deposits (alluvial). e.g. Gold placer mining
  • Quarrying: Extraction of stones and rocks. e.g. Marble quarrying in Rajasthan
Significance
Provides raw materials for industry; source of foreign exchange; generates employment; basis of industrial development.

Chapter 5 — Secondary Economic Activities (Pg 42–54)
16 Periods
Q.1
Define secondary economic activities. Explain factors affecting industrial location.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2021
Secondary Activities
Activities that process natural resources and transform them into finished goods — manufacturing and construction. Add value to raw materials.
Factors of Industrial Location
  • Raw material: Industries locate near source to reduce transport cost. e.g. Sugar mills in UP, Bihar near sugarcane fields
  • Power: Industries need electricity/fuel. e.g. Aluminium smelting near hydroelectric power
  • Labour: Abundant cheap labour attracts labour-intensive industries
  • Transport: Good road, rail, port facilities reduce distribution cost
  • Market: Industries locate near large markets. e.g. Consumer goods industries near cities
  • Capital: Banking facilities and investment climate
  • Government policy: Industrial zones, tax concessions, incentives
  • Water: For cooling, processing. e.g. Thermal power plants near rivers
∴ Industrial location is determined by minimizing production and distribution costs.
Q.2
Write a note on high-tech industries. Give examples.
2024, 2023, 2020
High-tech Industries
Industries based on advanced technology — computers, electronics, telecommunications, biotechnology, robotics. Knowledge-intensive.
Characteristics
  • Require highly skilled, educated workforce
  • Located near universities and research institutes
  • High R&D (Research and Development) investment
  • Produce high-value goods and services
  • Less dependent on raw materials or physical labour
Examples
Silicon Valley (California, USA) — world's leading IT hub; Bengaluru (India) — 'Silicon Valley of India'; Zhongguancun (China); Cambridge Science Park (UK).
Significance
Drive economic growth, create high-value jobs, lead to innovation and technological progress.
Q.3
Classify industries based on raw materials and ownership.
2022, 2021, 2019
Based on Raw Material
  • Agro-based: Raw material from agriculture. e.g. Cotton textile, sugar, jute
  • Mineral-based: Use mineral ore. e.g. Iron and steel, aluminium, cement
  • Forest-based: Timber, paper, furniture
  • Animal-based: Leather, wool, silk
Based on Ownership
  • Public sector: Owned by government. e.g. BHEL, SAIL, ONGC
  • Private sector: Owned by individuals/companies. e.g. Tata Steel, Reliance
  • Joint sector: Government + private jointly. e.g. HPCL, Maruti (initially)
  • Cooperative sector: Owned by producers. e.g. Amul (dairy), KRIBHCO (fertilizer)

Chapter 6 — Tertiary Economic Activities (Pg 55–65)
14 Periods
Q.1
Define tertiary activities. Explain trade, transport and tourism as tertiary activities.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2021
Tertiary Activities
Activities that provide services to primary and secondary sectors and to the population. Also called service sector. No physical goods produced.
Trade
Buying and selling of goods. Internal trade: within country (retail, wholesale). International trade: between countries (import/export). India's major exports: IT services, software, textiles, gems, pharmaceuticals.
Transport
Movement of goods and people. Types: Roadways, Railways, Airways, Waterways, Pipelines. Railways — most important for passenger and freight in India. Airways — fastest, most expensive.
Tourism
Travel for recreation, leisure, business. World's fastest growing industry. Foreign exchange earner. Creates employment — hotels, guides, transport. India: Taj Mahal, Kerala backwaters, Rajasthan palaces attract tourists.
∴ Tertiary sector is the largest employer in developed countries.
Q.2
What is the quaternary sector? Explain its significance in modern economy.
2024, 2022, 2020
Quaternary Sector
Knowledge-based activities focused on research, information, education, finance, management and decision-making. Sometimes called the "knowledge economy".
Examples
IT companies (Infosys, TCS), research universities, financial consultants, think tanks, management consulting firms, media, advertising.
Significance
  • Drives innovation and technological change
  • Creates high-value, high-paying jobs
  • Contributes to GDP growth in developed economies
  • India's IT sector is a major quaternary activity earner
Quinary Sector
Highest level of decision makers — top executives, government ministers, scientists. Apex of quaternary sector.
Q.3
What is communication? Explain modern means of communication and their significance.
2023, 2021, 2019
Communication
Transmission of information from one place to another.
Modern Means
  • Internet: Email, social media, e-commerce — fastest growing communication medium
  • Mobile phones: Revolutionized communication — India has 1+ billion subscribers
  • Satellites: GPS, satellite TV, weather forecasting
  • Fibre optic cables: High-speed data transmission undersea
  • Mass media: TV, radio, newspapers
Significance
Facilitates global business, enables e-commerce, helps in disaster management, supports education (e-learning), shrinks the world (global village concept by McLuhan).

Chapter 7 — Region and Regional Development (Pg 66–74)
14 Periods
Q.1
Define region. Explain types of regions with examples.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2019
Region
An area of Earth's surface with one or more common characteristics that distinguish it from surrounding areas. Regions are defined for analytical and administrative purposes.
Formal/Uniform Region
Area with common attributes (physical or human). e.g. Wheat belt, cotton belt, rice belt; Monsoon Asia; the Sahara desert; Maharashtra state.
Functional/Nodal Region
Organised around a focal point (node). Defined by linkages and flows. e.g. Mumbai Metropolitan Region — hinterland of Mumbai port; Delhi NCR — flows of commuters, goods.
Perceptual Region
Based on people's feelings and perception. No clear boundaries. e.g. "The South" in USA; "Middle East"; "North India."
Planning Region
Administratively defined for development planning. e.g. India's Five Year Plan regions, district planning regions.
∴ Regionalization helps in planning, administration and understanding spatial patterns.
Q.2
What is regional development? Explain core-periphery concept.
2024, 2023, 2021, 2020
Regional Development
Policies and strategies to reduce socioeconomic disparities between regions — raising standards of living in backward areas while maintaining growth in advanced regions.
Core Region
Highly developed, industrialized, urbanized areas with better infrastructure and quality of life. e.g. Mumbai-Pune region, Delhi NCR. Attracts resources, skilled labour, investment.
Periphery Region
Less developed areas with poor infrastructure, less investment, high unemployment, outmigration. e.g. Vidarbha in Maharashtra, parts of Bihar, Jharkhand.
Core-Periphery Model
Gunnar Myrdal: Development creates spatial imbalances. Core grows at expense of periphery (backwash effect). Government intervention needed to spread development (spread effect).
Strategies
Special Economic Zones (SEZ), backward area subsidies, infrastructure development, decentralization of industries.
∴ Goal of regional development = reduce core-periphery gap for balanced growth.
Q.3
Explain sustainable development. What are its pillars?
2024, 2023, 2022, 2020
Definition
"Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." — Brundtland Commission (1987)
3 Pillars
  • Economic pillar: Economic growth and poverty reduction without depleting resources
  • Social pillar: Social equity, health, education, human rights
  • Environmental pillar: Conservation of ecosystems, biodiversity, reducing pollution
SDGs
United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (2015): 17 goals to be achieved by 2030. India committed to all SDGs.
Challenges
Climate change, deforestation, water scarcity, pollution, overexploitation of resources, inequality.

Chapter 8 — Geography: Nature and Scope (Pg 75–81)
13 Periods
Q.1
Define Geography. Explain its nature and scope.
2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020
Definition
Geography is the study of Earth's surface — its physical features, inhabitants, phenomena and the relationship between humans and environment.
Meaning
From Greek: Geo (Earth) + Graphos (description). Literally means "description of the Earth."
Nature
  • Geography is both a physical and social science
  • Interdisciplinary — connects natural sciences with social sciences
  • Spatial science — focuses on 'where' and 'why there'
  • Studies relationships between humans and environment
  • Studies distribution of phenomena over Earth's surface
Scope
  • Physical Geography: Geomorphology, climatology, hydrology, oceanography, biogeography
  • Human Geography: Population, settlement, economic, cultural, political geography
  • Regional Geography: Study of specific regions
  • Practical/Applied Geography: Cartography, GIS, remote sensing
∴ Geography integrates physical and human aspects to explain spatial patterns.
Q.2
What is human geography? Explain the relationship between human beings and environment (Determinism, Possibilism, Neo-determinism).
2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2019
Human Geography
Study of the interrelationship between people, place, and environment. Focuses on human activities, patterns of living, cultural landscapes.
Determinism
Environment determines human activities. Humans are passive — they merely respond to nature. Associated with Ratzel, Huntington, Semple. e.g. "Arctic climate makes Inuit hunt and build igloos." Criticized as too rigid.
Possibilism
Nature provides possibilities; humans choose. Humans are active agents who modify environment. Associated with Vidal de la Blache (French school). e.g. Dutch reclaimed land from sea; Israel made desert productive. More optimistic view.
Neo-determinism
Balanced view — Stop and Go Determinism. Humans can modify environment but within limits set by nature. Associated with Griffith Taylor. e.g. Agriculture in desert is possible with irrigation but within limits of climate.
∴ Neo-determinism (Griffith Taylor) is the most accepted approach today — humans adapt, but nature sets limits.
Q.3
Explain the branches of Human Geography.
2023, 2022, 2020
Branches
  • Population Geography: Studies population distribution, density, growth, migration
  • Settlement Geography: Studies rural and urban settlements, their patterns and morphology
  • Economic Geography: Studies distribution of economic activities
  • Agricultural Geography: Types of farming, crop patterns
  • Industrial Geography: Location and distribution of industries
  • Transport Geography: Transport networks and routes
  • Cultural Geography: Influence of culture on landscape
  • Political Geography: Political divisions, boundaries, geopolitics
  • Historical Geography: Past geographical conditions
Q.4
What is the importance of maps in Geography? Explain GIS and Remote Sensing.
2024, 2022, 2021
Maps in Geography
Maps are the language of geography. They show spatial distribution of features, help in planning, decision making, navigation and understanding patterns.
GIS
Geographic Information System — computer-based system to capture, store, manipulate, analyse and display spatial data. Used in urban planning, disaster management, agricultural planning, military.
Remote Sensing
Acquiring information about Earth's surface without physical contact — using satellites, aircraft. Used in: land use mapping, weather forecasting, crop monitoring, flood mapping. e.g. ISRO's RESOURCESAT.
GPS
Global Positioning System — satellite-based navigation system. Used in Google Maps, car navigation, military, agriculture (precision farming).
