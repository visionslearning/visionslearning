SECTION B — Very Short Answers 1 Mark Each
(i) Define electric flux. State its SI unit.
Electric flux (Φ) is defined as the total number of electric field lines passing normally through a given surface. Mathematically: Φ = E⃗ · A⃗ = EA cosθ where θ is the angle between E⃗ and the area vector A⃗. SI unit: N·m²/C or V·m
(ii) State Gauss's law in electrostatics.
Gauss's Law: The total electric flux through any closed surface is equal to 1/ε₀ times the total charge enclosed within that surface. Mathematically: ∮ E⃗ · dA⃗ = q_enc / ε₀ where ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/N·m² (permittivity of free space)
(iii) What is drift velocity of electrons?
Drift velocity is the average velocity acquired by free electrons in a conductor when an electric field is applied. Relation: vd = I/(nAe) where n = number density of electrons, A = cross-sectional area, e = charge of electron
(iv) Define magnetic susceptibility.
Magnetic susceptibility (χ) is the ratio of magnetization (M) to the magnetizing field intensity (H). χ = M/H It has no unit. χ > 0 for paramagnetic, χ < 0 for diamagnetic, χ >> 0 for ferromagnetic materials.
(v) State Faraday's laws of electromagnetic induction.
First Law: Whenever there is a change in the magnetic flux linked with a circuit, an EMF is induced in it. Second Law: The magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux. e = −dΦ/dt (Negative sign → Lenz's law)
(vi) What is resonance in an LCR circuit?
Resonance in an LCR circuit occurs when the capacitive reactance equals the inductive reactance, so the impedance is minimum and current is maximum. Condition: XL = XC → ωL = 1/ωC Resonant frequency: f₀ = 1/(2π√LC)
(vii) Define critical angle for total internal reflection.
Critical angle (C) is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is exactly 90°. Condition for TIR: angle of incidence > critical angle sin C = 1/n = n₂/n₁ where n = refractive index
(viii) What is photoelectric effect? Who discovered it?
Photoelectric effect is the phenomenon of emission of electrons from the surface of a metal when light of suitable frequency (above threshold frequency) falls on it. Discovered by: Heinrich Hertz (1887) — experimentally observed Explained by: Albert Einstein (1905) using quantum theory
(ix) State Bohr's second postulate for hydrogen atom.
Bohr's Second Postulate (Quantisation of Angular Momentum): The electron can revolve around the nucleus only in those orbits in which its angular momentum is an integral multiple of h/2π. mvr = nh/2π, where n = 1, 2, 3... (principal quantum number)
(x) Define half-life of a radioactive element. Give its formula.
Half-life (T₁/₂) is the time required for a radioactive substance to decay to half of its initial amount. T₁/₂ = 0.693/λ = 0.693t/ln(N₀/N) where λ = decay constant
(xi) What is p-type semiconductor?
A p-type semiconductor is formed by doping a pure semiconductor (like Si) with a trivalent impurity (like Boron, Aluminium, Indium). The dopant creates electron vacancies called holes. Holes are the majority charge carriers in p-type semiconductor. Majority carriers: holes; Minority carriers: electrons
(xii) State De Morgan's theorem (any one).
De Morgan's First Theorem: The complement of a sum is equal to the product of the complements. ̄(A + B) = Ā · B̄ De Morgan's Second Theorem: The complement of a product is equal to the sum of the complements. ̄(A · B) = Ā + B̄
SECTION C — Short Answer I (2 marks each) Any 8 of 12
(i) Coulomb's law — vector form
Coulomb's Law: The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. Scalar form: F = kq₁q₂/r² where k = 1/4πε₀ = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² Vector form: F⃗₁₂ = kq₁q₂/r² · r̂₂₁ r̂₂₁ = unit vector from q₂ to q₁ The force is along the line joining the two charges.
(ii) Relation between electric field E and potential V
Work done by electric field in moving charge q from A to B: W = q(VA − VB) Also: W = F·dx = qE·dx (for small displacement) q(VA − VB) = qE·dx VA − VB = E·dx E = −dV/dx (negative gradient of potential) Negative sign indicates E points from high V to low V
(v) Numerical — Max EMF of rotating coil
Given: N = 100 turns, A = 0.01 m², B = 0.5 T, f = 50 Hz ω = 2πf = 2π × 50 = 100π rad/s Max EMF formula: e₀ = NBAω 100 × 0.5 × 0.01 × 100π 50 × 100π 5000π e₀ = 15,708 V ≈ 15.7 kV
(ix) Derive Einstein's photoelectric equation
Einstein's Quantum Theory: Light consists of photons each having energy E = hν When a photon strikes a metal surface: Energy used to remove electron from surface = φ (work function) Remaining energy → kinetic energy of emitted electron By energy conservation: hν = φ + ½mv²max ½mv²max = hν − φ = h(ν − ν₀) where ν₀ = threshold frequency = φ/h KE max = eV₀ (stopping potential)
SECTION D — Short Answer II (3 marks each) Any 6 of 10
(i) Gauss's law + Electric field due to infinite straight wire
Gauss's Law: ∮ E⃗·dA⃗ = q_enc/ε₀ Consider infinitely long straight wire with linear charge density λ. Draw a cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius r and length L. Flux through curved surface = E × 2πrL Flux through end caps = 0 (E ⊥ area vector) Total flux = E × 2πrL Charge enclosed = λL By Gauss's law: E × 2πrL = λL/ε₀ E = λ/(2πε₀r) E is directed radially outward for positive λ; inward for negative λ
(ii) Energy stored in a capacitor
When a capacitor is charged, work is done against the electric field. This work is stored as electrostatic potential energy. Consider charging capacitor from q = 0 to q = Q At intermediate charge q, potential V = q/C Work done for small charge dq: dW = V·dq = (q/C)dq Total work = ∫₀^Q (q/C)dq = Q²/2C Energy stored: U = Q²/2C = ½CV² = QV/2 This energy is stored in the electric field between the plates.
(vii) Bohr's postulates + radius and energy of nth orbit
Bohr's Postulates: 1. Electrons revolve in certain fixed circular orbits without radiating energy. 2. Angular momentum is quantised: mvr = nh/2π 3. Energy is emitted/absorbed only when electron jumps between orbits. Derivation of radius: Centripetal force = Coulomb force mv²/r = ke²/r² ...(1) From quantisation: mv = nh/2πr ...(2) From (1) and (2): rₙ = n²h²ε₀/(πme²) = n² × 0.529 Å Energy of nth orbit: Eₙ = −13.6/n² eV
SECTION E — Long Answers (4 marks each) Any 4 of 6
(i) Electric field of uniformly charged solid sphere + Numerical
Using Gauss's law for a uniformly charged solid sphere of radius R, charge Q: Case 1: Outside (r > R) Gaussian surface: sphere of radius r E × 4πr² = Q/ε₀ E = Q/(4πε₀r²) = kQ/r² (same as point charge) Case 2: On the surface (r = R) E = kQ/R² Case 3: Inside (r < R) Charge enclosed = Q × (4/3)πr³ / (4/3)πR³ = Qr³/R³ E × 4πr² = Qr³/(ε₀R³) E = Qr/(4πε₀R³) ∝ r Numerical: Q = 10 μC = 10 × 10⁻⁶ C, R = 5 cm = 0.05 m (1) At r = 10 cm = 0.1 m (outside): E = kQ/r² = 9×10⁹ × 10×10⁻⁶ / (0.1)² = 9×10⁶ N/C E = 9 × 10⁶ N/C (2) At r = R = 0.05 m (on surface): E = kQ/R² = 9×10⁹ × 10×10⁻⁶ / (0.05)² = 36×10⁶ N/C E = 3.6 × 10⁷ N/C
(iii) Mirror formula derivation + Numerical
Derivation of mirror formula for concave mirror: Consider object O beyond C. Image I formed between F and C. Using similar triangles (ΔA'B'F and ΔDEF): A'B'/AB = (v−f)/f ...(i) Using similar triangles (ΔA'B'P and ΔABP): A'B'/AB = v/u ...(ii) From (i) and (ii): v/u = (v−f)/f vf = u(v−f) = uv − uf Dividing by uvf: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f Numerical: u = −20 cm, f = −15 cm (concave, sign convention) 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−15) − 1/(−20) = −1/15 + 1/20 −4/60 + 3/60 = −1/60 v = −60 cm (real, inverted image, 60 cm in front of mirror) Magnification m = −v/u = −(−60)/(−20) = −3 (inverted, magnified 3×)
(iv) Interference + Young's double slit numerical
Interference of light: When two coherent light waves superpose, the resultant intensity at any point depends on the phase difference between the waves. This gives alternating bright and dark fringes. Constructive interference (bright fringe): path diff = nλ (n = 0, 1, 2...) Destructive interference (dark fringe): path diff = (2n−1)λ/2 Fringe width formula: β = λD/d Numerical: d = 0.3 mm = 3×10⁻⁴ m, D = 1.5 m, β = 3 mm = 3×10⁻³ m β = λD/d λ = βd/D = (3×10⁻³ × 3×10⁻⁴) / 1.5 9×10⁻⁷ / 1.5 = 6×10⁻⁷ m λ = 600 nm (orange-red light)
(v) Photoelectric effect + Numerical
Photoelectric effect and graph: Einstein equation: KE_max = hν − φ = eV₀ Graph of V₀ vs ν is a straight line: slope = h/e, x-intercept = threshold frequency ν₀ Numerical: ν = 7×10¹⁴ Hz, φ = 2.5 eV, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s hν = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 7×10¹⁴ = 46.41×10⁻²⁰ J Convert to eV: 46.41×10⁻²⁰ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.9 eV KE_max = hν − φ = 2.9 − 2.5 = 0.4 eV KE_max = 0.4 eV = 6.4 × 10⁻²⁰ J
(vi) Energy bands + Zener diode as voltage regulator
Energy Band Diagrams: Conductor: Valence band and conduction band overlap → electrons move freely Semiconductor: Small energy gap (~1 eV) between valence and conduction bands → some electrons can jump at room temperature Insulator: Large energy gap (~5 eV) → electrons cannot jump even with thermal energy Zener diode as voltage regulator: Zener diode is reverse biased. When reverse voltage reaches breakdown voltage Vz, current flows but voltage across it remains constant = Vz. Circuit: Input Vin → Series resistor Rs → Zener diode (in reverse) || Load RL Output voltage = Vz (constant) regardless of fluctuations in Vin or RL Regulated output = Vz = constant
