Ch1 i. What is the mole concept? State Avogadro's number. (1M)
A mole is the amount of substance containing as many elementary particles as there are atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12. Avogadro's number Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³ particles per mole.
Ch1 ii. Distinguish between empirical formula and molecular formula. (1M)
Empirical formula: Simplest whole number ratio of atoms in a compound (e.g., CH₂O for glucose). Molecular formula: Actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule (e.g., C₆H₁₂O₆). Molecular formula = n × Empirical formula.
Ch2 iii. What is molarity? Give its formula and unit. (1M)
Molarity (M): The number of moles of solute dissolved in 1 litre of solution. M = n / V(L) = (mass in g) / (M.mass × V in L) Unit: mol/L or mol dm⁻³ (M).
Ch2 iv. What is a standard solution? What are the requirements of a primary standard? (1M)
A standard solution is a solution of accurately known concentration. A primary standard must be: (1) highly pure, (2) stable (not hygroscopic), (3) high molar mass (to reduce weighing error), (4) readily soluble, (5) non-toxic.
Ch3 v. What is chromatography? Name two types. (1M)
Chromatography is an analytical technique used to separate, identify and purify components of a mixture based on their different rates of migration through a stationary phase in a mobile phase. Types: (1) Paper chromatography, (2) Thin layer chromatography (TLC), (3) Column chromatography.
Ch4 vi. State Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle. (1M)
It is impossible to determine simultaneously and precisely both the position and momentum of a subatomic particle. Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π where Δx = uncertainty in position, Δp = uncertainty in momentum, h = Planck's constant.
Ch4 vii. Write the electronic configuration of Fe (Z = 26). (1M)
Fe (Z = 26): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s² Or: [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²
(Argon core: 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶ = 18 electrons; remaining 8 electrons fill 3d and 4s)
Ch5 viii. Define bond order. What is the bond order of O₂ and N₂? (1M)
Bond order = (number of bonding electrons – number of antibonding electrons) / 2. Higher bond order → stronger bond → shorter bond length.
  • O₂: double bond → bond order = 2
  • N₂: triple bond → bond order = 3

Ch1 A. Calculate the percentage composition of water (H₂O). (H=1, O=16) (3M)
Molar mass of H₂O:
M(H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18 g/mol
% H = (2/18) × 100 = 11.11%
% O = (16/18) × 100 = 88.89%
H = 11.11%, O = 88.89% ✓ (total = 100%)
Ch1 B. What is limiting reagent? In the reaction N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, if 28 g of N₂ and 6 g of H₂ are taken, identify the limiting reagent. (N=14, H=1) (3M)
Limiting reagent: The reactant that is completely consumed first in a reaction and limits the amount of product formed.
Moles of N₂ = 28/28 = 1 mol; Moles of H₂ = 6/2 = 3 mol
Stoichiometry: 1 mol N₂ requires 3 mol H₂
Available: 1 mol N₂ and 3 mol H₂ → exactly stoichiometric
Both are consumed completely; neither is in excess.
Both are limiting reagents simultaneously (exact stoichiometric ratio). NH₃ produced = 2 mol = 34 g.
Ch2 C. What is titration? Explain acid-base titration with an example. (3M)
Titration: A quantitative analytical technique in which a solution of known concentration (titrant/standard solution) is added to a solution of unknown concentration until the reaction is just complete (endpoint).
  • Acid-Base Titration: Used to determine the concentration of an acid or base.
  • Example: HCl (unknown) vs NaOH (known standard). Indicator: phenolphthalein (colourless → pink at endpoint).
  • HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • At equivalence point: moles of acid = moles of base → M₁V₁ = M₂V₂
  • Burette: NaOH; Conical flask: HCl + indicator.
📐 Diagram: Burette with NaOH → conical flask with HCl + phenolphthalein. Label: burette, clamp, flask, colour change at endpoint.
Ch3 D. Explain the principle of paper chromatography. Define Rf value. (3M)
Principle: Paper chromatography is based on partition — components of a mixture distribute themselves between two phases: a stationary phase (water held on paper fibres) and a mobile phase (organic solvent). Components with greater affinity for mobile phase travel faster; those with greater affinity for stationary phase travel slower → separation occurs.
  • A spot of mixture is placed on the paper. Solvent rises by capillary action, carrying components at different rates.
Rf value (Retention Factor): Rf = Distance travelled by component / Distance travelled by solvent front
  • Rf is always between 0 and 1; it is characteristic for a compound under fixed conditions.
  • Higher Rf → more soluble in mobile phase → travels farther.
Ch4 E. State and explain Aufbau principle, Pauli's exclusion principle, and Hund's rule. (3M)
  • Aufbau Principle: Electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy — lower energy orbitals are filled first. Order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p... (n+l rule: lower n+l filled first; if equal n+l, lower n first).
  • Pauli's Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers identical. An orbital can hold maximum 2 electrons with opposite spins (↑↓).
  • Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: In degenerate orbitals (same energy, e.g., 3 p-orbitals), electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins (↑↑) before pairing. This minimises electron repulsion and gives maximum spin multiplicity.
Ch5 F. Explain ionic bond formation with the example of NaCl. Draw the electron dot structure. (3M)
Ionic Bond: A chemical bond formed by the complete transfer of one or more electrons from a metal to a non-metal, resulting in formation of oppositely charged ions attracted by electrostatic force.
  • Formation of NaCl:
  • Na (2,8,1) → loses 1 electron → Na⁺ (2,8) — achieves noble gas configuration
  • Cl (2,8,7) → gains 1 electron → Cl⁻ (2,8,8) — achieves noble gas configuration
  • Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl (ionic bond by electrostatic attraction)
  • Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (ionisation); Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (electron affinity)
Electron dot structure:
  • Na• + •Cl: → Na⁺ [:Cl:]⁻ (Na loses its valence electron to Cl)
📐 Draw Lewis dot structure: Na with 1 dot → Na⁺ (no dots) and [:Cl:]⁻ with 4 pairs of dots around Cl.

Ch1 A. A compound contains 40% C, 6.67% H, and 53.33% O by mass. Its molar mass is 180 g/mol. Find its empirical formula and molecular formula. (C=12, H=1, O=16) (4M)
Step 1 — Mole ratio:
C: 40/12 = 3.33 mol
H: 6.67/1 = 6.67 mol
O: 53.33/16 = 3.33 mol
Step 2 — Divide by smallest (3.33):
C: 3.33/3.33 = 1; H: 6.67/3.33 = 2; O: 3.33/3.33 = 1
Empirical formula: CH₂O (molar mass of empirical formula = 12+2+16 = 30 g/mol)
Step 3 — Find n: n = M(molecular)/M(empirical) = 180/30 = 6
Molecular formula = (CH₂O)₆ = C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose)
Ch2 B. Calculate the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving 4 g of NaOH in 500 mL of water. Also find the normality. (Na=23, O=16, H=1) (4M)
Molar mass of NaOH: 23+16+1 = 40 g/mol
Moles of NaOH: n = 4/40 = 0.1 mol
Volume: V = 500 mL = 0.5 L
Molarity: M = n/V = 0.1/0.5 = 0.2 mol/L (0.2 M)
Normality: For NaOH (acid-base), n-factor = 1 (one OH⁻)
N = M × n-factor = 0.2 × 1 = 0.2 N
Molarity = 0.2 M; Normality = 0.2 N
Ch4 C. Describe Bohr's model of atom. State Bohr's postulates and derive the expression for radius of nth orbit. (4M)
Bohr's Model (1913):
  • Postulate 1: Electrons revolve around the nucleus in fixed circular paths called orbits or shells without radiating energy.
  • Postulate 2: Only those orbits are allowed in which the angular momentum (mvr) of the electron is an integral multiple of h/2π: mvr = nh/2π (n = 1,2,3... = principal quantum number)
  • Postulate 3: Energy is emitted or absorbed as a photon only when an electron jumps from one orbit to another. ΔE = hν = E₂ – E₁
Radius of nth orbit:
  • Centripetal force = Electrostatic force: mv²/r = Ze²/4πε₀r²
  • From angular momentum: mvr = nh/2π → v = nh/2πmr
  • Substituting and solving: rₙ = n²a₀/Z
  • where a₀ = 0.529 Å (Bohr radius for H, n=1)
  • For Hydrogen (Z=1): r₁ = 0.529 Å, r₂ = 4×0.529 = 2.116 Å
Limitations: Cannot explain multi-electron atoms, Zeeman effect, or fine structure of spectra.
Ch5 D. Explain VSEPR theory. Predict the shape of NH₃ and H₂O using VSEPR theory. (4M)
VSEPR Theory (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion):
  • Electron pairs (both bond pairs and lone pairs) around the central atom repel each other and arrange themselves to minimise repulsion → determines molecular geometry.
  • Repulsion order: Lone pair–Lone pair > Lone pair–Bond pair > Bond pair–Bond pair
NH₃ (Ammonia):
  • N has 5 valence electrons; forms 3 bonds with H + 1 lone pair
  • Total electron pairs = 4 (3 BP + 1 LP) → tetrahedral electron geometry
  • Molecular shape: Trigonal pyramidal (lone pair pushes bonding pairs down)
  • Bond angle ≈ 107° (less than 109.5° due to LP repulsion)
H₂O (Water):
  • O has 6 valence electrons; forms 2 bonds with H + 2 lone pairs
  • Total electron pairs = 4 (2 BP + 2 LP) → tetrahedral electron geometry
  • Molecular shape: Bent/V-shape
  • Bond angle ≈ 104.5° (reduced further due to 2 LP repulsions)
📐 Draw: NH₃ with N at top, 3 H atoms at base (pyramid) + lone pair. H₂O with O at centre, 2 H atoms at sides, 2 lone pairs at top.
Ch5 E. Explain hybridisation. Describe sp³ hybridisation of carbon in methane (CH₄) with diagram. (4M)
Hybridisation: The process of mixing of atomic orbitals of similar energy to form new hybrid orbitals of equal energy and identical shape is called hybridisation.
  • Hybridisation explains molecular geometry and bond angles.
sp³ Hybridisation in Methane (CH₄):
  • Carbon ground state: 1s² 2s² 2p² (only 2 unpaired electrons)
  • Excited state: 1s² 2s¹ 2p³ (promoted: 4 unpaired electrons)
  • 1 s-orbital + 3 p-orbitals mix → 4 equivalent sp³ hybrid orbitals
  • 4 sp³ orbitals point to corners of a regular tetrahedron (bond angle = 109.5°)
  • Each sp³ orbital overlaps with 1s of H → 4 C–H sigma (σ) bonds
  • Shape: Tetrahedral
