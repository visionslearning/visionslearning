Section A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
Gauss's theorem states that ∮E·dA equals:
✔ Answer: (A) q/ε₀
Explanation Gauss's Law: ∮E⃗·dA⃗ = q_enclosed/ε₀ Total electric flux through any closed surface = enclosed charge / permittivity of free space
Q.1 (ii)
In electrolysis, Faraday's first law m = ZIt — Z is:
✔ Answer: (B) electrochemical equivalent
Explanation Z = electrochemical equivalent = mass deposited per coulomb of charge passed Z = M/(nF) = E/F   where M = molar mass, n = valency, F = 96500 C, E = equivalent mass
Q.1 (iii)
Magnetic susceptibility χ > 0 for:
✔ Answer: (B) paramagnetic materials
Explanation Paramagnetic: χ > 0 (small positive, e.g., Al, O₂) — weakly attracted to magnets Diamagnetic: χ < 0 (small negative, e.g., Bi, Cu) — weakly repelled Ferromagnetic: χ >> 1 (very large positive, e.g., Fe, Ni, Co) — strongly attracted
Q.1 (iv)
Speed of electromagnetic waves in free space:
✔ Answer: (A) 3 × 10⁸ m/s
Explanation c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (exactly 299,792,458 m/s) All EM waves travel at this speed in vacuum regardless of frequency.
Q.1 (v)
In n-type semiconductor, Fermi level is:
✔ Answer: (B) near the conduction band
Explanation n-type has extra electrons (pentavalent dopant) → Fermi level shifts UP toward conduction band p-type has extra holes (trivalent dopant) → Fermi level shifts DOWN toward valence band Intrinsic: Fermi level at centre of band gap
Q.1 (vi)
Nuclear density is approximately:
✔ Answer: (C) 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³
Explanation Nuclear radius: R = R₀A^(1/3), R₀ = 1.2 fm = 1.2×10⁻¹⁵ m ρ = M/(4/3)πR³ ≈ m_p × A / [(4/3)π(R₀A^(1/3))³] = 3m_p/(4πR₀³) ρ ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³ (same for all nuclei — nuclear density is constant)
Q.1 (vii)
Brewster's angle θ_B is given by:
✔ Answer: (A) θ_B = tan⁻¹(n₂/n₁)
Explanation Brewster's law: tan θ_B = n₂/n₁ = n (for glass in air) At θ_B, reflected light is completely plane-polarised. Reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular. For glass (n=1.5): θ_B = tan⁻¹(1.5) ≈ 56.3°
Q.1 (viii)
In full-wave rectifier, the ripple frequency is:
✔ Answer: (B) 2f
Explanation Full-wave rectifier: both +ve and −ve half-cycles are utilised. For each cycle of AC input (frequency f), there are TWO output pulses. Output frequency = 2f (ripple frequency = 2 × input frequency)
Q.1 (ix)
Resolving power of microscope is proportional to:
✔ Answer: (A) 1/λ
Explanation Resolving power of microscope = 2n sinα/λ (Rayleigh criterion) n = refractive index of medium, α = half angle of cone, λ = wavelength RP ∝ 1/λ — shorter wavelength gives better resolution (why electron microscope resolves much more than optical)
Q.1 (x)
Work function is minimum for:
✔ Answer: (C) caesium
Explanation Work function of Caesium (Cs) ≈ 2.0 eV — lowest among all metals (alkali metals have low work functions) Platinum: 5.65 eV, Copper: 4.7 eV, Gold: 5.1 eV — all high Cs is used in photoelectric cells and photomultiplier tubes.
Section B — Very Short Answers 8 × 1 = 8 Marks
Q.2 (i)
State the principle of conservation of charge.
StatementThe total electric charge in an isolated system remains constant. Charge can neither be created nor destroyed, only transferred from one body to another.
ExamplesIn pair production: γ → e⁻ + e⁺ (total charge = −1 + 1 = 0) ✓  |  Rubbing: electrons transferred (not created)
Q.2 (ii)
Define Seebeck coefficient.
DefinitionSeebeck coefficient (S or α) is the ratio of the thermoelectric EMF developed per degree temperature difference between the hot and cold junctions of a thermocouple.
FormulaS = dV/dT   Unit: V/K or μV/K
Q.2 (iii)
What is the right-hand thumb rule?
StatementIf a straight current-carrying conductor is held in the right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of current flow, then the curled fingers indicate the direction of the circular magnetic field lines around the conductor.
Q.2 (iv)
State Faraday's second law of electrolysis.
StatementWhen the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytic cells connected in series, the masses of substances deposited at the electrodes are directly proportional to their equivalent masses (chemical equivalents).
Formulam₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂   where E = equivalent mass = M/(valency)
Q.2 (v)
What is Doppler effect in light? Explain red shift and blue shift.
Doppler EffectThe apparent change in the frequency (and wavelength) of light due to relative motion between the source and observer.
Red ShiftWhen source moves AWAY from observer → wavelength increases → spectrum shifts toward red end. Used to detect receding galaxies (proof of expanding universe).
Blue ShiftWhen source moves TOWARD observer → wavelength decreases → spectrum shifts toward blue end.
Q.2 (vi)
Define atomic mass unit (u). What is its value in MeV?
DefinitionOne atomic mass unit (u) is defined as 1/12th of the mass of one atom of carbon-12 isotope (¹²C).
Values1 u = 1.66054 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 931.5 MeV/c²
Q.2 (vii)
What is Zener breakdown? State the condition.
DefinitionZener breakdown is a type of reverse breakdown in a heavily doped p-n junction where a strong electric field across the narrow depletion region directly ruptures covalent bonds, creating electron-hole pairs (quantum tunnelling).
ConditionOccurs at low reverse voltage (V_Z < 6V) in heavily doped junctions. Voltage remains constant at V_Z — used for voltage regulation.
Q.2 (viii)
What is the bandwidth of an amplifier?
DefinitionBandwidth is the range of frequencies over which the voltage gain of an amplifier remains at least 70.7% (i.e., 1/√2 = 0.707) of its maximum (mid-frequency) value, or within 3 dB of the maximum gain.
FormulaBandwidth = f₂ − f₁   where f₁ = lower cutoff frequency, f₂ = upper cutoff frequency
Q.2 (ix)
What is pulse modulation?
DefinitionPulse modulation is a type of digital modulation where the information signal modifies certain parameters (amplitude, width, or position) of a series of pulses that are used as the carrier.
TypesPAM (Pulse Amplitude Modulation), PWM (Pulse Width Modulation), PPM (Pulse Position Modulation), PCM (Pulse Code Modulation)
Q.2 (x)
Define specific resistance (resistivity).
DefinitionSpecific resistance (resistivity) ρ is the resistance offered by a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area.
FormulaR = ρL/A → ρ = RA/L   SI Unit: Ω·m
SignificanceCharacteristic property of the material, independent of dimensions. Good conductor: ρ ~ 10⁻⁸ Ω·m, Insulator: ρ ~ 10⁸ Ω·m.
Q.2 (xi)
State the principle of reversibility of light.
StatementIf a ray of light is reversed in direction after reflection or refraction, it retraces its original path exactly. The path of light is reversible.
ConsequenceUsed to derive Snell's law in reverse: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ works in both directions.
Q.2 (xii)
What is nuclear radius formula? State its significance.
FormulaR = R₀ A^(1/3)   where R₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ m (1.2 fm), A = mass number
Significance(1) Shows that nuclear radius increases with mass number as A^(1/3)
(2) Nuclear volume ∝ A → nuclear density is CONSTANT for all nuclei ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³

Q.3 (i)
Derive E = σ/ε₀ (field and surface charge density).
Derivation Consider a thin infinite plane sheet with surface charge density σ (C/m²). By symmetry: E is perpendicular to sheet and equal on both sides. Gaussian surface: a small cylinder (pillbox) of cross-sectional area A straddling the sheet Flux through: two flat faces = EA + EA = 2EA; curved side contributes 0 Charge enclosed = σA Gauss's law: 2EA = σA/ε₀
Result
E = σ/(2ε₀) (for infinite sheet)  |  E = σ/ε₀ (between plates of capacitor — two sheets)
Q.3 (ii)
State and derive Joule's law of heating.
StatementThe heat generated in a conductor is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance, and the time of flow: H ∝ I²Rt.
Derivation Consider resistance R carrying current I for time t. Work done by source = charge × potential difference W = Q × V = It × IR = I²Rt This work appears entirely as heat (no mechanical work):
Result
H = I²Rt = V²t/R = VIt   (Joule's law)
Q.3 (iii)
Explain cyclotron — construction, working and limitations.
ConstructionTwo D-shaped hollow metal chambers (dees) placed between poles of a strong electromagnet. High-frequency AC applied between the dees. Ion source at the centre.
Working Ion accelerated by electric field in the gap between dees (gains KE). Inside dee: magnetic force makes it move in semicircle (constant speed). Each time it crosses the gap (now reversed), it gains energy → radius increases → spiral outward. Cyclotron frequency: f_c = qB/2πm (independent of speed)
Limitations(1) Cannot accelerate electrons (too light, become relativistic quickly)
(2) As speed → relativistic, mass increases → not in sync with AC → cyclotron frequency condition fails
Q.3 (iv)
Derive EMF induced in a rotating coil.
SetupCoil of N turns, area A, rotating at angular velocity ω in magnetic field B.
Derivation At time t, angle between B and normal to coil: θ = ωt Flux: Φ = NBA cosωt EMF: e = −N(dΦ/dt) = NBAω sinωt = e₀ sinωt
Result
e = e₀ sinωt   where e₀ = NBAω (maximum EMF)
Q.3 (v)
Explain Brewster's law and polarisation by reflection.
Brewster's LawWhen light is incident at Brewster's angle (θ_B) on a surface, the reflected light is completely plane-polarised (only transverse oscillations perpendicular to plane of incidence).
Formulatan θ_B = n   (n = refractive index of denser medium)
ConditionAt θ_B: reflected ray ⊥ refracted ray (angle between them = 90°). θ_B + θ_r = 90°.
ApplicationPolaroid sunglasses (reduce glare from reflected light), photography (remove reflections), Brewster windows in lasers
Q.3 (xi)
Derive E_n = −13.6/n² eV.
Derivation KE = ½mv² = ke²/2r (from mv²/r = ke²/r²) PE = −ke²/r (electric PE of proton-electron system) Total E = KE + PE = ke²/2r − ke²/r = −ke²/2r Substitute r_n = n²h²/(4π²mke²): E_n = −me⁴k²/(2n²h²) = −13.6/n² eV
Values
E₁ = −13.6 eV (ground state)  |  E₂ = −3.4 eV  |  E₃ = −1.51 eV  |  E_∞ = 0 eV
Q.3 (xii)
What are matter waves? State their significance.
DefinitionMatter waves (de Broglie waves) are the waves associated with any moving material particle. Every particle of matter, like electrons, protons, atoms, has a wave-like behaviour.
Formulaλ = h/p = h/mv
Significance (1) Led to quantum mechanics and wave mechanics (Schrödinger equation)
(2) Explains Bohr's quantisation: electron orbit circumference = nλ (standing wave condition)
(3) Practical use: electron microscopy (very short λ → very high resolution)

Q.4 (i)
Derive V for a system of charges.
Superposition The total potential at point P due to a system of charges q₁, q₂, q₃... at distances r₁, r₂, r₃... is: V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ + ... = k(q₁/r₁ + q₂/r₂ + q₃/r₃ + ...) V = k Σ qᵢ/rᵢ Unlike field (vector), potential is SCALAR → simply add algebraically.
NoteSign matters: +q gives +V, −q gives −V. A point can have V=0 even if E≠0.
Q.4 (ii)
Derive force between two parallel current-carrying wires. Define one Ampere.
SetupTwo parallel wires, currents I₁ and I₂, separation d, length l.
Derivation B₁ (field of wire 1 at location of wire 2): B₁ = μ₀I₁/(2πd) Force on wire 2: F = I₂ × l × B₁ = I₂lμ₀I₁/(2πd)
Result
F/l = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd)   Parallel → attract; Antiparallel → repel
One Ampere One Ampere is the current that, when flowing in each of two parallel infinite straight conductors 1 m apart in vacuum, produces a force of 2 × 10⁻⁷ N per metre length between them.
Q.4 (iv)
Explain Davisson-Germer experiment.
SetupElectron gun shoots electrons (accelerated by voltage V) at a nickel crystal target. A movable detector measures reflected electron intensity at various angles.
Observation At V = 54 V and scattering angle = 50°, a sharp peak in intensity was observed. de Broglie wavelength of electrons at 54V: λ = h/mv = h/√(2meV) = 1.67 Å Bragg's law: 2d sinθ = nλ → with d = 0.91 Å (Ni spacing), λ = 1.65 Å The two values match well.
Conclusion
Electrons showed diffraction (wave property) — confirmed de Broglie's matter wave hypothesis

Q.5 (i)
(a) E on equatorial line of dipole. (b) Dipole in non-uniform electric field.
Equatorial Field E₊ and E₋ from +q and −q at point P: equal magnitude, components along axis cancel, perpendicular components add. E_eq = kp/(r²+a²)^(3/2)   (opposite to p⃗) For r >> a: E_eq ≈ kp/r³ (opposite to p⃗)
Dipole in Non-uniform E⃗ Force on +q ≠ Force on −q (different E at their positions) Net force ≠ 0 → translational motion of the dipole Net torque (as in uniform field) → rotational motion Combined: dipole experiences both translation and rotation → aligns with field AND moves toward stronger field region.

Q.5 (iii)
(a) LASER principle. (b) Types of LASER.
LASER Principle Stimulated Emission: An incident photon of exact frequency triggers an excited atom to emit a second photon of identical frequency, phase and direction — producing coherent, monochromatic light. Population Inversion: Normally, more atoms in lower state. For LASER, we need more atoms in excited state than ground state (population inversion) — achieved by optical pumping. Optical Pumping: External energy source (flash lamp, another laser) excites atoms to metastable state, creating population inversion.
Types Solid-state: Ruby LASER (Cr³⁺ ions in Al₂O₃) — 694 nm (red) Gas: He-Ne LASER — 632.8 nm (red) Semiconductor: GaAs LASER diode — infrared/visible, used in CD/DVD players
