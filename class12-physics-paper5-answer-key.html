Magnetic susceptibility χ > 0 for:
(A) diamagnetic materials
(B) paramagnetic materials
(C) non-magnetic materials

✔ Answer: (B) paramagnetic materials
Explanation Paramagnetic: χ > 0 (weakly attracted). Diamagnetic: χ < 0. Ferromagnetic: χ >> 1.

(A) near the valence band
(B) near the conduction band

Explanation n-type (extra electrons): Fermi level shifts UP toward conduction band. p-type (extra holes): Fermi level shifts DOWN toward valence band.

Q.2 (v) [1 Mark]
What is Doppler effect in light? Explain red shift and blue shift.
Doppler EffectThe apparent change in frequency (and wavelength) of light due to relative motion between source and observer.
Red ShiftSource moves AWAY: wavelength increases → spectrum shifts towards red. Used to detect receding galaxies (expanding universe).
Blue ShiftSource moves TOWARD observer: wavelength decreases → shift towards blue.

Section C — Short Answers 8 × 2 = 16 Marks
Q.3 (i) [2 Marks]
Derive the relation between electric field and surface charge density (E = σ/ε₀).
Gaussian pillbox surface: flux through two flat faces = 2EA. By Gauss's law: E = σ/(2ε₀) per sheet. For capacitor plates: E = σ/ε₀ (fields add up between plates).
Derivation For infinite plane sheet with surface charge density σ: Gaussian surface = cylinder (pillbox) of area A straddling the sheet Only two flat faces contribute (curved side: E⊥dA): Φ = 2EA Charge enclosed = σA By Gauss's law: 2EA = σA/ε₀ → E = σ/2ε₀ (for single sheet) Between capacitor plates (two sheets, same direction): E = σ/ε₀
Q.3 (ii) [2 Marks]
State and derive Joule's law of heating.
Joule heating: work done by source on charge Q=It across R is W = QV = It·IR = I²Rt — all converted to heat in resistor.
Derivation In time t, charge Q = It flows through resistance R under potential V = IR. Work done by source = charge × potential = It × IR = I²Rt All this work appears as heat (no mechanical work done in resistor): H = I²Rt = V²t/R = VIt   (Joule's law)
Q.3 (iii) [2 Marks]
Explain the cyclotron — construction, working and limitations.
Cyclotron: ion spirals outward gaining energy each time it crosses the gap
ConstructionTwo D-shaped hollow conductors (dees) between poles of electromagnet. High-frequency AC between dees. Ion source at centre.
WorkingIon accelerated by E in gap → semicircle in dee (B provides centripetal force) → crosses gap again (AC reverses) → accelerated again → radius increases → spirals outward.
Limitations(1) Cannot accelerate electrons (become relativistic too quickly)  (2) At relativistic speeds, mass increases → cyclotron condition fails
Q.3 (iv) [2 Marks]
Derive the expression for EMF induced in a rotating coil.
Coil rotating in B: e = NBAω sinωt = e₀ sinωt where e₀ = NBAω (max EMF)
Derivation Coil of N turns, area A, rotating at ω in field B. At angle θ = ωt: Flux: Φ = NBA cosωt EMF: e = −dΦ/dt = NBAω sinωt = e₀ sinωt
Result
e = e₀ sinωt   where e₀ = NBAω (maximum EMF)
Q.3 (v) [2 Marks]
Explain Brewster's law and polarisation by reflection with diagram.
At Brewster's angle: reflected ray is completely plane-polarised; reflected ⊥ refracted
Brewster's Law At Brewster's angle θ_B, reflected light is completely plane-polarised (electric field only in plane ⊥ to incident plane). tan θ_B = n   (Brewster's law)
ConditionReflected ray ⊥ refracted ray: θ_B + θ_r = 90°. For glass (n=1.5): θ_B = tan⁻¹(1.5) ≈ 56.3°

Two parallel wires with currents in same direction attract each other

Q.5 (i) [4 Marks]
(a) Derive the expression for electric field E on the equatorial line of an electric dipole. (b) Explain the behaviour of an electric dipole in a non-uniform electric field.
Equatorial E field: vertical components of E₊ and E₋ cancel; net E is along −p⃗ direction. E_eq = kp/r³ for r » a (half the axial field).
Equatorial E E_eq = kp/(r²+a²)^(3/2) ≈ kp/r³ (for r >> a, opposite to p⃗)
Non-uniform Field In non-uniform E⃗, forces on +q and −q are unequal → net force ≠ 0 Dipole experiences both: translational force (towards stronger field region) AND rotational torque Net force = p (dE/dx) in direction of increasing field
