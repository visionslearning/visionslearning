1.1 Basic Principles of Life ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Planet earth is made up of abiotic (non-living) and biotic (living) components
  • The biotic components are the living beings present around us
Characteristics of Living Organisms – Complete Table ▶
FeatureKey Points
MetabolismLiving beings need energy for survival. Metabolism = Catabolism (breaking molecules) + Anabolism (making new molecules). Most all-inclusive characteristic of life ★
GrowthFrom birth, organisms show well-orchestrated growth and development. Non-living things like sand mounds may grow but NOT from within.
AgeingGrowth has a limited time span. Molecules, organs and systems gradually lose effective working — this is the ageing process.
ReproductionOrganisms produce young ones (asexual or sexual). Note: Mules & sterile worker bees do NOT reproduce yet are living → NOT an all-inclusive characteristic.
DeathAs the body loses capacity to perform metabolism, the organism dies.
Responsiveness / IrritabilityLiving beings respond to thermal, chemical or biological changes in surroundings. Unique property of living organisms.
✍️ Key point: Metabolism is the most all-inclusive characteristic of life. Reproduction is NOT all-inclusive (mules don't reproduce).
1.2 Herbarium ★ EXAM HOT

📌 Key Points

  • Word herbarium (plural: herbaria) was coined by Pitton de Tournefort
  • Art of herbarium was initiated by Italian taxonomist Luca Ghini (1490–1556)
  • Herbaria are effective tools in taxonomic studies
  • A herbarium is essentially a dried plant specimen that is pressed, treated and mounted on a standard size sheet to preserve it
  • Label (lower right corner of sheet) contains: date & place of collection, detailed classification, ecological peculiarities, local names, name of collector
  • Ex-situ conservation tool
1.3 Botanical Gardens ★

📌 Key Points

  • Places where plants of different varieties collected from different parts of the world are grown in a scientific and systematic in vivo manner
  • Plants are labelled with scientific and common names
  • First botanical garden established in 1543 by Prof. Luca Ghini at Pisa, Italy
  • Ex-situ conservation tool
1.4 Museum ★

📌 Key Points

  • Museums are places where collections of preserved plant and animal specimens are kept
  • Plant and animal specimens preserved in formalin (10%–40% formaldehyde) in transparent jars
  • Larger animals (birds, mammals) are usually stuffed and preserved — this science is called Taxidermy
  • Specimens in dried form are also kept; collections include shells, skeletons, insect boxes
  • Biological museums in educational institutes are reference hubs of biodiversity studies
1.5 Zoological Parks (Zoo) ★

📌 Key Points

  • Wild animals are kept in captivity
  • Care is taken to provide conditions similar to their natural habitat — this is ex-situ conservation
  • A naturalist can study food habits and behavior of animals here
1.6 Biodiversity Parks ★

📌 Key Points

  • An ecological assemblage of species that form self-sustaining communities on degraded/barren landscapes
  • Example: Late Uttamrao Patil Biodiversity Park, Gureghar, Mahabaleshwar
  • In-situ conservation tool
Conservation Types – In-situ vs Ex-situ ▶

★★ 2 Mark Exam Questions – Chapter 1
Q1. What is a herbarium? How is it useful in taxonomic studies? 2 marks ▶

A herbarium is essentially a dried plant specimen that is pressed, treated and mounted on a standard size sheet to preserve it.

  • Word coined by Pitton de Tournefort; art initiated by Luca Ghini
  • Label (lower right corner) contains: date, place of collection, classification, ecological details, local names, collector's name
  • Herbaria are effective tools in taxonomic studies — help in identification and classification of plants
  • It is an ex-situ conservation tool
✍️ Definition + uses = full 2 marks
Q2. What is metabolism? Why is it the most all-inclusive characteristic of life? 2 marks ▶

Metabolism = Catabolism (breaking molecules) + Anabolism (making new molecules).

It is the sum total of all chemical reactions taking place inside a living organism.

Why most all-inclusive:

  • Every living organism, without exception, carries out metabolism
  • Even mules (which don't reproduce) and brain-dead patients perform metabolism at the cellular level
  • Reproduction is NOT all-inclusive (mules don't reproduce, yet they are living)
  • Growth is NOT unique to living things (crystals also grow)
  • Therefore, metabolism is the most reliable and all-inclusive characteristic of life

Q4. What are taxonomical aids? Give examples. 2 marks ▶

Taxonomical aids are tools that help in identification and classification of organisms, gathered through intensive laboratory and field studies.

Examples:

  • Herbaria – dried, pressed, mounted plant specimens
  • Botanical gardens – living plants grown in vivo
  • Museums – preserved plant and animal specimens
  • Biodiversity parks – self-sustaining communities on degraded land
  • Zoological parks – animals in conditions similar to natural habitat
  • Keys, Flora, Monograph, Manual, Catalogue
Q5. What is a key? What are its features? 2 marks ▶

A key is a taxonomical aid used for classification and identification of plants and animals.

Features:

  • Keys are based on contrasting characters
  • One contrasting character gets accepted, the other rejected
  • The statement in a key is called a lead
  • Keys are normally analytical in nature
  • Help identify species in a particular area

Q7. What are the characteristics of living organisms? Explain any three. 3 marks ▶

The main characteristics of living organisms are: Metabolism, Growth, Ageing, Reproduction, Death, Responsiveness/Irritability.

1. Metabolism: Metabolism = Catabolism + Anabolism. Living beings need energy for survival. It is the most all-inclusive characteristic of life.

2. Growth: From birth, organisms show well-orchestrated growth and development. Non-living things may grow but not from within (e.g. sand mounds).

3. Reproduction: Organisms produce young ones (asexual or sexual) to ensure continuity of race. Note: Mules & sterile worker bees do not reproduce yet are living → NOT all-inclusive.

✍️ Write any 3 with explanation = 3 marks. Always mention the exception for reproduction.

Q8. Write in detail about any four taxonomical aids. 4 marks ▶

1. Herbarium: Dried plant specimen pressed, treated and mounted on standard size sheet. Label contains date, place of collection, detailed classification, ecological peculiarities, local names, name of collector. Coined by Pitton de Tournefort; initiated by Luca Ghini. Ex-situ conservation tool.

2. Botanical Garden: Places where plants from different parts of the world are grown in vivo. Plants labelled with scientific and common names. First established by Luca Ghini at Pisa, Italy (1543). Ex-situ conservation.

3. Museum: Collections of preserved plant and animal specimens. Preserved in formalin (10–40% formaldehyde). Larger animals stuffed (taxidermy). Collections include shells, skeletons, insect boxes. Reference hubs of biodiversity studies.

4. Zoological Park (Zoo): Wild animals kept in captivity with conditions similar to their natural habitat. Used for ex-situ conservation. Naturalists study food habits and behavior of animals here.

✍️ 1 mark per aid = 4 marks. Mention key facts for each.
Q9. Describe biodiversity parks and their importance in conservation. 4 marks ▶

Biodiversity Parks: An ecological assemblage of species that form self-sustaining communities on degraded or barren landscapes.

Features:

  • Represents in-situ conservation — organisms live in their natural environment
  • Example: Late Uttamrao Patil Biodiversity Park, Gureghar, Mahabaleshwar — best model for conservation of natural heritage in urban landscape
  • Helps restore biodiversity in areas where it has been degraded

Importance of Biodiversity Conservation:

  • Biodiversity = degree of variation of life forms in an ecosystem
  • Essential to maintain ecological stability
  • Population explosion and over-exploitation have resulted in loss of biodiversity at an alarming rate
  • Loss of one variety of organisms can affect the entire ecosystem
  • Sacred groves — plants conserved in name of holy place; recognised by IUCN

Dr. S. P. Agharkar's contribution: Explored biodiversity of Western Ghats; discovered freshwater jellyfish; findings in Nature (1912); ARI, Pune named after him.
