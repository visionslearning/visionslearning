🌱 1.1 Characteristics of Living Organisms
Basic Principles of Life Planet earth is made up of abiotic (non-living) and biotic (living) components. The biotic components are the living beings present around us.
CharacteristicKey Points
MetabolismCatabolism (breaking molecules) + Anabolism (making new molecules). Most all-inclusive characteristic of life ★
GrowthWell-orchestrated growth from birth. Non-living things (sand mounds) grow but NOT from within.
AgeingGrowth has a limited time span. Molecules, organs gradually lose effective working.
ReproductionProduce young ones (asexual or sexual). NOT all-inclusive — mules & sterile worker bees do not reproduce yet are living.
DeathAs the body loses capacity to perform metabolism, the organism dies.
Responsiveness / IrritabilityRespond to thermal, chemical or biological changes in surroundings. Unique property of living organisms.
⭐ Most Asked: Metabolism is the most all-inclusive characteristic of life. Reproduction is NOT all-inclusive because mules don't reproduce yet are alive.
📋 1.2 Herbarium
Definition A herbarium is essentially a dried plant specimen that is pressed, treated and mounted on a standard size sheet to preserve it.
  • Word herbarium (plural: herbaria) was coined by Pitton de Tournefort
  • Art of herbarium was initiated by Italian taxonomist Luca Ghini (1490–1556)
  • Herbaria are effective tools in taxonomic studies
  • It is an ex-situ conservation tool
Label (lower right corner of sheet contains) • Date and place of collection
• Detailed classification of the plant
• Ecological peculiarities and characters
• Local names and name of the collector
✅ Herbarium = Ex-situ conservation. Living plants = In-vivo. Dried/pressed specimens = Herbarium.
🌺 1.3 Botanical Gardens
Definition Places where plants of different varieties collected from different parts of the world are grown in a scientific and systematic in vivo manner.
  • Plants are labelled with scientific and common names
  • First botanical garden established in 1543 by Prof. Luca Ghini at Pisa, Italy
  • Tool for ex-situ conservation
  • Plants are living specimens grown under controlled conditions
📌 Key difference: Botanical garden = living plants grown in vivo. Herbarium = dried/pressed specimens.
🏛️ 1.4 Museum
Definition Museums are places where collections of preserved plant and animal specimens are kept.
  • Plant and animal specimens preserved in formalin (10%–40% formaldehyde) in transparent jars
  • Jars are labelled with details of the specimens
  • Larger animals (birds, mammals) are usually stuffed and preserved — this science is called Taxidermy
  • Specimens in dried form are also kept
  • Collections include shells, skeletons, insect boxes
  • Biological museums in educational institutes are reference hubs of biodiversity studies
⭐ Taxidermy = science of stuffing and preserving larger animals like birds and mammals.
🦁 1.5 Zoological Parks (Zoo)
Definition Wild animals are kept in captivity. Care is taken to provide conditions similar to their natural habitat.
  • Represents ex-situ conservation
  • A naturalist can study food habits and behavior of animals here
  • Conditions are made similar to the natural habitat of the animals
🌳 1.6 Biodiversity Parks
Definition An ecological assemblage of species that form self-sustaining communities on degraded/barren landscapes.
  • Represents in-situ conservation — organisms live in conditions closest to natural habitat
  • Example: Late Uttamrao Patil Biodiversity Park, Gureghar, Mahabaleshwar — best model for conservation of natural heritage in urban landscape
  • Helps restore biodiversity on degraded land
  • Sacred groves — plants conserved in name of holy place; recognised by IUCN
✅ Biodiversity park = In-situ conservation (in natural/restored habitat). Zoo = Ex-situ conservation (outside natural habitat).
🔒 Types of Conservation
Biodiversity Biodiversity is the degree of variation of life forms in an ecosystem. It is essential to maintain ecological stability. Population explosion and over-exploitation have resulted in loss of biodiversity at an alarming rate.
TypeMeaningExamples
In-situConservation in the natural habitat of the organism — slowing/stopping/reversing lossBiodiversity parks, sacred groves, national parks, wildlife sanctuaries
Ex-situConservation outside the natural habitatZoos, botanical gardens, herbaria, seed banks
Other Biodiversity Tools
ToolDescription
FloraPlant life occurring in a particular area at a particular time
MonographDescribes any one selected biological group in detail
ManualProvides information and keys for identification of species in a particular area
CatalogueSystematic record/listing of organisms
⚠️ Loss of one variety of organisms can affect the entire ecosystem. Biodiversity helps maintain stability.
🔑 1.7 Key (Taxonomical Aid)
Definition A key is a taxonomical aid used for classification and identification of plants and animals.
  • Keys are based on contrasting characters
  • One contrasting character gets accepted, the other rejected
  • The statement in a key is called a lead
  • Keys are normally analytical in nature
  • Help identify species in a particular area
✅ Key = based on contrasting characters. Statements in key = leads.

★★ 2 Mark Questions
  • What is metabolism? Why is it the most all-inclusive characteristic of life? 2 marks
    Metabolism = Catabolism + Anabolism. Every living organism carries out metabolism without exception. Mules (no reproduction) and brain-dead patients still perform metabolism. Reproduction is NOT all-inclusive because mules and sterile worker bees do not reproduce yet are living. Hence metabolism is the most reliable all-inclusive characteristic.
  • Distinguish between in-situ and ex-situ conservation. 2 marks
    In-situ: conservation in natural habitat; e.g. biodiversity parks, sacred groves.
    Ex-situ: conservation outside natural habitat; e.g. zoos, botanical gardens, herbaria.
    In-situ preserves natural conditions; ex-situ protects from predators/poachers.
  • What are taxonomical aids? Give examples. 2 marks
    Taxonomical aids are tools that help in identification and classification of organisms, gathered through intensive laboratory and field studies. Examples: Herbaria, Botanical gardens, Museums, Biodiversity parks, Zoological parks, Keys, Flora, Monograph, Manual, Catalogue.
  • What is a key? What are its features? 2 marks
    A key is a taxonomical aid used for classification and identification of plants and animals. Features: based on contrasting characters; one character accepted, other rejected; statements = leads; normally analytical in nature.
  • Write a note on herbarium and its importance. 2 marks
    Herbarium = dried plant specimen pressed, treated, mounted on standard sheet. Coined by Pitton de Tournefort; initiated by Luca Ghini. Label contains: date, place, classification, ecological details, local names, collector's name. Effective tool in taxonomic studies; ex-situ conservation.
★★★ 3 Mark Questions
  • Write about any three taxonomical aids used in biology. 3 marks
    1. Herbarium: Dried plant specimen pressed, treated, mounted on standard sheet. Label: date, place, classification, ecological details. Ex-situ; effective for taxonomic studies. Initiated by Luca Ghini.
    2. Botanical Garden: Plants from different parts of world grown in vivo. Labelled with scientific and common names. First at Pisa by Luca Ghini (1543). Ex-situ conservation.
    3. Museum: Preserved in formalin (10–40%). Larger animals stuffed (taxidermy). Collections include shells, skeletons, insect boxes. Reference hubs of biodiversity studies.
  • What are the characteristics of living organisms? Explain any three. 3 marks
    1. Metabolism: Catabolism + Anabolism. Most all-inclusive characteristic of life.
    2. Growth: Well-orchestrated growth from birth. Non-living things grow but not from within.
    3. Reproduction: Produce young ones (asexual/sexual). NOT all-inclusive — mules and sterile worker bees don't reproduce yet are living.

Sacred groves = in-situ; Zoo = ex-situ; Botanical garden = ex-situ.
