Section A — MCQ Answers 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by:
✔ Answer: (B) lowering activation energy
Explanation A catalyst provides an alternative reaction mechanism with lower activation energy (Ea). More molecules can now reach the lower Ea → faster reaction. Catalyst is NOT consumed; it does NOT change ΔH or equilibrium constant K.
Q.1 (ii)
Colloidal range of particle size is:
✔ Answer: (B) 1–1000 nm
Explanation True solution: particles < 1 nm (molecules/ions) Colloid: particles 1–1000 nm (1 nm to 1 μm) Suspension (coarse): particles > 1000 nm (visible under microscope, settle)
Q.1 (iii)
Molar conductance increases with dilution for:
✔ Answer: (A) strong electrolytes
Explanation Strong electrolytes (NaCl, HCl): already fully ionised; on dilution, interionic attractions decrease → ions move more freely → Λm increases (reaches Λ°m) Weak electrolytes (CH₃COOH): degree of dissociation increases with dilution → many more ions → Λm increases sharply at high dilution
Q.1 (iv)
A Grignard reagent is represented as:
✔ Answer: (B) RMgX
Explanation Grignard reagent = organomagnesium halide: RMgX (R = alkyl/aryl, X = Cl, Br, I) C₂H₅Br + Mg (dry ether) → C₂H₅MgBr (ethyl magnesium bromide) Very reactive — used for C–C bond formation. React with CO₂, aldehydes, ketones.
Q.1 (v)
DNA base pairing A = T uses:
✔ Answer: (A) 2 hydrogen bonds
Explanation A (adenine) = T (thymine): 2 hydrogen bonds G (guanine) ≡ C (cytosine): 3 hydrogen bonds (stronger, hence GC-rich DNA has higher melting point) This specific base pairing (Chargaff's rules: A=T, G=C) gives DNA its double helix structure.
Q.1 (vi)
The monomer of natural rubber is:
✔ Answer: (C) isoprene
Explanation Natural rubber = polyisoprene (cis-1,4-polyisoprene). Monomer = isoprene (2-methylbuta-1,3-diene) nCH₂=C(CH₃)–CH=CH₂ → [–CH₂–C(CH₃)=CH–CH₂–]_n (natural rubber) Neoprene = polychloroprene (monomer: chloroprene = 2-chloro-1,3-butadiene)
Q.1 (vii)
Excess AgNO₃ with [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl gives:
✔ Answer: (A) 1 mole AgCl
Explanation [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl → [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ + Cl⁻ (only the outer sphere Cl⁻ precipitates) The two Cl inside the coordination sphere do NOT precipitate with AgNO₃ Cl⁻(aq) + AgNO₃ → AgCl↓ + NO₃⁻ → only 1 mole of AgCl precipitates
Q.1 (viii)
Baeyer's test uses:
✔ Answer: (B) alkaline KMnO₄
Explanation Baeyer's test (test for unsaturation): uses cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄ (purple) Alkenes decolourise KMnO₄: CH₂=CH₂ + [O] → ethylene glycol (diol) Purple KMnO₄ → decolourised (or brown MnO₂ precipitate) → confirms double bond
Q.1 (ix)
Zwitter ions are formed by:
✔ Answer: (C) amino acids
Explanation Amino acids have both –NH₂ (basic) and –COOH (acidic) groups in the same molecule. In aqueous solution: –NH₂ accepts H⁺ → –NH₃⁺ and –COOH loses H⁺ → –COO⁻ Resulting species has both + and – charges simultaneously = zwitter ion (dipolar ion)
Q.1 (x)
When an acid chloride reacts with water, it gives:
✔ Answer: (B) carboxylic acid
Explanation RCOCl + H₂O → RCOOH + HCl Hydrolysis of acid chloride (acyl chloride) gives carboxylic acid. Vigorous reaction with water — HCl fumes produced.

Q.2 (i)
What is a photochemical reaction? Give one example.
DefinitionA photochemical reaction is a chemical reaction that is initiated or driven by absorption of light (photons). The energy of absorbed photons provides the activation energy for the reaction.
ExamplePhotosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + hν → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Halogenation: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (UV light initiates free radical chain)
Q.2 (ii)
Define adsorption isotherm.
DefinitionAn adsorption isotherm is a graph showing the relationship between the amount of gas adsorbed per unit mass of adsorbent (x/m) and the pressure (P) of the gas at constant temperature.
TypesFreundlich isotherm: x/m = kP^(1/n) (empirical)  |  Langmuir isotherm: x/m = abP/(1+bP) (theoretical, monolayer)
Q.2 (iii)
Define mole fraction. How does it differ from molarity?
Mole Fractionx_A = n_A/(n_A + n_B) — dimensionless; does NOT change with temperature
MolarityM = n/V(L) — units: mol/L; CHANGES with temperature (∵ volume changes)
Key DifferenceMole fraction is temperature-independent; molarity decreases as T increases (volume expands)
Q.2 (iv)
State Markovnikov's rule.
StatementIn the addition of HX (or similar unsymmetrical reagent) to an unsymmetrical alkene, the H adds to the carbon that already has more hydrogen atoms, and X adds to the carbon with fewer hydrogen atoms (more substituted carbon).
ExampleCH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CHBr–CH₃ (2-bromopropane, Markovnikov product)
ReasonMore substituted carbocation (2° or 3°) is more stable → Markovnikov product favoured
Q.2 (v)
What is the isoelectric point of an amino acid?
DefinitionThe isoelectric point (pI) is the pH at which an amino acid exists predominantly as a zwitter ion (equal concentration of positive and negative charges; net charge = 0). At pI, the amino acid has minimum solubility and does not migrate in an electric field.
ValueFor most neutral amino acids: pI ≈ 6.0 (between pKa1 and pKa2). Acidic amino acids: pI < 7, Basic: pI > 7.
Q.2 (vi)
What is an atactic polymer? Give one example.
DefinitionAtactic polymer: substituents are arranged randomly (no regular pattern) along the polymer chain. These polymers are amorphous (non-crystalline) and have lower melting points.
ExampleAtactic polypropylene — amorphous, sticky, used as an adhesive
Q.2 (vii)
Define spectrochemical series.
DefinitionThe spectrochemical series is the arrangement of ligands in order of their increasing crystal field splitting energy (Δ) that they cause when coordinated to the same metal ion.
SeriesI⁻ < Br⁻ < S²⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ < CO
(weak field) → → → → → → → → → → → (strong field)
Q.2 (viii)
What is meant by osmotic pressure?
DefinitionOsmotic pressure (π) is the minimum excess pressure that must be applied to the solution side to prevent the flow of solvent (osmosis) from the pure solvent side through a semipermeable membrane.
Formulaπ = CRT   C = molar concentration, R = gas constant, T = absolute temperature (K)
Q.2 (ix)
What is catabolism? Give one example.
DefinitionCatabolism is the metabolic process of breaking down complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy (usually stored as ATP).
ExampleCellular respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + 38 ATP (glucose breakdown)
Q.2 (x)
Define recrystallisation. Why is it used?
DefinitionRecrystallisation is a technique for purifying a solid compound by dissolving it in a hot solvent and then allowing it to slowly crystallise upon cooling. Pure crystals separate; impurities remain in solution.
Why usedTo obtain a pure crystalline solid from a crude product. The solubility difference between the compound and its impurities is exploited.
Q.2 (xi)
What is auto-catalysis? Give one example.
DefinitionAuto-catalysis is a reaction in which one of the products of the reaction itself acts as a catalyst for the same reaction, causing the reaction rate to increase as the reaction proceeds (sigmoidal rate-time curve).
ExamplePermanganate oxidation of oxalic acid: MnO₄⁻ + H₂C₂O₄ → Mn²⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O. Mn²⁺ (product) catalyses the reaction → rate increases initially.
Q.2 (xii)
Define coordination entity with an example.
DefinitionA coordination entity is a central metal atom or ion bonded to a definite number of ligands (neutral molecules or ions) through coordinate (dative) bonds. It is enclosed in square brackets in formulae.
Example[Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ — Fe²⁺ is central atom; CN⁻ are ligands; coordination number = 6; the entire species in brackets is the coordination entity.

Q.3 (i)
Derive the integrated rate law for first-order reaction. Hence derive the half-life formula.
Rate Law −d[A]/dt = k[A] → ∫d[A]/[A] = −k∫dt ln[A] = −kt + ln[A]₀ → [A] = [A]₀e^(−kt)
Half-life At t = t₁/₂: [A] = [A]₀/2 → ln([A]₀/2) = −kt₁/₂ + ln[A]₀ −ln2 = −kt₁/₂ → t₁/₂ = ln2/k = 0.693/k
Result
k = (2.303/t)log([A]₀/[A])  |  t₁/₂ = 0.693/k (independent of [A]₀)
Q.3 (ii)
Compare Freundlich and Langmuir adsorption isotherms.
Freundlich Isotherm x/m = k·P^(1/n) (1/n = 0 to 1) Empirical — no theoretical basis. log(x/m) vs log P is linear. Fails at high pressure (no saturation).
Langmuir Isotherm x/m = abP/(1+bP) → at high P: x/m = a (saturated monolayer) Based on kinetic theory — assumes monolayer, uniform surface, no interaction between adsorbed molecules. More accurate.
Q.3 (iii)
Explain osmosis and derive the Van't Hoff equation for dilute solutions.
OsmosisSpontaneous flow of solvent from lower concentration to higher concentration through a semipermeable membrane, driven by the difference in chemical potential.
Van't Hoff Equation By analogy with ideal gas: in dilute solutions, solute particles behave like ideal gas molecules PV = nRT → πV = nRT → π = (n/V)RT = CRT Van't Hoff equation: π = CRT (valid for dilute, ideal solutions)
Q.3 (iv)
Explain Markovnikov's addition of HBr to propene — mechanism and product.
Mechanism Step 1: H⁺ (from HBr) adds to CH₂= end of propene (more H atoms) → forms 2° carbocation: CH₃–CH⁺–CH₃ (more stable than 1° CH₃–CH₂–CH₂⁺) Step 2: Br⁻ attacks 2° carbocation → 2-bromopropane CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CHBr–CH₃ (Markovnikov product)
Product
2-bromopropane (major product, Markovnikov's addition)

Q.3 (vi)
Distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary amines using Hinsberg's test.
ReagentHinsberg's reagent = benzenesulphonyl chloride (C₆H₅SO₂Cl), in NaOH solution
Primary (RNH₂)Reacts → sulphonamide (R–NHSO₂C₆H₅). This has acidic H on N → dissolves in NaOH (sodium salt forms). On acidification → precipitate.
Secondary (R₂NH)Reacts → N,N-disubstituted sulphonamide. No acidic H → does NOT dissolve in NaOH → insoluble precipitate in alkaline medium.
Tertiary (R₃N)Does NOT react with Hinsberg's reagent (no N–H available for reaction) → forms soluble hydrochloride salt or no reaction in some conditions.
Q.3 (vii)
Derive the rate expression for NO₂ + CO reaction from given mechanism.
Mechanism Step 1 (slow, RDS): NO₂ + NO₂ → NO₃ + NO Step 2 (fast): NO₃ + CO → NO₂ + CO₂
Rate Expression Rate is determined by the slow step (rate-determining step): Rate = k[NO₂][NO₂] = k[NO₂]² Overall reaction: 2NO₂ + CO → NO₃ + NO + CO₂ → simplifies to NO₂ + CO → NO + CO₂ But RATE is second order in NO₂ (from mechanism, not from overall balanced equation)
Answer
Rate = k[NO₂]²   (second order overall; first order in each NO₂ molecule)
Q.3 (viii)
Explain bonding in [Ni(CO)₄] — hybridisation, geometry and type of bonding.
Oxidation StateNi is in 0 oxidation state (CO is neutral). Ni⁰: [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² → electrons reconfigure to [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s⁰
HybridisationNi⁰ uses sp³ hybrid orbitals (4 orbitals). Four CO ligands donate lone pairs → sp³ hybridisation.
GeometryTetrahedral (sp³ = 4 bonds, tetrahedral arrangement)
Bonding σ bond: CO lone pair → Ni (donor) π back-bonding: Ni filled d-orbitals → CO empty π* antibonding orbitals (synergic bonding) Makes the complex extra stable (explains why Ni⁰ readily forms carbonyl complex)

Q.4 (iii)
Explain crystal field theory for octahedral and tetrahedral complexes with energy level diagrams.
Octahedral 6 ligands approach along ±x, ±y, ±z axes. d_z², d_x²-y² (e_g) face ligands → raised. d_xy, d_xz, d_yz (t₂g) → lowered. Energy splitting: t₂g: −2/5Δ₀  |  e_g: +3/5Δ₀  |  Net: Δ₀ (crystal field splitting)
Tetrahedral 4 ligands approach along alternate corners of a cube. None point directly at d orbitals. d_xy, d_xz, d_yz (t₂) raised; d_z², d_x²-y² (e) lowered (reversed from octahedral) Δt = (4/9)Δ₀   (smaller splitting → generally high spin)
ApplicationsColour of complexes (absorbed energy = hν = Δ), magnetism (unpaired e in high/low spin), spectrochemical series

Q.4 (vi)
Explain Raoult's law for non-volatile solute. Derive the expression for vapour pressure lowering.
Raoult's Law p_A = p°_A × x_A = p°_A × (1 − x_B) = p°_A − p°_A x_B p°_A − p_A = p°_A × x_B (vapour pressure lowering = Δp)
Relative Lowering Δp/p°_A = x_B = n_B/(n_A + n_B) ≈ n_B/n_A (for dilute solutions)
Significance Relative lowering of vapour pressure is a colligative property — depends only on mole fraction of solute. Δp/p°_A = W_B × M_A / (M_B × W_A)   (for dilute solutions: n_A >> n_B)

Q.5 (ii)
(a) Nernst equation and concentration cells. (b) EMF for Zn|Zn²⁺(0.1M)||H⁺(1M)|H₂.
Nernst Equation E = E° − (RT/nF) × ln Q = E° − (0.0592/n) × log Q   (at 25°C) Q = reaction quotient (concentrations of products/reactants to their stoichiometric powers)
Concentration CellTwo electrodes of same metal in different concentrations. E° = 0 (same electrodes). EMF comes purely from concentration difference: E = (0.0592/n) × log([low conc.]/[high conc.])
Numerical Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (anode), 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ (cathode), E°Zn = −0.76V, E°H = 0V, n=2 E° = E°_cathode − E°_anode = 0 − (−0.76) = +0.76 V E = 0.76 − (0.0592/2) × log([Zn²⁺]/[H⁺]²) = 0.76 − 0.0296 × log(0.1/1) = 0.76 − 0.0296 × (−1)
Answer
E = 0.76 + 0.0296 = 0.7896 V ≈ 0.79 V
Q.5 (v)
(a) Derive expression for elevation of boiling point. (b) Find b.p. of Ca(OH)₂ solution.
Derivation Adding non-volatile solute → vapour pressure decreases (Raoult's law) Solution must be heated to higher T to reach 1 atm → boiling point elevation ΔTb ΔTb = Kb × m   where Kb = ebullioscopic constant = RT²_b M_A/1000L_v For water: Kb = 0.52 K·kg/mol
Numerical Ca(OH)₂: M = 74, w = 7.4 g, w_water = 500 g, Kb = 0.52, i = 3 (Ca(OH)₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻) m = (7.4 × 1000)/(74 × 500) = 7400/37000 = 0.2 mol/kg ΔTb = i × Kb × m = 3 × 0.52 × 0.2 = 0.312°C
Answer
Boiling point = 100 + 0.312 = 100.312°C
