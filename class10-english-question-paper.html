SECTION A — READING SKILLS 20 Marks
Q.1 Read the following passage and do all the activities. [12 Marks]
Read and answer (Pre-reading activity — A₁):
Name one person in history who showed great courage and changed the world.

Passage:
Courage is not the absence of fear. It is the decision that something is more important than fear. Throughout history, ordinary people have done extraordinary things simply because they believed in what was right. A young student once stood before an examination board, trembling with fear, yet she answered every question calmly. She had prepared not just with books but with determination and self-belief.

Similarly, a young warrior in France, centuries ago, heard voices that she believed were guiding her. Despite being mocked and doubted, she led armies and changed the course of a nation's history. She was imprisoned, tried, and sentenced — yet she did not give up her beliefs. Her story reminds us that true courage means standing up for what you believe, even at great personal cost.

Today, courage takes different forms. A student who speaks the truth in class, a child who stands up to a bully, a young person who chooses the right path over the popular one — all of these are acts of courage. Courage is not about being fearless. It is about acting rightly even when you are afraid.

Q.6 Read the following extract and do the activities: [10 Marks]
Extract based on: "The Thief's Story" (Unit 1.2) — original passage context
He was about fifteen, with a thin, honest-looking face. He had been following me for the past ten minutes, watching me closely as I made my way through the crowded bazaar. At last, he caught up with me.

"I know a boy who can help you carry your bags," he said cheerfully. "His name is Deepak, and he is very reliable."

I looked at him carefully. There was something about his eyes — they were alert, intelligent, and yet oddly watchful. I suspected he was talking about himself, but I let it pass.

"What makes you so trustworthy?" I asked. He didn't hesitate. "I never steal from people who trust me," he said quietly. It was a strange answer — honest in a strange way. But I needed help, and so I hired him. Over the days that followed, I realised he was skilled, quick, and surprisingly careful with my belongings.

Then one evening, I gave him a task that tested everything. I handed him a bag of money and told him to deposit it at the bank. He ran off down the street — and did not return that evening.
