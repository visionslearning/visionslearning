Class 11 Geography · Chapter 1

🌍 Earth Movements

Notes · Key Points · Important Q&A · Maharashtra Board

⭐ Key Points at a Glance

  • Earth movements = Internal processes → Slow movements (tectonic) + Sudden movements (earthquake, volcano)
  • Slow movements: Vertical (Epeirogenic — continent building) + Horizontal (Orogenic — mountain building)
  • Folding (compressional forces) → Fold mountains: Himalayas, Alps, Rockies, Andes
  • Faulting (tensional forces) → Block mountains (horst) + Rift valleys (graben)
  • Types of folds: Symmetrical, Asymmetrical, Overturned, Recumbent, Isoclinal
  • Types of faults: Normal, Reverse, Tear (Strike-slip), Thrust
  • Earthquake: Focus (seismic focus) → Epicentre → Seismic waves (P, S, L waves)
  • Shadow zone: 105°–140° from epicentre = no P or S waves; beyond 105° = no S waves
  • Richter scale = magnitude (energy); Mercalli scale = intensity (damage: I to XII)
  • Volcano: Opening in earth's surface ejecting lava, gases, solid material
  • Volcanic landforms: Lava dome, Lava plateau, Caldera, Crater lake, Cinder cone, Composite cone, Volcanic plug
  • Ring of Fire = Circum-Pacific Belt — most active earthquake and volcano zone
  • India seismic zones: Zone I (Least risk) to Zone V (Very high risk)
1

Earth Movements — Introduction & Classification

Landforms (hills, mountains, plateaus, valleys) are shaped by internal and external forces. Internal processes are classified as:

TypeNatureExamples
Slow MovementsVery slow; cannot be observed; effects seen over millions of yearsMountain building, continental distribution
Sudden MovementsEffects within seconds or hours; episodicEarthquakes, Volcanic eruptions

Evidences of earth movements:

  • After Great Tsunami 2004 — coast around Sumatra rose by few centimetres
  • Formation of three Himalayan ranges — Siwaliks, Middle Himalayas, Greater Himalayas
  • Iceland volcanic eruption (November 1963) — new island appeared above sea surface
  • Megapode Island lost after tsunami
  • Near Mazgaon Dockyard, Mumbai — forest-covered land got drowned (trunks still found)
  • Kachchh earthquake (June 16, 1819) — 1550 sq.km land raised = "Allah's Bund"
2

Slow Movements — Vertical & Horizontal

Tectonic movements = Earth movements resulting from internal forces. Based on direction → Vertical + Horizontal.

1. Vertical (Epeirogenic) Movements

  • Due to internal forces — movement towards or away from Earth's centre
  • Raise or subside large portions of crust
  • Raised crust above sea level → formation of continents → called continent-building movements
  • Also cause formation of extensive plateaus
  • Slow, influence huge area; NOT related to tension or pressure — cause upliftment or subsidence

2. Horizontal (Orogenic) Movements

  • Work in horizontal direction → produce compression or tension in rock strata
  • Lead to folds or cracks → form mountains → called mountain-building movements
  • Faster than epeirogenic; lesser extent
  • Produce either folds or faults → fold mountains or block mountains
Force TypeDirectionEffectLandform
TensionalForces move AWAY from each otherRuptures, cracks, fractures, faultsRift valleys, Block mountains
CompressionalForces move TOWARDS each otherFolding and faultingFold mountains
3

Folding — Types of Folds & Fold Mountains

Folding: When compressional forces are applied to ductile/flexible rocks, they bend into folds instead of breaking. Soft and elastic rocks are most affected.

Parts of a fold: Limbs (both sides) + Axial plane (divides fold into two) + Axis (may be vertical, inclined or horizontal)

  • Anticline: Limbs slope downward, central portion raised up
  • Syncline: Limbs slope towards each other, central part at lower elevation

Types of Folds:

TypeCharacteristics
SymmetricalAxial plane is vertical; limbs inclined at same angle
AsymmetricalAxial plane is inclined; limbs at different angles
OverturnedOne limb lies above the other; limbs slope unequally in same direction
RecumbentAxial plane almost horizontal; one limb lies over other horizontally
IsoclinalLimbs slope in same direction with same amount; axial plane may be vertical/inclined/horizontal

Fold Mountains:

  • Old fold mountains (over 200 million years): Aravalis (highest peak 1722m AMSL), Urals (Russia), Appalachians (USA)
  • Young fold mountains (10–25 million years): Rockies, Himalayas (Mt. Everest 8848m AMSL), Alps, Andes
4

Faulting — Types of Faults, Block Mountains & Rift Valleys

Faulting: When rocks near Earth's surface (not under high pressure; too rigid to fold) break under tectonic force → fractures/ruptures/faults. Rock masses move relative to each other along the fault plane.

Types of Faults:

TypeDescription
Normal faultPortion slides DOWN along fault plane; exposed fault plane faces sky
Reverse faultPortion is thrown UPWARD relative to other side; fault plane faces ground
Tear (Strike-slip) faultRock strata move horizontally along fault plane; no vertical displacement
Thrust faultOne side detaches and moves OVER the other side; fault plane angle < 45°

Block Mountain (Horst):

  • Block enclosed by faults rises above → becomes horst/block mountain
  • Steep faulted edges; flat top (no peak)
  • Examples: Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany), Satpuras (Maharashtra), Meghalaya Plateau (India)

Rift Valley (Graben):

  • Central portion subsides between two adjacent faults → graben/rift valley
  • Steep walls formed by fault planes
  • Examples: African Rift Valley, Narmada Valley (India), Tapi Valley (India)
5

Earthquake — Focus, Epicentre, Seismic Waves, Shadow Zone

Earthquake: Stress accumulates in rock strata → released suddenly → produces energy waves → earth's crust shakes.
  • Seismic focus: Point inside earth where stress is released
  • Epicentre: Point directly above focus on earth's surface; maximum intensity here
  • Intensity decreases with distance from epicentre
  • Seismograph: Instrument that records earthquake waves
  • Isoseismal line: Imaginary line connecting places of uniform earthquake intensity

Seismic Waves:

WaveNatureMedium
P-waves (Primary)Longitudinal/compressional wavesPass through ALL mediums (solid, liquid, gas); experience refraction
S-waves (Secondary)Transverse/shear wavesPass ONLY through solid medium
L-waves (Surface/Love waves)Surface wavesTravel along Earth's surface; most destructive

Shadow Zone:

Shadow zone = area on Earth's surface where earthquake waves of a particular earthquake are NOT recorded.
  • Within 105° from epicentre → both P and S waves recorded
  • Between 105° and 140° from epicentre → shadow zone for both P and S waves
  • Beyond 140° from epicentre → P waves recorded, S waves NOT
  • Entire zone beyond 105° does NOT receive S waves
  • L-waves have NO shadow zone — they travel on surface everywhere

Measuring Earthquakes:

ScaleMeasuresToolUnits
Richter ScaleMagnitude (energy released)Seismograph (logarithmic)<2.0 to 10.0+
Mercalli ScaleIntensity (observed effects)ObservationI (not felt) to XII (total destruction)

Note: Energy in magnitude 5 earthquake = 32 times more than magnitude 4 earthquake.

Causes of Earthquakes:

  • Volcanicity: Associated with volcanic eruptions (generally shallow)
  • Tectonic movements: Movement of large and small tectonic plates along divergent and convergent margins
  • Anthropogenic causes: Atomic explosions, large construction, drilling, blasting, large-scale mining

Seismic Zones in India:

ZoneDegree of RiskExamples
ILeastParts of stable peninsula
IILowParts of South India
IIIModerateParts of Maharashtra, Gujarat
IVHighParts of Himachal Pradesh, UP, Delhi
VVery HighKashmir, Assam, Andaman & Nicobar, parts of Gujarat

Volcanoes — Types, Materials & Volcanic Landforms

Volcano: An opening in Earth's surface through which gases, molten lava and solid material are ejected from the upper mantle onto the surface.

Classification by origin of eruption:

  • Eruption through cones (Central/conical volcano)
  • Eruption through fissures (Fissure volcano)

Classification by activity:

TypeDescriptionExample
Active volcanoErupts frequently or continuouslyStromboli (Italy), Mauna Loa (Hawaii)
Dormant volcanoNot erupted recently but may erupt againMt. Vesuvius (Italy), Fujiyama (Japan)
Extinct/Dead volcanoNo eruption expected; crater filled with waterLonar Lake (Maharashtra)

Volcanic Materials:

TypeDescription
Liquid — Magma/LavaMagma (below surface) → Lava (on surface). Acidic lava: high silica, thick, slow-moving, high melting point. Basic lava: low silica, fluid, flows far, low melting point.
SolidVolcanic dust (very fine), Ash (small particles), Breccias (angular fragments), Volcanic bombs (solidified lava fragments)
GaseousInflammable gases; form cauliflower-shaped cloud over crater during eruption

Lava PlateauLava spreads from fissure volcano over large areaDeccan Trap, India

Composite ConeAlternate layers of lava AND cinder; symmetricalMt. St. Helens (USA), Fujiyama (Japan)

Distribution Belts of Earthquakes and Volcanoes:

🔁 Quick Revision — One Liners

  • Tectonic movements → Vertical (Epeirogenic) + Horizontal (Orogenic)
  • Tensional forces → pull away → faults → rift valleys + block mountains
  • Compressional forces → push together → folds → fold mountains
  • Fold mountains: Himalayas, Alps, Rockies, Andes (young); Aravalis, Urals, Appalachians (old)
  • Block mountains: Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany), Satpuras (India)
  • Rift valleys: African Rift Valley, Narmada Valley, Tapi Valley (India)
  • Anticline = limbs slope downward, centre raised; Syncline = limbs slope toward each other, centre lower
  • Seismic focus = inside earth; Epicentre = directly above on surface
  • P-waves: all mediums; S-waves: solids only; L-waves: surface only (most destructive)
  • Shadow zone: 105°–140° from epicentre; L-waves have NO shadow zone
  • Richter = magnitude (energy); Mercalli = intensity (I to XII)
  • Magma = below surface; Lava = on surface; Acidic lava = thick + slow; Basic lava = fluid + fast
  • Deccan Trap = lava plateau; Lonar Lake (Maharashtra) = crater lake
  • Ring of Fire = Circum-Pacific Belt; Cotopaxi = highest volcanic mountain
  • India Zone V = Very high seismic risk (Kashmir, Assam, A&N Islands)

Q3
Distinguish between syncline and anticline.★★
Show ▾
  • Anticline: Limbs (sides of fold) slope downward from the centre. Central portion is raised up. Forms a hill or ridge shape.
  • Syncline: Limbs slope towards each other and the central part is at lower elevation. Forms a valley or trough shape.
They alternate with each other in folded terrain. Anticlines form the ridges and synclines form the valleys between ridges.
Q4
Explain different types of faults with examples.★★★
Show ▾
Types of faults:
  • Normal fault: A portion of land slides DOWN along the fault plane. The exposed portion of the fault plane faces the sky. Forms rift valleys. E.g., Narmada Valley, Tapi Valley.
  • Reverse fault: A portion of land is thrown UPWARD relative to the other side. The fault plane faces the ground. Shortens the crust horizontally.
  • Tear fault (Strike-slip fault): Rock strata on either side of the fault plane do NOT have vertical displacement. Movement is HORIZONTAL along the fault plane. E.g., San Andreas Fault, California.
  • Thrust fault: The portion on one side of the fault plane gets detached and moves OVER the land on the other side. The angle of fault plane is less than 45°. E.g., in Himalayan region.

Q6
Explain the concept of shadow zone of an earthquake.★★★
Show ▾
Shadow zone = A specific area on Earth's surface where the waves of a particular earthquake are NOT recorded by seismographs.
  • For each earthquake there is a different shadow zone
  • Seismographs within 105° from the epicentre receive BOTH P-waves and S-waves
  • A zone between 105° and 140° from the epicentre is the shadow zone for BOTH P and S waves
  • Seismographs beyond 140° from epicentre receive P-waves but NOT S-waves
  • The entire zone beyond 105° does not receive S-waves (because S-waves cannot pass through the liquid outer core)
  • The shadow zone of P-waves appears as a band (105°–140°) around the Earth
  • L-waves have NO shadow zone — they travel along Earth's surface and reach everywhere
Significance: Study of shadow zones helped scientists understand Earth's internal structure — the presence of liquid outer core was inferred from S-wave shadow zone.
Q7
Explain with examples, different types of landforms produced by volcanic eruption.★★★
Show ▾
Volcanic Landforms:
  • Lava Dome: Magma solidifies around the volcanic mouth. Acidic lava → high steep dome; Basic lava → broad flat dome.
  • Lava Plateau: Lava from fissure volcano spreads over large area and solidifies into flat plateau. E.g., Deccan Trap, India (formed millions of years ago).
  • Caldera: Large deep depression (up to 10 km wide, hundreds of metres deep) formed after a massive eruption relieves pressure. Smaller calderas = craters. May turn into lakes.
  • Crater Lake: Funnel-shaped crater of an extinct volcano fills with rainwater. E.g., Lonar Lake, Maharashtra.
  • Volcanic Plug: Lava solidifies inside the volcanic neck (pipe), forming a hard plug that remains after surrounding rock erodes.
  • Cinder Cone: Ash, cinder and breccias deposited around volcanic mouth form a conical hill with steep slopes. E.g., Mt. Nuovo, Italy.
  • Composite Cone: Built of alternate layers of lava and cinder — symmetrical shape. E.g., Mt. St. Helens (USA), Fujiyama (Japan).
Q8
Write a note on volcanic materials.★★
Show ▾
Three types of volcanic materials:
  • 1. Liquid material (Magma/Lava):
    • Magma = molten rock below surface; Lava = when it appears on surface
    • Acidic lava — high silica content; high melting point; thick, viscous; moves slowly; forms steep domes
    • Basic lava — low silica content; low melting point; fluid; flows over long distances; forms broad plateaus
  • 2. Solid material:
    • Volcanic dust — very fine particles
    • Ash — small solid particles
    • Breccias — solid angular fragments
    • Volcanic bombs — lava solidifies in air into fragments before reaching ground
  • 3. Gaseous material: Various inflammable gases form a cauliflower-shaped cloud over the crater during eruption. These gases produce flames.

Q10
Give geographical reasons: People living in the Himalayas are more vulnerable to earthquakes.★★
Show ▾
People in the Himalayas are more vulnerable to earthquakes because:
  • The Himalayas are young fold mountains formed by the collision of the Indo-Australian and Eurasian tectonic plates — this collision still continues
  • The Himalayan region lies at a convergent plate boundary — where maximum tectonic stress is generated
  • India is still moving northward pushing under the Eurasian plate — causing frequent release of stress
  • The region falls in Seismic Zone IV and V (High to Very High risk) in India's seismic zonation
  • Earthquakes in Uttar Kashi and Assam are well-known examples of Himalayan tectonic earthquakes
Q11
Give geographical reasons: Extinct conical volcanoes often form crater lakes.★★
Show ▾
  • When a conical volcano becomes extinct, volcanic activity stops permanently
  • The top of the volcano has a funnel-shaped depression (crater) that was the vent of the volcano
  • Since there is no more eruption, the crater is no longer active
  • Rainwater, groundwater and snowmelt gradually accumulate in this funnel-shaped depression
  • Over time, the crater fills with water to form a crater lake
  • The steep walls of the crater act as natural boundaries retaining the water
  • E.g., Lonar Lake, Maharashtra, India is a crater lake
Q12
Give geographical reasons: L-waves do not have a shadow zone.★★★
Show ▾
  • L-waves (Love/Surface waves) travel entirely along the surface of the Earth
  • They do NOT travel through the interior of the Earth (unlike P and S waves which travel through the Earth's interior)
  • Since they travel along the surface, they are not affected by the Earth's internal structure (core or mantle boundaries)
  • P and S waves have shadow zones because they travel through the interior and get refracted/blocked by the liquid outer core
  • L-waves travel along the Earth's curved surface and can reach all points on the surface regardless of the location of the epicentre
  • Therefore, L-waves have no shadow zone
Q13
Give geographical reasons: Soft rocks form folds while hard rocks form faults.★★
Show ▾
  • Soft/ductile/flexible rocks (deep in the crust, under high confining pressure) can bend and deform without breaking when compressional forces are applied slowly
  • They respond to tectonic forces by changing shape (bending) → forming folds and fold mountains
  • Hard/brittle/rigid rocks near the Earth's surface (under low confining pressure) cannot bend — they are too rigid
  • When tectonic force exceeds their strength, they break abruptly along fracture planes → forming faults, block mountains, and rift valleys
  • The depth, temperature, and pressure determine whether a rock behaves as ductile or brittle
Q14
What is the Ring of Fire? Name four volcanoes found in it.★★★
Show ▾
Ring of Fire (Circum-Pacific Belt) is a belt of active volcanoes and frequent earthquakes surrounding the Pacific Ocean. It includes the eastern and western coastal areas of the Pacific Ocean.
  • It is the most active volcanic and earthquake zone on Earth
  • It contains most of the world's high volcanic cones and volcanic mountains
  • Named "Ring of Fire" because of the numerous active volcanoes forming a ring around the Pacific
Four volcanoes in the Ring of Fire:
  • Cotopaxi (Ecuador) — highest active volcanic mountain in the world
  • Fujiyama (Japan)
  • Mt. St. Helens (Washington, USA)
  • Mayon (Philippines)
  • Also: Pinatubo (Philippines), Paricutin (Mexico)
Q15
Identify the correct correlation for the following: A: South-East Asia, Japan and Pacific islands are most vulnerable to earthquakes. R: They are located in the Ring of Fire.★★
Show ▾
Answer: Option 3 — Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A.

Explanation:
  • A is correct — South-East Asia, Japan and Pacific island nations experience frequent and severe earthquakes and volcanic eruptions
  • R is correct — These areas are located along the Circum-Pacific Belt (Ring of Fire) — a highly active tectonic zone where multiple plate boundaries (convergent and divergent) exist
  • R correctly explains A — their location on the Ring of Fire (convergent plate boundaries, subduction zones) is the direct cause of their high vulnerability to earthquakes and volcanic eruptions
