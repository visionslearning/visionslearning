Q.2 (i)
Ch 1VSA 1M
State the theorem of parallel axes.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
The moment of inertia of an object about any axis is equal to its moment of inertia about a parallel axis through the centre of mass plus the product of its mass and the square of the perpendicular distance between the two axes.
I_O = I_C + Mh²
Q.2 (ii)
Ch 1VSA 1M
Write the formula for the minimum speed at the top of a vertical circle.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
At the topmost point, for minimum speed the tension T = 0. Gravity provides the centripetal force:
v_min (at top) = √(rg)
Corresponding minimum speed at bottom: v_min (at bottom) = √(5rg)
Q.2 (iii)
Ch 1VSA 1M
Define radius of gyration.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
Radius of gyration (K): The distance from the axis of rotation at which the entire mass of the body must be concentrated so that its moment of inertia is the same as the actual moment of inertia.
I = MK² → K = √(I/M)
Unit: metre (m)
Q.2 (iv)
Ch 1VSA 1M
State the law of conservation of angular momentum.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
If the net external torque acting on a system is zero, the total angular momentum of the system remains constant.
If τ_ext = 0, then L = Iω = constant
Example: I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂ (ballet dancer spinning faster when arms pulled in)
Q.2 (v)
Ch 7VSA 1M
State Huygens' principle.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
Every point on a wavefront acts as a secondary source of light and emits spherical secondary wavelets in all directions with the same speed as the original wave. The new wavefront at any later instant is the common tangent (envelope) to all these secondary wavelets.
Q.2 (vi)
Ch 7VSA 1M
What are coherent sources of light?
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
Two sources of light are said to be coherent if they emit light waves of the same frequency, same wavelength, and have a constant phase difference. Coherent sources are essential for producing sustained interference patterns.
Q.2 (vii)
Ch 7VSA 1M
State Malus' law.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
When plane polarised light is incident on an analyser, the intensity of transmitted light is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle (θ) between the plane of polarisation and the transmission axis of the analyser.
I = I₀ cos²θ
Q.2 (viii)
Ch 7VSA 1M
What is a wavefront? Name its types.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer
Wavefront: The locus of all points in a medium which are in the same phase of vibration at a given instant.
Types:
  • Spherical wavefront — from a point source
  • Cylindrical wavefront — from a line source
  • Plane wavefront — from a source very far away

Q.3
Ch 12M
Distinguish between centripetal force and centrifugal force.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
  • Centripetal force: Real force directed towards the centre of circular path. It is the resultant of all real forces acting on the body. Example: tension, friction, gravity providing centripetal force.
  • Centrifugal force: Pseudo (fictitious) force experienced in a rotating (non-inertial) frame. Directed away from the centre. Magnitude = mω²r. It is not a real force but can be measured.
Q.4
Ch 12M
State the theorem of perpendicular axes. Write its limitations.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
Statement: The moment of inertia of a laminar (2D) object about an axis perpendicular to its plane is equal to the sum of its moments of inertia about two mutually perpendicular axes in its plane, all three axes being concurrent.
I_z = I_x + I_y
Limitation: This theorem is applicable only to laminar (flat, 2D) objects like ring, disc, square plate etc. It cannot be applied to 3D objects like sphere, cylinder etc.

Q.8

What is interference of light? State the conditions for constructive and destructive interference.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
Interference: The phenomenon of superposition of two coherent light waves producing alternating bright (constructive) and dark (destructive) regions on a screen.

Constructive interference (bright fringe):
Path difference Δ = nλ    (n = 0, ±1, ±2 …)
Waves are in phase → amplitude doubles → intensity = 4I₀

Destructive interference (dark fringe):
Path difference Δ = (2n−1)λ/2    (n = 1, 2, 3 …)
Waves are out of phase → amplitude = 0 → intensity = 0

Q.10
Ch 72M
What is polarisation of light? What does it prove about the nature of light?
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)
Polarisation: The phenomenon of restricting the vibration of light waves to a single plane (plane of polarisation) is called polarisation.
Unpolarised light: Vibrations in all planes perpendicular to direction of propagation.
Plane polarised light: Vibrations confined to only one plane.

Conclusion about nature of light: Polarisation is possible only for transverse waves. The fact that light can be polarised proves that light is a transverse wave. Longitudinal waves (like sound) cannot be polarised.

Q.14
Ch 72M
Distinguish between interference and diffraction of light. (Any two points)
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (2M)

Q.15
Ch 13M
State and prove the theorem of parallel axes for moment of inertia.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Statement: The moment of inertia of an object about any axis is equal to the sum of its moment of inertia about a parallel axis passing through the centre of mass, and the product of mass and square of the perpendicular distance between the two axes.
I_O = I_C + Mh²
Proof: Consider a particle of mass mᵢ at distance rᵢ from axis O and distance r'ᵢ from parallel axis through C (at distance h from O).
I_O = Σmᵢrᵢ² rᵢ = r'ᵢ + h (using geometry) I_O = Σmᵢ(r'ᵢ + h)² = Σmᵢr'ᵢ² + 2hΣmᵢr'ᵢ + h²Σmᵢ Since C is centre of mass, Σmᵢr'ᵢ = 0:
I_O = I_C + 0 + Mh² = I_C + Mh² ✓
Q.16
Ch 13M
Derive an expression for the kinetic energy of a body rotating with uniform angular velocity.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Consider a rigid body rotating about an axis with angular velocity ω.
Let the body consist of n particles of masses m₁, m₂, ... at distances r₁, r₂, ... from the axis.
Linear velocity of each particle: vᵢ = ωrᵢ
KE of each particle: KEᵢ = ½mᵢvᵢ² = ½mᵢω²rᵢ²
Total KE of the body: KE = Σ½mᵢω²rᵢ² = ½ω² Σmᵢrᵢ² Since Σmᵢrᵢ² = I (moment of inertia):
KE_rot = ½Iω²
This is the rotational analogue of translational KE = ½mv².
Q.17
Ch 13M
Explain the motion of a vehicle on a banked road. Obtain the expression for the maximum safe speed.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
A banked road is tilted at angle θ to the horizontal. Forces on vehicle: weight mg (down), normal reaction N (perpendicular to road), friction f (along road surface).

For maximum speed (tendency to skid outward → friction acts inward and down the bank):
Vertically: N cosθ = mg + f sinθ   ...(1)
Horizontally: N sinθ + f cosθ = mv²/r   ...(2)
With f = μN, dividing (2) by (1):
v_max = √[rg(tanθ + μ)/(1 − μtanθ)]
For minimum speed (tendency to skid inward → friction acts up the bank):
v_min = √[rg(tanθ − μ)/(1 + μtanθ)]
Most safe speed (no friction):
v_safe = √(rg tanθ)

Q.19
Ch 73M
Derive an expression for fringe width in Young's Double Slit Experiment.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Setup: Two slits S₁ and S₂, separation d, screen at distance D. Point P at height y from centre O.
Path difference: For D >> d:
Δ = S₂P − S₁P = yd/D
Bright fringes (Δ = nλ): y_n = nλD/d    (n = 0, ±1, ±2 …) Fringe width (distance between consecutive bright fringes): β = y_(n+1) − y_n = (n+1)λD/d − nλD/d
β = λD/d
Key results:
  • β ∝ λ (increases with wavelength)
  • β ∝ D (increases with screen distance)
  • β ∝ 1/d (decreases with slit separation)
  • Bright and dark fringes have equal width
Q.20
Ch 73M
State and prove Brewster's law.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Statement: When unpolarised light is incident at Brewster's angle (i_B) on a surface, the reflected light is completely plane polarised. At Brewster's angle, the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular to each other.
i_B + r = 90° → r = (90° − i_B)
Proof (derivation of formula):
By Snell's law: n = sin i_B / sin r
Substituting r = (90° − i_B): n = sin i_B / sin(90° − i_B) = sin i_B / cos i_B
n = tan i_B → i_B = tan⁻¹(n)
Example: For glass n = 1.5: i_B = tan⁻¹(1.5) ≈ 56.3°

Q.22
Ch 13M
State the principle of conservation of angular momentum. Give two examples.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Principle: If the net external torque acting on a system is zero, the total angular momentum of the system remains constant (conserved).
τ_ext = 0 → L = Iω = constant → I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
Examples:
  • Ballet dancer / ice skater: Pulls arms in → moment of inertia I decreases → angular velocity ω increases to keep L constant. Extends arms → I increases → ω decreases (slow spin).
  • Diver: After leaving the springboard, pulls arms and legs close to body → I decreases → spins faster to complete more rotations. Extends body before entering water → ω decreases → smooth entry.
Q.23
Ch 73M
Explain the construction and working of a Polaroid. State its applications.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Polaroid: A device that produces plane polarised light from unpolarised light.

Construction: A large crystal of quinine iodosulphate (herapathite) is embedded in a transparent sheet of nitrocellulose. All crystals are aligned in the same direction. The sheet transmits only light vibrations parallel to its transmission axis and absorbs the perpendicular component.

Working: Unpolarised light enters the polaroid → only vibrations parallel to the transmission axis pass through → emergent light is plane polarised. Intensity = I₀/2.

Applications:
  • Polaroid sunglasses — reduce glare from reflected sunlight
  • Camera filters — reduce reflections, enhance contrast
  • LCD screens
  • Stress analysis in transparent materials

Q.25
Ch 73M
What is resolving power of an optical instrument? State Rayleigh's criterion. Write the formula for limit of resolution of a telescope.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)
Resolving power: The ability of an optical instrument to distinguish (resolve) two closely placed objects/points as separate and distinct. Higher resolving power = can see finer detail.

Rayleigh's criterion: Two images are said to be just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one point object coincides with the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other point object.

Limit of resolution (minimum resolvable angle) for telescope:
θ_min = 1.22λ/D
where λ = wavelength of light used, D = diameter of objective lens/mirror.

To increase resolving power: Use smaller λ (blue or UV light), or use larger diameter objective.

Ch 73M
Plane polarised light of intensity I₀ passes through an analyser. Find the intensity when the analyser is at (i) 0° (ii) 30° (iii) 60° (iv) 90° to the polariser.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (3M)

Q.27
Ch 14M
What is a conical pendulum? Derive expressions for its time period, frequency and tension in the string.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (4M)
Conical pendulum: A pendulum in which the bob revolves in a horizontal circle while the string traces out a cone. The string makes angle θ with vertical (semi-vertical angle).

Forces on bob: Weight mg (downward), Tension T (along string).
Equations:
Vertically: T cosθ = mg      ...(1)
Horizontally (centripetal): T sinθ = mω²r    ...(2)
Dividing (2) by (1): tanθ = ω²r/g
Since r = L sinθ: tanθ = ω²L sinθ/g → ω² = g/(L cosθ)

Time Period:
T = 2π/ω = 2π√(L cosθ/g)
Frequency:
n = 1/T = (1/2π)√(g/L cosθ)
Tension: From (1):
T_string = mg/cosθ
Key observations: As θ increases → cosθ decreases → period decreases (faster rotation). T_string always > mg.
Q.28
Ch 14M
State and prove the law of conservation of angular momentum. Give two applications. A disc of moment of inertia 2 kg·m² is spinning at 5 rad/s. A mass of 0.5 kg is placed at a distance of 2 m from the centre. Find the new angular velocity.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (4M)
Statement: If net external torque = 0, angular momentum L = Iω = constant.
I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
Proof: τ = dL/dt. If τ_ext = 0, dL/dt = 0, so L = constant.

Applications:
  • Ballet dancer — pulls arms in → I decreases → ω increases
  • Diver — tucks body → faster spin; extends → slower entry

I₁ = 2 kg·m², ω₁ = 5 rad/s, m = 0.5 kg, r = 2 m
I₂ = I₁ + mr² = 2 + 0.5 × (2)² = 2 + 2 = 4 kg·m²
By conservation: I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
ω₂ = I₁ω₁/I₂ = (2 × 5)/4 = 10/4 = 2.5 rad/s
New angular velocity = 2.5 rad/s

Q.30
Ch 74M
Explain the phenomenon of diffraction of light at a single slit. Obtain the conditions for central maximum, secondary maxima and minima. Draw the intensity distribution graph.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (4M)
Single slit diffraction: When light passes through a narrow slit of width 'a', secondary wavelets from different parts of the same wavefront interfere to produce a diffraction pattern on the screen.

Central maximum: At θ = 0°, all secondary wavelets arrive in phase → maximum intensity. This is the brightest and widest fringe.
Width of central maximum = 2λD/a
Conditions for minima:
a sinθ = nλ    (n = ±1, ±2, ±3 …)
Conditions for secondary maxima:
a sinθ = (2n+1)λ/2    (n = ±1, ±2 …)
Intensity distribution:
  • Central maximum — highest intensity, widest
  • 1st secondary maximum — intensity ≈ 4.5% of central
  • 2nd secondary maximum — intensity ≈ 1.6% of central
  • Minima on both sides of each maximum — zero intensity
  • Pattern: symmetric about central maximum
Key fact: As slit width 'a' decreases, central maximum becomes wider (more diffraction). As 'a' increases, central maximum becomes narrower.
Q.31
Ch 14M
Define moment of inertia. Derive expressions for MI of a thin uniform rod about: (i) an axis through its centre perpendicular to its length (ii) an axis through one end perpendicular to its length.
▼ Ans
✅ Answer (4M)
Moment of Inertia: The sum of products of mass of each particle and the square of its perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation.
I = Σmᵢrᵢ² = ∫r²dm
(i) Thin rod — axis through centre, ⊥ to length:
Length = L, mass = M, linear mass density λ = M/L
Consider small element dx at distance x from centre: dm = (M/L)dx I_C = ∫₋L/2^(L/2) x²(M/L)dx = (M/L)[x³/3]₋L/2^(L/2)
I_C = ML²/12
(ii) Axis through one end, ⊥ to length:
Using parallel axes theorem (h = L/2): I_end = I_C + M(L/2)² = ML²/12 + ML²/4
I_end = ML²/12 + 3ML²/12 = ML²/3
