Mathematics

Chapter 1 — Rational Numbers

📌 Key Points
  • A rational number is a number that can be written as p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0
  • Rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication
  • Division by zero is not defined
  • Between any two rational numbers there are infinite rational numbers
  • Zero (0) is the additive identity — a + 0 = a
  • One (1) is the multiplicative identity — a × 1 = a
  • Commutative property: a + b = b + a and a × b = b × a
  • Associative property: (a+b)+c = a+(b+c)
✅ Digest Answers
Q. What is a rational number?
A number that can be expressed in the form p/q where p and q are integers and q is not equal to zero. Example: 3/4, -5/2, 7/1
Q. What is additive inverse?
The additive inverse of a number a is -a, such that a + (-a) = 0. Example: additive inverse of 3/5 is -3/5
Q. What is multiplicative inverse?
The multiplicative inverse of a/b is b/a, such that a/b × b/a = 1. Example: multiplicative inverse of 2/3 is 3/2

Mathematics

Chapter 2 — Linear Equations in One Variable

📌 Key Points
  • A linear equation has the form ax + b = 0 where a ≠ 0
  • It has only one variable with degree 1
  • The value of variable that satisfies the equation is called its solution or root
  • To solve: perform same operation on both sides to keep equation balanced
  • Cross multiplication is used when equation has fractions on both sides
  • Transposing: moving a term from one side changes its sign
✅ Digest Answers
Q. Solve: 2x + 5 = 11
2x = 11 - 5 = 6 → x = 6/2 = 3
Q. What is transposition?
Moving a term from one side of equation to other side by changing its sign. Example: x + 3 = 7 → x = 7 - 3 = 4

Chapter 3 — Understanding Quadrilaterals

📌 Key Points
  • Sum of interior angles of a quadrilateral = 360°
  • Parallelogram: opposite sides equal and parallel, opposite angles equal
  • Rectangle: parallelogram with all angles 90°
  • Rhombus: parallelogram with all sides equal
  • Square: rectangle with all sides equal
  • Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides
  • Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other
  • Diagonals of a rhombus bisect at right angles
✅ Digest Answers
Q. What is a parallelogram?
A quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel and equal. Its opposite angles are equal and diagonals bisect each other.
Q. Difference between rhombus and square?
Both have all sides equal. But a square has all angles = 90° while a rhombus does not necessarily have right angles.

Science

Chapter 1 — Crop Production and Management

📌 Key Points
  • Kharif crops: Sown in June-July, harvested in September-October. Example: Rice, Maize, Cotton
  • Rabi crops: Sown in October-November, harvested in March-April. Example: Wheat, Mustard, Pea
  • Irrigation: supply of water to crops at regular intervals
  • Fertilizers: chemical substances that provide nutrients. Example: Urea, NPK
  • Manure: natural organic matter from decomposed plant/animal waste
  • Weeding: removal of unwanted plants (weeds) from fields
  • Threshing: separating grains from stalks after harvesting
  • Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium are the three main nutrients for crops
✅ Digest Answers
Q. What is the difference between fertilizer and manure?
Fertilizer is a chemical substance that provides specific nutrients quickly. Manure is organic matter from decomposed waste — it improves soil texture and adds nutrients slowly. Manure is better for long-term soil health.
Q. What is crop rotation?
Growing different crops alternately on the same land. It maintains soil fertility and reduces pests. Example: growing wheat after legumes restores nitrogen in soil.
Q. Name any two Kharif and two Rabi crops.
Kharif: Rice, Maize. Rabi: Wheat, Mustard.

Chapter 2 — Microorganisms: Friend and Foe

📌 Key Points
  • Microorganisms are classified into: Bacteria, Fungi, Protozoa, Algae, Viruses
  • Viruses are not living organisms — they reproduce only inside a host cell
  • Antibiotics: medicines that kill bacteria. Example: Penicillin (discovered by Alexander Fleming)
  • Vaccine: weakened germs injected to develop immunity
  • Fermentation: process by which yeast converts sugar into alcohol and CO₂
  • Bacteria help in making curd, cheese, vinegar
  • Nitrogen-fixing bacteria: Rhizobium lives in roots of legume plants
  • Disease-causing microorganisms are called pathogens
✅ Digest Answers
Q. What are antibiotics? Give an example.
Antibiotics are medicines made from microorganisms that kill or stop the growth of bacteria. Example: Penicillin, Streptomycin. They do NOT work against viruses.
Q. What is fermentation?
The process by which yeast breaks down sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide in the absence of oxygen. It is used to make bread, wine and beer.
Q. How do vaccines work?
Vaccines contain weakened or dead germs. When injected, they stimulate the body to produce antibodies. These antibodies remain in the body and fight the actual disease in future.

Chapter 6 — Combustion and Flame

📌 Key Points
  • Combustion: chemical process in which substance reacts with oxygen to give heat and light
  • Fuel: substance that burns to produce energy. Types: solid (wood), liquid (petrol), gas (LPG)
  • Ignition temperature: minimum temperature at which a substance catches fire
  • Inflammable substances: substances with very low ignition temperature. Example: petrol, alcohol
  • Fire triangle: Fuel + Oxygen + Heat — all three needed for fire
  • To extinguish fire: remove any one component of fire triangle
  • CO₂ is the best fire extinguisher — cuts off oxygen supply
  • Calorific value: amount of heat produced by complete combustion of 1 kg of fuel
✅ Digest Answers
Q. Why is CO₂ used in fire extinguishers?
CO₂ is heavier than oxygen. It forms a blanket over the burning substance and cuts off the oxygen supply, which stops combustion. It does not harm electrical equipment.
Q. What is ignition temperature?
The minimum temperature at which a substance catches fire and starts burning is called its ignition temperature. Below this temperature, combustion cannot occur even if oxygen is present.

English Grammar

Parts of Speech — Quick Reference

📌 Key Points
  • Noun: name of a person, place, thing or idea. Example: Shashikant, Mumbai, book
  • Pronoun: word used in place of noun. Example: he, she, they, it
  • Verb: word showing action or state. Example: run, eat, is, was
  • Adjective: describes a noun. Example: tall boy, red pen
  • Adverb: describes a verb, adjective or adverb. Example: quickly, very, well
  • Preposition: shows relation between noun and other words. Example: in, on, at, by
  • Conjunction: joins words or sentences. Example: and, but, or, because
  • Interjection: expresses sudden emotion. Example: Oh! Wow! Hurrah!
✅ Digest Answers
Q. What is an adjective? Give 3 examples.
An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun. Examples: beautiful flower, tall building, hot coffee. It answers questions like "which?", "what kind?", "how many?"
Q. Difference between adverb and adjective?
Adjective describes a noun (She is beautiful). Adverb describes a verb, adjective or another adverb (She sings beautifully). Adverbs often end in -ly.
