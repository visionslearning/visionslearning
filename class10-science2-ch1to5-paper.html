Ch1 A. Explain Mendel's Law of Segregation with a monohybrid cross. (4M)
Mendel's Law of Segregation: During the formation of gametes (sex cells), the two alleles of a gene separate (segregate) so that each gamete carries only one allele.

Monohybrid Cross (Tall × Dwarf pea plant):
  • Parents: TT (Tall) × tt (Dwarf)
  • F₁ generation: All Tt (Tall) — dominant trait expressed
  • F₁ × F₁: Tt × Tt →
Tt
TTT (Tall)Tt (Tall)
tTt (Tall)tt (Dwarf)
  • F₂ ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (Phenotypic ratio)
  • Genotypic ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
  • This proves alleles segregate into separate gametes — Law of Segregation.

Ch2 B. Explain the process of photosynthesis. Write the overall equation. (4M)
Photosynthesis: The process by which green plants prepare their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide.

Overall Equation:
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → (sunlight, chlorophyll) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Conditions required:
  • Sunlight (energy source)
  • Chlorophyll (green pigment in chloroplasts — absorbs light)
  • Water (absorbed by roots from soil)
  • Carbon dioxide (absorbed through stomata)
Two stages:
  • Light reaction (in thylakoid): Light energy splits water (photolysis) → releases O₂ → produces ATP and NADPH.
  • Dark reaction / Calvin cycle (in stroma): CO₂ is fixed using ATP and NADPH → glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is produced.
Importance:
  • Produces food for all organisms (base of food chain).
  • Releases O₂ — essential for respiration.
  • Maintains CO₂-O₂ balance in atmosphere.
📐 Draw a leaf cross-section showing chloroplasts, CO₂ entering through stomata, O₂ released, and sunlight arrows.
Ch4 C. What is biodiversity? Explain its importance and threats to biodiversity. (4M)
Biodiversity: The variety of life on Earth — including all species of plants, animals, microorganisms, and the ecosystems they form. It exists at three levels: genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity.

Importance of Biodiversity:
  • Maintains ecological balance and food chains.
  • Provides food, medicine, raw materials, and oxygen.
  • Helps in soil formation, water purification, and climate regulation.
  • Has aesthetic, cultural, and economic value.
Threats to Biodiversity:
  • Habitat destruction: Deforestation, urbanisation, agriculture.
  • Pollution: Air, water, and soil pollution killing species.
  • Overexploitation: Overhunting, overfishing, overharvesting.
  • Invasive species: Introduced species competing with native species.
  • Climate change: Global warming altering habitats.
Conservation: Protected areas (national parks, wildlife sanctuaries), biodiversity hotspots protection, ex-situ conservation (zoos, seed banks).
Ch5 D. Explain solar energy as a source of green energy. Describe two devices that use solar energy. (4M)
Solar Energy:
  • Energy from the Sun in the form of electromagnetic radiation (light and heat).
  • Renewable, inexhaustible, pollution-free, and available everywhere.
  • India receives ~300 sunny days per year — huge solar potential.
Two devices using solar energy:

1. Solar Cell (Photovoltaic Cell):
  • Converts sunlight directly into electrical energy using the photoelectric effect.
  • Made of semiconductor material (silicon).
  • Multiple cells form a solar panel; panels connected → solar power plant.
  • Uses: calculators, street lights, satellites, rooftop power generation.
2. Solar Water Heater:
  • Uses solar thermal energy to heat water for domestic use.
  • Consists of flat plate collector (absorbs heat), insulated tank, and pipes.
  • Black surface absorbs maximum sunlight → heats water to 60–80°C.
  • Saves electricity; environment-friendly; used in homes, hotels, hospitals.
📐 Draw: (1) Solar cell — p-n junction semiconductor with sunlight arrows → electric current. (2) Solar water heater — collector + tank + inlet/outlet pipes.

Ch1 A. Explain Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection. How does it explain the evolution of long-necked giraffes? (4M)
Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection (1859):
  • Variation: Individuals in a population show natural variation in their traits.
  • Overproduction: Organisms produce more offspring than can survive (struggle for existence).
  • Survival of the Fittest: Individuals with favourable variations survive and reproduce; those with unfavourable variations die out.
  • Natural Selection: Nature "selects" which individuals survive based on their traits and environment.
  • Inheritance: Favourable traits are passed to offspring → gradually the population changes → evolution occurs.
  • Gradual process: Evolution happens over many generations, not suddenly.
Example — Evolution of long-necked giraffes:
  • Ancestral giraffes had varying neck lengths (natural variation).
  • Those with longer necks could reach higher leaves → better food access → survived and reproduced.
  • Short-necked giraffes couldn't reach food → less fit → died out.
  • Long-necked trait was inherited by offspring → over many generations → all giraffes became long-necked.
Evidence: Fossil record, comparative anatomy (homologous organs), vestigial organs, biogeography.
Ch2 B. Describe the human digestive system with a neat labelled diagram. Explain the role of each organ. (4M)
Human Digestive System: The system that breaks down food into absorbable nutrients is the digestive system.
  • Mouth: Teeth mechanically grind food; salivary amylase begins starch digestion → food becomes bolus.
  • Oesophagus: Muscular tube that carries food from mouth to stomach by peristalsis.
  • Stomach: Secretes gastric juice (HCl + pepsin). HCl kills bacteria, pepsin digests proteins → food becomes chyme.
  • Small intestine: Main site of digestion and absorption. Receives bile (from liver) and pancreatic juice (from pancreas). Bile emulsifies fats; pancreatic enzymes digest carbohydrates, proteins, fats. Villi absorb nutrients into blood.
  • Large intestine: Absorbs water and minerals; forms faeces.
  • Rectum and Anus: Stores and expels faeces (egestion).
  • Liver: Produces bile for fat emulsification; detoxification.
  • Pancreas: Produces pancreatic juice with enzymes for all food types.

Ch3 C. Explain the process of excretion in humans. Describe the structure and function of the kidney (nephron). (4M)
Excretion: The process of removing metabolic waste products from the body. Main excretory product = urea (from protein metabolism).

Human Excretory System:
  • Two kidneys, two ureters, urinary bladder, urethra.
Kidney Structure:
  • Bean-shaped organs on either side of the vertebral column.
  • Each kidney contains ~1 million nephrons (functional units).
Nephron — Structure and Function:
  • Glomerulus: A ball of capillaries inside the Bowman's capsule. Blood is filtered under pressure → filtrate enters Bowman's capsule (ultrafiltration). Removes water, urea, glucose, salts from blood.
  • Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT): Reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, useful salts, and water back into blood.
  • Loop of Henle: Concentrates urine by reabsorbing more water.
  • Distal convoluted tubule (DCT): Fine-tunes salt and water balance; selective reabsorption.
  • Collecting duct: Collects concentrated urine → drains into renal pelvis → ureter → bladder → excreted as urine.
Composition of urine: 95% water, 2% urea, salts, uric acid, creatinine.

🧪 Activity A Ch2 · Life Processes — Photosynthesis Experiment
Setup: A potted plant is kept in dark for 48 hours (de-starched). Then one leaf is partially covered with black paper and the plant is kept in sunlight for 4–6 hours. The leaf is then tested for starch using iodine solution.
Answer the following questions based on this activity: (4M)
  • (i) Why is the plant kept in dark for 48 hours?
    → To de-starch the plant — remove all existing starch so the experiment is not affected by previously stored starch. This ensures any starch detected is newly produced during the experiment.
  • (ii) What result do you expect when you test the uncovered part of the leaf with iodine?
    → The uncovered part turns blue-black (positive starch test) because it received sunlight and photosynthesis occurred → starch was produced.
  • (iii) What result do you expect for the covered part of the leaf?
    → The covered part remains yellow-brown (no colour change) because it did not receive sunlight → photosynthesis did NOT occur → no starch produced.
  • (iv) What is the conclusion of this activity?
    → Sunlight is essential for photosynthesis. Without sunlight, plants cannot make food (starch). The activity proves that light is a necessary condition for photosynthesis to occur in green plants.
🧪 Activity B Ch1 · Heredity — Coin Toss Probability
Setup: A student tosses two coins together 100 times and records the results. The results were: both heads (HH) = 26 times; one head one tail (HT or TH) = 49 times; both tails (TT) = 25 times. The student compares this to a Punnett square for a monohybrid cross (Tt × Tt).
Answer the following questions: (4M)
  • (i) In the Punnett square for Tt × Tt, what is the expected genotypic ratio?
    → Expected genotypic ratio = 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (i.e., 25% TT, 50% Tt, 25% tt).
  • (ii) How does the coin toss result compare to the Punnett square ratio?
    → HH ≈ 26% matches TT (25%); HT+TH ≈ 49% matches 2Tt (50%); TT ≈ 25% matches tt (25%). The observed results are close to expected 1:2:1 ratio — slight differences are due to chance (probability).
  • (iii) What does HH, HT, TT represent in genetics terms?
    → HH = homozygous dominant (TT); HT/TH = heterozygous (Tt); TT = homozygous recessive (tt).
  • (iv) What is the conclusion of this activity?
    → Mendelian genetics follows the laws of probability. The coin toss simulates random separation of alleles during gamete formation (Law of Segregation). Ratios are approximate in small samples but approach 1:2:1 with larger sample sizes.
🧪 Activity C Ch4 & Ch5 · Environmental Management + Green Energy
Setup: A student surveys a village and observes: (1) People are burning crop residue in open fields. (2) A nearby river is polluted with plastic waste. (3) A solar panel installed on one house is generating electricity. (4) Trees were cut to make a road. The student is asked to analyse these observations.
Answer the following questions based on this activity: (4M)
  • (i) Identify two harmful practices from the observations and explain their effects.
    → (a) Burning crop residue — causes severe air pollution, releases CO₂, CO, particulate matter → causes respiratory diseases, contributes to greenhouse effect and global warming.
    → (b) Plastic pollution in river — kills aquatic organisms, enters food chain through bioaccumulation, contaminates drinking water, harms fish and birds.
  • (ii) How does the solar panel benefit the villager? Name one limitation of solar energy.
    → Benefits: Generates free, clean electricity from sunlight → saves electricity bills, no pollution, no fuel needed, low maintenance.
    → Limitation: Solar energy is only available during daytime and depends on weather (reduced on cloudy days); initial installation cost is high.
  • (iii) What are the effects of deforestation (cutting trees for road)?
    → Loss of habitat for wildlife → loss of biodiversity; soil erosion and landslides; disruption of water cycle; increase in CO₂ (fewer trees to absorb it) → global warming; loss of shade → local temperature rise.
  • (iv) Suggest two eco-friendly alternatives the village can adopt.
    → (a) Use crop residue as biogas raw material (instead of burning) — produces clean cooking fuel.
    → (b) Waste management — segregate dry/wet waste, compost organic waste, ban single-use plastics, use jute/cloth bags.
