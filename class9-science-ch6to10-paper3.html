5 Marks
Ch6⭐ i. Distinguish between monocots and dicots (one point each).
Answer Monocots have one cotyledon and parallel leaf venation (e.g., wheat, maize), while dicots have two cotyledons and reticulate (network) leaf venation (e.g., mango, rose).
Ch7⭐ MOST IMP ii. What is meant by the term 'trophic level'?
Answer Each feeding level or step in a food chain is called a trophic level — producers occupy the 1st trophic level, primary consumers (herbivores) occupy the 2nd, secondary consumers the 3rd, and so on.
Ch8⭐ MOST IMP iii. What is vaccination? How does it protect us from diseases?
Answer Vaccination is the process of introducing a weakened or dead pathogen (vaccine) into the body to stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies, so that the person becomes immune and protected from that disease in the future without actually getting sick.
Ch9⭐ MOST IMP iv. What is the full form of CFC? How does it affect the environment?
Answer CFC stands for Chlorofluorocarbon — a chemical used in refrigerants and aerosol sprays; when released into the atmosphere, CFCs rise to the stratosphere and destroy the ozone (O₃) layer, allowing harmful UV radiation to reach Earth's surface, causing skin cancer and eye damage.
Ch10⭐ IMP v. What is a computer virus? How is it different from a biological virus?
Answer A computer virus is a malicious software program that can copy itself, spread to other files/computers, and damage or destroy data; unlike a biological virus (which infects living cells), a computer virus infects computer programs and data files but cannot harm living organisms.

Ch6⭐ MOST IMP A. Write the distinguishing features of Gymnosperms with 3 examples and draw their classification position. (4M)
Gymnosperms — Features: • The word means 'naked seed' (Gymno = naked, sperma = seed) • Seeds are not enclosed in a fruit — they are exposed/naked on cone scales • Do not produce flowers; produce cones (strobili) • Mostly evergreen trees with needle-like or scale-like leaves • Have well-developed vascular tissue (xylem and phloem) • Reproduction by seeds — seeds not in fruit, just on cone • Examples: Pine (Pinus), Cycas (Sago palm), Cedar (Cedrus), Fir (Abies), Deodar Position in classification: Plantae → Phanerogams → Gymnosperms (naked seeds) → vs Angiosperms (enclosed seeds)
📐 Draw: Pine cone and Cycas showing naked seeds on scales. Also draw: needle-like leaves of pine tree.
Ch7⭐ MOST IMP B. Explain the energy pyramid. Why does the energy decrease at each trophic level? (4M)
Ecological/Energy Pyramid: A graphical representation showing the amount of energy at each trophic level — it is widest at the base (producers) and narrows at the top (top predators). Lindemann's 10% Law: Only 10% of energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next level. 90% is lost due to: • Respiration — energy used for life activities • Heat loss to environment • Excretion — energy lost in wastes • Not all parts of an organism are eaten (bones, shells) Example: Grass: 10,000 J → Grasshopper: 1,000 J → Frog: 100 J → Snake: 10 J → Eagle: 1 J Significance: Explains why food chains are short (3–5 levels) and why there are fewer top predators in nature.
📐 Draw pyramid with 4 levels: Producers (10,000 J) at base, then 1,000 J, 100 J, 10 J at top. Label each trophic level and show 90% loss arrows.

Ch9⭐ MOST IMP D. Explain the causes and effects of air pollution. Name three air pollutants. (4M)
Air Pollution: Addition of harmful gases, dust, smoke, and chemicals into the atmosphere that make it unhealthy for living organisms. Three Major Air Pollutants: 1. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — burning fossil fuels → greenhouse effect 2. Sulphur dioxide (SO₂) — factories, power plants → acid rain 3. Carbon monoxide (CO) — vehicle exhaust → poisonous gas, reduces O₂ in blood Causes: • Burning fossil fuels (coal, petrol, diesel) in vehicles and industries • Crop burning, waste burning in open • Industrial chimneys releasing toxic gases • Deforestation (fewer trees to absorb CO₂) Effects: • Respiratory diseases — asthma, bronchitis, lung cancer • Acid rain — damages crops, buildings, aquatic life • Global warming — melting ice, rising sea levels • Ozone depletion — UV radiation reaches Earth → skin cancer Control: Use CNG/electric vehicles; plant trees; industrial filters; solar energy instead of coal.

Ch7⭐ MOST IMP A. Draw and explain a neat labelled diagram of a pond ecosystem. Name the biotic and abiotic components. (4M)
Pond Ecosystem — a natural aquatic ecosystem: Abiotic Components: • Sunlight — energy source for photosynthesis • Water — medium for all life processes • Temperature, pH, dissolved gases (O₂, CO₂) • Minerals/nutrients in water (nitrates, phosphates) Biotic Components: • Producers: Algae, aquatic plants (Hydrilla, Lotus) — photosynthesis • Primary Consumers: Zooplankton, small insects, tadpoles — eat algae • Secondary Consumers: Small fish — eat insects/tadpoles • Tertiary Consumers: Large fish, frogs — eat small fish • Decomposers: Bacteria, fungi — break down dead matter at pond bottom Energy Flow: Sunlight → Algae → Zooplankton → Small fish → Large fish → Decomposers → Nutrients → Algae (cycle continues) Feature: A pond is a self-sustaining ecosystem — it maintains its own balance as long as human interference is minimal.
📐 Draw pond cross-section: Sunlight at top → Lotus/algae (floating) → Small fish → Large fish → Mud at bottom (decomposers). Label all biotic and abiotic components with arrows showing energy flow.
Ch8⭐ MOST IMP B. Explain the role of microbes in agriculture. How do they help in increasing soil fertility? (4M)
Role of Microbes in Agriculture: 1. Nitrogen Fixation: • Rhizobium bacteria live in root nodules of leguminous plants (peas, beans, groundnut) • Convert atmospheric N₂ → ammonia (NH₃) → nitrates → absorbed by plants as protein • Farmers grow legumes to naturally enrich the soil (biofertiliser) 2. Decomposition: • Bacteria and fungi break down dead plant/animal matter → humus • Humus improves soil structure, water retention, and mineral content 3. Biofertilisers: • Azotobacter, Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) — free-living nitrogen fixers in soil • Mycorrhizal fungi help plant roots absorb water and minerals 4. Biopesticides: • Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) bacteria — produces toxins that kill insect pests • Safer than chemical pesticides — no environmental harm 5. Composting: • Microbes decompose organic waste (kitchen waste, crop residue) → compost manure • Enriches soil with organic matter — better than chemical fertilisers
Ch9⭐ MOST IMP C. What is environmental management? Explain the need for conservation and the 3R principle. Give the role of government and citizens. (4M)
Environmental Management: The process of taking steps to protect, conserve and sustainably use the natural environment — air, water, soil, forests, and wildlife — for present and future generations. Need for Conservation: • Natural resources (forests, water, fossil fuels) are limited and being overused • Pollution and habitat destruction threaten biodiversity • Climate change affects food security and human health • Future generations need these resources 3R Principle: • Reduce: Use less — buy only what you need; avoid over-packaging; save water and electricity • Reuse: Use items multiple times — cloth bags instead of plastic, refillable bottles • Recycle: Convert waste into new products — paper, glass, metals, e-waste recycling Role of Government: • Pass and enforce environmental laws (Environment Protection Act 1986) • Create national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves • Ban on plastic, pollution control regulations Role of Citizens: • Plant trees; save water; use public transport • Segregate waste; avoid burning garbage; report pollution • Participate in environmental campaigns (World Environment Day, Swachh Bharat)

Ch8 A student is given a slide under the microscope. She sees tiny, rod-shaped structures with no nucleus. Answer the following questions: (4M)
i. What type of microorganism is the student observing? The student is observing Bacteria — rod-shaped bacteria are called bacilli (singular: bacillus). They are prokaryotes — no membrane-bound nucleus. ii. Name the structural features that confirm this is a bacterium and not a plant cell or animal cell. • No nucleus (prokaryote) — DNA is in the cytoplasm as a nucleoid • Cell wall present (made of peptidoglycan, not cellulose) • No membrane-bound organelles (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi) • Very small size (1–10 micrometres) iii. Name one harmful and one useful rod-shaped bacterium. Harmful: Mycobacterium tuberculosis (causes Tuberculosis / TB) Useful: Lactobacillus acidophilus (converts milk to curd; used in probiotics) iv. How do bacteria reproduce? How fast can they multiply? Bacteria reproduce by binary fission — one bacterium splits into two identical cells. Under favourable conditions, they can divide every 20–30 minutes. So in 10 hours, one bacterium can give rise to millions of bacteria! This is why infections spread rapidly.
