Q.1 (i)
Primary oocyte is:
✔ Answer: (A) Diploid (2n)
Explanation Primary oocyte = formed from oogonium by mitosis → still 2n (diploid). It is arrested in prophase I of meiosis from birth until ovulation. After LH surge → meiosis I completes → secondary oocyte (n) + polar body
Q.1 (ii)
Trisomy 21 results in:
✔ Answer: (B) Down syndrome
Explanation Down syndrome (Mongolism): extra copy of chromosome 21 (trisomy 21 = 47 chromosomes). Features: intellectual disability, distinctive facial features, short stature, Simian crease, heart defects. Risk increases with maternal age. Turner syndrome = 45,X; Klinefelter = 47,XXY; Edwards = trisomy 18.
Q.1 (iii)
'One gene one enzyme' hypothesis proposed by:
✔ Answer: (C) Beadle and Tatum
Explanation George Beadle and Edward Tatum (1941) — worked on Neurospora crassa (bread mould). Used X-ray induced mutants that couldn't grow on minimal medium without specific nutrients. Concluded: each gene controls the synthesis of one enzyme → Nobel Prize 1958.
Q.1 (iv)
Agrobacterium tumefaciens is used in:
✔ Answer: (A) Plant transformation (gene transfer)
Explanation A. tumefaciens contains Ti (tumour-inducing) plasmid. Naturally transfers T-DNA into plant cell genome. Gene of interest inserted into T-DNA → A. tumefaciens infects plant → gene integrates into plant genome → transgenic plant. Used to create transgenic plants (Bt cotton, Golden Rice, herbicide-resistant crops).
Q.1 (v)
The enzyme RuBisCO is located in:
✔ Answer: (B) Stroma of chloroplast
Explanation RuBisCO (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase) is located in the stroma of the chloroplast. Most abundant enzyme on Earth. Catalyses the first step of the Calvin cycle (carbon fixation): CO₂ + RuBP → 2 × 3-PGA.
Q.1 (vi)
Leguminous plants fix nitrogen through:
✔ Answer: (B) Rhizobium in root nodules
Explanation Rhizobium forms a symbiotic association with leguminous plants (peas, beans, soybean) in root nodules. Rhizobium fixes N₂ → NH₃ using enzyme nitrogenase. Plant provides photosynthate; bacteria provide fixed nitrogen.
Q.1 (vii)
Cry proteins are produced by:
✔ Answer: (A) Bacillus thuringiensis
Explanation Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) — soil bacterium. Produces crystal (Cry) proteins encoded by cry genes. Cry proteins are insecticidal — toxic to specific insects when activated in alkaline insect midgut.
Q.1 (viii)
Threatened species category as per IUCN:
✔ Answer: Critically Endangered, Endangered, and Vulnerable
IUCN Categories EX (Extinct), EW (Extinct in Wild), CR (Critically Endangered), EN (Endangered), VU (Vulnerable), NT (Near Threatened), LC (Least Concern) CR + EN + VU together = "Threatened" species requiring conservation action.
Q.1 (ix)
Linkage group number equals:
✔ Answer: Number of pairs of homologous chromosomes (haploid number n)
Explanation All genes on the same chromosome form one linkage group. Drosophila: 4 pairs of chromosomes → 4 linkage groups. Humans: 23 pairs → 23 linkage groups.
Q.1 (x)
IVF (in vitro fertilisation) was first successfully done by:
✔ Answer: Robert Edwards and Patrick Steptoe (1978)
Explanation First test-tube baby: Louise Brown, born 25 July 1978 (UK). Robert Edwards received Nobel Prize in Physiology/Medicine 2010 for developing IVF.

Q.2 — Key Definitions
CapacitationThe physiological process in which sperm become capable of fertilising an egg — occurs in the female reproductive tract (takes ~7 hours). Involves removal of surface proteins and changes in membrane properties.
Polygenic inheritanceA character controlled by two or more genes with additive effects. Example: skin colour in humans (governed by 3+ gene pairs). Produces continuous variation (bell-shaped distribution).
ExonThe coding sequences in a eukaryotic gene that remain in the mature mRNA after RNA splicing (introns removed). Exons are expressed — they code for protein.
ElisaEnzyme-Linked ImmunoSorbent Assay — a diagnostic test that uses antibodies and enzyme-linked colour reactions to detect specific antigens (e.g., HIV, hepatitis B) in blood or urine samples.
PhotoperiodismThe response of plants to relative lengths of day and night (photoperiod) in triggering flowering. Short-day plants flower when nights are longer than critical period; long-day plants when nights are shorter.
Pedigree analysisA chart showing the phenotypes/genotypes of a family over several generations, used to study inheritance patterns of genetic traits or disorders.
BioreactorA vessel/system in which microorganisms or cells are grown under controlled conditions to produce useful products (enzymes, antibiotics, vaccines, recombinant proteins). Large-scale production of biologics.
Species richnessThe number of different species present in an area or community. One component of biodiversity. Increases with area (species-area relationship: log S = log C + z log A).

Q.3 (i)
Describe the menstrual cycle with hormone changes.
Cycle Overview28 days. Regulated by: GnRH (hypothalamus) → FSH, LH (anterior pituitary) → Estrogen, Progesterone (ovary)
Phases Days 1–5: Menstruation. Endometrium sheds. FSH rises. Corpus luteum degenerates. Days 6–13: Proliferative/follicular. FSH → follicle development → Estrogen surge → endometrium rebuilds. Day 14: LH surge → ovulation (secondary oocyte released). Days 15–28: Luteal. Corpus luteum → Progesterone → endometrium thickened. No fertilisation → corpus luteum degenerates → hormones drop → cycle repeats.
Q.3 (v)
Explain biomagnification with an example.
DefinitionBiomagnification (bioaccumulation in food chain) is the progressive increase in concentration of a non-biodegradable toxic substance at each successive trophic level.
Example — DDT DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane) sprayed on water → absorbed by phytoplankton (0.003 ppm) → zooplankton (0.04 ppm) → small fish (0.5 ppm) → large fish (2 ppm) → fish-eating birds (25 ppm) Effect: DDT causes eggshell thinning in birds (osprey, pelicans, eagles) → egg breakage → population decline
Q.3 (vii)
Write brief notes on: IVF, GIFT and Surrogacy.
IVFIn Vitro Fertilisation: eggs collected from ovaries, fertilised with sperm in laboratory. Embryo transferred to uterus at 8-cell stage. Used when Fallopian tubes are blocked. "Test tube baby."
GIFTGamete Intrafallopian Transfer: eggs and sperm are collected and directly transferred into the Fallopian tube for natural fertilisation to occur in the tube itself. Fallopian tube must be functional.
SurrogacyEmbryo from genetic parents (or IVF) is implanted into another woman (surrogate) who carries the pregnancy to term. Used when mother cannot carry a pregnancy due to uterine problems.

Q.4 (ii)
Explain sex determination in humans and birds.
Humans (XY System) Females: XX (homogametic — one type of gamete: X) Males: XY (heterogametic — two types: X or Y) Sex determined by sperm: Y-bearing sperm → male (XY); X-bearing sperm → female (XX) SRY gene on Y chromosome → testis determining factor → male development
Birds (ZW System) Males: ZZ (homogametic)  |  Females: ZW (heterogametic) Sex determined by egg (unlike humans): W-bearing egg → female (ZW); Z-bearing egg → male (ZZ)
Q.4 (iv)
Explain Z-scheme of light reactions in photosynthesis.
Z-Scheme The path of electrons in non-cyclic photophosphorylation is Z-shaped on an energy diagram. PS II (P680): Absorbs light → excited electrons → pass to PQ (plastoquinone) Water splitting: 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (fills electron hole in PS II) ETC: PQ → Cyt b6f complex → Plastocyanin (PC) → PS I. Proton gradient drives ATP synthesis. PS I (P700): Absorbs light → excited electrons → Ferredoxin (Fd) → NADP⁺ reductase → NADPH Net: H₂O → (PS II) → PQ → Cyt b6f → PC → (PS I) → Fd → NADP⁺ (= Z-shaped path)
Q.4 (vi)
Describe principle and steps of gel electrophoresis for DNA fingerprinting.
PrincipleDNA (negatively charged) migrates toward the anode (+) in an electric field. Smaller fragments migrate faster through agarose gel pores than larger ones → separation by size.
Steps 1. Extract DNA from sample 2. Digest with restriction enzymes → fragments 3. Load into agarose gel, apply electric current → fragments separate by size 4. Southern blotting: transfer to nylon membrane 5. Hybridise with radioactive probes (VNTR or STR probes) 6. Autoradiography: X-ray film reveals unique banding pattern = DNA fingerprint

Q.5 (i)
(a) Male reproductive system diagram + hormonal control. (b) Spermatogenesis.
Male Reproductive System Components: Testes (pair, in scrotum) → epididymis → vas deferens → seminal vesicle → prostate gland → urethra → penis Scrotum keeps testes 2-3°C cooler than body (required for spermatogenesis)
Hormonal Control GnRH (hypothalamus) → anterior pituitary → FSH + LH FSH: Acts on Sertoli cells → promotes spermatogenesis and inhibin production LH: Acts on Leydig cells → testosterone production Testosterone: Maintains secondary sexual characters, promotes spermatogenesis Inhibin: Negative feedback on FSH (controls sperm production rate)
Spermatogenesis Spermatogonium (2n) → Primary spermatocyte (2n) → Secondary spermatocyte (n) → Spermatid (n) → Spermatozoa (n) Spermatid → Spermatozoa by spermiogenesis (morphological changes, acrosome from Golgi, mitochondria form midpiece)
Q.5 (v)
(a) Recombinant DNA technology — insulin production. (b) PCR.
Insulin Production Problem: Diabetics need insulin; animal insulin causes reactions Solution: Human insulin gene (A chain + B chain) synthesised chemically Process: Gene inserted into E. coli plasmid → bacteria express human insulin → chains extracted and joined by disulfide bonds Product: Humulin (Eli Lilly, 1982) — first recombinant pharmaceutical
PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) Amplifies specific DNA sequence in vitro exponentially. Developed by Kary Mullis (1983). Step 1 — Denaturation (94°C): DNA strands separate Step 2 — Annealing (~55°C): Primers bind to complementary sequences on each strand Step 3 — Extension (72°C): Taq polymerase extends new DNA strands (5'→3') Each cycle doubles the DNA → n cycles = 2ⁿ copies
