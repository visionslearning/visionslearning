📢 1. Notice Writing Class 11 & 12 5 Marks
📌 What is a Notice?

A notice is a formal written announcement to inform a specific group of people about an event, meeting, activity, or important information. It is brief, clear and to the point.

⭐ Key Points: Always boxed/bordered. Written in third person. No personal pronouns. Word limit: 50–80 words.
📋 Format
NAME OF ORGANISATION / SCHOOL   NOTICE   Date: ___________   Title / Heading (CAPITAL LETTERS)   Body — What? When? Where? Who? Why?   Name of Issuer Designation
📝 Sample Notice
VISIONS CLASSES, AMBERNATH

NOTICE

Date: 1st September 2026

ANNUAL SCIENCE EXHIBITION

All students of Class 11 and 12 are hereby informed that the Annual Science Exhibition will be held on 15th September 2026 in the school auditorium from 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. Students wishing to participate must submit their project titles to the Science department by 8th September 2026. All are encouraged to attend.

                                 Shashikant                                  Director, Visions Classes
📄 2. Circular Writing Class 11 & 12 5 Marks
📌 What is a Circular?

A circular is an official communication sent to a group of people (employees, students, members) to convey important instructions, policies or information. Unlike a notice, a circular may be slightly longer and more detailed.

⭐ Difference from Notice: Notice = displayed on board for all. Circular = distributed/sent to specific individuals. Circular can use "You are requested..."
📋 Format
NAME OF INSTITUTION   CIRCULAR   Circular No.: ___ Date: ___________ To: (Designation/Group)   Subject: ___________________________________   Body — Purpose, Instructions, Details   You are requested / All concerned are requested...   Signature Name and Designation
📝 Sample Circular
VISIONS CLASSES, AMBERNATH

CIRCULAR

Circular No.: 05                                                 Date: 1st September 2026 To: All Students of Class 11 and 12

Subject: Change of Class Timings

This is to inform all students that with effect from 5th September 2026, the class timings will be changed from 7:00 a.m. – 9:00 a.m. to 8:00 a.m. – 10:00 a.m. This change is due to the repair work being carried out in the morning. All students are requested to note the revised timings and inform their parents accordingly.

                                 Shashikant                                  Director
✉️ 3. Formal Letter Writing Class 11 & 12 8 Marks
📌 Types of Formal Letters
  • Letter to the Editor — to express opinion on social issues
  • Letter of Complaint — to complain about a problem/service
  • Letter of Application (Job) — applying for a post
  • Letter to Authority — requesting action from officials
  • Business Letter — for orders, enquiries, complaints to companies
⭐ Formal language only. No contractions (don't → do not). Use "I/We" appropriately. Tone = polite but firm.
📋 Format
Sender's Address Date   Receiver's Designation / Name Organisation / Address   Subject: _________________________________   Dear Sir/Madam, (or Respected Sir/Madam)   Para 1: Introduction / Purpose of writing Para 2: Details / Main content Para 3: Request / Expected action   Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely, Signature Full Name
📝 Sample — Letter to Editor
B-12, New Colony,
Ambernath – 421 501.
1st September 2026

The Editor,
The Times of India,
Mumbai.

Subject: Increasing menace of plastic pollution in our city

Dear Sir/Madam,

Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I would like to draw the attention of the concerned authorities towards the growing menace of plastic pollution in our city. Despite several bans on single-use plastics, they continue to be used freely in markets and residential areas, causing serious harm to our environment and health.

I request the civic authorities to strictly implement the plastic ban and impose heavy fines on violators. Awareness campaigns in schools and public places can also help bring about a change in mindset.

I hope this matter receives the urgent attention it deserves.

Yours faithfully,
Ramesh Sharma
💌 4. Informal Letter Writing Class 11 & 12 8 Marks
📌 What is an Informal Letter?

Written to friends, family members, relatives. Uses friendly, conversational language. Can use contractions and first names. Tone = warm, personal, expressive.

📋 Format
Sender's Address Date   Dear [Name / Friend / Uncle / etc.],   Para 1: Greetings / Opening Para 2: Main purpose / Content Para 3: Closing / Asking about them   Yours lovingly / With love / Your friend, Signature / Name
📝 Sample — Letter to Friend
B-12, New Colony,
Ambernath – 421 501.
1st September 2026

Dear Rahul,

Hope you and your family are doing well. I'm writing to tell you about my recent visit to Lonavala during the summer holidays. It was absolutely wonderful!

We stayed for three days and visited Bhushi Dam, Tiger's Leap and Karla Caves. The weather was perfect and the greenery was breathtaking. The most exciting part was the night trek organised by the resort. I wish you had been there with me!

How are your studies going? Write back soon and tell me about your holidays.

With love,
Rohan
📝 5. Essay Writing Class 11 & 12 10 Marks
📌 Types of Essays
  • Descriptive — describes a person, place, thing or event
  • Narrative — tells a story or sequence of events
  • Expository — explains a topic with facts
  • Argumentative / Persuasive — presents arguments for/against
  • Reflective — personal thoughts/opinions on a topic
⭐ Word limit: 250–300 words. Structure: Introduction → Body (3 paragraphs) → Conclusion. Use linking words: however, furthermore, therefore, in conclusion, on the other hand.
📋 Structure
Title (Underline it)   Introduction: Define topic / Hook sentence / Thesis statement   Body Para 1: First main point + example/evidence Body Para 2: Second point + example Body Para 3: Third point / Counter-argument (if argumentative)   Conclusion: Summary + Personal view / Call to action
📝 Sample Essay — Social Media: Boon or Bane?
Social Media: Boon or Bane?

In the twenty-first century, social media has become an inseparable part of our lives. Platforms like Instagram, Twitter, YouTube and WhatsApp connect millions of people across the world. However, the question remains — is social media truly a blessing or a curse?

On the positive side, social media has revolutionised communication. People can instantly connect with friends and family across the globe. It is also a powerful platform for education, business and social awareness. Many students use YouTube for learning and teachers use WhatsApp for sharing study material.

However, social media also has its dark side. Excessive use leads to addiction, anxiety, and loss of productivity. Cyberbullying, fake news and privacy violations are major concerns. Young people often compare their lives to the filtered realities they see online, leading to depression and low self-esteem.

In conclusion, social media is like a double-edged sword. Its benefits are immense if used wisely, but its misuse can be harmful. The key lies in responsible and mindful use — limiting screen time, verifying information before sharing, and prioritising real-world relationships over virtual ones.
📰 6. Report Writing Class 11 & 12 8 Marks
📌 Types of Reports
  • Newspaper Report — factual account of an event for publication
  • Report for Principal / Authority — formal account of an incident/event
⭐ Always in third person. Past tense. 5W+1H: Who, What, When, Where, Why, How. Headline in CAPITALS. Byline = reporter's name.
📋 Format — Newspaper Report
HEADLINE (BOLD, CAPITAL LETTERS) By [Reporter's Name] | Place, Date   Opening paragraph: Most important facts (Who? What? Where? When?)   Middle paragraphs: Details, quotes, background information   Closing paragraph: Future action / Outcome / Official statement
📝 Sample Newspaper Report
TREE PLANTATION DRIVE HELD AT VISIONS CLASSES
By Our Correspondent | Ambernath, 1st September 2026

In a bid to promote environmental awareness, Visions Classes, Ambernath organised a tree plantation drive on its premises on Saturday, 30th August 2026. More than 150 students and staff participated enthusiastically in the event.

The drive was inaugurated by the Director, Mr Shashikant, who planted the first sapling. Speaking on the occasion, he emphasised the importance of greenery and urged students to plant at least one tree every year. A total of 100 saplings of various species were planted around the campus.

The event concluded with a pledge by all participants to protect trees and work towards a greener future. The management expressed satisfaction over the success of the programme.
🎤 7. Speech Writing Class 11 & 12 8 Marks
📌 What is a Speech?

A speech is a formal oral presentation written to be delivered in front of an audience. It may be for a school function, debate, or awareness campaign. It should be engaging, well-structured and persuasive.

⭐ Begin with addressing audience. Use rhetorical questions, examples, statistics. End with a memorable closing line or call to action. Word limit: 150–200 words.
📋 Format
Respected [Principal/Teachers/Guests] and Dear Friends,   Good morning/afternoon/evening!   Introduction: State the topic + why it matters   Body: Main points with examples/statistics   Conclusion: Summary + Inspiring closing line   Thank you!
📝 Sample Speech — Save Water
Respected teachers and dear friends,

Good morning! Today I stand before you to speak on a topic that affects every single one of us — Save Water.

Water is life. Without it, no living being can survive. Yet today, water scarcity is one of the most serious challenges facing the world. Did you know that only 3% of the Earth's water is fresh and fit for human use? And we are wasting it carelessly every day through leaking taps, long showers, and overwatering fields.

The solution lies in our hands. Simple habits — turning off taps when not in use, harvesting rainwater, reusing water wherever possible — can make a massive difference. Remember: every drop counts.

Let us pledge today to use water wisely and pass on this habit to others. As the saying goes, "Water is the driving force of all nature."

Thank you!
💡 8. Expansion of an Idea / Proverb Class 11 5 Marks
📌 What is Expansion of Idea?

Expanding an idea means explaining the deeper meaning of a proverb, quote or statement in about 100–150 words. You should explain its literal meaning, deeper meaning, and give examples from real life.

⭐ Structure: Meaning → Explanation → Examples → Conclusion/Moral. Never just repeat the proverb.
📝 Sample — "A Stitch in Time Saves Nine"
A Stitch in Time Saves Nine

This proverb literally means that if we mend a small tear in a cloth immediately, we prevent it from becoming a bigger tear that would need nine stitches instead of one. In simple words, timely action prevents bigger problems.

The deeper meaning is that if we deal with problems as soon as they arise, we save ourselves from greater trouble later. This applies to all aspects of life. A student who studies regularly does not have to struggle before exams. A patient who treats an illness early avoids serious complications. A country that solves social problems promptly avoids revolutions.

Procrastination, or the habit of postponing tasks, is one of the biggest obstacles to success. Time lost can never be recovered. Whether it is studies, health, relationships or business — early action is always better than delayed regret.

In conclusion, this proverb teaches us the value of being proactive and dealing with issues promptly before they become unmanageable.
📝 Other Important Proverbs for Practice
  • Actions speak louder than words
  • Where there is a will, there is a way
  • Honesty is the best policy
  • All that glitters is not gold
  • Knowledge is power
  • Health is wealth
  • Every cloud has a silver lining
  • United we stand, divided we fall
📋 9. Summary / Precis Writing Class 11 & 12 8 Marks
📌 What is a Precis/Summary?

A precis (precise) is a condensed version of a passage, retaining all the main ideas and essential points. It should be approximately 1/3rd of the original length. Written in your own words, in third person, past tense.

⭐ Steps: 1. Read carefully. 2. Underline key ideas. 3. Write in own words. 4. Do NOT include examples, repetitions or minor details. 5. Give a suitable title.
📋 Rules for Precis Writing
  • Write in third person (he/she/they, not I/we)
  • Use past tense throughout
  • Do NOT copy sentences from the original
  • Include only main points — no examples, no repetition
  • Word limit = approximately one-third of original
  • Give a suitable title
  • Write in continuous prose (paragraphs, not points)
💬 10. Dialogue Writing Class 11 5 Marks
📌 What is Dialogue Writing?

Dialogue writing is writing a conversation between two or more people. It should be natural, relevant to the topic and clearly show each speaker's words.

⭐ Use colon (:) after speaker's name. New line for each speaker. Keep language simple and natural. Context must be clear.
📋 Format
Title: (optional)   Person A: (dialogue) Person B: (dialogue) Person A: (dialogue) Person B: (dialogue) ... (minimum 8–10 exchanges)
📝 Sample — Dialogue on Importance of Reading
Title: The Importance of Reading

Rohan: Hi Priya! What are you reading?
Priya: I'm reading 'Wings of Fire' by Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam. Have you read it?
Rohan: No, I haven't. Is it interesting?
Priya: It's absolutely inspiring! It's the autobiography of our former President. It teaches you never to give up on your dreams.
Rohan: That sounds great. But honestly, I rarely read books. I prefer watching videos.
Priya: Videos are good, but books improve your vocabulary, concentration and imagination. They also reduce stress!
Rohan: I never thought of that. Can you suggest a good book for beginners?
Priya: Start with 'The Alchemist' by Paulo Coelho. It's short, simple and motivating.
Rohan: Alright! I'll give it a try. Thank you for the recommendation.
Priya: Sure! Once you start, you'll never stop. Happy reading!
📖 11. Story Writing Class 11 8 Marks
📌 What is Story Writing?

Story writing involves creating a narrative based on a given beginning, ending, outline or moral. A good story has a clear plot, interesting characters, a conflict and a resolution.

⭐ Elements: Setting, Characters, Plot (Beginning→Conflict→Climax→Resolution), Moral (at end). Word limit: 150–200 words. Give a title.
📋 Structure
Title   Beginning: Introduce characters and setting Rising Action: Introduce the conflict/problem Climax: The peak of the problem/tension Resolution: How the problem is solved Moral: (one sentence at the end)
📝 Sample Story — The Honest Woodcutter
The Honest Woodcutter

Once upon a time, a poor woodcutter lived near a dense forest. Every day he would go to the forest to cut wood and sell it in the village market to feed his family. One afternoon, while cutting a tree near a river, his axe accidentally slipped and fell into the deep water. The woodcutter was heartbroken as it was his only tool.

Suddenly, a river goddess appeared before him. She dived into the river and brought out a golden axe. "Is this yours?" she asked. The woodcutter shook his head. She then brought a silver axe. Again, he refused. Finally, she brought his old iron axe. The woodcutter smiled and said, "Yes! That is mine!"

Pleased by his honesty, the goddess rewarded him with all three axes. The woodcutter returned home happily and lived a prosperous life.

Moral: Honesty is the best policy.
🎙️ 12. Interview Writing Class 12 5 Marks
📌 What is Interview Writing?

Writing an interview involves preparing questions and answers for a conversation with a notable person — a scientist, author, athlete, social worker, etc. It tests your ability to ask relevant, thoughtful questions.

⭐ Prepare at least 8–10 questions. Questions should be open-ended (not Yes/No). Include a brief introduction of the person. Topics: life, achievements, challenges, advice.
📋 Format
Title: Interview with [Name/Designation] Introduction: (2–3 lines about the person)   Interviewer (Q): Question [Name] (A): Answer   (Repeat 8–10 times)   Closing: Thank the interviewee
📝 Sample Interview — A Social Worker
Interview with Ms Kavita Sharma, a dedicated social worker from Ambernath

Ms Kavita Sharma has spent the last fifteen years working for the upliftment of underprivileged children through her NGO 'Bright Futures'. We had the privilege of speaking with her about her journey.

Q: What inspired you to take up social work?
A: I grew up in a small village and saw many children drop out of school due to poverty. That disturbed me deeply and I decided to do something meaningful.

Q: What are the biggest challenges you face?
A: Lack of funding and changing mindsets are the biggest hurdles. Many families still do not value girls' education.

Q: What has been your proudest achievement so far?
A: We have helped over 500 children complete their schooling and secure government scholarships. Seeing them succeed is my greatest reward.

Q: What advice would you give to young people who wish to contribute to society?
A: Start small but start today. Even volunteering for two hours a week can make a difference. Every act of kindness matters.

Q: Thank you for your time and your inspiring work.
A: Thank you for spreading the word. Together, we can build a better society.
