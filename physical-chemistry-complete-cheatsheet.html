Raman Spectroscopy

Discovered by C.V. Raman (1928) | Nobel Prize 1930
TypeFrequencyIntensity

IR active = change in dipole moment

Mutual Exclusion: centrosymmetric (CO₂) → IR active = Raman inactive

Applications

  • Study of gases, liquids, solids
  • Complementary to IR
  • Useful for non-polar molecules (N₂, O₂) — no IR
  • IR + Raman = complete molecular structure

Polarisation & Debye Equation

PT = Pe + Pa + Po
  • Pe – Electronic: electron cloud displacement (all molecules, temp-independent)
  • Pa – Atomic/Ionic: atom displacement (temp-independent)
  • Po – Orientation: permanent dipole alignment (polar only, ↓ as T↑)

Clausius-Mosotti (non-polar)

PM = [(εr−1)/(εr+2)] × (M/ρ) = (4/3)πNAα

Debye Equation (polar)

PT = (4/3)πNAα + μ²NA/3kT = A + B/T
  • A = (4/3)πNAα (temp-independent)
  • B = μ²NA/3k (from slope)
Plot PT vs 1/T → straight line
Slope = μ²NA/3k → μ = √(3k×slope/NA)
⭐ Chapter 1 – Exam Q&A (Condensed)
Q1. What is dipole moment? How used to determine structure? (5M)
μ = q×d (Debye). If μ=0 → symmetric (CO₂ linear, BF₃ planar). If μ≠0 → unsymmetric (SO₂ angular, NH₃ pyramidal). Distinguishes cis/trans isomers (cis > trans). Identifies ortho/meta/para benzene isomers.
Q2. Why CO₂ has μ=0 but SO₂ has 1.63D? (4M)
CO₂: Linear (O=C=O) — two C=O bond moments equal & opposite → cancel → μ=0.
SO₂: Angular/bent — lone pair on S → bond moments don't cancel → net μ=1.63D.
Q3. Identify ortho/meta/para by dipole moment (5M)
μ = 2μcos(θ/2) for same substituents.
Ortho(60°)→2.6D | Meta(120°)→1.5D | Para(180°)→0D
Calculated = experimental → reliable identification.
Q4. Types of molecular vibrations with diagrams (6M)
A. Stretching (bond length change): Symmetric, Asymmetric
B. Bending: In-plane: Scissoring, Rocking | Out-of-plane: Wagging, Twisting
Non-linear: 3N–6 modes | Linear: 3N–5 modes
Q5. Fingerprint region – significance? (3M)
Region: 900–1400 cm⁻¹. Contains complex vibration bands. Unique for every compound. No two compounds show same pattern. Used for identification. Small structural change = large pattern change.
Q6. Raman Effect. Stokes & Anti-Stokes lines? (6M)
Raman (1928): monochromatic light scattered — most = Rayleigh (same freq). ~1% has different freq = Raman Effect.
Stokes: ν_s < ν₀ (molecule gains energy, low freq side, stronger)
Anti-Stokes: ν_as > ν₀ (molecule loses energy, high freq side, weaker)
Raman Shift Δν = ν₀−ν_s = ν_as−ν₀
Q7. Isotopic effect in IR? (4M)
Heavier isotope → larger μ (reduced mass) → lower ν̃ (inversely ∝ √μ)
O–H: ~3600 | O–D: ~2630 cm⁻¹ | Ratio = √2 ≈ 1.414
Used to confirm O–H or N–H groups.
Q8. IR active vs Raman active condition? (3M)
IR: change in dipole moment during vibration
Raman: change in polarisability during vibration
Mutual Exclusion: CO₂ (centrosymmetric) — IR↔Raman mutually exclusive
Q9. Debye equation. Determine μ experimentally? (5M)
PT = (4/3)πNAα + μ²NA/3kT = A + B/T
Steps: Measure PT at several T → Plot PT vs 1/T → Slope = μ²NA/3k → μ = √(3k×slope/NA) | Intercept = (4/3)πNAα = polarisability
Q10. Types of molecular polarisation? (4M)
Pe (electronic): e⁻ cloud displaced; all molecules; temp-independent
Pa (atomic/ionic): atom/ion displaced; temp-independent
Po (orientation): permanent dipoles align; polar only; temp-dependent (↓ with T↑)
Non-polar: PT=Pe+Pa | Polar: PT=Pe+Pa+Po
🌡️ CHAPTER 2 – COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES

Raoult's Law & Vapour Pressure

Colligative = depends on NUMBER of particles, not nature
p = p° × x₁  | (p°−p)/p° = x₂
M₂ = (W₂ × M₁ × p°) / (W₁ × (p°−p))

Factors affecting VP

  • Nature of liquid: weak forces → high VP
  • Temperature: ↑T → ↑VP
  • Non-volatile solute: lowers VP

Boiling Point Elevation

ΔTb = Kb × m
Kb = RT_b² × M₁ / (1000 × lv)

Freezing Point Depression

ΔTf = Kf × m
Kf = RT_f² × M₁ / (1000 × lf)
M₂ = (1000 × Kf × W₂) / (ΔTf × W₁)
Kf camphor = 37.7 K·kg·mol⁻¹ (Rast method)
Beckmann thermometer = adjustable range thermometer

Osmotic Pressure & Van't Hoff

Osmosis: solvent flows from dilute→concentrated through SPM
πV = nRT → π = CRT → M = WRT/πV

Van't Hoff Laws

  • Boyle's law: πV = const (at const T)
  • Charles' law: π/T = const (at const V)

Reverse Osmosis

Pressure > π applied on solution → solvent flows to pure side
Feed water → RO membrane → Permeate (pure) + Reject stream

Van't Hoff Factor (i)

i = observed CP / calculated CP (no dissociation)
ΔTb=i·Kb·m | ΔTf=i·Kf·m | π=i·CRT
Electrolytei
Glucose, urea1
NaCl, KCl2
BaCl₂, K₂SO₄3
⭐ Chapter 2 – Exam Q&A (Condensed)
Q1. State Raoult's Law. Derive relative lowering of VP. (6M)
Raoult's Law: p = p° × x₁
Since x₁+x₂=1 → x₁=1−x₂
p = p°(1−x₂) → p°−p = p°x₂
∴ (p°−p)/p° = x₂ = mole fraction of solute
Depends only on number of particles → colligative property.
Q2. Osmosis & osmotic pressure. Van't Hoff equation. (6M)
Osmosis: spontaneous solvent flow through SPM from dilute→conc.
π: min pressure on solution to stop osmosis.
Boyle: πV=const | Charles: π/T=const
Combined: πV = nRT → π = CRT → M = WRT/πV
Analogous to ideal gas PV=nRT.
Q3. Depression of freezing point. Derive Kf. (6M)
ΔTf = Kf × m (solute lowers VP → FP decreases)
Kf = RT_f² × M₁ / (1000 × lf)
Kf = depression when 1 mol solute dissolved in 1 kg solvent
T_f = FP of pure solvent (K) | lf = latent heat of fusion/gram
Q4. Beckmann method for mol. wt by ΔTf. (5M)
Apparatus: freezing tube + Beckmann thermometer + stirrer + cooling bath
Steps: ① Weigh W₁ solvent, cool → note T_f° ② Add W₂ solute, cool → note T_f ③ ΔTf = T_f°−T_f
M₂ = (1000×Kf×W₂)/(ΔTf×W₁)
Rast method: camphor (Kf=37.7), large Kf → accurate for small amounts
Q5. Reverse Osmosis. Applications. (4M)
RO: pressure > π applied on solution side → solvent flows solution→pure solvent (reverse of normal osmosis)
Feed water → permeate (pure) + reject stream (concentrated)
Applications: seawater desalination | household water purifiers | heavy metal/bacteria removal | industrial wastewater treatment
Q6. Van't Hoff factor. Modify colligative equations. (4M)
i = total moles of particles / moles of solute dissolved
Electrolytes dissociate → more particles → greater colligative effect
ΔTb=i·Kb·m | ΔTf=i·Kf·m | Δp/p°=i·x₂ | π=i·CRT
Glucose: i=1 | NaCl: i=2 | BaCl₂: i=3 | K₂SO₄: i=3
Q7. Vapour pressure. Factors affecting it. (4M)
VP: pressure of vapour above liquid in dynamic equilibrium at given T
Factors: ① Nature (weak IMF→high VP e.g. ether) ② Temperature (↑T→↑VP) ③ Non-volatile solute (lowers VP — Raoult's Law)
Q8. NUMERICAL – Mol. wt by Rast method
Given: Kf=37.7 | W₂=0.033g | W₁=0.450g | ΔTf=17.7K
M₂ = (1000×37.7×0.033)/(17.7×0.450)
= 1244.1/7.965 = ≈ 156 g·mol⁻¹
