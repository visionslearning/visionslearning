4.1
History of Novel
Q1. What is a Novel? Give its definition.
❓ Question

What is a novel? Define it with its key features.

✅ Answer
A novel is a long, fictional prose narrative that portrays characters, events, and settings in a complex and detailed way. It tells a story that unfolds over many chapters and explores human experience in depth. Key features include: a plot, characters, setting, theme, point of view, and style.
2 marks

4.2
To Sir, With Love — E.R. Braithwaite

📖 Novel at a Glance

Author: E.R. Braithwaite (1912–2016) Full name: Eustace Edward Ricardo Braithwaite Type: Autobiographical / Social Novel Setting: East End of London, 1950s Protagonist: Rick Braithwaite (the teacher) School: Greenslade Secondary Modern School
🎯 Themes:
Racial discrimination · Education and dignity · Student-teacher relationship · Overcoming prejudice · Identity and self-respect
Q1. Who is the protagonist of "To Sir, With Love"? Describe him briefly.
❓ Question

Who is Rick Braithwaite? Write about his background and challenges.

✅ Answer
Rick Braithwaite (E.R. Braithwaite) is the protagonist and narrator of the novel. He is a black, educated man from British Guiana (now Guyana) who served in World War II. Despite his engineering degree, he faces racial discrimination and cannot find a job in his field in post-war Britain. Out of necessity, he accepts a teaching job at Greenslade Secondary Modern School in London's East End, where he faces a class of difficult, underprivileged students. Through patience, respect and a changed approach to teaching, he transforms both his students and himself.
3–4 marks for character description
Q2. What challenges did Braithwaite face at Greenslade School?
❓ Question

Describe the initial difficulties Braithwaite faced when he began teaching at Greenslade School.

✅ Answer
When Braithwaite joined Greenslade, he faced several serious challenges:
• Students were undisciplined, indifferent and disrespectful towards teachers
• They used abusive language and deliberately destroyed class materials
• They showed no interest in learning
• Braithwaite also faced racial prejudice — both from students and some staff members
• His modern, formal teaching methods initially had no effect on the students
He overcame these by changing his approach — treating students with dignity, addressing them as "Mr." and "Miss", and connecting their lessons to real life.
2 marks for any two challenges clearly described
Q3. How did Braithwaite change his teaching method? What was the result?
❓ Question

Describe Braithwaite's new teaching approach and its effect on students.

✅ Answer
Braithwaite made a dramatic change in his teaching approach. He began to:
• Address students formally as "Mr." and "Miss" — treating them with dignity
• Focus on practical, real-life lessons rather than theoretical textbook work
• Take students on educational visits to museums, galleries and theatres
• Encourage students to discuss current events and social issues
• Establish a Student Council to give students responsibility
Result: The students gradually changed. They became more responsible, respectful and engaged. They developed trust and affection for "Sir" Braithwaite and eventually grew into caring, thoughtful young adults. The novel ends with students collecting money for a funeral — a powerful symbol of their transformation.
3 marks
Q4. What is the theme of "To Sir, With Love"? (2 marks)
❓ Question

State the central theme of the novel "To Sir, With Love".

✅ Answer
The central theme of the novel is the power of education, dignity and human respect to overcome racial prejudice and social inequality. Braithwaite shows that when a teacher treats students as individuals with dignity, they respond with effort and growth. The novel also explores racial discrimination in post-war Britain and how one man's integrity can break down barriers of prejudice and indifference.
2 marks

Q6. Write about racial discrimination in the novel. (Short answer ~50 words)
❓ Question

How does racial discrimination affect Braithwaite's life in post-war Britain?

✅ Answer
Despite his engineering degree and war service, Braithwaite is refused jobs because of his race. Even in school, some students initially refuse to interact with him due to racial prejudice. A white girl who likes him faces social pressure. These experiences show that racial discrimination was deeply rooted in British society at the time, affecting even educated, qualified individuals.
2 marks — any 2 relevant points
4.3
Around the World in Eighty Days — Jules Verne

📖 Novel at a Glance

Author: Jules Gabriel Verne (1828–1905) Nationality: French Type: Adventure / Science Fiction Novel Setting: Around the world — 1872 Protagonist: Phileas Fogg Companion: Passepartout (servant)
🎯 Themes:
Adventure and perseverance · Precision and discipline · Trust and friendship · Technology and progress · Cultural diversity
Q1. Who is Phileas Fogg? Describe his character.
❓ Question

Describe the character of Phileas Fogg with examples from the novel.

✅ Answer
Phileas Fogg is the eccentric English gentleman and protagonist of the novel. He is:
• Extraordinarily precise and punctual — his life runs like clockwork
• Wealthy but mysterious — nobody knows how he earned his money
• Calm and unemotional — never loses composure even in crisis
• Generous and fair — he helps Aouda, rescues her from death, and is loyal to Passepartout
• Member of the Reform Club, London
He bets £20,000 with his club members that he can travel around the world in exactly 80 days — and this bet drives the entire plot of the novel.
3 marks — any 3 qualities with examples
Q2. Why does Phileas Fogg bet to travel around the world in 80 days?
❓ Question

What was the bet and how did the journey begin?

✅ Answer
At the Reform Club in London, Fogg's fellow members argue that a new railway line in India makes it impossible to travel around the world in 80 days. Fogg disagrees and bets £20,000 (half his fortune) that he can complete the journey in exactly 80 days. He leaves immediately on October 2, 1872, with his new French servant Passepartout, carrying a travel bag and a diary to record every step of the journey. He must return to London by December 21, 1872 — 80 days later — to win the bet.
2 marks
Q3. Who is Passepartout? What is his role?
❓ Question

Describe Passepartout and his importance in the novel.

✅ Answer
Jean Passepartout is Fogg's loyal French servant who joins him just before the journey begins. He is:
• Cheerful, enthusiastic and resourceful — a comic contrast to Fogg's coldness
• Deeply loyal — he risks his life multiple times to help Fogg
• Clumsy but well-intentioned — his mistakes sometimes cause delays
He is the emotional heart of the novel — his warmth makes the story human. Together, Fogg and Passepartout represent the perfect pair: logic and emotion working together to overcome challenges.
2 marks
Q4. Who is Aouda? What is her importance?
❓ Question

Describe Aouda's character and her significance in the novel.

✅ Answer
Aouda is a young, educated Indian woman whom Fogg and Passepartout rescue in India. She was about to be forced into a ritual sacrifice (Sati) and Fogg bravely saves her life. Aouda then accompanies them on the rest of the journey. She is intelligent, grateful and composed. At the end of the novel, Aouda proposes to Fogg and they get married. Her presence humanises Fogg and adds a romantic dimension to what is otherwise a story of cold calculation and adventure.
2 marks
Q5. How does Fogg win the bet? What is the twist at the end?
❓ Question

Describe the climax and ending of the novel.

✅ Answer
Fogg returns to London and believes he has arrived one day late — he thinks he has lost the bet. He is heartbroken. However, he then realises that because he travelled eastward (in the direction of the sun), he gained one full day without realising it. He had forgotten to count this "extra day" gained by crossing time zones. He rushes to the Reform Club in the final seconds and arrives exactly on time — winning the £20,000 bet! The story ends with Fogg marrying Aouda, having gained not just the money but, more importantly, love and friendship.
3 marks

The Sign of Four — Sir Arthur Conan Doyle

📖 Novel at a Glance

Author: Sir Arthur Ignatius Conan Doyle (1859–1930) Nationality: British (Scottish) Type: Detective / Mystery Novel Setting: London & Thames River, 1888 Protagonist: Sherlock Holmes Narrator: Dr. John H. Watson
🎯 Themes:
Justice and crime · Logic vs. emotion · Greed and betrayal · Loyalty and friendship · British imperialism / colonial past
Q1. Who is Sherlock Holmes? Describe his character and methods.
❓ Question

Describe the character of Sherlock Holmes with his methods of detection.

✅ Answer
Sherlock Holmes is the brilliant fictional detective created by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. In "The Sign of Four" he is:
• Extraordinarily observant — notices what others miss ("Elementary, my dear Watson")
• Logical and analytical — uses deductive reasoning to solve crimes
• Eccentric — plays the violin, uses cocaine when bored (a controversial detail)
• Emotionally detached — rarely shows feelings; sees emotion as a hindrance
• Confident and direct — sometimes arrogant but always correct
His method: observe every detail, form hypotheses, test them, deduce the truth. He solves the mystery of the Agra Treasure through brilliant observation of footprints, tools, and physical evidence.
3 marks
Q2. Who is Dr. Watson? What is his role in the novel?
❓ Question

Describe Dr. Watson's character and his importance in "The Sign of Four".

✅ Answer
Dr. John H. Watson is Holmes's close friend and the narrator of the novel. He is:
• A retired army doctor — brave, loyal and practical
• The emotional contrast to Holmes — he shows warmth, empathy and falls in love with Mary Morstan during the case
• The reader's guide — through his eyes, we experience the mystery as it unfolds
Watson is not as brilliant as Holmes but his loyalty and courage are essential. His narration makes Holmes's genius accessible and the story human. The novel also ends with Watson's romance with Mary Morstan adding a personal dimension.
2 marks
Q3. What is the mystery in "The Sign of Four"? Briefly describe the plot.
❓ Question

Summarise the plot of "The Sign of Four" in about 80 words.

✅ Answer
Mary Morstan comes to Holmes for help — she has been receiving mysterious pearls for years and her father disappeared. Holmes and Watson investigate and discover the secret of the "Agra Treasure" — a vast fortune stolen in India involving four men who made a pact (the "Sign of Four"). One of them, Jonathan Small, was betrayed by his partners. The treasure is stolen by Small and his companion Tonga, leading to a thrilling chase on the River Thames. Holmes solves the case, Small is arrested, but the treasure is lost forever in the river.
2–3 marks for plot summary
Q4. Who are the "Sign of Four"? What was their pact?
❓ Question

Explain who the four men are and what their pact involved.

✅ Answer
The "Sign of Four" refers to four men who made a secret pact to steal the Agra Treasure in India:
1. Jonathan Small — English soldier; the main villain and narrator of the backstory
2. Abdullah Khan — Indian soldier
3. Dost Akbar — Indian soldier
4. Mahomet Singh — Indian soldier
They murdered a merchant for his treasure, hid it and swore loyalty to each other. However, they were betrayed, imprisoned and the treasure hidden in a box. Jonathan Small later escapes and seeks revenge on those who betrayed him, leading to the events of the novel.
2 marks — names + brief pact description
Q5. What is the Agra Treasure? What happens to it at the end?
❓ Question

Describe the Agra Treasure and its fate in the novel.

✅ Answer
The Agra Treasure is a vast collection of precious jewels and gems that was originally the property of a wealthy Indian merchant. It was stolen by the four men during the Indian Mutiny (1857). The treasure was hidden and its secret passed down through the years. Mary Morstan's father, Captain Morstan, was one of those who knew about it. In the climax, during the chase on the Thames, Jonathan Small throws the treasure chest into the river rather than let Holmes capture it. The treasure is lost forever — but justice is served and Mary and Watson find love.
2–3 marks

Q8. What is the theme of "The Sign of Four"? (Short answer)
❓ Question

State the main theme of the novel in 2–3 sentences.

✅ Answer
The central theme of "The Sign of Four" is the triumph of logic, reason and justice over crime and greed. Holmes's deductive method shows that careful observation and reasoning will always expose the truth. The novel also explores themes of loyalty and betrayal — the four men's pact ultimately destroys them — and the legacy of British colonialism in India, as the treasure represents wealth stolen from an occupied land.
2 marks
