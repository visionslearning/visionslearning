Ch 1 — Chemical Reactions & Equations
Q1
What is a chemical reaction? Write any 4 characteristics of a chemical reaction.
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer

Chemical Reaction: A process in which one or more substances (reactants) are converted into one or more new substances (products) with different properties is called a chemical reaction.

Characteristics:

1. Change in colour — e.g., iron rusts from grey to reddish-brown

2. Evolution of gas — e.g., Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑

3. Change in temperature — exothermic (heat released) or endothermic (heat absorbed)

4. Formation of precipitate — e.g., AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃

5. Change in smell / state

Q2
What is a balanced chemical equation? Balance the following: Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
🔴 High Freq2 Marks
✅ Answer

Balanced equation: An equation in which the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides (reactants = products), following the Law of Conservation of Mass.

3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

Check: Fe = 3, H = 8, O = 4 on both sides ✓

Q3
Explain the types of chemical reactions with one example each: (i) Combination (ii) Decomposition (iii) Displacement (iv) Double Displacement
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

(i) Combination: Two or more substances combine to form one product.

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

(ii) Decomposition: One substance breaks into two or more simpler substances.

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electrolysis)

(iii) Displacement: More reactive element displaces less reactive element from its compound.

Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

(iv) Double Displacement: Exchange of ions between two compounds; often forms precipitate.

AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
Q4
What is oxidation and reduction? Give one example of a redox reaction. What is a reducing agent?
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Oxidation: Loss of electrons OR gain of oxygen OR loss of hydrogen.

Reduction: Gain of electrons OR loss of oxygen OR gain of hydrogen.

Redox reaction example:

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

CuO is reduced (loses O); H₂ is oxidised (gains O)

Reducing agent: A substance that causes reduction in another substance while itself getting oxidised. In above: H₂ is the reducing agent.

Ch 3 — Metals & Non-Metals
Q5
Write 4 physical properties and 4 chemical properties of metals.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

Physical Properties of Metals:

1. Lustrous (shiny) — 2. Good conductor of heat and electricity — 3. Malleable (can be beaten into sheets) — 4. Ductile (can be drawn into wires) — 5. High melting and boiling points (except Na, K, Hg)

Chemical Properties of Metals:

1. React with oxygen: 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O

2. React with water: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑

3. React with acids: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑

4. React with salt solutions (displacement): Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

Q6
What is the reactivity series? Write 5 metals in order of decreasing reactivity. What is its importance?
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Reactivity Series: An arrangement of metals in decreasing order of their chemical reactivity (ability to lose electrons / react with water, acid, salt solutions).

Order (decreasing reactivity):

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Ag > Au

Importance:

1. Predicts whether a displacement reaction will occur (more reactive displaces less reactive)

2. Helps choose suitable metals for extraction methods

3. Determines which metals can react with water/acids

Q7
What is corrosion? What is rusting of iron? Write the chemical equation. How can rusting be prevented?
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer

Corrosion: Gradual deterioration of a metal by chemical reaction with its environment (air, water, acids). It is a slow oxidation process.

Rusting of Iron: When iron is exposed to oxygen and moisture (water), it slowly forms a reddish-brown coating of iron oxide (rust).

4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust)

Prevention of Rusting:

1. Painting/varnishing — 2. Galvanizing (coating with zinc) — 3. Electroplating — 4. Alloying (stainless steel) — 5. Applying oil/grease

Ch 4 — Carbon & its Compounds
Q8
What is covalent bond? Why does carbon form covalent bonds? Explain with example.
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Covalent Bond: A chemical bond formed by the sharing of electron pairs between two atoms.

Why carbon forms covalent bonds:

1. Carbon has 4 valence electrons — it would need to gain or lose 4 electrons to achieve noble gas configuration, which requires too much energy.

2. It is easier for carbon to share 4 electrons with other atoms — forming 4 covalent bonds.

Example: Methane (CH₄) — carbon shares one electron each with 4 hydrogen atoms. Each H provides 1 electron → 4 C-H bonds formed.

C + 4H → CH₄ (4 covalent bonds)

Ch 6 — Refraction of Light
Q10
State the laws of refraction. What is the refractive index? Give the formula and unit.
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer

Laws of Refraction:

1. The incident ray, refracted ray and normal all lie in the same plane.

2. Snell's Law: The ratio of sin of angle of incidence to sin of angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media.

n = sin i / sin r = c / v = n₂/n₁

Refractive Index (n): The ratio of speed of light in vacuum (c) to speed of light in the medium (v).

n = c / v

Unit: No unit (dimensionless). n > 1 for all optical media.

Q11
What is total internal reflection? State conditions for TIR. Give two applications.
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Total Internal Reflection (TIR): When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, light is completely reflected back into the denser medium — no refraction occurs.

Conditions for TIR:

1. Light must travel from denser to rarer medium (e.g., glass to air)

2. Angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle (i > θc)

Applications:

1. Optical fibre — used in internet cables and medical endoscopes

2. Diamond sparkles due to TIR from many internal surfaces

3. Mirage in deserts — TIR of sky light in hot air layers

Ch 1 — Life Processes: Nutrition
Q1
What is photosynthesis? Write the equation. What are the conditions necessary for photosynthesis?
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer

Photosynthesis: The process by which green plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to produce glucose (food) and release oxygen as a by-product.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Conditions necessary:

1. Sunlight — source of energy for the reaction

2. Chlorophyll — green pigment in leaves that absorbs sunlight

3. CO₂ — enters through stomata in leaves

4. Water — absorbed from soil through roots; transported to leaves

Ch 2 — Life Processes: Transportation
Q3
Draw and label the structure of the human heart. Explain double circulation.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

Human Heart: 4-chambered organ — Right Atrium (RA), Right Ventricle (RV), Left Atrium (LA), Left Ventricle (LV).

Double Circulation: Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit.

Pulmonary circulation: Right side of heart → lungs (for oxygenation) → Left side of heart

Systemic circulation: Left side → entire body → Right side

Key points:

• Left side carries oxygenated blood; Right side carries deoxygenated blood

• Left ventricle has thicker walls (pumps blood to entire body under high pressure)

• Valves prevent backflow of blood

Q4
What is the nephron? Explain the process of urine formation in the nephron with a labelled diagram.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

Nephron: The functional unit of the kidney. Each kidney has about 1 million nephrons. It filters blood and forms urine.

Process of urine formation:

Step 1 — Filtration: Blood enters glomerulus (capillary knot). High pressure forces water, glucose, amino acids, urea, salts into Bowman's capsule → forms filtrate.

Step 2 — Reabsorption: As filtrate passes through tubule, useful substances (glucose, amino acids, water, salts) are reabsorbed back into blood.

Step 3 — Secretion: Remaining waste (urea, excess salts) stays in tubule and forms urine.

Urine: Water + urea + salts → flows to ureter → bladder → excreted through urethra.

Ch 4 — Heredity & Evolution
Q5
State Mendel's laws of inheritance. Explain monohybrid cross with a suitable example and Punnett square.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

Mendel's Laws:

1. Law of Segregation: Each trait is controlled by two alleles; during gamete formation, the alleles separate so each gamete carries only one allele.

2. Law of Independent Assortment: Genes for different traits are inherited independently of each other (when on different chromosomes).

Monohybrid Cross (Tall × Dwarf pea plant):

T = Tall (dominant); t = dwarf (recessive)

Parents: TT × tt → F1: all Tt (Tall)

F1 × F1: Tt × Tt

Punnett square: TT : Tt : tt = 1:2:1 (genotypic); Tall:Dwarf = 3:1 (phenotypic)

Conclusion: Dominant trait (Tall) appears 3× more than recessive trait (Dwarf) in F2 generation.

Q6
How is the sex of a child determined in human beings? Who is responsible for the sex of a child?
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Sex chromosomes in humans: Females have XX chromosomes; Males have XY chromosomes.

Gametes formed:

• Mother (XX) produces only X-type eggs.

• Father (XY) produces X-type sperm (50%) and Y-type sperm (50%).

Sex determination:

• If X sperm fertilises egg → XX → Girl

• If Y sperm fertilises egg → XY → Boy

Ratio: 50% boys : 50% girls

Conclusion: The sex of a child is determined by the father's sperm, not the mother. The mother always contributes X chromosome.

Ch 5 — Magnetic Effects of Current
Q7
State Fleming's Left Hand Rule. When is it used? Explain the working of an electric motor with a labelled diagram.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

Fleming's Left Hand Rule: Stretch the thumb, index finger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular. If the index finger points in the direction of the magnetic field (B) and the middle finger points in the direction of current (I), then the thumb points in the direction of the force (F) on the conductor.

Used for: Finding the direction of force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (used in motors).

Electric Motor: A device that converts electrical energy into mechanical (rotational) energy.

Working:

1. A rectangular coil (ABCD) is placed in a magnetic field between N and S poles.

2. When current flows, force acts on arms AB and CD (by Fleming's LHR) in opposite directions → coil rotates.

3. Split ring commutator reverses current every half rotation → coil continues to rotate in same direction.

🧪 Activity — Ch 5: Magnetic Effect of Current
Q: Describe an activity to show that a current-carrying conductor experiences a force in a magnetic field.
  1. Place a small aluminium rod AB horizontally across two rails connected to a battery and switch.
  2. Place a horseshoe magnet so that the rod AB lies between its poles.
  3. Close the switch and pass current through the rod. Observe the rod.
  4. Reverse the direction of current and observe again.
Observation: When current flows, the rod jumps in one direction. When current is reversed, the rod jumps in the opposite direction.
Conclusion: A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force (Lorentz force). Direction determined by Fleming's Left Hand Rule.

Q1
Solve: 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0 by factorisation method.
🔴 Every Year2 Marks
✅ Answer
2x² − 5x + 3 = 0 Split middle term: −5x = −2x − 3x (product = 2×3 = 6) 2x² − 2x − 3x + 3 = 0 2x(x − 1) − 3(x − 1) = 0 (2x − 3)(x − 1) = 0
x = 3/2 or x = 1

Find the discriminant of x² − 4x + 4 = 0 and determine the nature of roots.
🔴 High Freq2 Marks
✅ Answer
a = 1, b = −4, c = 4 D = b² − 4ac = (−4)² − 4(1)(4) = 16 − 16 = 0
D = 0 → Two equal real roots
x = −b/2a = 4/2 = 2

Both roots are equal: x = 2, x = 2

Geometry — Circle Theorems
Q6
State and prove the theorem: "The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact."
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer

Statement: The tangent drawn at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.

Given: Circle with centre O, tangent AB at point P; OP is radius.

To Prove: OP ⊥ AB

Proof: Let Q be any other point on tangent AB. Since AB is a tangent, it touches the circle only at P. So Q lies outside the circle → OQ > OP (radius is shortest distance from centre to tangent line). This is true for every point Q on AB except P. Therefore, OP is the shortest distance from O to AB. The shortest distance from a point to a line is perpendicular. Hence OP ⊥ AB. ∎

Q7
From an external point, two tangents are drawn to a circle. Prove that they are equal in length.
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer

Given: External point P; tangents PA and PB to circle with centre O, touching at A and B.

To Prove: PA = PB

Proof: In triangles OAP and OBP:

OA = OB (radii of same circle)

OP = OP (common side)

∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (radius ⊥ tangent)

By RHS congruence: △OAP ≅ △OBP

∴ PA = PB (CPCT) ✓

History — Nationalism in India
Q1
What was the Non-Cooperation Movement? When was it launched? What were its main features?
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Non-Cooperation Movement: A mass movement launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920 against British rule, calling upon Indians to withdraw cooperation from the British government.

Main Features:

1. Surrender of titles and honorary offices given by British

2. Boycott of government schools, colleges, law courts

3. Boycott of foreign goods — use of Swadeshi goods

4. Resignation from government jobs

5. Boycott of elections

End: Withdrawn by Gandhi in February 1922 after Chauri Chaura incident (police station set on fire by mob).

Q2
What was the Civil Disobedience Movement? Explain the Salt March (Dandi March) with its significance.
🔴 Every Year4 Marks
✅ Answer

Civil Disobedience Movement (1930): A movement in which Indians refused to follow unjust laws of the British government. Launched by Gandhi with the specific demand to abolish the salt tax.

Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930):

Gandhi walked 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi (a coastal village in Gujarat) with 78 followers. On reaching Dandi, he made salt from seawater — thus breaking the Salt Law which gave the British monopoly over salt production.

Significance:

1. First mass movement involving all sections of society including women

2. Attracted worldwide attention to India's freedom struggle

3. Showed that Indians could challenge British laws non-violently

Economics — Development
Q4
What is development? Explain two important aspects of development. Why do different countries have different development goals?
🔴 High Freq3 Marks
✅ Answer

Development: Improvement in the quality of life and standard of living of people. It is not just about income but includes education, health, freedom, security and equality.

Two important aspects:

1. Economic development — increase in per capita income, industrialisation

Different development goals: Different people have different needs, aspirations and situations. A landless labourer wants regular employment; a farmer wants better crop prices; a student wants good education. Hence, development goals differ based on individual and social needs.

Grammar — Tenses, Voice, Narration
Q1
Change the following sentences from Active to Passive voice: (a) She writes a letter. (b) They were playing cricket. (c) The teacher has corrected the papers.
🔴 Every Year3 Marks
✅ Answer
(a) A letter is written by her. (Simple Present → is/am/are + V3)
