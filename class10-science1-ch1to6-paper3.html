Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
Ch2 i. What is the Modern Periodic Law?
The Modern Periodic Law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers.
Ch3 ii. What is a redox reaction?
A redox reaction is one in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously — one substance loses electrons (oxidised) and another gains electrons (reduced); e.g., CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O.
Ch4 iii. What is electric power? Give its formula and SI unit.
Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is consumed in a circuit; P = VI = I²R = V²/R, and its SI unit is Watt (W).
Ch5 iv. What is thermal expansion?
Thermal expansion is the increase in the dimensions (length, area, or volume) of a substance when it is heated, due to increased kinetic energy of its particles.
Ch6 v. State the first law of refraction.
The first law of refraction states that the incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal to the refracting surface at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

Ch3 B. Types of decomposition reactions (2 marks)
  • 1. Thermal decomposition: Breakdown caused by heat. E.g., CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (limestone → quicklime)
  • 2. Electrolytic decomposition: Breakdown by passing electric current. E.g., 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electrolysis of water)
  • 3. Photolytic / Photochemical decomposition: Breakdown by light. E.g., 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ (used in black & white photography)
  • Decomposition is the opposite of a combination reaction.
  • These reactions generally require energy input (heat, electricity, or light).
Ch4 C. Electric motor — principle and working (2 marks)
  • Principle: A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a mechanical force (Fleming's Left Hand Rule). This is the motor effect.
  • Working: When current flows through the coil placed in a magnetic field, forces act on the two sides of the coil in opposite directions, causing it to rotate.
  • The split-ring commutator reverses the current direction every half rotation, ensuring the coil rotates continuously in one direction.
  • Energy conversion: Electrical energy → Mechanical (kinetic) energy.
  • Uses: Electric fans, washing machines, water pumps, electric vehicles.
📐 Diagram: Rectangular coil ABCD between magnets N and S. Label: armature, commutator, brushes, axle, direction of current, direction of rotation.
Ch5 D. Anomalous expansion of water (2 marks)
  • Most substances expand on heating and contract on cooling. But water behaves abnormally between 0°C and 4°C.
  • Water contracts (density increases) as it is heated from 0°C to 4°C — then expands normally above 4°C.
  • Maximum density of water is at 4°C (1000 kg/m³).
  • Below 4°C, water expands on further cooling. Ice (0°C) is less dense than water → ice floats on water.
  • Importance: Aquatic life survives in winter — ice forms on top of ponds/lakes, water below remains liquid at ~4°C providing habitat for fish.
Ch6 E. Atmospheric refraction — apparent shift of stars and Sun (2 marks)
  • Atmospheric refraction is the refraction of light as it passes through the different layers of Earth's atmosphere (each layer has different density → different refractive index).
  • Twinkling of stars: Light from a star continuously refracts through moving atmospheric layers of varying density → apparent position of star changes rapidly → star appears to twinkle.
  • Planets do not twinkle because they are closer and subtend a small disc → light from many points averages out.
  • Apparent shift: Stars near the horizon appear slightly higher than their actual position due to atmospheric refraction bending light upward.
  • Sunrise and sunset: The Sun is visible about 2 minutes before it actually rises and 2 minutes after it actually sets, due to atmospheric refraction.

Ch1 A. Explain the concept of gravitational potential energy and derive its expression. Also explain the relationship between gravitational PE and kinetic energy during free fall. (4 marks)
Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE):
  • The energy possessed by an object due to its position above the ground (in a gravitational field) is called gravitational potential energy.
  • Derivation: Work done against gravity in lifting an object of mass m to height h: W = F × d = mg × h ∴ GPE = mgh where m = mass (kg), g = 9.8 m/s², h = height (m).
  • GPE is zero at the reference level (ground) and increases with height.
During Free Fall (Conservation of Energy):
  • When an object falls freely, GPE converts to Kinetic Energy (KE).
  • At height h: GPE = mgh, KE = 0, Total Energy = mgh
  • At midpoint h/2: GPE = mgh/2, KE = mgh/2, Total = mgh
  • Just before hitting ground: GPE = 0, KE = mgh = ½mv², Total = mgh
  • Total mechanical energy (KE + PE) remains constant at every point during free fall — this is conservation of mechanical energy.
  • This applies in absence of air resistance (ideal free fall).
Ch3 B. What are exothermic and endothermic reactions? Give two examples of each with chemical equations. (4 marks)
Exothermic Reactions:
  • Reactions that release heat energy to the surroundings are called exothermic reactions.
  • Temperature of surroundings increases; ΔH is negative.
  • Examples:
    • 1. Combustion of methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat
    • 2. Respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
    • 3. Neutralisation: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O + Heat
Endothermic Reactions:
  • Reactions that absorb heat energy from the surroundings are called endothermic reactions.
  • Temperature of surroundings decreases; ΔH is positive.
  • Examples:
    • 1. Thermal decomposition of limestone: CaCO₃ + Heat → CaO + CO₂
    • 2. Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
    • 3. Decomposition of water: 2H₂O + Electricity → 2H₂ + O₂
Ch4 C. Explain the heating effect of electric current. Derive Joule's law of heating. Give three applications. (4 marks)
Heating Effect of Electric Current:
  • When electric current flows through a conductor, it faces resistance. The electrical energy is converted into heat energy. This is called the heating effect of electric current (or Joule heating).
Derivation of Joule's Law:
  • Work done by current I against resistance R in time t:
    Voltage V = Work done/Charge = W/Q → W = VQ = VIt
    By Ohm's law: V = IR ∴ H = I²Rt where H = heat produced (J), I = current (A), R = resistance (Ω), t = time (s).
  • This is Joule's Law: Heat ∝ I² × R × t
Applications:
  • 1. Electric heater / iron: Resistance wire (nichrome) produces heat when current flows.
  • 2. Electric bulb (filament): Tungsten filament heats to ~3000°C and emits light.
  • 3. Electric fuse: Thin wire with low melting point melts when excess current flows, protecting the circuit.

Ch1 A. A body of mass 5 kg is at a height of 10 m above the ground. Find its gravitational potential energy. (g = 10 m/s²) (2 marks)
Given: m = 5 kg, h = 10 m, g = 10 m/s²
Formula: PE = mgh
PE = 5 × 10 × 10
∴ Gravitational PE = 500 J
Ch1 B. The weight of an object on Earth is 60 N. What is its weight on Moon? (g_moon = g_earth / 6) (2 marks)
Given: W_Earth = 60 N, g_Moon = g_Earth/6
W = mg; on Moon: W_Moon = m × g_Moon = m × g_Earth/6
W_Moon = W_Earth / 6 = 60 / 6
∴ Weight on Moon = 10 N
Ch4 C. An electric iron of resistance 40 Ω is connected to 220 V supply. Find the current drawn and the power consumed. (2 marks)
Given: R = 40 Ω, V = 220 V
Current: I = V/R = 220/40 = 5.5 A
Power: P = VI = 220 × 5.5
∴ I = 5.5 A, Power P = 1210 W
Ch5 D. A metal ball of mass 1 kg is heated from 20°C to 120°C. If the specific heat of metal is 500 J/kg·K, find the heat absorbed. (2 marks)
Given: m = 1 kg, T₁ = 20°C, T₂ = 120°C, c = 500 J/kg·K
ΔT = 120 – 20 = 100 K
Formula: Q = mcΔT
Q = 1 × 500 × 100
∴ Heat absorbed Q = 50,000 J = 5 × 10⁴ J
Ch6 E. A ray of light passes from air into glass. The angle of incidence is 45° and the refractive index of glass is 1.5. Find the angle of refraction. (sin 45° = 0.707) (2 marks)
Given: i = 45°, n = 1.5 (air to glass), sin 45° = 0.707
Formula: n = sin i / sin r → sin r = sin i / n
sin r = 0.707 / 1.5 = 0.471
r = sin⁻¹(0.471) ≈ 28.1°
∴ Angle of refraction ≈ 28.1°
