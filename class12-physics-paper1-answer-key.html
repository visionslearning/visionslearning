Q.1 (i)
The SI unit of electric flux is:
(A) N·m²/C
(B) N/C
(C) C/N·m²
(D) V·m
✔ Correct Answer: (A) N·m²/C
Explanation Electric flux Φ = E⃗ · A⃗ = EA cosθ Unit = (N/C) × m² = N·m²/C  (also written as V·m or N·m²·C⁻¹)
Q.1 (ii)
When the separation between the plates of a capacitor is doubled, its capacitance:
(A) doubles
(B) becomes half
(C) remains same
(D) becomes four times
✔ Correct Answer: (B) becomes half
Explanation C = ε₀A/d   If d → 2d:   C' = ε₀A/2d = C/2 Capacitance is inversely proportional to plate separation.
Q.1 (iii)
The SI unit of self-inductance is:
(A) Tesla
(B) Weber
(C) Henry
(D) Farad
✔ Correct Answer: (C) Henry
Explanation e = −L(dI/dt) → L = e/(dI/dt) = V/(A/s) = V·s/A = Henry (H) 1 H = 1 Ω·s = 1 Wb/A
Q.1 (iv)
When light travels from glass to air, it:
(A) bends towards the normal
(B) bends away from the normal
(C) travels straight
(D) gets reflected totally
✔ Correct Answer: (B) bends away from the normal
Refraction from glass (denser) to air (rarer) — θ₂ > θ₁ (bends away from normal)
Explanation Snell's law: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂. Since n_glass > n_air, sinθ₂ > sinθ₁ → θ₂ > θ₁ Going denser → rarer: angle of refraction > angle of incidence → bends AWAY from normal.
Q.1 (v)
The energy of a photon is given by:
(A) hλ
(B) hν
(C) h/ν
(D) ν/h
✔ Correct Answer: (B) hν
Explanation E = hν = hc/λ   (Planck's quantum relation) h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, ν = frequency. Higher frequency → higher energy photon.
Q.1 (vi)
The half-life of a radioactive substance is 10 years. After 40 years, the fraction remaining is:
(A) 1/8
(B) 1/16
(C) 1/4
(D) 1/32
✔ Correct Answer: (B) 1/16
Solution Given: T₁/₂ = 10 years, t = 40 years Number of half-lives n = t/T₁/₂ = 40/10 = 4 Fraction remaining = (1/2)ⁿ = (1/2)⁴ = 1/16
Q.1 (vii)
In the photoelectric effect, the stopping potential depends on:
(A) intensity of light
(B) frequency of light
(C) area of metal surface
(D) temperature of metal
✔ Correct Answer: (B) frequency of light
Explanation Einstein's equation: eV₀ = hν − φ → V₀ = (hν − φ)/e V₀ depends only on ν (frequency). Intensity determines the number of electrons (photocurrent), NOT their energy.
Q.1 (viii)
A transistor has β = 100 and I_b = 20 μA. The collector current I_c is:
(A) 20 mA
(B) 2 mA
(C) 0.2 mA
(D) 200 μA
✔ Correct Answer: (B) 2 mA
Solution β = 100, I_b = 20 μA = 20 × 10⁻⁶ A I_c = β × I_b = 100 × 20 × 10⁻⁶ = 2000 × 10⁻⁶ A = 2 mA
Q.1 (ix)
In Young's double-slit experiment, if the slit separation is halved, the fringe width:
(A) remains same
(B) doubles
(C) becomes half
(D) becomes four times
✔ Correct Answer: (B) doubles
Explanation β = λD/d   If d → d/2:   β' = λD/(d/2) = 2λD/d = 2β Fringe width is inversely proportional to slit separation. Halving d doubles β.
Q.1 (x)
Lenz's law is a consequence of the law of conservation of:
(A) charge
(B) energy
(C) momentum
(D) mass
✔ Correct Answer: (B) energy
Explanation Lenz's law: induced current opposes the change in flux causing it. If it aided, perpetual motion would result — violating energy conservation.

Q.3 (v)  [2 Marks]
Explain Huygens' principle of wave propagation.
Secondary wavelets from old wavefront form the new wavefront
Point 1Every point on a given wavefront acts as a fresh source of secondary spherical wavelets spreading in all directions with the same speed as the original wave.
Point 2The new wavefront at any subsequent instant is the forward envelope (common tangent) of all these secondary wavelets.
UsesExplains reflection, refraction, and diffraction of light.

Q.4 (ii)  [3 Marks]
Derive the mirror formula for a concave mirror.
Fig: Concave mirror ray diagram — object AB beyond C gives real, inverted, diminished image A'B' between C and F. Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f.
SetupObject AB at u, image A'B' at v, focal length f — all measured from pole P.
Step 1 △A'B'F ~ △MPF (AA similarity): A'B'/MP = A'F/FP = (v−f)/f   ...(1)
Step 2 △A'B'P ~ △ABP (AA similarity): A'B'/AB = v/u   ...(2)
Derivation From (1) and (2): v/u = (v−f)/f → vf = uv − uf Dividing both sides by uvf: 1/u = 1/f − 1/v → 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Mirror Formula
1/v + 1/u = 1/f

Q.5 (i)  [4 Marks]
(a) State and prove Gauss's law in electrostatics. (b) Using Gauss's law, derive the expression for electric field due to an infinite plane sheet of charge.
Gaussian pillbox for infinite plane sheet with charge density σ
Gauss's Law — StatementThe total electric flux through any closed surface = q_enclosed / ε₀
∮E⃗·dA⃗ = q_enc/ε₀
Proof For point charge q on sphere of radius r: E = kq/r² (uniform) Φ = E × 4πr² = (kq/r²) × 4πr² = q/ε₀ ✓
Infinite Plane Sheet Gaussian surface = cylinder (pillbox) of face area A, straddling the sheet Only flat faces contribute (curved side: E ⊥ dA → Φ = 0) Total flux = EA + EA = 2EA Charge enclosed = σA 2EA = σA/ε₀
Result
E = σ/(2ε₀) — directed perpendicular to sheet, away from +σ

Convex lens forming real image of object at 30 cm

Q.5 (iv)  [4 Marks]
(a) Derive Einstein's photoelectric equation and explain each term. (b) Light of frequency ν = 8 × 10¹⁴ Hz falls on a metal with work function φ = 3 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons.
Photoelectric effect: photons eject electrons from metal surface
Einstein's Equation One photon gives ALL its energy hν to one electron. hν = φ + KE_max   →   KE_max = hν − φ
Each Term hν = energy of incident photon  |  φ = hν₀ = work function (min. energy to free electron)  |  KE_max = max kinetic energy of emitted electron
Numerical ν = 8×10¹⁴ Hz, φ = 3 eV, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s hν = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 8×10¹⁴ = 53.04×10⁻²⁰ J = 53.04×10⁻²⁰/1.6×10⁻¹⁹ eV = 3.315 eV KE_max = 3.315 − 3.000 = 0.315 eV
Answer
KE_max = 0.315 eV = 5.04 × 10⁻²⁰ J
Q.5 (vi)  [4 Marks]
(a) Draw and explain energy band diagrams for a conductor, semiconductor and insulator. (b) Draw the circuit diagram of a Zener diode as a voltage regulator and explain its working.
Energy band diagrams: Conductor (overlap) | Semiconductor (small Eg) | Insulator (large Eg)
Zener diode voltage regulator circuit: V_out = V_Z (constant)
Energy BandsConductor: CB and VB overlap → free electrons always. Semiconductor: Band gap ~1 eV → partial conduction at room temp. Insulator: Band gap >3 eV → no conduction.
Zener Regulator Working Zener diode in reverse bias across load R_L, series resistance R_s. When V_in rises above V_Z (breakdown voltage): Zener conducts in reverse → current through R_s increases → extra voltage drops across R_s. Voltage across R_L (= across Zener) = V_Z = constant
