Section A — Q.1: Multiple Choice Questions 10 × 1 = 10 Marks
Q.1 (i)
The SI unit of electric flux is:
✔ Answer: (A) N·m²/C
Explanation Electric flux is defined as Φ = E⃗ · A⃗ = EA cosθ Units: E is in N/C and A is in m² Unit of Φ = N/C × m² = N·m²/C
Q.1 (ii)
When the separation between plates of a capacitor is doubled, its capacitance:
✔ Answer: (B) becomes half
Explanation C = ε₀A/d If d is doubled → d' = 2d C' = ε₀A/2d = C/2 Capacitance becomes half
Q.1 (iii)
The SI unit of self-inductance is:
✔ Answer: (C) Henry
Explanation e = −L(dI/dt) → L = e/(dI/dt) Unit = Volt × second / Ampere = V·s/A 1 Henry (H) = 1 V·s/A = 1 Ω·s
Q.1 (iv)
When light travels from glass to air, it:
✔ Answer: (B) bends away from the normal
Explanation By Snell's law: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂ n_glass > n_air, so sinθ₂ > sinθ₁ → θ₂ > θ₁ Refracted ray bends away from the normal when going from denser to rarer medium
Q.1 (v)
The energy of a photon is given by:
✔ Answer: (B) hν
Explanation Planck's quantum theory: energy is quantised E = hν = hc/λ where h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s (Planck's constant), ν = frequency
Q.1 (vi)
The half-life of a radioactive substance is 10 years. After 40 years, the fraction remaining is:
✔ Answer: (B) 1/16
Solution Given: T₁/₂ = 10 years, t = 40 years Number of half-lives n = t / T₁/₂ = 40/10 = 4 N/N₀ = (1/2)ⁿ = (1/2)⁴ = 1/16 Fraction remaining = 1/16
Q.1 (vii)
In the photoelectric effect, the stopping potential depends on:
✔ Answer: (B) frequency of light
Explanation Einstein's photoelectric equation: eV₀ = hν − φ Stopping potential V₀ = (hν − φ)/e V₀ depends only on frequency ν (not on intensity) Intensity determines number of electrons (photocurrent), not stopping potential
Q.1 (viii)
A transistor has β = 100 and I_b = 20 μA. The collector current I_c is:
✔ Answer: (B) 2 mA
Solution Given: β = 100, I_b = 20 μA = 20 × 10⁻⁶ A I_c = β × I_b = 100 × 20 × 10⁻⁶ = 2000 × 10⁻⁶ A I_c = 2 × 10⁻³ A = 2 mA
Q.1 (ix)
In Young's double-slit experiment, if the slit separation is halved, the fringe width:
✔ Answer: (B) doubles
Explanation β = λD/d If d is halved → d' = d/2 β' = λD/(d/2) = 2λD/d = 2β Fringe width doubles
Q.1 (x)
Lenz's law is a consequence of the law of conservation of:
✔ Answer: (B) energy
Explanation Lenz's law: The induced current flows in such a direction that it opposes the change in magnetic flux causing it. If the induced current aided the flux change, it would produce more change → more current → perpetual motion. This would violate conservation of energy. Hence Lenz's law upholds energy conservation.

Q.2 (v)
What is total internal reflection (TIR)?
DefinitionWhen a ray of light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, the ray is completely reflected back into the denser medium. This is called Total Internal Reflection.
Conditions (1) Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.
(2) Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle (i > C).
ExamplesOptical fibre, diamond sparkle, mirage

Q.2 (vii)

DefinitionA rectifier is an electronic circuit that converts alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC). It uses one or more p-n junction diodes.
Types (1) Half-wave rectifier: Uses one diode. Allows only one half-cycle. Output frequency = input frequency.
(2) Full-wave rectifier: Uses two or four diodes. Utilises both half-cycles. Output frequency = 2 × input frequency.

Q.2 (ix)
Define nuclear binding energy.
DefinitionNuclear binding energy is the energy required to completely separate all nucleons (protons and neutrons) in a nucleus from one another, overcoming the nuclear force.
FormulaB.E. = Δm × c²   where Δm = mass defect = (sum of individual nucleon masses) − (actual nuclear mass)
SignificanceGreater the B.E. per nucleon, more stable the nucleus. Fe-56 has highest B.E. per nucleon (≈ 8.8 MeV).

Q.3 (v)
Explain Huygens' principle of wave propagation.
Principle Point 1: Every point on a given wavefront acts as a fresh source of secondary spherical wavelets which spread in all directions with the same speed as the original wave. Point 2: The new wavefront at any subsequent instant of time is the forward envelope (common tangent) of all these secondary wavelets.
ApplicationsExplains reflection of light, refraction of light, and diffraction of light using wave theory.
Q.3 (vi)
State the laws of photoelectric effect.
Laws 1. For a given metal, there exists a minimum frequency (ν₀) called threshold frequency below which no photoelectric emission occurs, regardless of intensity. 2. The photoelectric current is directly proportional to the intensity of incident light (above threshold frequency). 3. The maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons depends only on frequency of light, not on intensity. 4. The photoelectric effect is an instantaneous process (no time delay).

Q.4 (i)
Derive the expression for electric field E due to a uniformly charged ring on its axis.
SetupConsider a ring of radius R and total charge Q. Find E at point P on the axis at distance x from the centre.
Step 1 Consider a small element dq of the ring. Distance from element to P: r = √(R² + x²) Electric field due to dq at P: dE = k·dq/r² = k·dq/(R²+x²)
Step 2 By symmetry, the components of dE perpendicular to the axis cancel out. Only axial components (along x) add up: dE_x = dE · cosθ = dE · x/r = k·dq·x/(R²+x²)^(3/2)
Result E = ∫dE_x = kQx/(R² + x²)^(3/2)
Answer
E = kQx / (R² + x²)^(3/2)   directed along the axis

Q.5 (i)
(a) State and prove Gauss's law. (b) Derive electric field due to an infinite plane sheet of charge.
StatementThe total electric flux through any closed surface (Gaussian surface) is equal to 1/ε₀ times the total charge enclosed within that surface.
∮E⃗·dA⃗ = q_enc/ε₀
Proof For a point charge q, the electric field at distance r: E = kq/r² = q/(4πε₀r²) Choose Gaussian surface = sphere of radius r centred on q Φ = ∮E·dA = E × 4πr² = [q/(4πε₀r²)] × 4πr² Φ = q/ε₀ ✓ (proved)
Infinite Plane Sheet Let plane sheet have surface charge density σ (C/m²) By symmetry, E is perpendicular to sheet, pointing outwards from both surfaces Gaussian surface: a cylinder (pillbox) of cross-sectional area A, passing through sheet Only flat faces contribute to flux: Φ = EA + EA = 2EA Charge enclosed = σA By Gauss's law: 2EA = σA/ε₀
Answer
E = σ/2ε₀ (for each side of an infinite plane sheet)

Q.5 (v)

Q.5 (vi)
(a) Energy band diagrams for conductor, semiconductor and insulator. (b) Zener diode as voltage regulator.
ConductorValence band and conduction band overlap. Free electrons always available at room temperature. Very high conductivity. e.g., Copper, Silver.
SemiconductorSmall band gap (~1 eV). e.g., Si = 1.1 eV, Ge = 0.67 eV. At room temperature, some electrons gain enough thermal energy to jump to conduction band. Conductivity between conductor and insulator.
InsulatorLarge band gap (> 3 eV). e.g., Diamond = 5.4 eV, Glass = ~5 eV. Electrons cannot cross this large gap at room temperature. Very low / no conductivity.
Zener Diode as Voltage Regulator Zener diode connected in reverse bias across load R_L, with series resistance R_s. When input voltage V_in > V_Z (Zener breakdown voltage), Zener conducts in reverse. Zener maintains constant V_Z across load despite variations in V_in or load current. Excess voltage drops across R_s. Output voltage V_out = V_Z = constant (regulated output)
