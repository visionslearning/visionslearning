Answer the following questions in one sentence each.
5 Marks
Ch1 i. State Kepler's Third Law of planetary motion.
Kepler's Third Law (Law of Periods) states that the square of the orbital period (T²) of a planet is directly proportional to the cube of its mean orbital radius (R³): T² ∝ R³.
Ch4 ii. What is Fleming's Left Hand Rule?
Fleming's Left Hand Rule states that if the index finger points in the direction of the magnetic field, the middle finger in the direction of current, then the thumb points in the direction of the force (motion) experienced by the conductor.
Ch3 iii. What is a precipitate? Give one example of a precipitation reaction.
A precipitate is an insoluble solid that forms when two solutions react; e.g., Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ (white precipitate) + 2NaCl.
Ch5 iv. What is meant by 'thermal equilibrium'?
Thermal equilibrium is the state when two objects in contact reach the same temperature and there is no net flow of heat between them.
Ch2 v. What is electronegativity? How does it vary across a period?
Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract shared electrons towards itself in a covalent bond; it increases from left to right across a period as nuclear charge increases.

Ch1 A. Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion (2 marks)
  • First Law (Law of Orbits): Every planet revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one of the two foci of the ellipse.
  • Second Law (Law of Areas): The line joining the planet to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time → planet moves fastest when nearest the Sun (perihelion) and slowest when farthest (aphelion).
  • Third Law (Law of Periods): The square of the orbital period (T) of a planet is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (R) of its orbit. T² ∝ R³ or T²/R³ = constant
  • Newton used Kepler's third law to derive his Law of Gravitation.
Ch2 B. Trends in Modern Periodic Table: Ionisation energy and metallic character (2 marks)
  • Ionisation energy: The energy required to remove the outermost electron from a neutral gaseous atom.
  • Across a period (left → right): ionisation energy increases (more protons hold electrons tighter).
  • Down a group (top → bottom): ionisation energy decreases (outer electrons farther from nucleus, easier to remove).
  • Metallic character: The tendency of an element to lose electrons and form positive ions.
  • Across a period (left → right): metallic character decreases (elements become less metallic, more non-metallic).
  • Down a group: metallic character increases (easier to lose electrons from larger atoms).
Ch4 C. Electric generator — principle and use (2 marks)
  • Principle: Electromagnetic induction — when a coil rotates in a magnetic field, the magnetic flux through it changes, inducing an EMF (Faraday's Law). This is the motor effect in reverse.
  • Working: A coil is rotated between the poles of a magnet (or by rotating the magnet around the coil). The changing flux induces an alternating EMF in the coil.
  • AC generator: Uses slip rings → produces alternating current (AC).
  • DC generator: Uses split-ring commutator → converts AC to direct current (DC).
  • Energy conversion: Mechanical energy → Electrical energy.
  • Uses: Power stations, portable generators, bicycle dynamos.

Ch5 D. Land breeze and sea breeze (convection in nature) (2 marks)
  • Land and sea breezes are natural examples of convection currents in air.
  • Sea breeze (day): During the day, land heats up faster than the sea → air over land rises (becomes less dense) → cool air from sea flows towards land → sea breeze blows from sea to land.
  • Land breeze (night): During the night, land cools faster than sea → air over sea is warmer and rises → cool air from land flows towards sea → land breeze blows from land to sea.
  • These are examples of natural convection due to temperature difference between land and water.
  • Similar convection currents cause trade winds and monsoons on a larger scale.
Ch6 E. Dispersion of light and rainbow (2 marks)
  • Dispersion of light: The splitting of white light into its constituent colours (VIBGYOR) when it passes through a prism is called dispersion. It occurs because different colours (wavelengths) of light have different refractive indices in glass/water.
  • Violet has highest refractive index (bends most); Red has lowest (bends least).
  • Order: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (VIBGYOR).
  • Rainbow: A rainbow is formed by dispersion of sunlight by raindrops in the atmosphere.
    • Sunlight enters a water droplet → refraction + dispersion occurs.
    • Total internal reflection inside the drop reflects the light.
    • Light exits the drop → refraction again → colours separate.
    • Red is at the outer edge (least bent), Violet at inner edge (most bent).

Ch2 A. Explain the position of elements in the Modern Periodic Table. How are metals, non-metals and metalloids distinguished? (4 marks)
Structure of the Modern Periodic Table:
  • Contains 118 elements, arranged in 18 groups (vertical) and 7 periods (horizontal).
  • Elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic number.
  • Elements in the same group have similar valence electron configuration and similar chemical properties.
Metals (left side of periodic table):
  • Found on the left side and centre (Groups 1, 2, and transition metals).
  • Properties: lustrous, malleable, ductile, good conductors of heat and electricity, lose electrons easily, form positive ions.
  • Examples: Na, Mg, Al, Fe, Cu, Zn, Au, Ag
Non-metals (right side):
  • Found on the upper right side (Groups 14–17) plus noble gases (Group 18).
  • Properties: non-lustrous, brittle, poor conductors, gain electrons easily, form negative ions or share electrons.
  • Examples: C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl, Br, I
Metalloids (along the staircase line):
  • Elements along the diagonal dividing metals and non-metals.
  • Show intermediate properties of both metals and non-metals.
  • Examples: B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te — called semiconductors; used in electronic chips (Si, Ge).
Ch5 B. What is the principle of calorimetry? State the law of heat exchange and apply it to derive a formula for finding the specific heat of a metal. (4 marks)
Calorimetry:
  • Calorimetry is the measurement of heat exchange during physical or chemical processes using a calorimeter (an insulated container).
Principle / Law of Heat Exchange:
  • When two bodies at different temperatures are mixed (in absence of heat loss), the heat lost by the hotter body equals the heat gained by the cooler body. Heat lost = Heat gained
Experiment — Finding specific heat of a metal:
  • Setup: Heat a metal piece (mass m₁, specific heat c₁) to temperature T₁. Place it in a calorimeter containing water (mass m₂, specific heat c₂ = 4200 J/kg·K) at temperature T₂. Final temperature = T (equilibrium).
  • Heat lost by metal: Q₁ = m₁ × c₁ × (T₁ – T)
  • Heat gained by water: Q₂ = m₂ × c₂ × (T – T₂)
  • By law of heat exchange: Q₁ = Q₂ m₁c₁(T₁ – T) = m₂c₂(T – T₂) c₁ = [m₂c₂(T – T₂)] / [m₁(T₁ – T)]
  • Since m₁, m₂, c₂, T₁, T₂, T are all known or measured, c₁ (specific heat of metal) can be calculated.
Ch6 C. Explain the phenomena of refraction through a prism. How does a prism cause dispersion of white light? Name the colours of the visible spectrum. (4 marks)
Refraction through a Prism:
  • A prism is a triangular glass body with two refracting surfaces inclined at an angle (angle of prism, A).
  • When a ray of light enters the prism, it refracts (bends) at the first surface (towards normal as it enters glass).
  • Inside the prism, light travels at an angle and strikes the second surface where it refracts again (away from normal as it exits).
  • The total bending of the emergent ray from the original incident direction is called the angle of deviation (D).
  • The deviation depends on the angle of incidence, angle of prism, and refractive index of glass.
Dispersion by a Prism:
  • White light is composed of seven colours (VIBGYOR) with different wavelengths.
  • Different colours have different refractive indices in glass (violet highest, red lowest).
  • So each colour bends by a different amount → white light splits into a band of colours — this is called the spectrum.
  • Violet deviates most (highest n), Red deviates least (lowest n).
Visible Spectrum (VIBGYOR):
  • Violet | Indigo | Blue | Green | Yellow | Orange | Red
  • Wavelength range: ~380 nm (violet) to ~750 nm (red)

Ch1 A. A satellite is orbiting Earth at a height where g = 8 m/s². If the orbital radius is 6.8 × 10⁶ m, find the orbital velocity of the satellite. (2 marks)
Given: g = 8 m/s², r = 6.8 × 10⁶ m
Formula: Centripetal acceleration = g = v²/r v = √(g × r)
v = √(8 × 6.8 × 10⁶)
v = √(5.44 × 10⁷)
v = 7.38 × 10³ m/s
∴ Orbital velocity ≈ 7380 m/s ≈ 7.4 km/s
Ch4 B. A 100 W bulb is used for 5 hours daily. Calculate the energy consumed in one month (30 days) in kWh. (2 marks)
Given: P = 100 W = 0.1 kW, daily use = 5 hours, days = 30
Formula: Energy (kWh) = Power (kW) × Time (h)
Energy per day = 0.1 × 5 = 0.5 kWh
Energy per month = 0.5 × 30
∴ Energy consumed = 15 kWh (= 15 units)
Ch5 C. A calorimeter contains 200 g of water at 20°C. A 100 g metal piece at 80°C is dropped in. The final temperature is 25°C. Find the specific heat of the metal. (c_water = 4200 J/kg·K) (2 marks)
Given: m₁(metal)=0.1 kg, T₁=80°C; m₂(water)=0.2 kg, T₂=20°C; T=25°C; c₂=4200
Law of heat exchange: m₁c₁(T₁–T) = m₂c₂(T–T₂)
0.1 × c₁ × (80–25) = 0.2 × 4200 × (25–20)
0.1 × c₁ × 55 = 0.2 × 4200 × 5
5.5 × c₁ = 4200
c₁ = 4200 / 5.5
∴ Specific heat of metal ≈ 763.6 J/kg·K
Ch6 D. The wavelength of light in vacuum is 500 nm. If the refractive index of water is 1.33, find the wavelength of this light in water. (2 marks)
Given: λ₀ = 500 nm, n = 1.33
Formula: n = λ₀ / λ_m → λ_m = λ₀ / n
λ_m = 500 / 1.33
λ_m = 375.9 nm
∴ Wavelength in water ≈ 376 nm
Ch4 E. Three resistors of 3 Ω, 6 Ω and 9 Ω are connected in series. Find the total resistance and current if connected to 36 V supply. (2 marks)
Given: R₁ = 3 Ω, R₂ = 6 Ω, R₃ = 9 Ω, V = 36 V
Series: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
R_total = 3 + 6 + 9 = 18 Ω
Current: I = V/R = 36/18
∴ Total resistance = 18 Ω, Current = 2 A
