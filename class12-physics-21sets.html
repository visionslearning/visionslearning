Q.3 — Short Answer I (any 7) 📋 Based on HSC 2020–2024 14 Marks (2M each)
i.
State and write the mathematical form of the theorem of perpendicular axes🟠 HSC 2024·2022·2021. Give one application.
Statement: MOI of a laminar object about axis ⊥ to its plane = sum of MOIs about two mutually ⊥ axes in its plane, all concurrent.
I_z = I_x + I_y
Application: For disc: I_z = ½MR², by symmetry I_x = I_y = ¼MR²
ii.
Find the MOI of a uniform disc about a tangent in its plane using parallel and perpendicular axis theorems.
I_diameter = ¼MR² (by perpendicular axes)
I_tangent = I_diameter + MR² (parallel axes, h = R)
I_tangent = ¼MR² + MR² = 5/4 MR²
iii.
Write the differences between moment of inertia and radius of gyration.
MOI (I): I = Σmᵢrᵢ² — depends on mass AND its distribution about axis. Unit: kg·m²
Radius of gyration (K): I = MK² — describes how mass is distributed; K = √(I/M). Unit: m
MOI changes with axis; K is effective distance from axis.
iv.
What is the significance of the moment of inertia in rotational motion?
MOI is the rotational analogue of mass (inertia). It measures resistance to change in angular velocity.
• Larger I → harder to start/stop rotation
• F = ma → τ = Iα; p = mv → L = Iω
• Used in flywheels (store rotational energy), gyroscopes
v.
State and explain the law of conservation of angular momentum 🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·2020 with one example.
Law: If τ_net = 0, then L = Iω = constant.
Example: Diver — stretches body before jump (large I, small ω). After leaving board, folds body (small I) → ω increases → more rotations in air. I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
vi.
Derive the expression for kinetic energy of rolling without slipping: E = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)
E = ½Mv² + ½Iω² (translational + rotational)
I = MK², ω = v/R
E = ½Mv² + ½MK²(v/R)² = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)

Q.4 — Short Answer II (any 4) 📋 Based on HSC 2020–2024 12 Marks (3M each)
i.
State and prove the theorem of parallel axes 🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·2020 for moment of inertia.
Statement: I_O = I_C + Mh²
Proof: For mass element dm at D:
I_O = ∫(DO)²dm = ∫([DC]²+2NC·h+h²)dm
= ∫DC²dm + 2h∫NC·dm + h²∫dm
∫DC²dm = I_C; ∫dm = M; ∫NC·dm = 0 (definition of CM)
∴ I_O = I_C + Mh² ✓
ii.
Derive the expression for angular momentum L = Iω🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2021·2020 for a rigid body.
Consider N particles rotating with same ω. For first particle: L₁ = p₁r₁ = m₁v₁r₁ = m₁r₁²ω
L = Σmᵢrᵢ²·ω = Iω
L = Iω (analogous to p = mv) ✓
iii.
Prove that T_B − T_A = 6mg🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021 for vertical circular motion of mass on a string.
At top: T_A + mg = mv_A²/r … (1)
At bottom: T_B − mg = mv_B²/r … (2)
Energy conservation: v_B² − v_A² = 4rg … (3)
(2)−(1): T_B − T_A − 2mg = m(v_B²−v_A²)/r = 4mg
∴ T_B − T_A = 6mg ✓ (independent of v and r)
iv.
What is a conical pendulum🟠 HSC 2023·2022·2021? Derive the expression for its period: T = 2π√(Lcosθ/g)
Bob of mass m, string L at angle θ with vertical.
T₀cosθ = mg … (1); T₀sinθ = mω²Lsinθ … (2)
(2)/(1): tanθ = ω²Lcosθ/g → ω² = g/(Lcosθ)
T = 2π/ω = 2π√(Lcosθ/g) ✓
String can NEVER become horizontal (would need infinite speed).
v.
Derive expression for acceleration of a body rolling down an inclined plane.
Mgh = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²) [energy conservation]
v² = 2g·s sinθ/(1 + K²/R²); using v² = 2as:
a = g sinθ/(1 + K²/R²) ✓
Rolling is slower than sliding (1 + K²/R² > 1).
vi.
What is the minimum speed at the top of a vertical circle (string)? Derive it.
At top: T_A + mg = mv_A²/r
For minimum speed, T_A = 0 (string just taut):
v_A(min) = √(rg)
At bottom using energy conservation (h = 2r):
v_B(min) = √(5rg)
vii.
Derive τ = Iα🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·2020 for a rigid body.
For N particles with angular acc α: tangential acc aᵢ = rᵢα
Force on first: f₁ = m₁a₁ = m₁r₁α; Torque: τ₁ = m₁r₁²α
Total τ = (Σmᵢrᵢ²)α = Iα
τ = Iα ✓ (analogous to F = ma)
Q.5 — Long Answer (any 2) 📋 Based on HSC 2020–2024 8 Marks (4M each)
a.
State and prove the theorem of perpendicular axes for a laminar body. Apply it to find MOI of a disc about its diameter.
Statement: I_z = I_x + I_y (for laminar objects only)
Proof: For mass element dm at P(x,y): distances from x, y, z axes are y, x, √(x²+y²)
I_x = ∫y²dm; I_y = ∫x²dm
I_z = ∫(x²+y²)dm = I_x + I_y ✓
Application (disc diameter):
I_z = ½MR² (own axis); by symmetry I_x = I_y = I_diameter
I_z = I_x + I_y = 2I_diameter → I_diameter = ¼MR²
b.
Derive an expression for the period and frequency of revolution of a conical pendulum. What are the factors affecting it?
Setup: String length L, mass m, semi-vertical angle θ, radius r = Lsinθ
Vertical: T₀cosθ = mg
Horizontal (centripetal): T₀sinθ = mω²r = mω²Lsinθ
Dividing: tanθ = ω²Lcosθ/g → ω = √(g/Lcosθ)
T = 2π√(Lcosθ/g); n = (1/2π)√(g/Lcosθ)
Factors: (1) Length L — longer string → longer period (2) θ — larger θ → smaller cosθ → shorter T (higher frequency) (3) g — higher gravity → shorter period. String can NEVER be horizontal (θ = 90°) → infinite frequency impossible.
c.
Derive expression for the MOI of a uniform disc about its central axis. I = ½MR²
Consider disc of mass M, radius R. Surface density σ = M/πR²
Elementary ring of radius r, width dr: dm = σ·2πr·dr = (2M/R²)·r·dr
dI = dm·r² = (2M/R²)·r³·dr
I = ∫₀ᴿ (2M/R²)r³dr = (2M/R²)·[r⁴/4]₀ᴿ = (2M/R²)·R⁴/4
I = ½MR² ✓
d.
State and prove the law of conservation of angular momentum. Give two applications from daily life.
Statement: If τ_ext = 0, then L = Iω = constant.
Proof: τ = dL/dt. If τ = 0 → dL/dt = 0 → L = constant → I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
Application 1 — Ballet dancer: Pulls arms in → I decreases → ω increases (spins faster). Arms out → I increases → ω decreases.
Application 2 — Diver: Stretches body on board (large I, small ω). Folds mid-air → I decreases sharply → ω increases → more rotations. Stretches before water entry → smooth entry.

N1. A flywheel (uniform disc) of mass 20 kg and diameter 0.6 m rotates at 1200 rpm. Find its angular momentum and rotational KE. (π² = 10)
M = 20 kg | R = 0.3 m | n = 20 rps | ω = 2πn = 40π rad/s

L = Iω = 0.9×40π = 36π ≈ 113 kg·m²/s
KE = ½Iω² = ½×0.9×(40π)² = ½×0.9×16000π² = ½×0.9×160000 = 72000 J
∴ L ≈ 113 kg·m²/s; KE = 72,000 J ✓
N2. A solid sphere of mass 2 kg and radius 0.1 m rolls on a horizontal surface at 5 m/s. Find total KE.
M = 2 kg | R = 0.1 m | v = 5 m/s | K²/R² = 2/5 (solid sphere)
E = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²) = ½×2×25×(1 + 2/5) = 25×(7/5)
∴ E = 35 J ✓

At B: T_B − mg = mv_B²/r; using v_B² = v_A² + 4rg → v_B(min) = √(5rg)

Explain the working of a banked road. Derive the expression for angle of banking and speed limits (v_max and v_min) for a vehicle on a banked road with friction.

With friction (μ):
For v > v_safe, friction acts down the incline:
v_max = √[rg(tanθ+μ)/(1−μ tanθ)]
For v < v_safe, friction acts up the incline:
v_min = √[rg(tanθ−μ)/(1+μ tanθ)]
If μ ≥ tan θ → v_min = 0 (car can remain stationary on banked road)

ii.
Explain two applications of Bernoulli's theorem.
1. Aerofoil (aircraft lift): Curved upper surface → air moves faster over top → lower pressure on top → net upward force (lift).
2. Atomiser/spray gun: Fast air over narrow tube → low pressure → liquid sucked up and sprayed.
iii.
Derive excess pressure inside a soap bubble🔴 HSC 2024·2023·2022·2021·2020: ΔP = 4T/r.
Soap bubble has 2 surfaces. Each contributes ΔP = 2T/r.
Total ΔP = 2 × (2T/r) = 4T/r ✓
Liquid drop (1 surface): ΔP = 2T/r
iv.
Write the differences between viscosity🟠 HSC 2023·2022·2021 of liquids and gases on heating.
Liquids: Viscosity decreases with temperature — cohesive forces between molecules weaken → easier flow. e.g., honey flows easily when hot.
Gases: Viscosity increases with temperature — kinetic energy of gas molecules increases → more molecular collisions → more resistance to flow.
v.
Explain capillarity with the formula h = 2T cosθ/ρgr.
Surface tension force (up) = F = T×2πr×cosθ
Weight of liquid = mg = ρ×πr²h×g
At equilibrium: T×2πr cosθ = ρπr²hg
h = 2T cosθ/(ρgr) ✓
Thinner tube → greater rise. Used in plants (xylem vessels).
vi.
Explain the working of a Venturi meter.
Venturi meter measures flow speed using pressure difference. At narrowing (throat), speed increases → pressure decreases (Bernoulli). By measuring P₁ − P₂ (using manometer) and knowing areas A₁ and A₂: v = A₂√(2(P₁−P₂)/ρ(A₁²−A₂²))
Used in pipes, carburettors, aircraft speed measurement.
vii.
Explain the phenomenon of Magnus effect.
When a spinning ball moves through air, the spinning motion drags air around it. On one side, dragged air adds to flow speed → low pressure. Other side: speed reduces → high pressure. Net force perpendicular to motion → ball curves. e.g., spin in cricket/tennis/football.
viii.
Write 3 properties of an ideal fluid.
1. Incompressible — density constant
2. Non-viscous — no internal friction
3. Steady flow — velocity at any point is constant in time
4. Irrotational — no turbulence or eddies
ix.
Explain streamline flow and turbulent flow. What is Reynolds number?
Streamline flow: Smooth, parallel layers; each fluid particle follows a definite path; no mixing of layers.
Turbulent flow: Irregular, chaotic motion; eddies form; energy is wasted.
Reynolds number: Re = ρvd/η
Re < 1000: laminar/streamline; Re > 2000: turbulent
x.
Write 4 factors affecting surface tension of a liquid.
1. Temperature — decreases with increase in temperature (zero at critical temp)
2. Impurities — soluble impurities (soap) decrease T; insoluble (dust) may increase T
3. Electrification — decreases surface tension
4. Nature of liquid — different liquids have different T (Hg = 0.465 N/m; water = 0.073 N/m)
